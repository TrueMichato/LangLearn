## Overall Impression

**LangLearn is a genuinely good teacher hiding behind a dashboard.** Three taps into Learn → Grammar → Lesson 1, the app is delightful and specific: a locked linear curriculum, romaji support, TTS with speed control, inline quizzes, cited sources. That is better than most paid apps.

But a new student never gets there. Onboarding ends warm and then drops them onto a **3.62-viewport wall of zeros**, whose single loudest call to action — "Add a word" — asks a beginner to type 食べる / たべる / "to eat", which is precisely the knowledge they came here to acquire. And the *one* place the app actually tries to route them somewhere useful, the "Continue Grammar" suggestion, is wired to a route that doesn't exist and renders a blank white screen.

Your instinct is right, and the diagnosis is narrower than "the design is unclear": **the app never sequences itself.** It presents an inventory of 12 activities and a scoreboard, and asks a beginner to be their own curriculum designer on day one. The single biggest opportunity is to make the first session a *guided lesson*, not a dashboard.

## What's Working

1. **The guided curricula already exist — they're just buried.** Both `Grammar.tsx` (LessonView) and `VocabLessons.tsx` implement a **locked linear path** with only Lesson 1 open. That is exactly the "you are here → do this next" scaffolding a newcomer needs. The fix for the biggest problem is mostly a *surfacing* problem, not a *build* problem.
2. **The kind-learning contract is honored in copy, consistently.** "🎉 No cards to review!", "every bit counts", amber-not-red grading, no streak-shaming. Detector confirms the discipline too: 0 `gray-*`, 0 gradient text, 1 remaining side-stripe (down from 12). The rebuild away from "AI slop" genuinely landed.
3. **Technically clean and fast.** Zero JS errors, zero failed content fetches across all five newcomer pages, correct `BASE_URL` handling, working cold-start, real offline-first architecture. Nothing here is broken *engineering* — the gaps are all sequencing and framing.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] "Continue Grammar" navigates to a blank, chrome-less dead end
- **What**: `src/lib/study-suggestions.ts:91` sets `route: '/learn/grammar'`. `src/App.tsx` registers `/grammar`, never `/learn/grammar`, and there is **no catch-all route**. Console confirms `No routes matched location "/learn/grammar"`. Because the unmatched path sits under the `<Route element={<Shell/>}>` layout group, **nothing renders — not even the header or bottom nav.**
- **Why it matters**: This suggestion fires for essentially every new user (they have grammar content and zero words). The app's single most-encouraged first action produces a white screen with no way back except browser Back. Nothing destroys a first-run more efficiently.
- **Fix**: Change line 91 to `route: '/grammar'`. Then add `<Route path="*" element={<Navigate to="/" replace />} />` inside the Shell group in `App.tsx` so no future typo can ever strand a user. Add a test asserting every `route` string in `study-suggestions.ts` matches a registered path.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Onboarding ends on a dashboard instead of inside a lesson
- **What**: `OnboardingOverlay.tsx` captures only language selection (with **ja + ru pre-checked**, `settingsStore.ts:94`), explains "the **SM-2 algorithm**" to a novice (line 176), then calls `completeOnboarding()` straight to the empty Home. No proficiency, no goal, no first action. A user who taps "Skip" is silently enrolled in **two** languages they may not have chosen.
- **Why it matters**: This is the one moment you have a new learner's full attention, and it ends with a task-less scoreboard. Peak-end is violated at the worst possible place — the warm peak is immediately followed by the emptiest screen in the app.
- **Fix**: Cut "SM-2 algorithm" (say "reviews come back right before you'd forget"). Make language selection single-select-by-default with "add another later" as the secondary path. Add one step capturing level (never studied / know some / returning) and a daily goal. **End onboarding by launching Vocab Lesson 1 or Letters — not by dismissing to Home.**
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable onboard`

### [P1] The zero-data Dashboard is a 3.62-viewport wall of zeros with no single path
- **What**: `Dashboard.tsx` unconditionally renders StudyPlan, the add-word banner, 4 StatCards, SuggestedNext, MistakeDeck, DailyChallenge, WeeklyGoals, ReviewForecast, Milestones, VocabSize, an "SRS Analytics" link, streak, badges, study tip, and heat map — **all empty on day one**. Measured 3058px at an 844px viewport. Only `LanguageStats` is gated on `totalWords > 0`.
- **Why it matters**: Violates the app's own stated principle ("one obvious next action per screen") and 6 of 8 cognitive-load checks. To a beginner, 0/65 locked badges and four 0% milestones read as *"did I do something wrong?"*, not *"here's your potential."* It also leaks jargon ("SRS Analytics") onto the first screen.
- **Fix**: Gate the instrumentation block (Milestones, ReviewForecast, Analytics link, BadgeCollection, HeatMap, WeeklyGoals) behind `totalWords > 0 || totalStudySeconds > 0`, mirroring the existing `LanguageStats` pattern. In its place render **one** first-run card: "New here? Start with Greetings →" pointing at Vocab Lesson 1, with Letters and the Reader Library as the two secondary options. Rename "SRS Analytics" to "Your stats".
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable distill`

### [P1] The hero first-action asks the beginner for knowledge they don't have
- **What**: The zero-state hero (`Dashboard.tsx:159-171`) is "Get started by adding your first word ✨" → `AddWordModal`, which demands Word (食べる) + Reading (たべる) + Meaning.
- **Why it matters**: The loudest CTA in the app is unusable from zero. It quizzes instead of teaching, and it silently assumes the user arrived with a vocabulary list — an Anki-import mental model, not a beginner's. Review's empty state repeats the same mistake ("+ Add some words").
- **Fix**: Make the primary zero-state CTA **"Learn your first words" → `/vocab-lessons`** (or `/letters/:lang` for script-first languages). Demote manual Add-Word to a text link ("Already have a word list? Add one manually"). Change Review's empty state to "Your reviews start after your first lesson →".
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] The Learn hub is a 12-item toolbox with no recommended sequence
- **What**: `Learn.tsx` renders **12 equal-weight cards** across groups of 6 / 4 / 2. Its own pedagogy note ("comprehension before output") never becomes an actual path. Measured 1.46× viewport, so half the options are below the fold.
- **Why it matters**: 12 options at one decision point is 3× the working-memory limit. A beginner cannot tell that "Cloze Practice" and "Minimal Pairs" are advanced and "Letters" is where they should start. This is where the app's depth becomes a liability.
- **Fix**: Add a pinned "Start here" step at the top reflecting the user's actual next unlock, keep 3–4 beginner activities visible, and collapse the rest behind "Explore more (8)". Order by the pedagogy note the page already states.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] The best beginner content is two taps behind a blank input box
- **What**: `Reader.tsx` defaults to a "Paste your text here…" textarea; the curated **Library** of ~10 graded texts per language is a secondary tab. Vocab and Grammar lessons are both 2 taps inside Learn.
- **Why it matters**: The Reader's default state asks a beginner to supply their own Japanese text. The app ships genuinely good graded content and hides it behind an empty form — the same "bring your own material" assumption as Add-Word.
- **Fix**: Default the Reader to the **Library** tab whenever the user has no saved texts; make Import the secondary tab. Surface one Library text on the first-run Dashboard card.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable onboard`

### [P2] The header's most prominent CTA is a mystery stopwatch
- **What**: `StudyTimer.tsx`'s "▶ Start studying" calls `start('srs')` — it starts a clock labeled with the raw activity key and **navigates nowhere**.
- **Why it matters**: The label promises "take me to studying"; the result is a ticking timer on the same empty screen. For Casey (5-minute burst on a commute), that's the whole session spent.
- **Fix**: Either relabel to "Track time" with a clock icon, or make it route to the top suggested activity and start the timer there. Never render raw activity keys — map `'srs'` → "Review". Also fix `VocabLessonView.tsx:87`, which starts the timer as `'grammar'` during a *vocabulary* lesson (this also corrupts the `activity === 'grammar'` logic in `study-suggestions.ts:169`).
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable clarify`

### [P2] The first button a new user presses fails WCAG AA
- **What**: The onboarding **Next** button uses `.gradient-primary` (`index.css:215`), which ends in `#8b5cf6` — violet-500, not the designated `indigo-600`. Measured white-on-violet contrast: **4.2:1**, below the 4.5:1 floor. The muted `#62748e` on `#e2e8f0` pairing measures **3.9:1** and recurs on every page. Separately, `min-h-[44px]` covers only **113 of 297** buttons (38%), and 6 form fields lack `id`/`name`.
- **Why it matters**: PRODUCT.md calls AA a floor and a product requirement. The violation is on the literal first interaction, and `.gradient-primary` propagates the off-palette violet through 18 files, quietly breaking the One Accent Rule.
- **Fix**: Retarget `.gradient-primary` to the indigo ramp (`#4f46e5 → #4338ca`) or replace it with solid `bg-indigo-600` on buttons. Darken the muted-on-slate-200 pairing to `slate-600`. Add `id`/`name` to the 6 flagged fields. Audit the 184 buttons under 44px.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable audit`

## Questions to Consider

1. What if onboarding **ended inside the user's first two-minute lesson** — so they earn their first XP *before* they ever see a stat? Would the wall of zeros even be a problem then?
2. Should the Dashboard **hide every progress instrument until there is data to show**, so day one is a single "begin here" path and day seven is the scoreboard they've earned?
3. Is manual "Add a word" the **wrong first metaphor entirely**? What if first words could *only* arrive from lessons and the reader — so the app always teaches before it tests?
4. Could the 12-tile Learn hub collapse into **one recommended next step** with everything else in an "Explore more" drawer — turning an inventory into a curriculum?
5. For "a language or two," should first-run force **one primary language**, with the second explicitly deferred until the first has momentum?
