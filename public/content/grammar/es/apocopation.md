# Apocopation (buen, gran, san, primer, etc.)

**Apocopation** (*apócope* in Spanish) is the dropping of a final vowel or syllable from certain adjectives **before specific nouns**. You'll say **un buen amigo** — never *un bueno amigo*. The rule is small but pervasive: get it wrong and your Spanish instantly sounds non-native.

## What Apocopates Before a Masculine Singular Noun

These adjectives **lose their final -o** when placed **before** a masculine singular noun:

| Full form | Apocopated | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **bueno** | **buen** | un **buen** libro — a good book |
| **malo** | **mal** | un **mal** día — a bad day |
| **primero** | **primer** | el **primer** capítulo — the first chapter |
| **tercero** | **tercer** | el **tercer** piso — the third floor |
| **alguno** | **algún** | **algún** día — some day |
| **ninguno** | **ningún** | **ningún** problema — no problem |
| **uno** | **un** | **un** hombre — a man |
| **postrero** | **postrer** | el **postrer** intento — the final attempt *(literary)* |

Crucial details:

- The drop happens **only** before a **masculine singular** noun.
- After the noun, or before a **feminine / plural** noun, use the **full form**:
  - **un buen amigo** ✓ → **una buena amiga** ✓ → **unos buenos amigos** ✓
  - **el primer día** ✓ → **la primera vez** ✓ → **los primeros días** ✓
- **Algún** and **ningún** carry a written accent because the stress shifts to the final syllable.

## Apocopation Survives a Modifier

The apocope still applies even when **another word** sits between the adjective and the noun:

- **El primer y único intento** — the first and only attempt.
- **Un buen, aunque pequeño, regalo** — a good, though small, gift.

The trigger is being **before** a masculine singular noun in the phrase — not literal adjacency.

## Grande → Gran (Before ANY Singular Noun)

**Grande** is special: it apocopates to **gran** before **any singular noun**, masculine or feminine. Plus, the meaning shifts depending on position:

| Position | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Before noun (singular)** | **gran** | great (importance, quality) |
| **After noun** | **grande** | big (physical size) |

Examples:

- **Un gran hombre** (oon grahn OHM-breh) — a great man.
- **Un hombre grande** (oon OHM-breh GRAHN-deh) — a big (tall, large) man.
- **Una gran ciudad** — a great city.
- **Una ciudad grande** — a big city.
- **Grandes oportunidades** (plural — no apocope) — great opportunities.

**Plural** stays **grandes** in both positions. Only the **singular** apocopates.

## Santo → San (Before Most Male Saints' Names)

**Santo** apocopates to **san** before a **male saint's name** — with one exception: it stays **Santo** when the name **starts with Do- or To-**.

| Apocope | No Apocope |
|---|---|
| **San Pedro** | **Santo Domingo** *(Do-)* |
| **San Juan** | **Santo Tomás** *(To-)* |
| **San José** | **Santo Toribio** |
| **San Pablo** | |
| **San Francisco** | |

The feminine **Santa** **never apocopates**:

- **Santa María**, **Santa Bárbara**, **Santa Teresa**.

This is partly euphonic (avoiding *San Tomás*, which would clash awkwardly with the Sant-/Tom- sounds).

## Ciento → Cien (Before a Noun or Mil/Millones)

**Ciento** (100) becomes **cien** when it directly precedes:

1. **A noun**: **cien personas** (seen pehr-SOH-nahs) — a hundred people.
2. **Mil** or **millones**: **cien mil** (100,000) — **cien millones** (100 million).

It stays **ciento** in compound numbers:

- **ciento uno** (101), **ciento cincuenta** (150), **doscientos** (200 — different word entirely).

| Form | When | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **cien** | Before noun, mil, millones | **cien libros**, **cien mil euros** |
| **ciento** | Counting / compound numbers | **ciento veinte (120)** |

## Cualquiera → Cualquier (Before Any Singular Noun)

**Cualquiera** ("any") apocopates to **cualquier** before any singular noun (masculine or feminine):

- **Cualquier libro** — any book.
- **Cualquier mujer** — any woman.
- **Cualquiera de los dos** *(after noun / standalone)* — either of the two.

The plural is **cualesquiera** (rare, literary) — usually replaced by *cualquiera de* + plural noun.

## Mini-Dialogue

> — Hola, soy **San** Juan. Bueno, en realidad me llamo Juan, pero mi familia me dice "**San** Juan" porque vivo en la calle de **Santo** Domingo. _(soy sahn HWAHN; KAH-yeh deh SAHN-toh doh-MEEN-goh)_
> — ¡Vaya nombre! ¿Es la **primera** vez que vienes? _(ehs lah pree-MEH-rah behs)_
> — Sí, soy el **primer** turista que recibe esta semana. _(soy ehl pree-MEHR too-REES-tah)_
> — Pues bienvenido. ¿Quieres un **buen** café? Aquí lo hacen **buenísimo**.
> — **¡Gracias!** Eres un **gran** anfitrión. _(EH-rehs oon grahn ahn-fee-TRYOHN)_
> — Y aquí no hay **ningún** problema con eso. _(noh ay neen-GOON proh-BLEH-mah)_
>
> Translation: "Hi, I'm 'San Juan'. Well, actually my name is Juan, but my family calls me 'San Juan' because I live on Santo Domingo Street." — "What a name! Is it your first time coming here?" — "Yes, I'm the first tourist they've had this week." — "Well, welcome. Want a good coffee? Here they make it amazingly." — "Thanks! You're a great host." — "And no problem with that here at all."

Notice **san**, **santo** (before Do-), **primera** (before fem. noun → no apocope), **primer** (before masc.), **buen**, **gran**, and **ningún** — all in one short exchange.

## Quick Apocope Reference

- **bueno → buen** | **malo → mal** | **primero → primer** | **tercero → tercer** | **alguno → algún** | **ninguno → ningún** | **uno → un** — only before **masculine singular** nouns.
- **grande → gran** — before **any singular** noun.
- **santo → san** — before **male saint names** (not Do-/To-).
- **ciento → cien** — before **nouns**, *mil*, *millones*.
- **cualquiera → cualquier** — before **any singular** noun.
