# Present Perfect (Pretérito Perfecto Compuesto)

The present perfect connects the past to the present. Formation is straightforward — **haber in the present + past participle** — but its usage varies significantly between Spain and Latin America, making it one of the most important regional differences to understand.

## Formation

**haber (present) + past participle**

The past participle is always **invariable** in this construction — it never changes to agree with the subject or object.

### Haber in the Present Tense

| Person | Form | Pronunciation |
|--------|------|---------------|
| yo | **he** | eh |
| tú | **has** | ahs |
| él/ella/usted | **ha** | ah |
| nosotros | **hemos** | EH-mohs |
| vosotros | **habéis** | ah-BAYS |
| ellos/ustedes | **han** | ahn |

### Regular Past Participles

| Ending | Participle | Example |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| -ar | -ado | hablar → **hablado** |
| -er | -ido | comer → **comido** |
| -ir | -ido | vivir → **vivido** |

### Irregular Past Participles

| Infinitive | Participle | Infinitive | Participle |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| hacer | **hecho** | abrir | **abierto** |
| decir | **dicho** | volver | **vuelto** |
| ver | **visto** | romper | **roto** |
| poner | **puesto** | morir | **muerto** |
| escribir | **escrito** | cubrir | **cubierto** |

## Key Usage — Life Experiences

Both Spain **and** Latin America use the present perfect for **life experiences** with no specific time reference:

- **¿Has estado en Perú?** (ahs ehs-TAH-doh ehn peh-ROO) — Have you (ever) been to Peru?
- **He viajado por toda Sudamérica** — I have traveled throughout South America
- **Nunca he probado el sushi** — I have never tried sushi
- **¿Alguna vez has visto una aurora boreal?** — Have you ever seen the northern lights?

## Regional Difference — Spain vs. Latin America

This is one of the biggest usage splits in the Spanish-speaking world:

### Spain 🇪🇸 — Present Perfect for Recent/Connected Past

In Spain, the present perfect is used freely for recent past events or any action still connected to the present:

- **Hoy he comido pizza** (OH-ee eh koh-MEE-doh PEE-thah) — Today I ate/have eaten pizza
- **Esta semana he trabajado mucho** — This week I have worked a lot
- **Esta mañana me he levantado tarde** — This morning I got up late

### Latin America 🌎 — Preterite Preferred for Recent Events

In most of Latin America, the **simple preterite** (*pretérito indefinido*) is used even for recent events:

- **Hoy comí pizza** (OH-ee koh-MEE pee-SAH) — Today I ate pizza ✅ (standard LatAm)
- **Esta semana trabajé mucho** — This week I worked a lot ✅ (standard LatAm)

The present perfect is **not wrong** in Latin America for recent events, but it sounds formal or bookish in everyday conversation.

| Situation | Spain | Latin America |
|-----------|-------|---------------|
| Recent past (today/this week) | present perfect | **preterite** |
| Life experiences | present perfect | present perfect |
| Clearly present-connected ("yet", "already") | present perfect | either, often preterite |

## Trigger Words

These signal the present perfect — or in LatAm, can appear with the preterite too:

| Word/Phrase | Meaning |
|-------------|---------|
| **hoy** | today |
| **esta semana / este mes / este año** | this week / month / year |
| **ya** | already |
| **todavía no** | not yet |
| **alguna vez** | ever |
| **nunca** | never |
| **recientemente** | recently |

- **¿Ya has terminado?** — Have you finished already?
- **Todavía no he recibido respuesta** — I still haven't received a reply

## Participle Invariability — A Common Mistake

In the present perfect, the past participle **never agrees** with anything. This differs from the *ser + participle* passive:

| Construction | Agreement? | Example |
|-------------|-----------|---------|
| Present perfect (haber + participle) | ❌ No | *Ella ha **escrito** la carta.* |
| Formal passive (ser + participle) | ✅ Yes | *La carta fue **escrita** por ella.* |
