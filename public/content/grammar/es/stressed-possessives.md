# Stressed Possessives (el mío, la tuya, etc.)

Spanish has **two sets** of possessives: **short (unstressed)** ones that go **before** the noun (*mi libro*) and **long (stressed)** ones that go **after** the noun or stand alone as pronouns (*un libro mío*, *el mío*). The stressed forms add emphasis and let you avoid repeating the noun.

## The Two Sets — Side by Side

| Person | Short (before noun) | Long / Stressed (after noun or pronoun) |
|---|---|---|
| yo | **mi / mis** | **mío / mía / míos / mías** |
| tú | **tu / tus** | **tuyo / tuya / tuyos / tuyas** |
| él / ella / usted | **su / sus** | **suyo / suya / suyos / suyas** |
| nosotros | **nuestro / nuestra / nuestros / nuestras** | **nuestro / nuestra / nuestros / nuestras** (same) |
| vosotros | **vuestro / vuestra / vuestros / vuestras** | **vuestro / vuestra / vuestros / vuestras** (same) |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | **su / sus** | **suyo / suya / suyos / suyas** |

Note: **nuestro/a** and **vuestro/a** have a **single form** that serves both positions. Only the others (mi, tu, su) have distinct short and long variants.

## Position 1: After the Noun ("A Friend of Mine")

Place the long possessive **after the noun**, with no article needed:

- **Un amigo mío** (oon ah-MEE-goh MEE-oh) — A friend of mine.
- **Una prima tuya** — A cousin of yours.
- **Unos vecinos nuestros** — Some neighbors of ours.
- **Este libro suyo** — This book of his/hers/yours/theirs.

The English equivalent is "a/some [noun] of mine/yours" — Spanish drops the "of" and uses the long form.

**Agreement rule**: the stressed possessive agrees in **gender and number** with the **thing possessed**, not the owner:

- **una amiga mía** ✓ (a female friend of mine — *mía* matches *amiga*)
- **dos libros nuestros** ✓ (two books of ours — *nuestros* matches *libros*)

## Position 2: Pronoun ("Mine, Yours")

> **el / la / los / las + long form** = standalone pronoun

- **Este es el mío** (EHS-teh ehs ehl MEE-oh) — This one is mine.
- **La casa de Ana es grande; la tuya es pequeña** — Ana's house is big; yours is small.
- **No tengo bolígrafo. ¿Me prestas el tuyo?** — I don't have a pen. Can you lend me yours?
- **Mis hijos juegan con los suyos** — My kids play with theirs.

Notice you **need the article** (el/la/los/las) to make the possessive stand alone as a pronoun.

| Short (with noun) | Standalone Pronoun |
|---|---|
| **mi libro** — my book | **el mío** — mine |
| **tu casa** — your house | **la tuya** — yours |
| **sus llaves** — his keys | **las suyas** — his (ones) |
| **nuestro coche** — our car | **el nuestro** — ours |

## Position 3: After Ser (Predicate Use)

After **ser**, the long form is used without an article:

- **Este libro es mío** (EHS-teh LEE-broh ehs MEE-oh) — This book is mine.
- **La culpa es tuya** — The fault is yours.
- **Los juguetes son nuestros** — The toys are ours.
- **¿Esta llave es suya?** — Is this key yours/his/hers?

Compare with using **de**:

- **Es mío** ≈ **Es de mí** (rarely used) — It's mine.
- **Es de Juan** — It's Juan's. (When the owner is named, prefer **de + name**.)

## The Ambiguity of "Suyo" — and How to Fix It

**Suyo / suya / suyos / suyas** can mean **his, hers, yours (formal), theirs** — context isn't always enough. Spanish disambiguates with **de + pronoun**:

| Ambiguous | Disambiguated |
|---|---|
| **el libro suyo** | el libro **de él** (his) / **de ella** (hers) / **de usted** (your, formal) / **de ellos** (theirs) |
| **Es suyo** | Es **de él** / **de ella** / **de ustedes** / **de ellos** |

This **de + pronoun** trick is used constantly in real Spanish to clarify ownership.

## Stressed vs. Unstressed: Same Word, Different Meaning Position

Compare:

- **Mi amigo** — My friend. *(neutral)*
- **Un amigo mío** — A friend of mine. *(introduces one of several)*
- **El amigo es mío** — The friend is mine. *(predicative)*
- **El mío** — Mine. *(standalone — replaces noun)*

Each form does a different syntactic job.

## Mini-Dialogue

> — ¿De quién es este abrigo? ¿Es **tuyo**? _(deh kyehn ehs EHS-teh ah-BREE-goh, ehs TOO-yoh)_
> — No, no es **mío**. El **mío** es negro. _(noh, noh ehs MEE-oh; ehl MEE-oh ehs NEH-groh)_
> — Pues entonces debe ser de Andrés. ¿O quizá de Marta?
> — Si es **suyo**, lo guardamos. ¡Ah, mira! Aquí está una amiga **nuestra**, Laura. ¿Es **tuyo**, Laura? _(see ehs SOO-yoh; ah-KEE ehs-TAH OO-nah ah-MEE-gah NWEHS-trah)_
> — Sí, es **mío**, gracias.
>
> Translation: "Whose coat is this? Is it yours?" — "No, it's not mine. Mine is black." — "Then it must be Andrés's. Or maybe Marta's?" — "If it's hers/his, we'll keep it. Oh, look! Here's a friend of ours, Laura. Is it yours, Laura?" — "Yes, it's mine, thanks."

Notice **tuyo** (predicative), **mío** (predicative), **el mío** (pronoun with article), **suyo** (ambiguous), and **nuestra** (after noun) — all naturally in one exchange.

## Quick Reference

- **mi/tu/su/nuestro/vuestro + noun** → before the noun (basic).
- **noun + mío/tuyo/suyo/nuestro/vuestro** → after the noun ("of mine, of yours").
- **el/la/los/las + mío/tuyo/suyo/…** → standalone pronoun ("mine, yours").
- **ser + mío/tuyo/suyo/…** → predicate ("is mine, is yours").
