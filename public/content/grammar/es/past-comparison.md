# Comparing Past Tenses (Indefinido vs Imperfecto)

Spanish has two main simple past tenses that learners often confuse. The **pretérito indefinido** reports completed, single events; the **pretérito imperfecto** provides background, habits, and ongoing states. Knowing which to use — and combining them — is one of the most important skills in Spanish storytelling.

## The Core Difference at a Glance

| | Pretérito Indefinido | Pretérito Imperfecto |
|---|---|---|
| **What it expresses** | Completed single event | Ongoing / habitual / descriptive |
| **Mental image** | A snapshot — something happened | A film playing — in progress or repeated |
| **English equivalent** | "I ate" / "I did" | "I was eating" / "I used to eat" |
| **Start / end** | Clear beginning and end | No clear endpoint |

## Trigger Words as a Guide

| Indefinido triggers | Imperfecto triggers |
|---------------------|---------------------|
| **ayer** (yesterday) | **siempre** (always) |
| **anoche** (last night) | **a menudo** (often) |
| **una vez** (once) | **todos los días** (every day) |
| **el año pasado** (last year) | **mientras** (while) |
| **de repente** (suddenly) | **cuando era niño** (when I was a child) |
| **en 2015** (in 2015) | **antes** (before / back then) |
| **hace dos días** (two days ago) | **normalmente** (normally) |

> ⚠️ Trigger words are **guides**, not guarantees. Verbal **aspect** — whether the action is viewed as complete or ongoing — is the real deciding factor.

## Side-by-Side Example Pairs

| Indefinido (completed) | Imperfecto (habitual / ongoing) |
|------------------------|---------------------------------|
| **Comí pizza ayer.** — I ate pizza yesterday. | **Comía pizza todos los viernes.** — I used to eat pizza every Friday. |
| **Llamé a mi mamá.** — I called my mom. | **Llamaba a mi mamá cada semana.** — I used to call my mom every week. |
| **Vivió en Madrid tres años.** — He lived in Madrid for three years (then left). | **Vivía en Madrid cuando era joven.** — He used to live in Madrid when he was young. |

## The Classic Combination: Background + Event

The most powerful pattern uses both tenses together: the imperfecto sets the **scene**, and the indefinido delivers the **plot event** that interrupts or punctuates it.

> **Mientras estudiaba, sonó el teléfono.**
> While I was studying *(background — imperfecto)*, the phone rang *(event — indefinido)*.

> **Cuando llegué a casa, mis hijos dormían.**
> When I arrived home *(event — indefinido)*, my children were sleeping *(background — imperfecto)*.

> **Era una noche tranquila cuando de repente escuchamos un grito.**
> It was a quiet night *(description — imperfecto)* when suddenly we heard a scream *(event — indefinido)*.

The imperfecto is the canvas; the indefinido is the brushstroke.

## The Aspect Concept

The real key is **verbal aspect** — how the speaker views the action:

- **Perfective** (indefinido): the action is viewed as a **whole, complete unit**. *Leí el libro.* (I read the book — and finished it.)
- **Imperfective** (imperfecto): the action is viewed as **in progress or recurring**. *Leía el libro.* (I was reading the book / I used to read books.)

This is why the same verb can carry different meanings depending on tense:

| Verb | Indefinido meaning | Imperfecto meaning |
|------|-------------------|--------------------|
| **saber** | I found out (at that moment) | I knew (ongoing state) |
| **conocer** | I met someone (for the first time) | I knew / was acquainted with |
| **querer** | I tried to | I wanted to |
| **poder** | I managed to | I was able to / could |

- **Supe la verdad ayer** (SOO-peh) — I found out the truth yesterday
- **Sabía la verdad todo el tiempo** (sah-BEE-ah) — I knew the truth all along
