# Passive Voice (ser + participio, se passive)

Spanish has two main ways to express passive meaning. The **formal passive** (ser + past participle) closely mirrors English, while the **se passive** is far more common in everyday speech and writing. Understanding both is essential for reading and listening comprehension.

## Formal Passive: ser + Past Participle

The structure is: **subject + ser (conjugated) + past participle (+ por + agent)**

The past participle must **agree in gender and number** with the subject, just like an adjective.

- **El libro fue escrito por Cervantes** (ehl LEE-broh fweh ehs-KREE-toh) — The book was written by Cervantes
- **La carta fue escrita ayer** (lah KAR-tah fweh ehs-KREE-tah) — The letter was written yesterday
- **Los edificios fueron construidos en 1950** — The buildings were built in 1950
- **Las leyes son aprobadas por el congreso** — The laws are approved by congress

| Subject gender/number | Participle form |
|-----------------------|-----------------|
| Masculine singular | -ado / -ido |
| Feminine singular | -ada / -ida |
| Masculine plural | -ados / -idos |
| Feminine plural | -adas / -idas |

### Regular Past Participles

| Infinitive ending | Participle | Example |
|-------------------|-----------|---------|
| -ar | -ado | hablar → hablado |
| -er | -ido | comer → comido |
| -ir | -ido | vivir → vivido |

### Irregular Past Participles

These must be memorized:

| Infinitive | Participle | Meaning |
|-----------|-----------|---------|
| hacer | **hecho** | done / made |
| decir | **dicho** | said |
| ver | **visto** | seen |
| poner | **puesto** | put |
| abrir | **abierto** | opened |
| escribir | **escrito** | written |
| volver | **vuelto** | returned |
| romper | **roto** | broken |
| morir | **muerto** | died |
| cubrir | **cubierto** | covered |

## Se Passive — Everyday Usage

The **se passive** (also called *pasiva refleja*) is the go-to construction in spoken and written Spanish. No agent is expressed.

Structure: **se + verb (3rd person singular or plural) + subject**

The verb agrees in number with the grammatical subject:

- **Se vende casa** (seh VEHN-deh KAH-sah) — House for sale *(lit. "A house is sold")*
- **Se venden casas** (seh VEHN-dehn KAH-sahs) — Houses for sale *(plural verb for plural subject)*
- **Se hablan varios idiomas aquí** (seh AH-blahn VAH-ryohs ee-DYOH-mahs) — Several languages are spoken here
- **Se firmó el acuerdo ayer** — The agreement was signed yesterday

### Impersonal se with People

When the "subject" is a person (not a thing), Spanish uses the impersonal *se* with a **singular verb** and the **personal a**:

- **Se busca a los culpables** (seh BOOS-kah ah lohs kool-PAH-blehs) — The culprits are being sought
- **Se detuvo a los sospechosos** — The suspects were detained

## Comparing the Two Constructions

| | Formal passive (ser) | Se passive |
|--|----------------------|------------|
| **Register** | Formal / written | Everyday speech and writing |
| **Agent expressed** | Yes, with *por* | No |
| **Participle agreement** | Yes (gender + number) | N/A |
| **Common in speech?** | Rarely | Very common |

- **Formal:** La novela fue publicada por la editorial en 2020.
- **Everyday:** La novela se publicó en 2020.

> ⚠️ **Key tip:** In conversational Spanish, the active voice or *se* passive is strongly preferred over *ser + participio*. Reserve the formal passive for journalism, history, law, and academic writing.
