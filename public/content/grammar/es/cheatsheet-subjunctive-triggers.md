# Cheat Sheet: Subjunctive Triggers (When to Use El Subjuntivo)

The Spanish subjunctive isn't a "tense" — it's a **mood** that signals the speaker views the action as non-factual: wished-for, doubted, emotional, hypothetical, or yet-to-happen. The hardest part isn't conjugating it; it's spotting the **trigger** in the main clause. This cheat sheet collects every trigger you actually need.

> 📌 The subjunctive almost always lives in a **subordinate clause** introduced by **que**. Find the trigger in the main clause, and the subjunctive follows.

## The WEIRDO Mnemonic

| Letter | Category | Example triggers |
|---|---|---|
| **W** | **W**ishes / volition | *querer que*, *desear que* |
| **E** | **E**motions | *alegrarse de que*, *sentir que* |
| **I** | **I**mpersonal expressions | *es importante que*, *es posible que* |
| **R** | **R**ecommendations | *recomendar que*, *sugerir que* |
| **D** | **D**oubt / denial | *dudar que*, *no creer que* |
| **O** | **O**jalá | *Ojalá llueva* — I hope it rains |

WEIRDO covers most of what triggers the subjunctive — but it isn't exhaustive. The category lists below are the real working reference.

## Volition / Influence — Subjunctive in Subordinate Clause

When the main-clause subject **wants, suggests, demands, or orders** that someone else do something, the subordinate verb is subjunctive.

| Trigger | Example |
|---|---|
| querer que | *Quiero que vengas.* — I want you to come. |
| desear que | *Deseo que tengas suerte.* — I wish you luck. |
| esperar que | *Espero que llegues pronto.* — I hope you arrive soon. |
| exigir que | *Exijo que respondas.* — I demand that you respond. |
| sugerir que | *Sugiero que hables con él.* — I suggest you talk to him. |
| recomendar que | *Te recomiendo que estudies.* — I recommend that you study. |
| aconsejar que | *Le aconsejo que descanse.* — I advise him to rest. |
| pedir que | *Te pido que me escuches.* — I'm asking you to listen. |
| decir que *(commanding)* | *Te digo que vengas.* — I'm telling you to come. *(command — subj.)* vs. *Te digo que viene* — I'm telling you (that) he's coming. *(report — indicative)* |

> ⚠️ Same-subject sentences drop the *que* and use the infinitive: *Quiero ir* (I want to go), not *Quiero que vaya yo*.

## Emotion — Subjunctive After Emotional Reactions

When the main clause expresses an emotional reaction to a subordinate event, the subordinate verb is subjunctive — even if the event is factually true.

| Trigger | Example |
|---|---|
| alegrarse de que | *Me alegro de que estés aquí.* — I'm glad you're here. |
| sorprenderse de que | *Me sorprende que diga eso.* — I'm surprised he says that. |
| sentir que | *Siento que tengas que irte.* — I'm sorry you have to leave. |
| temer que | *Temo que sea tarde.* — I fear it's late. |
| lamentar que | *Lamento que no puedas venir.* — I regret you can't come. |
| encantar que | *Me encanta que canten.* — I love that they sing. |
| gustar que | *Me gusta que estudies.* — I like that you study. |

## Doubt / Denial — Subjunctive When Reality Is Negated or Questioned

The subjunctive marks that the speaker doesn't fully accept the truth of the subordinate clause.

| Trigger | Example |
|---|---|
| dudar que | *Dudo que venga.* — I doubt he'll come. |
| no creer que | *No creo que sea verdad.* — I don't believe it's true. |
| no pensar que | *No pienso que tengas razón.* — I don't think you're right. |
| negar que | *Niega que sea suyo.* — He denies that it's his. |
| no es verdad que | *No es verdad que sepa francés.* — It's not true that he knows French. |
| no es cierto que | *No es cierto que vivan aquí.* — It's not true that they live here. |

> 💡 Affirmative *creer que* and *pensar que* take **indicative** — they assert belief: *Creo que viene* (I believe he's coming). Negate them and the subjunctive returns.

## Impersonal "Es + Adjective + Que"

Most impersonal *es + adjetivo + que* expressions take subjunctive — **unless** they affirm a fact.

| Subjunctive triggers | Indicative (affirm reality) |
|---|---|
| es importante que | **es seguro que** *(it is certain that)* |
| es necesario que | **es verdad que** *(it is true that)* |
| es posible que | **es cierto que** *(it is true that)* |
| es bueno que | **es obvio que** *(it is obvious that)* |
| es mejor que | **es evidente que** *(it is evident that)* |
| es probable que | |
| es triste que | |
| es raro que | |
| es lógico que | |
| es una pena que | |

> ⚠️ Negate the **indicative** ones and they switch: *No es verdad que sepa francés* — subjunctive returns, because the affirmation is now denied.

## Adverbial Conjunctions That **Always** Take Subjunctive

Six conjunctions express purpose, condition, or anteriority that is by definition non-factual. They always trigger subjunctive — no exceptions.

| Trigger | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| para que | so that | *Te lo digo para que lo sepas.* — I'm telling you so you know. |
| sin que | without (someone doing) | *Salió sin que nadie lo viera.* — He left without anyone seeing him. |
| antes de que | before | *Llegué antes de que empezara.* — I arrived before it started. |
| a menos que | unless | *Iremos a menos que llueva.* — We'll go unless it rains. |
| con tal de que | provided that | *Iré con tal de que vengas tú.* — I'll go provided you come. |
| en caso de que | in case | *Llévate paraguas en caso de que llueva.* — Take an umbrella in case it rains. |
| a fin de que | in order that | *Hablo claro a fin de que entiendan.* — I speak clearly so they understand. |

## Adverbial Conjunctions That Take Subjunctive Only When Future / Hypothetical

These conjunctions take **subjunctive when the subordinate event is future or hypothetical**, and **indicative when it's habitual or already happened**.

| Conjunction | Subjunctive (future / hypothetical) | Indicative (habitual / past) |
|---|---|---|
| cuando | *Cuando vengas, hablamos.* — When you come, we'll talk. | *Cuando viene, hablamos.* — When he comes (usually), we talk. |
| mientras | *Mientras estudies, te ayudo.* — As long as you study, I'll help. | *Mientras estudiaba, llamó.* — While he was studying, he called. |
| hasta que | *Espera hasta que llegue.* — Wait until he arrives. | *Esperé hasta que llegó.* — I waited until he arrived. |
| en cuanto | *En cuanto termine, te aviso.* — As soon as I finish, I'll let you know. | *En cuanto termina, sale.* — As soon as he finishes (usually), he leaves. |
| tan pronto como | *Te llamo tan pronto como pueda.* — I'll call as soon as I can. | *Me llamaba tan pronto como podía.* — He called me as soon as he could. |
| aunque | *Aunque llueva, iremos.* — Even if it rains, we'll go. *(hypothetical)* | *Aunque llueve, iremos.* — Although it's (actually) raining, we'll go. |
| después de que | *Hablaremos después de que comas.* — We'll talk after you eat. | *Hablamos después de que comió.* — We talked after he ate. |

## Relative Clauses — Indefinite vs Definite Antecedent

The mood of a relative clause depends on whether the **antecedent** (the noun being described) is specific and real, or indefinite/nonexistent.

| Antecedent | Mood | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Specific / known** | **Indicative** | *Busco el libro que es interesante.* — I'm looking for the (specific) book that is interesting. |
| **Indefinite / unknown / nonexistent** | **Subjunctive** | *Busco un libro que sea interesante.* — I'm looking for any book that might be interesting. |
| **Nonexistent** | **Subjunctive** | *No hay nadie que sepa la respuesta.* — There is no one who knows the answer. |

> 💡 Quick test: can you replace the antecedent with "a specific X I have in mind"? → indicative. Or with "any X that fits"? → subjunctive.

## The "Real vs Hypothetical" Rule — Parallel Examples

Many triggers flip between indicative and subjunctive depending on whether the situation is real or hypothetical. Compare:

| Real / asserted (indicative) | Hypothetical / negated (subjunctive) |
|---|---|
| *Creo que viene.* — I think he's coming. | *No creo que venga.* — I don't think he's coming. |
| *Es verdad que sabe.* — It's true he knows. | *No es verdad que sepa.* — It's not true that he knows. |
| *Cuando llega, comemos.* (habit) | *Cuando llegue, comeremos.* (future) |
| *Aunque está cansado, trabaja.* (fact) | *Aunque esté cansado, trabajará.* (hypothetical) |
| *Busco al hombre que habla francés.* (specific) | *Busco un hombre que hable francés.* (any) |

## Sequence of Tenses — Quick Chart

When the main-clause trigger is **present, future, or imperative**, the subordinate uses **present subjunctive** (or **present perfect subjunctive** for completed action). When the main clause is in any **past tense or conditional**, the subordinate uses **imperfect subjunctive** (or **pluperfect subjunctive** for prior action).

| Main-clause trigger | Subordinate subjunctive |
|---|---|
| Present → *Quiero que…* | **Present subj.** → *vengas* |
| Present → *Me alegra que…* | **Present perfect subj.** → *hayas venido* (already happened) |
| Future → *Querré que…* | **Present subj.** → *vengas* |
| Imperfecto → *Quería que…* | **Imperfect subj.** → *vinieras* |
| Indefinido → *Quise que…* | **Imperfect subj.** → *vinieras* |
| Pluscuamperfecto → *Había querido que…* | **Pluperfect subj.** → *hubieras venido* |
| Condicional → *Querría que…* | **Imperfect subj.** → *vinieras* |

## Mini-Dialogue

> — **Quiero que vengas** a la cena, **aunque llegues** tarde. *(KYEH-roh keh BEHN-gahs ah lah SEH-nah, AWN-keh YEH-gehs TAHR-deh)*
> — No sé… **dudo que pueda** salir del trabajo a tiempo. *(noh seh, DOO-doh keh PWEH-dah sah-LEER dehl trah-BAH-hoh ah TYEHM-poh)*
> — **Ojalá puedas**. **Es importante que estés** allí. *(oh-hah-LAH PWEH-dahs. ehs eem-pohr-TAHN-teh keh ehs-TEHS ah-YEE)*
> — Voy a intentarlo. Te aviso **en cuanto termine** la reunión. *(boy ah een-tehn-TAHR-loh. teh ah-BEE-soh ehn KWAHN-toh tehr-MEE-neh lah reh-oo-NYOHN)*
>
> **Translation:** — I want you to come to dinner, even if you arrive late. — I don't know… I doubt I can leave work on time. — I hope you can. It's important that you're there. — I'll try. I'll let you know as soon as the meeting ends.

> 💡 Notice **two triggers** in this exchange: *quiero que* (volition) and *aunque + subjunctive* (hypothetical concession), plus *dudo que*, *ojalá*, *es importante que*, and *en cuanto* (future).
