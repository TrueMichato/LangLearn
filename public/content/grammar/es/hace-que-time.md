# Hace + Time + Que / Desde Hace (Time Expressions)

The Spanish verb **hacer** is recycled for time expressions in three closely related — and easily confused — constructions. The critical distinction is between **"for [time]"** (ongoing) and **"[time] ago"** (one-time event). The tense you use with **hace** changes the meaning completely.

## Construction 1: Hace + Time + Que + Present (Ongoing)

> **Hace + [time] + que + [verb in present]** = "I've been doing X for [time]"

- **Hace dos años que estudio español** (AH-seh dohs AH-nyohs keh ehs-TOO-dyoh ehs-pah-NYOHL) — I've been studying Spanish for two years.
- **Hace una hora que espero** — I've been waiting for an hour.
- **Hace mucho tiempo que vivo aquí** — I've been living here for a long time.

The action **started in the past and is still happening**. Spanish uses the **present** tense here — never the perfect.

## Construction 2: Present + Desde Hace + Time (Same Meaning)

> **[verb in present] + desde hace + [time]** = "I've been doing X for [time]"

- **Estudio español desde hace dos años** (ehs-TOO-dyoh ehs-pah-NYOHL DEHS-deh AH-seh dohs AH-nyohs) — I've been studying Spanish for two years.
- **Vivo aquí desde hace mucho tiempo** — I've been living here for a long time.
- **Trabaja en el banco desde hace seis meses** — She has been working at the bank for six months.

This is the **mirror version** of Construction 1 — same meaning, different word order. Both are equally common.

| Construction 1 | Construction 2 |
|---|---|
| **Hace dos años que estudio español** | **Estudio español desde hace dos años** |
| Time first, then **que**, then verb | Verb first, then **desde hace**, then time |

## Construction 3: Hace + Time + Preterite ("Ago")

> **Hace + [time] + [verb in preterite]** = "[time] ago, X happened"

- **Hace dos años estudié en España** (AH-seh dohs AH-nyohs ehs-too-DYEH ehn ehs-PAH-nyah) — Two years ago, I studied in Spain.
- **Hace una hora llegué** — I arrived an hour ago.
- **Hace tres días lo vi** — Three days ago, I saw him.

The action is **a one-time completed event** in the past. Spanish uses the **preterite** tense.

**Note**: the "que" is optional but usually dropped:
- *Hace dos años estudié en España* ✓ (more common)
- *Hace dos años que estudié en España* ✓ (also fine, slightly emphatic)

## The Critical Distinction

The same opening — **hace dos años** — produces **opposite meanings** depending on the verb tense.

| Tense | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hace 2 años que + present** | I've been doing X for 2 years (ongoing) | **Hace dos años que estudio.** — I've been studying for 2 years. |
| **Hace 2 años + preterite** | I did X 2 years ago (single event) | **Hace dos años estudié.** — I studied 2 years ago. |

This is the single most important rule of the whole topic. Learn it and you've mastered 90% of it.

## Questions: How Long?

To ask "for how long?" or "how long ago?":

- **¿Cuánto tiempo hace que estudias español?** — How long have you been studying Spanish?
- **¿Desde cuándo estudias español?** — Since when have you been studying Spanish?
- **¿Cuánto tiempo hace que llegaste?** (preterite) — How long ago did you arrive?

The verb tense in the question signals which meaning is intended.

## Negative: Time Without an Action

> **Hace + time + que + no + verb (present)** = "I haven't done X for [time]"

- **Hace dos meses que no lo veo** (AH-seh dohs MEH-sehs keh noh loh BEH-oh) — I haven't seen him for two months.
- **Hace mucho que no como pescado** — I haven't eaten fish for a long time.

This **competes** with *llevar + sin* — both are correct:
- *Hace dos meses que no lo veo* = *Llevo dos meses sin verlo*

## Mini-Dialogue

> — ¿**Hace** mucho tiempo que vives en Barcelona? _(AH-seh MOO-choh TYEHM-poh keh BEE-behs ehn bahr-seh-LOH-nah)_
> — Sí, vivo aquí **desde hace** seis años. _(see, BEE-boh ah-KEE DEHS-deh AH-seh says AH-nyohs)_
> — ¡Vaya! ¿Y cuándo te mudaste?
> — **Hace** seis años exactamente; llegué un día como hoy. _(AH-seh says AH-nyohs ehk-sahk-tah-MEHN-teh, yeh-GEH oon DEE-ah KOH-moh oy)_
> — Y a tu familia, ¿cuánto **hace que** no la ves?
> — Uf, **hace** tres meses **que no** los visito. _(AH-seh trehs MEH-sehs keh noh lohs bee-SEE-toh)_
>
> Translation: "Have you been living in Barcelona for long?" — "Yes, I've been living here for six years." — "Wow! And when did you move?" — "Exactly six years ago; I arrived on a day like today." — "And your family, how long has it been since you've seen them?" — "Ugh, I haven't visited them for three months."

Notice all three constructions appearing naturally: **hace + present** (ongoing), **desde hace** (mirror form), **hace + preterite** (ago), and the **negative** (haven't done X).
