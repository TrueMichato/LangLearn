# Refranes as Grammar (Proverbs Illustrate Structure)

Spanish **refranes** (proverbs) are tiny grammar museums. They preserve archaic verb forms, idiomatic word orders, and condensed structures that modern conversation has smoothed away. Because native speakers still cite them daily — at the dinner table, in news editorials, on TV — recognising the grammar inside each refrán pays double: you learn the saying *and* you internalise the structure it lives in.

This lesson walks through six grammatical patterns, each anchored to a proverb you will likely hear in any Spanish-speaking country.

## Gnomic Present — Eternal Truths

The **presente de indicativo** in proverbs functions as a *gnomic present*: a timeless statement of how the world works. Even though refranes describe potential or abstract situations, the indicative is used because the speaker presents the truth as **universal and factual**, not hypothetical.

- **Camarón que se duerme se lo lleva la corriente.** (kah-mah-ROHN keh seh DWEHR-meh seh loh YEH-bah lah koh-RRYEHN-teh) — *The shrimp that falls asleep gets carried away by the current.* (Verbs `se duerme`, `se lo lleva` — both presente indicativo.)
- **El que mucho abarca poco aprieta.** (ehl keh MOO-choh ah-BAHR-kah POH-koh ah-PRYEH-tah) — *He who grasps too much holds little tightly.* (`abarca`, `aprieta` — gnomic present.)
- **A buen entendedor pocas palabras bastan.** (ah bwehn ehn-tehn-deh-DOHR POH-kahs pah-LAH-brahs BAHS-tahn) — *To a good listener, few words suffice.* (`bastan` — present indicative of a universal claim.)

> 💡 You might expect the subjunctive after relative clauses like *el que…*, but proverbs deliberately stay in the indicative to mark the statement as eternally true.

## Imperatives in Proverbs

Refranes love to give orders. Direct second-person commands — both **affirmative** (`dime`, `cría`) and **negative** (`no le mires`) — make proverbs feel like advice from an elder.

- **Dime con quién andas y te diré quién eres.** (DEE-meh kohn kyehn AHN-dahs ee teh dee-REH kyehn EH-rehs) — *Tell me who you walk with and I'll tell you who you are.* (Imperative `dime` + future indicative `te diré`.)
- **A caballo regalado no le mires el diente.** (ah kah-BAH-yoh reh-gah-LAH-doh noh leh MEE-rehs ehl DYEHN-teh) — *Don't look a gift horse in the mouth.* (Negative imperative `no le mires` = present subjunctive after `no`.)
- **Cría cuervos y te sacarán los ojos.** (KREE-ah KWEHR-bohs ee teh sah-kah-RAHN lohs OH-hohs) — *Raise crows and they'll peck out your eyes.* (Imperative `cría` + ironic future `te sacarán`.)

| Infinitive | Tú imperative (affirmative) | Tú negative |
|---|---|---|
| decir | **di** (`dime`) | no digas |
| criar | **cría** | no críes |
| mirar | **mira** | **no mires** |
| hacer | **haz** | no hagas |
| salir | sal | no salgas |

> ⚠️ Affirmative `tú` commands use the bare 3rd-person singular present (`mira`, `come`), while negative `tú` commands switch to the **present subjunctive** (`no mires`, `no comas`).

## Subjunctive in Proverbs

Most proverbs prefer the indicative, but a few reach for the **subjunctive** to mark a concession or hypothetical:

- **Aunque la mona se vista de seda, mona se queda.** (AWN-keh lah MOH-nah seh BEES-tah deh SEH-dah, MOH-nah seh KEH-dah) — *Even if the monkey dresses in silk, a monkey she remains.* (`aunque + subjuntivo` because the concession is hypothetical: *even supposing she did*.)
- **Donde fueres, haz lo que vieres.** (DOHN-deh FWEH-rehs, ahs loh keh BYEH-rehs) — *Wherever you may go, do what you see done.* (Archaic **future subjunctive** — fossilised in proverbs. Modern Spanish would say *Donde vayas, haz lo que veas*.)
- **No hay mal que por bien no venga.** (noh ahy mahl keh pohr byehn noh BEHN-gah) — *There is no bad thing from which good doesn't come.* (`No hay X que + subjuntivo` is the standard pattern for non-existence claims.)

## Conditional & Si-Clauses in Proverbs

Refranes often package advice as a conditional, mixing `si` + present with an imperative or future:

- **Si quieres paz, prepárate para la guerra.** (see KYEH-rehs pahs, preh-PAH-rah-teh PAH-rah lah GEH-rrah) — *If you want peace, prepare for war.* (`si + presente` → imperative `prepárate`.)
- **Si te he visto, no me acuerdo.** (see teh eh BEES-toh, noh meh ah-KWEHR-doh) — *If I saw you, I don't remember.* (`si + pretérito perfecto` → present indicative — used to dismiss someone playfully.)

For **counterfactual** proverbs (events contrary to fact), Spanish uses the imperfect subjunctive in the `si`-clause and conditional in the result clause:

> **Si yo tuviera tu juventud, conquistaría el mundo.** — *If I had your youth, I would conquer the world.* (`si + imperfecto de subjuntivo` → `condicional simple`.)

## Comparatives & Quantifiers

`Más vale X que Y` is a fixed comparative pattern meaning *"X is worth more than Y"* or simply *"better X than Y"*. It heads dozens of proverbs:

- **Más vale prevenir que curar.** (mahs BAH-leh preh-beh-NEER keh koo-RAHR) — *Better to prevent than to cure.*
- **Más vale tarde que nunca.** (mahs BAH-leh TAHR-deh keh NOON-kah) — *Better late than never.*
- **Más vale pájaro en mano que ciento volando.** (mahs BAH-leh PAH-hah-roh ehn MAH-noh keh SYEHN-toh boh-LAHN-doh) — *A bird in the hand is worth a hundred flying.*

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Más vale** A **que** B | A is better/worth more than B | *Más vale prevenir que curar* |
| **Más** + adj. + **que** | more … than | *Más rápido que un rayo* |
| **Tan** + adj. + **como** | as … as | *Tan claro como el agua* |

## Mini-Dialogue

> — María acaba de quejarse de su regalo de cumpleaños.
> — Ay, dile que a caballo regalado no le mires el diente. *(ahy, DEE-leh keh ah kah-BAH-yoh reh-gah-LAH-doh noh leh MEE-rehs ehl DYEHN-teh)*
> — Tiene razón. Pero ya sabes cómo es ella.
> — Sí, en boca cerrada no entran moscas. Mejor no le digas nada más. *(see, ehn BOH-kah seh-RRAH-dah noh EHN-trahn MOHS-kahs)*
>
> **Translation:** María just complained about her birthday gift. — Tell her not to look a gift horse in the mouth. — She's right, but you know how she is. — Yes, silence is golden. Better not say anything more to her.
