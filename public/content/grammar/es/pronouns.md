# Subject Pronouns & Verb Agreement

Spanish subject pronouns tell us who is performing the action. Unlike English, Spanish verbs are highly inflected — each person and number has its own ending — so the pronoun is often unnecessary and even sounds awkward when overused. Knowing when to include or drop the pronoun is the key to sounding natural.

## The Subject Pronoun Table

| Person | Pronoun | Pronunciation | Notes |
|--------|---------|--------------|-------|
| 1st singular | **yo** | yoh | I |
| 2nd singular | **tú** | too | you (informal) |
| 3rd singular | **él / ella / usted** | ehl / EH-yah / oos-TEHD | he / she / you (formal) |
| 1st plural | **nosotros / nosotras** | noh-SOH-trohs | we (masc. or mixed / all-fem.) |
| 2nd plural (Spain) | **vosotros / vosotras** | voh-SOH-trohs | you all — Spain only* |
| 3rd plural | **ellos / ellas / ustedes** | EH-yohs / EH-yahs / oos-TEH-dehs | they (m.) / they (f.) / you all |

> *🇪🇸 **Vosotros** is used in Spain for informal "you all." In Latin America, **ustedes** covers both formal and informal plural — *vosotros* simply does not exist there.

## Spanish Is Pro-Drop

Spanish is a **pro-drop** language — the subject pronoun can (and usually should) be omitted, because the verb ending already tells you who is doing the action:

- **Hablo español** (AH-bloh ehs-pah-NYOHL) — I speak Spanish *(the -o ending signals yo)*
- **¿Hablas inglés?** — Do you speak English? *(the -as ending signals tú)*
- **Trabajan mucho** (trah-BAH-hahn) — They work a lot *(-an signals ellos/ellas/ustedes)*

Include the pronoun when you need **emphasis or contrast**:

- **Yo trabajo, tú no** (yoh trah-BAH-hoh, too noh) — *I* work, *you* don't
- **Ella estudia, él duerme** — *She* studies, *he* sleeps
- **Nosotros pagamos, ellos no** — *We* pay, they don't

## Usted vs Tú — Formality and Geography

The choice between **tú** and **usted** varies by country and even city:

| Region | Informal singular | Formal singular | Informal plural | Formal plural |
|--------|-------------------|----------------|----------------|--------------|
| Most of Latin America | **tú** | **usted** | **ustedes** | **ustedes** |
| Argentina / Uruguay | **vos** | **usted** | **ustedes** | **ustedes** |
| Colombia (many contexts) | **usted** | **usted** | **ustedes** | **ustedes** |
| Spain | **tú** | **usted** | **vosotros** | **ustedes** |

- **¿Cómo estás?** (KOH-moh ehs-TAHS) — How are you? (informal — tú)
- **¿Cómo está usted?** (oos-TEHD) — How are you? (formal — boss, elder, stranger)
- **¿Cómo están ustedes?** — How are all of you? (Latin America — formal or informal plural)

> 📌 In Colombia, using **usted** with close friends and family is very common and carries warmth, not distance.

### Vos — Argentina and the River Plate Region

**Vos** replaces *tú* in Argentina, Uruguay, and parts of Central America, with its own verb forms:

- **Vos tenés razón** (vohs teh-NEHS rah-SOHN) — You're right *(vs tú tienes)*
- **¿Vos querés café?** — Do you want coffee? *(vs ¿tú quieres?)*

## Él / Ella / Usted — Third Person Verb Agreement

All three share the same verb form:

- **Él trabaja mucho** (ehl trah-BAH-hah) — He works a lot
- **Ella trabaja mucho** — She works a lot
- **Usted trabaja mucho** — You work a lot (formal)

Because they share a form, the pronoun is often kept for clarity:

- **Está cansado** — He / she / it is tired *(pronoun helps avoid ambiguity)*

## Nosotros / Nosotras

Use *nosotros* for all-male or mixed groups; *nosotras* for all-female groups:

- **Nosotros comemos pizza** (noh-SOH-trohs) — We (mixed/male group) eat pizza
- **Nosotras comemos pizza** — We (all-female group) eat pizza

## Quick Reference: Pronoun Usage

| Situation | Recommendation |
|-----------|---------------|
| Normal statement | Drop the pronoun |
| Emphasis / contrast | Keep the pronoun |
| Ambiguous él/ella/usted | Keep the pronoun for clarity |
| Plural you (Latin America) | **ustedes** always |
| Plural you (Spain, informal) | **vosotros** |
