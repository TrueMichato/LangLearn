# Demonstratives (este/ese/aquel)

Spanish demonstratives form a three-tier system based on distance — not just from the speaker, but also from the listener. Think of it as: **near me**, **near you**, and **far from both of us**. Each tier has masculine, feminine, and neuter forms, and all must agree with the noun they modify.

## The Three Tiers at a Glance

| Distance | Masc. sg. | Fem. sg. | Masc. pl. | Fem. pl. | English |
|----------|-----------|---------|-----------|---------|---------|
| Near speaker | **este** | **esta** | **estos** | **estas** | this / these |
| Near listener | **ese** | **esa** | **esos** | **esas** | that / those |
| Far from both | **aquel** | **aquella** | **aquellos** | **aquellas** | that / those (over there) |

- **Este libro es mío** (EHS-teh LEE-broh ehs MEE-oh) — This book is mine *(I'm holding it)*
- **Ese libro es tuyo** (EH-seh) — That book is yours *(near you)*
- **Aquel libro es de Ana** (ah-KEHL) — That book over there is Ana's *(far from both of us)*

## Agreeing with the Noun

Demonstratives must agree in **gender and number** with the noun they modify:

- **esta silla** (EHS-tah SEE-yah) — this chair *(fem. sg.)*
- **estos zapatos** (EHS-tohs sah-PAH-tohs) — these shoes *(masc. pl.)*
- **esas mesas** (EH-sahs MEH-sahs) — those tables *(fem. pl.)*
- **aquellos árboles** (ah-KEH-yohs AHR-boh-lehs) — those trees over there *(masc. pl.)*
- **aquella montaña** (ah-KEH-yah mohn-TAH-nyah) — that mountain over there *(fem. sg.)*

## Neuter Forms: Esto, Eso, Aquello

When you refer to an **unidentified thing** or a **whole situation**, use the neuter forms. They have no plural, never modify a noun, and require no gender agreement:

- **¿Qué es esto?** (keh ehs EHS-toh) — What is this? *(pointing at something unidentified)*
- **No me gusta eso** (noh meh GOOS-tah EH-soh) — I don't like that *(referring to a situation)*
- **¿Viste aquello?** (BYEHS-teh ah-KEH-yoh) — Did you see that? *(vague, far away)*
- **Eso no es justo** — That is not fair *(commenting on a general situation)*

## Demonstratives with Time

Demonstratives are widely used in time expressions:

| Expression | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|
| **esta semana** | this week |
| **este mes** | this month |
| **este año** | this year |
| **ese día** | that day |
| **ese año** | that year |
| **aquella época** | that era / that period (distant past) |
| **aquellos tiempos** | those times (distant, often nostalgic) |

- **Esta semana estoy muy ocupado** (EHS-tah SEH-mah-nah) — I am very busy this week
- **En aquellos tiempos todo era más sencillo** — In those days everything was simpler
- **Ese año fue muy difícil** — That year was very difficult

## The Tilde — No Longer Required

Before the **2010 RAE orthography update**, demonstratives used as pronouns (without a following noun) were written with a tilde: *éste, ése, aquél*. Since 2010, the RAE states the tilde is **optional and generally unnecessary**. Modern Spanish writing omits these accents:

- **Este es mi favorito** ✓ — This one is my favorite
- **¿Cuál prefieres, este o ese?** ✓ — Which do you prefer, this one or that one?

You may still see the accented forms in older texts — both are accepted, but current style guides recommend dropping the accent.
