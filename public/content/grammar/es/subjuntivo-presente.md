# Present Subjunctive (Subjuntivo Presente)

The **subjuntivo** is the mood of doubt, desire, emotion, and hypothesis. While the indicative states facts ("She studies"), the subjunctive expresses subjectivity ("I want her to study"). It appears in subordinate clauses triggered by certain expressions — and once you learn those triggers, the pattern clicks into place.

## Formation — The "Opposite Vowel" Rule

Take the **yo form** of the present indicative, drop the **-o**, and add the **opposite** vowel endings:

- **-ar verbs** → use **-e** endings (the "e" vowel is opposite to -ar's "a")
- **-er / -ir verbs** → use **-a** endings (the "a" vowel is opposite to -er/-ir)

| Pronoun | hablar (-ar) | comer (-er) | vivir (-ir) |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-------------|
| yo | **hable** | coma | viva |
| tú | **hables** | comas | vivas |
| él/ella/usted | **hable** | coma | viva |
| nosotros | **hablemos** | comamos | vivamos |
| ustedes / ellos | **hablen** | coman | vivan |

Because the form is built from the **yo indicative**, verbs irregular in yo carry that irregularity into all subjunctive forms:

- tener → tengo → **tenga**, tengas, tenga...
- venir → vengo → **venga**, vengas...
- conocer → conozco → **conozca**, conozcas...
- poner → pongo → **ponga**, pongas...

## Fully Irregular Subjunctives (Memorize These)

A handful of verbs have irregular subjunctive forms that don't follow the yo-drop rule:

| Infinitive | Subjunctive (yo) | All forms |
|------------|-----------------|-----------|
| ser | **sea** | sea, seas, sea, seamos, sean |
| estar | **esté** | esté, estés, esté, estemos, estén |
| ir | **vaya** | vaya, vayas, vaya, vayamos, vayan |
| haber | **haya** | haya, hayas, haya, hayamos, hayan |
| dar | **dé** | dé, des, dé, demos, den |
| saber | **sepa** | sepa, sepas, sepa, sepamos, sepan |
| ver | **vea** | vea, veas, vea, veamos, vean |

## WEIRDO — Trigger Categories

Subjunctive is triggered in a subordinate **que**-clause whenever the main clause expresses one of these:

| Letter | Category | Trigger Examples |
|--------|----------|-----------------|
| **W** | Wishes | querer que, esperar que, desear que, ojalá |
| **E** | Emotion | alegrarse de que, sentir que, tener miedo de que |
| **I** | Impersonal expressions | es importante que, es necesario que, es bueno que |
| **R** | Recommendations | recomendar que, sugerir que, pedir que, aconsejar que |
| **D** | Doubt / Denial | dudar que, no creer que, no pensar que, negar que |
| **O** | Ojalá | ojalá (+ any subjunctive) |

## Examples in Context

**Wishes:**
- **Quiero que vengas** (KYEH-roh keh VEN-gahs) — I want you to come
- **Espero que estés bien** (es-PEH-roh keh es-TEHS) — I hope you're well

**Emotion:**
- **Me alegra que puedas ir** — I'm glad you can go
- **Siento que no tengas tiempo** — I'm sorry you don't have time

**Impersonal:**
- **Es importante que estudies** (es-TOO-dyehs) — It's important that you study
- **Es necesario que llegues temprano** — It's necessary that you arrive early

**Doubt:**
- **Dudo que sea verdad** (DOO-doh keh SEH-ah) — I doubt it's true
- **No creo que venga hoy** — I don't think he's coming today

**Ojalá:**
- **Ojalá llueva** (oh-hah-LAH YWEH-vah) — Hopefully it will rain / I hope it rains
- **Ojalá tengas suerte** — I hope you have luck

## Subjunctive vs Indicative — The Trigger Test

If the main clause expresses a **fact or certainty**, use the indicative in the subordinate clause:

- **Sé que viene** — I know he is coming (fact → indicative)
- **No sé si viene** — I don't know if he's coming (uncertainty → no trigger, still indicative)
- **Dudo que venga** — I doubt he's coming (doubt → subjunctive ✓)
