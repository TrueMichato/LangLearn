# Imperative Mood (Commands)

The **imperative** is the verb form used to give commands, instructions, or requests. Spanish has distinct affirmative and negative forms, and the correct form depends on **who** you are addressing. Latin American Spanish primarily uses **tú**, **usted**, and **ustedes**; vosotros is included for reference.

## Affirmative Commands — Regular Verbs

| Subject | -AR (hablar) | -ER (comer) | -IR (vivir) | Formation rule |
|---------|-------------|------------|------------|----------------|
| **tú** | **habla** | **come** | **vive** | 3rd-person singular present indicative |
| **usted** | **hable** | **coma** | **viva** | Present subjunctive singular |
| **ustedes** | **hablen** | **coman** | **vivan** | Present subjunctive plural |
| **vosotros** *(Spain)* | **hablad** | **comed** | **vivid** | Infinitive with -r → -d |

- **Habla más despacio, por favor** (AH-blah) — Speak more slowly, please *(tú)*
- **Hable con el médico** (AH-bleh) — Speak with the doctor *(usted)*
- **Coman bien esta noche** — Eat well tonight *(ustedes)*

## Negative Commands — All Forms Use the Subjunctive

For **every** subject, negative commands use the **present subjunctive**:

| Subject | No + -AR | No + -ER | No + -IR |
|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| **tú** | no hables | no comas | no vivas |
| **usted** | no hable | no coma | no viva |
| **ustedes** | no hablen | no coman | no vivan |
| **vosotros** | no habléis | no comáis | no viváis |

- **No hables tan rápido** — Don't speak so fast *(tú)*
- **No coma eso** — Don't eat that *(usted)*

> 📌 Notice that the **usted** affirmative and negative are identical in form — the word **no** is the only difference.

## Irregular Affirmative Tú Commands

Eight high-frequency verbs have irregular short forms for the affirmative **tú** command only:

| Infinitive | Tú command | Pronunciation |
|-----------|-----------|---------------|
| **decir** | **di** | dee |
| **hacer** | **haz** | ahs |
| **ir** | **ve** | beh |
| **venir** | **ven** | behn |
| **tener** | **ten** | tehn |
| **poner** | **pon** | pohn |
| **salir** | **sal** | sahl |
| **ser** | **sé** | seh |

> 💡 Mnemonic: **Di-Haz-Ve-Ven / Ten-Pon-Sal-Sé** — eight two- or three-letter forms, all shorter than their infinitives.

- **¡Ven aquí!** (behn ah-KEE) — Come here!
- **Haz la tarea** (ahs lah tah-REH-ah) — Do your homework
- **Sé amable con todos** — Be kind to everyone
- **Sal de aquí ahora mismo** — Leave here right now

## Object Pronouns with Commands

Pronoun placement shifts between affirmative and negative commands:

| Command type | Pronoun placement | Example |
|-------------|------------------|---------|
| **Affirmative** | Attached to the end of the verb | **Dámelo** — give it to me |
| **Negative** | Before the verb, separate | **No me lo des** — don't give it to me |

- **Cómpramelo** (KOHM-prah-meh-loh) — Buy it for me *(affirmative: attached)*
- **No me lo compres** — Don't buy it for me *(negative: before)*
- **Escúchame** — Listen to me *(affirmative: -me attached)*
- **No me escuches** — Don't listen to me *(negative: before)*

> ⚠️ Adding pronouns to affirmative commands often requires a written accent to preserve the original stress: *compra* → **cómpralo**, *escribe* → **escríbelo**, *levanta* → **levántate**.
