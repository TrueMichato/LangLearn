# Latin American vs European Spanish

Spanish is spoken by over 500 million people across more than 20 countries. While fully mutually intelligible, Latin American and European (Castilian) Spanish differ in pronouns, pronunciation, tense preference, and everyday vocabulary. This lesson maps the most important contrasts.

## Pronouns: Plural "You"

The most immediately noticeable grammatical difference involves second-person plural address:

| Context | Latin America | Spain |
|---|---|---|
| Informal plural "you" | **ustedes** + 3rd-person plural verb | **vosotros/as** + 2nd-person plural verb |
| Formal plural "you" | **ustedes** | **ustedes** |

- LatAm: **¿Ustedes quieren comer?** (oos-TEH-des kyeh-rehn koh-MEHR) — Do you all want to eat?
- Spain: **¿Vosotros queréis comer?** (boh-SOH-trohs keh-RAYS koh-MEHR) — Do you all want to eat?

## Voseo: The vos Pronoun

Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay, and parts of Central America and Colombia use **vos** instead of **tú** as the informal singular pronoun. Voseo verb forms stress the **last syllable** and drop the diphthong:

| Verb | tú (standard) | vos (voseo) |
|---|---|---|
| tener | tú tienes | vos tenés |
| ser | tú eres | vos sos |
| poder | tú puedes | vos podés |
| comer | tú comes | vos comés |
| vivir | tú vives | vos vivís |
| hablar | tú hablas | vos hablás |

## Pronunciation Differences

### Seseo vs. Distinción

| Feature | Region | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Seseo** | All of Latin America + Andalusia + Canary Islands | c (before e/i) and z pronounced like **s** |
| **Distinción** | Most of Spain (Castile, north) | c (before e/i) and z pronounced like English **th** in "thin" |

- *cena* (dinner): LatAm → **SEH-nah** ; Spain → **THEH-nah**
- *zapato* (shoe): LatAm → **sah-PAH-toh** ; Spain → **thah-PAH-toh**

Neither form is more "correct" — both are standard within their regions.

### Yeísmo

Most Spanish speakers worldwide (and virtually all of Latin America) pronounce **ll** and **y** identically, like the English *y* in "yes". Some Andean and Paraguayan communities still distinguish them, but this is increasingly rare.

## Tense Preference: Recent Past

Spain strongly prefers the **pretérito perfecto compuesto** (have + past participle) for events connected to the present; Latin America prefers the **pretérito indefinido** (simple past) for the same situations:

| Meaning | Latin America | Spain |
|---|---|---|
| I already ate | **Ya comí.** | **Ya he comido.** |
| Did you call? | **¿Llamaste?** | **¿Has llamado?** |
| I lost my keys | **Perdí las llaves.** | **He perdido las llaves.** |
