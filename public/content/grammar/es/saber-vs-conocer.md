# Saber vs Conocer (Two Verbs for 'To Know')

Spanish splits the English verb "to know" into two separate verbs. **Saber** means to know facts, information, or how to do something. **Conocer** means to be acquainted or familiar with people, places, or things. Choosing the wrong one is a classic learner mistake — and the two verbs even shift meaning in the preterite.

## Saber — Facts, Information, Skills

Use **saber** (sah-BEHR) for:

- **Facts and information:** **Sé que es tarde** (seh keh ehs TAR-deh) — I know it's late.
- **How to do something** (saber + infinitive): **Sé hablar inglés** (seh ah-BLAR een-GLEHS) — I know how to speak English.
- **Memorized data** (numbers, dates, addresses): **No sé su teléfono** — I don't know his phone number.

Note: there is **no "cómo"** with skills. ❌ *Sé cómo hablar inglés* → ✅ *Sé hablar inglés*.

### Conjugation: Saber (Present)

| Pronoun | Form | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|
| yo | **sé** | seh |
| tú | **sabes** | SAH-behs |
| él / ella / usted | **sabe** | SAH-beh |
| nosotros | **sabemos** | sah-BEH-mohs |
| vosotros | **sabéis** | sah-BAYS |
| ellos / ustedes | **saben** | SAH-behn |

The only irregular form is **yo sé** — don't forget the written accent (distinguishes it from the pronoun *se*).

## Conocer — People, Places, Things

Use **conocer** (koh-noh-SEHR) for being **acquainted or familiar** with someone or something:

- **People:** **Conozco a María** (koh-NOHS-koh ah mah-REE-ah) — I know María. *(Personal "a" required before a person.)*
- **Places:** **Conozco Madrid** — I know / I've been to Madrid.
- **Things you're familiar with:** **Conozco esta canción** — I know this song.

### Conjugation: Conocer (Present)

The yo form has the **-zco** spelling: **conozco**.

| Pronoun | Form | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|
| yo | **conozco** | koh-NOHS-koh |
| tú | **conoces** | koh-NOH-sehs |
| él / ella / usted | **conoce** | koh-NOH-seh |
| nosotros | **conocemos** | koh-noh-SEH-mohs |
| vosotros | **conocéis** | koh-noh-SAYS |
| ellos / ustedes | **conocen** | koh-NOH-sehn |

## Meaning Shift in the Preterite

In the **pretérito indefinido**, both verbs change meaning subtly:

| Verb | Imperfect (background) | Preterite (the moment) |
|---|---|---|
| saber | **sabía** — I knew | **supe** — I found out, I learned |
| conocer | **conocía** — I knew (was familiar with) | **conocí** — I met (for the first time) |

- **Supe la verdad ayer** (SOO-peh lah behr-DAHD ah-YEHR) — I found out the truth yesterday.
- **Conocí a tu hermano en la fiesta** — I met your brother at the party.

This is a high-frequency nuance: native speakers use this shift constantly.

## Mini-Dialogue

> — ¿**Conoces** a Laura? _(koh-NOH-sehs ah LOW-rah)_
> — Sí, la **conocí** el año pasado. **Sé** que es ingeniera, pero no **sé** dónde trabaja ahora. _(see, lah koh-noh-SEE ehl AH-nyoh pah-SAH-doh. seh keh ehs een-heh-NYEH-rah, peh-roh noh seh DOHN-deh trah-BAH-hah ah-OH-rah)_
>
> Translation: "Do you know Laura?" — "Yes, I met her last year. I know she's an engineer, but I don't know where she works now."

Notice how both verbs co-exist naturally: *conocer* for the person, *saber* for the facts about her.

## Quick Decision Guide

1. Is it a fact, piece of info, or how-to skill? → **saber**
2. Is it a person, place, or thing you're acquainted with? → **conocer**
3. Past — moment of finding out / meeting? → preterite (*supe*, *conocí*)
4. Past — already knew, ongoing? → imperfect (*sabía*, *conocía*)
