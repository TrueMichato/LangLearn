# Comparatives & Superlatives

Spanish makes comparing people and things straightforward with **más/menos + adjective + que** for unequal comparisons and **tan + adjective + como** for equal ones. A small set of irregular forms handle better, worse, older, and younger. Superlatives simply add the definite article, and the absolute superlative ending **-ísimo** means "extremely."

## Comparatives of Inequality

Use **más** (more) or **menos** (less/fewer) + adjective/adverb/noun + **que**:

- **María es más alta que Ana** (mahs AHL-tah keh) — María is taller than Ana
- **Este libro es menos interesante que el otro** — This book is less interesting than the other
- **Tengo más dinero que tú** — I have more money than you
- **Habla más rápido que yo** — She speaks faster than me

> 📌 Use **de** instead of **que** when comparing against a number: **Hay más de cien personas.** (There are more than a hundred people.)

## Comparatives of Equality

Use **tan + adjective/adverb + como** or **tanto/a/os/as + noun + como**:

- **Es tan alto como tú** (tan AHL-toh KOH-moh) — He is as tall as you
- **Habla tan rápido como su madre** — She speaks as fast as her mother
- **Tengo tantos libros como tú** (TAHN-tohs) — I have as many books as you
- **Hay tanta gente como ayer** (TAHN-tah) — There are as many people as yesterday

| Pattern | Used with | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **tan + adj/adv + como** | Adjectives and adverbs | tan inteligente como |
| **tanto/a + noun + como** | Singular uncountable nouns | tanto tiempo como |
| **tantos/as + noun + como** | Plural nouns | tantas horas como |

## Irregular Comparatives

Four common adjectives use special comparative forms — **never** add más or menos to these:

| Adjective | Irregular comparative | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|-----------|----------------------|---------------|---------|
| **bueno** (good) | **mejor** | meh-HOHR | better |
| **malo** (bad) | **peor** | peh-OHR | worse |
| **grande** / **viejo** (old/big) | **mayor** | mah-YOHR | older / greater |
| **pequeño** / **joven** (young/small) | **menor** | meh-NOHR | younger / lesser |

- **Este restaurante es mejor que el otro** — This restaurant is better than the other
- **Mi nota fue peor que la tuya** — My grade was worse than yours
- **Soy mayor que mi hermano** (mah-YOHR) — I am older than my brother
- **Ella es menor que yo** — She is younger than me

> ⚠️ **Mayor/menor** refer to age or rank. For physical size, you can still use **más grande / más pequeño**: *Este perro es más grande que ese.* (This dog is bigger than that one.)

## Relative Superlatives

Add the **definite article** (el/la/los/las) before the comparative to form the relative superlative:

- **Es la más alta de la clase** (ehs lah mahs AHL-tah deh) — She is the tallest in the class
- **Fue el peor partido del año** — It was the worst match of the year
- **Son los mejores tacos del barrio** — They are the best tacos in the neighborhood

> 📌 Always use **de** (not *en*) after the superlative: *el mejor restaurante **de** la ciudad* — the best restaurant in the city.

## Absolute Superlatives (-ísimo)

To say "extremely" or "super," add **-ísimo/-ísima/-ísimos/-ísimas** to the adjective stem (drop the final vowel first):

| Adjective | -ísimo form | Meaning |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **grande** | **grandísimo** | enormous |
| **fácil** | **facilísimo** | super easy |
| **rico** | **riquísimo** | absolutely delicious |
| **cansado** | **cansadísimo** | exhausted |
| **bueno** | **buenísimo** | excellent |
| **largo** | **larguísimo** | extremely long |

> ⚠️ Spelling changes before -ísimo: **rico → riquísimo** (c → qu), **largo → larguísimo** (g → gu) — to preserve the original consonant sound.

- **Estoy cansadísimo** (kahn-sah-DEE-see-moh) — I am absolutely exhausted
- **La sopa está riquísima** — The soup is incredibly delicious
- **Es facilísimo** (fah-see-LEE-see-moh) — It is super easy

**Muy + adjective** is the everyday alternative: **muy alto** ≈ **altísimo**, **muy caro** ≈ **carísimo**.
