# Llevar + Gerundio (Have Been Doing X for Time)

To say **"I've been studying Spanish for two years"** in Spanish, you don't use the present perfect. You use a compact, idiomatic construction: **llevar + tiempo + gerundio**. This is the natural, everyday way to talk about ongoing duration.

## The Core Construction

> **Llevar (conjugated) + [duration] + gerundio (-ando / -iendo)**

- **Llevo dos años estudiando español** (YEH-boh dohs AH-nyohs ehs-too-DYAHN-doh ehs-pah-NYOHL) — I've been studying Spanish for two years.
- **Lleva tres meses trabajando aquí** — She's been working here for three months.
- **Llevamos una hora esperando** — We've been waiting for an hour.

The verb **llevar** literally means "to carry" — here it "carries" the time you've spent doing the action.

## Why Not Use the Perfect?

English says "I have been studying for X" using a perfect-progressive. Spanish has equivalents (*he estado estudiando*), but they sound **clunky and unnatural for duration**. **Llevar + gerundio** is the idiomatic choice — used constantly by native speakers.

| English | Spanish (natural) | Spanish (awkward) |
|---|---|---|
| I've been living here for 5 years | **Llevo cinco años viviendo aquí** | He estado viviendo aquí cinco años |
| She's been studying medicine for ages | **Lleva mucho tiempo estudiando medicina** | Ha estado estudiando medicina mucho tiempo |

## Word Order

The standard pattern is **llevar → time → gerundio**, but the time and gerundio can swap:

- **Llevo dos horas leyendo** (common) — I've been reading for two hours.
- **Llevo leyendo dos horas** (also common) — same meaning.

Both are correct; the meaning is identical. Spoken Spanish slightly prefers placing the duration first.

## Negative: Llevar + sin + Infinitivo

To say **"I haven't done X for [time]"**, use **llevar + tiempo + sin + infinitivo**:

- **Llevo dos meses sin verlo** (YEH-boh dohs MEH-sehs seen behr-LOH) — I haven't seen him for two months.
- **Llevamos años sin hablar** — We haven't spoken in years.
- **Llevo una semana sin fumar** — I haven't smoked for a week.

Note: with the **negative**, the gerundio is **replaced** by **sin + infinitive**.

## Imperfect: "Had Been Doing X For Y"

For the past version ("had been doing"), conjugate **llevar in the imperfect**:

- **Llevaba dos años viviendo en Madrid cuando lo conocí** (yeh-BAH-bah dohs AH-nyohs bee-BYEHN-doh ehn mah-DREED) — I had been living in Madrid for two years when I met him.
- **Llevábamos horas esperando** — We had been waiting for hours.

This is parallel to the imperfect-progressive: the duration was ongoing at a moment in the past.

## Question Form: "How Long Have You Been…?"

> **¿Cuánto tiempo llevas + gerundio?**

- **¿Cuánto tiempo llevas estudiando español?** (KWAHN-toh TYEHM-poh YEH-bahs ehs-too-DYAHN-doh ehs-pah-NYOHL) — How long have you been studying Spanish?
- **¿Cuántos años lleváis viviendo aquí?** — How many years have you been living here?

Answer naturally with the same structure: *Llevo seis meses estudiando.*

## Mini-Dialogue

> — Hola, ¿**cuánto tiempo llevas** trabajando en esta empresa? _(KWAHN-toh TYEHM-poh YEH-bahs trah-bah-HAHN-doh)_
> — **Llevo** cinco años aquí, pero **llevo** trabajando en este sector más de diez. _(YEH-boh SEEN-koh AH-nyohs ah-KEE)_
> — ¡Qué experiencia! ¿Y tu hermano sigue en el mismo sitio?
> — Qué va, **lleva** un mes **sin** trabajar. Está buscando algo nuevo. _(YEH-bah oon mehs seen trah-bah-HAR)_
>
> Translation: "Hi, how long have you been working at this company?" — "I've been here five years, but I've been working in this sector for more than ten." — "What experience! Is your brother still at the same place?" — "No way, he's been out of work for a month. He's looking for something new."

Notice the **affirmative** (llevo trabajando), **negative** (lleva sin trabajar), and **question** (cuánto tiempo llevas) all naturally co-occurring.

## Quick Patterns to Memorize

- **Llevo + X tiempo + ‑ando/‑iendo** → I've been doing X for [time]
- **Llevaba + X tiempo + ‑ando/‑iendo** → I had been doing X for [time]
- **Llevo + X tiempo + sin + infinitivo** → I haven't done X for [time]
- **¿Cuánto tiempo llevas + ‑ando/‑iendo?** → How long have you been…?
