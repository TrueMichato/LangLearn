# Pedir vs Preguntar (Two Verbs for 'To Ask')

English uses one verb — "to ask" — for two very different actions. Spanish splits them: **pedir** means to **request** something (an object, a favor, an action), while **preguntar** means to **ask a question** seeking information. Mixing them up is one of the most common intermediate-learner errors.

## Pedir — To Request Something

Use **pedir** (peh-DEER) when you ask **for** something — a thing, a favor, or an action:

- **Pido un café** (PEE-doh oon kah-FEH) — I'm ordering a coffee.
- **Te pido un favor** (teh PEE-doh oon fah-BOHR) — I'm asking you a favor.
- **Pidió ayuda** (pee-DYOH ah-YOO-dah) — He asked for help.

Notice: in English you say *ask for* — in Spanish, **no preposition** is used. The thing requested is the direct object.

### Conjugation: Pedir (Stem-changing e → i)

**Pedir** changes its stem **e → i** in the present (except *nosotros / vosotros*), and also in the preterite for *él/ellos*.

| Pronoun | Present | Preterite |
|---|---|---|
| yo | **pido** | pedí |
| tú | **pides** | pediste |
| él / ella / usted | **pide** | **pidió** |
| nosotros | pedimos | pedimos |
| vosotros | pedís | pedisteis |
| ellos / ustedes | **piden** | **pidieron** |

The stem change in **pidió / pidieron** is easy to forget — many learners say *pedió* by mistake.

## Preguntar — To Ask a Question

Use **preguntar** (preh-goon-TAHR) when you ask **for information** — a question that expects an answer:

- **Te pregunto algo** (teh preh-GOON-toh AHL-goh) — I'm asking you something.
- **Preguntó dónde estaba el baño** — She asked where the bathroom was.
- **¿Puedo preguntarte una cosa?** — Can I ask you something?

**Preguntar** is a regular *-ar* verb: *pregunto, preguntas, pregunta, preguntamos, preguntáis, preguntan*.

### Preguntar por vs Preguntar

- **preguntar algo** = to ask something (the question itself).
- **preguntar por (alguien / algo)** = to ask **about** someone/something, or to ask **after** their well-being.

Examples:

- **Me preguntó la hora** — She asked me the time.
- **Me preguntó por ti** (meh preh-goon-TOH pohr tee) — She asked about you / asked how you were.

## Side-by-Side Comparison

| English | Spanish | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| Ask for the bill | **Pedir la cuenta** | pedir |
| Ask a question | **Hacer / preguntar una pregunta** | preguntar |
| Ask for help | **Pedir ayuda** | pedir |
| Ask the time | **Preguntar la hora** | preguntar |
| Ask after someone | **Preguntar por alguien** | preguntar |
| Ask for permission | **Pedir permiso** | pedir |
| Ask why | **Preguntar por qué** | preguntar |

Tip: a useful test — **can you replace "ask" with "request"?** If yes → **pedir**. Otherwise → **preguntar**.

## Mini-Dialogue

> — Perdona, ¿te puedo **preguntar** una cosa? _(pehr-DOH-nah, teh PWEH-doh preh-goon-TAHR OO-nah KOH-sah)_
> — Claro, dime.
> — ¿Sabes dónde está la biblioteca? Y… también quería **pedirte** un favor: ¿me prestas tu diccionario? _(SAH-behs DOHN-deh ehs-TAH lah bee-blee-oh-TEH-kah. ee tahm-BYEHN keh-REE-ah peh-DEER-teh oon fah-BOHR)_
>
> Translation: "Excuse me, can I ask you something? — Sure, go ahead. — Do you know where the library is? And… I also wanted to ask you a favor: can you lend me your dictionary?"

Notice how the speaker uses **preguntar** to ask a question, then **pedir** to request a favor — both in the same exchange.
