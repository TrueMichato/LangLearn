# Adverbs (Adverbios) & -mente Endings

Adverbs are among the most flexible words in Spanish — they can modify verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs, and they never change form for gender or number. Understanding how to build them with **-mente** and when to use common irregular adverbs will sharpen your expression immediately.

## Forming Adverbs with -mente

Take the **feminine singular** form of the adjective and add **-mente**:

| Adjective (masc.) | Feminine form | Adverb | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| rápido | rápida | **rápidamente** | quickly |
| claro | clara | **claramente** | clearly |
| lento | lenta | **lentamente** | slowly |
| cuidadoso | cuidadosa | **cuidadosamente** | carefully |
| tranquilo | tranquila | **tranquilamente** | calmly |
| fácil | fácil | **fácilmente** | easily |
| feliz | feliz | **felizmente** | happily |

Invariable adjectives (same for masculine and feminine) simply add **-mente** directly: *fácil → fácilmente*.

If the adjective carries a written accent, the adverb **keeps** that accent: *rápida → rápidamente*, *fácil → fácilmente*.

## Two -mente Adverbs in a Row

When two **-mente** adverbs appear in sequence, only the **last** one keeps the suffix — the first uses the bare feminine adjective form:

- **Lo hizo lenta y cuidadosamente.** — He did it slowly and carefully.
- **Habló clara y directamente.** — She spoke clearly and directly.
- **Viajó rápida y económicamente.** — She traveled quickly and cheaply.

## Common Adverbs That Do Not Use -mente

### Manner
- **bien** (byehn) — well
- **mal** (mahl) — badly

### Quantity
- **muy** (mwee) — very (only before adjectives or adverbs)
- **mucho** (MOO-choh) — a lot / much (after verbs, or before nouns as a quantifier)
- **poco** (POH-koh) — little / a bit
- **bastante** (bahs-TAHN-teh) — quite / enough
- **demasiado** (deh-mah-SYAH-doh) — too much

### Frequency
- **siempre** (SYEHM-preh) — always
- **nunca** / **jamás** (NOON-kah / hah-MAHS) — never / ever
- **a veces** (ah BEH-ses) — sometimes
- **ya** (yah) — already / now / right away

### Place
- **aquí** / **acá** (ah-KEE / ah-KAH) — here
- **allí** / **allá** (ah-YEE / ah-YAH) — there
- **cerca** (SEHR-kah) — near
- **lejos** (LEH-hohs) — far

### Time
- **hoy** (oy) — today
- **ayer** (ah-YEHR) — yesterday
- **mañana** (mah-NYAH-nah) — tomorrow / morning
- **pronto** (PROHN-toh) — soon
- **temprano** (tehm-PRAH-noh) — early
- **tarde** (TAHR-deh) — late
- **todavía** (toh-dah-BEE-ah) — still / yet

## muy vs. mucho

This is a very common learner error:

| Word | Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **muy** | before an adjective | **Ella es muy inteligente.** — She is very intelligent. |
| **muy** | before an adverb | **Habla muy rápido.** — He speaks very fast. |
| **mucho** | after a verb (quantity) | **Trabajo mucho.** — I work a lot. |
| **mucho/a/os/as** | before a noun (quantifier) | **Tengo muchos amigos.** — I have many friends. |

Never say *~~muy mucho~~* — the combination is redundant and ungrammatical.
