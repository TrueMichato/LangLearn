# Advanced Prepositions & Conjunctions

Spanish has a rich set of prepositions and conjunctions beyond the basics. Mastering these connectors lets you express nuanced ideas — timing, conditions, concession, and purpose — with precision and natural flow.

## Less Common Prepositions

These prepositions appear frequently in formal writing and speech:

| Preposition | Pronunciation | Core Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ante** | AHN-teh | before / in the presence of | ante el juez — before the judge |
| **bajo** | BAH-hoh | under (often figurative) | bajo presión — under pressure |
| **contra** | KOHN-trah | against | luchar contra el frío — fight against the cold |
| **hacia** | AH-syah | toward / around (time) | hacia las cinco — around five o'clock |
| **hasta** | AHS-tah | until / up to / even | hasta mañana — until tomorrow |
| **mediante** | meh-DYAHN-teh | by means of (formal) | mediante un acuerdo — by means of an agreement |
| **según** | seh-GOON | according to | según ella — according to her |
| **tras** | trahs | after / behind (formal) | tras la guerra — after the war |
| **durante** | doo-RAHN-teh | during | durante el verano — during the summer |

### Usage Notes

- **hasta** can also mean "even": **Hasta yo lo sé** (AHS-tah yoh loh seh) — Even I know that.
- **según** can open a sentence directly: **Según el informe...** — According to the report...
- **ante** is preferred over *delante de* in formal and legal contexts.
- **tras** overlaps with *después de* but is more literary: **tras la guerra** vs. *después de la guerra*.

## Key Conjunctions

### aunque — although / even though / even if

**aunque** (OWN-keh) triggers the **indicative** when the speaker treats the information as fact, and the **subjunctive** when the situation is hypothetical or dismissed as irrelevant:

- **Aunque está cansado, trabaja.** — Although he is tired (fact), he works.
- **Aunque esté cansado, trabajará.** — Even if he is tired (hypothetical), he will work.

### pero vs. sino / sino que

Use **pero** (PEH-roh) for simple contrast between two ideas. Use **sino** (SEE-noh) after a negation to introduce a replacement for the negated element:

- **No es alto, sino bajo.** — He is not tall, but rather short. (replacement noun/adjective)
- **No cantó, sino que bailó.** — She did not sing, but rather danced. (clause → use *sino que*)

### mientras — while

**mientras** (MYEHN-trahs) links simultaneous actions:

- **Escucho música mientras estudio.** — I listen to music while I study.
- **Mientras cocinaba, ella leía.** — While she was cooking, she was reading.

### Causal Conjunctions

These are largely interchangeable but differ in formality:

| Conjunction | Pronunciation | Register |
|---|---|---|
| **porque** | POHR-keh | neutral / everyday |
| **ya que** | yah keh | slightly formal |
| **puesto que** | PWES-toh keh | formal / written |
| **dado que** | DAH-doh keh | formal / written |

- **Ya que no tienes tiempo, te ayudo.** — Since you do not have time, I will help you.
- **Puesto que todos están de acuerdo, procedemos.** — Since everyone agrees, we proceed.

### Concessive & Conditional Conjunctions + Subjunctive

These conjunctions require the subjunctive because they introduce conditions or concessions that are not treated as established facts:

- **a pesar de que** (ah peh-SAR deh keh) — despite the fact that
  - **A pesar de que llueve, salimos.** — Despite the fact that it is raining, we are going out.
- **con tal de que** + subjunctive — provided that
  - **Puedes ir con tal de que termines.** — You can go provided that you finish.
- **a menos que** + subjunctive — unless
  - **No iré a menos que me inviten.** — I will not go unless they invite me.
