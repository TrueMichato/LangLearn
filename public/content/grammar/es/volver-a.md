# Volver a + Infinitivo (Doing Something Again)

To express **"doing X again"** in Spanish, native speakers reach first not for *otra vez* or *de nuevo*, but for the idiomatic **volver a + infinitivo**. It compresses "again" into the verb itself and is one of the most frequently used periphrases in everyday speech.

## The Core Construction

> **Volver (conjugated) + a + infinitivo** = "to do X again"

- **Vuelvo a leer el libro** (BWEHL-boh ah leh-EHR ehl LEE-broh) — I'm reading the book again.
- **Volvió a llamar** — He called again.
- **Vamos a volver a intentarlo** — We're going to try it again.
- **No volveré a hacerlo** — I won't do it again.

The literal meaning of **volver** is "to return" — here you're "returning to" doing something.

## Conjugation: Volver (Stem-changing o → ue)

**Volver** is a stem-changing verb (**o → ue** in stressed forms) with an **irregular past participle**: **vuelto**.

| Pronoun | Present | Preterite | Past Participle |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | **vuelvo** | volví | — |
| tú | **vuelves** | volviste | — |
| él / ella / usted | **vuelve** | volvió | — |
| nosotros | volvemos | volvimos | — |
| vosotros | volvéis | volvisteis | — |
| ellos / ustedes | **vuelven** | volvieron | — |
| (compound tenses) | | | **vuelto** |

So: **he vuelto a llamar** — I've called again.

## Comparing with De Nuevo / Otra Vez

All three express "again", but **volver a + infinitivo** is the most natural and economical:

| Spanish | English |
|---|---|
| **Lo voy a hacer otra vez** | I'm going to do it again. *(plain)* |
| **Lo voy a hacer de nuevo** | I'm going to do it again. *(slightly more formal)* |
| **Lo voy a volver a hacer** | I'm going to do it again. *(most idiomatic)* |

You can even **combine them for emphasis**: *Vuelve a llamarme otra vez* — Call me again (one more time).

## Pronoun Placement

Object pronouns can climb to the front of **volver** or attach to the **infinitive**:

- **Lo vuelvo a leer** = **Vuelvo a leerlo** — I'm reading it again.
- **Te volveré a llamar** = **Volveré a llamarte** — I'll call you again.

Both are correct; the climbed form (Lo vuelvo a leer) is slightly more common in speech.

## Negative: "Never Do X Again"

> **No volver a + infinitivo** = "to never do X again"

- **No volveré a verlo** (noh bohl-beh-REH ah behr-LOH) — I'll never see him again.
- **¡No vuelvas a decir eso!** — Don't say that again!
- **Nunca volvió a llamar** — He never called again.

This is an emphatic, slightly emotional way to swear off something. Stronger than *no lo haré otra vez*.

## Volver a vs Regresar a

**Don't confuse:**

- **Volver a casa** = "to return home" — physical return. *(volver + a + place)*
- **Volver a hacer** = "to do again" — the idiom. *(volver + a + infinitive)*

The two meanings of **volver a** depend entirely on what follows: a noun (place) → return; an infinitive → again.

| Construction | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Vuelvo a casa** | I'm going back home. |
| **Vuelvo a comer** | I'm eating again. |
| **Vuelvo a Madrid** | I'm returning to Madrid. |
| **Vuelvo a estudiar** | I'm studying again. |

## Mini-Dialogue

> — ¿Has hablado con Pedro? _(ahs ah-BLAH-doh kohn PEH-droh)_
> — Sí, ayer **volví a** llamarle, pero no contestó. _(see, ah-YEHR bohl-BEE ah yah-MAR-leh)_
> — Pues prueba esta noche. Si no contesta, no **vuelvas a** intentarlo más. _(pwehs PRWEH-bah EHS-tah NOH-cheh)_
> — Vale. Y si nada funciona, **vuelvo a** escribirle un mensaje. _(BWEHL-boh ah ehs-kree-BEER-leh)_
>
> Translation: "Have you spoken with Pedro?" — "Yes, yesterday I called him again, but he didn't answer." — "Well, try tonight. If he doesn't answer, don't try again." — "Okay. And if nothing works, I'll text him again."

Notice the **preterite** (*volví a*), the **negative imperative** (*no vuelvas a*), and the **present** (*vuelvo a*) all flowing naturally.

## Useful Frequent Examples

- **Vuelve a empezar** — Start again.
- **Volvió a casarse** — He/She got married again.
- **No volveré a fumar** — I'll never smoke again.
- **Voy a volver a leerlo** — I'm going to re-read it.
- **¿Lo vuelves a hacer?** — Are you doing it again?
