# Soler + Infinitivo (Habitual Actions)

**Soler + infinitivo** is the elegant Spanish way to express **habit** — what someone usually or typically does. English uses adverbs like "usually" or "tends to", but Spanish has a dedicated verb. It's high-frequency, native-sounding, and used constantly in everyday conversation.

## The Core Construction

> **Soler (conjugated) + infinitivo** = "to usually / typically + verb"

- **Suelo desayunar a las ocho** (SWEH-loh deh-sah-yoo-NAHR ah lahs OH-choh) — I usually have breakfast at eight.
- **Suele llover en abril** — It usually rains in April.
- **Solemos cenar tarde** — We usually have dinner late.
- **¿Sueles ir al gimnasio?** — Do you usually go to the gym?

The English equivalent is a sentence with **"usually"**, **"tend to"**, or **"normally"** — but in Spanish it's wrapped into the verb.

## Conjugation: Soler (Stem-changing o → ue)

**Soler** is a stem-changing verb: **o → ue** in stressed forms (everywhere except *nosotros* and *vosotros*).

| Pronoun | Form | Pronunciation |
|---|---|---|
| yo | **suelo** | SWEH-loh |
| tú | **sueles** | SWEH-lehs |
| él / ella / usted | **suele** | SWEH-leh |
| nosotros | **solemos** | soh-LEH-mohs |
| vosotros | **soléis** | soh-LAYS |
| ellos / ustedes | **suelen** | SWEH-lehn |

## Critical Rule: Only Present and Imperfect

Like **acabar de**, **soler** is **defective** — it works only in two tenses:

1. **Present** (suelo, sueles…) → "usually do" — habitual in the present.
2. **Imperfect** (solía, solías…) → "used to" — habitual in the past.

It is **not used in the preterite**, **future**, **conditional**, or **subjunctive** in everyday Spanish.

## Imperfect: "Used To"

> **Solía + infinitivo** = "I used to + verb"

- **Solía jugar al fútbol cuando era niño** (soh-LEE-ah hoo-GAHR ahl FOOT-bohl) — I used to play soccer when I was a kid.
- **Solíamos visitar a mis abuelos los domingos** — We used to visit my grandparents on Sundays.
- **Antes solía fumar, pero ya no** — I used to smoke, but not anymore.

This is the cleanest way to translate English **"used to"** for habits. (For "used to + state", *imperfect alone* also works: *Vivía en Madrid* — I used to live in Madrid.)

| Imperfect Form | Pronunciation |
|---|---|
| yo solía | soh-LEE-ah |
| tú solías | soh-LEE-ahs |
| él / ella solía | soh-LEE-ah |
| nosotros solíamos | soh-LEE-ah-mohs |
| vosotros solíais | soh-LEE-ahys |
| ellos solían | soh-LEE-ahn |

## Soler vs the Plain Present

Spanish's plain present already conveys habit (*Estudio español = I study Spanish*), so when do you need *soler*?

Use **soler** when you want to **emphasize regularity** — to mark something as a recurring pattern rather than a current state.

- **Estudio español** — I study Spanish. *(neutral)*
- **Suelo estudiar español por la noche** — I usually study Spanish at night. *(emphasizes habit)*

Native speakers reach for **soler** especially when adding details about **when**, **how often**, or **under what circumstances** something happens.

## Negative: "Usually Don't"

Place **no** before **soler**:

- **No suelo desayunar** (noh SWEH-loh deh-sah-yoo-NAHR) — I don't usually have breakfast.
- **No solemos salir entre semana** — We don't usually go out on weekdays.
- **No solía hacer ejercicio** — I didn't use to exercise.

This is a softer, more natural negation than *nunca* (never).

## Mini-Dialogue

> — ¿Qué **sueles** hacer los fines de semana? _(keh SWEH-lehs ah-SEHR lohs FEE-nehs deh seh-MAH-nah)_
> — **Suelo** quedarme en casa, leer un poco, ver alguna serie. Pero cuando era estudiante, **solía** salir todos los sábados. _(SWEH-loh keh-DAR-meh ehn KAH-sah; soh-LEE-ah sah-LEER toh-dohs lohs SAH-bah-dohs)_
> — Ah, ya. ¿Y tu hermana?
> — Ella **suele** ir al campo los domingos. **No suele** quedarse en la ciudad. _(EH-yah SWEH-leh eer ahl KAHM-poh; noh SWEH-leh keh-DAR-seh)_
>
> Translation: "What do you usually do on weekends?" — "I usually stay home, read a bit, watch some series. But when I was a student, I used to go out every Saturday." — "Ah, got it. And your sister?" — "She usually goes to the countryside on Sundays. She doesn't usually stay in the city."

Notice **suelo** (present habit), **solía** (past habit), and **no suele** (negative habit) all together.

## Quick Recap

- **Suelo / Sueles / Suele / Solemos / Soléis / Suelen + infinitivo** → "usually + verb"
- **Solía / Solías / Solía / Solíamos / Solíais / Solían + infinitivo** → "used to + verb"
- **Only present and imperfect** — no other tenses
- **No suelo + inf** → "don't usually + verb"
