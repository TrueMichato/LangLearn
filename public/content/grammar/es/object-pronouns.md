# Direct & Indirect Object Pronouns (lo, la, le, se)

Object pronouns replace or reference nouns without repeating them. Spanish has two sets: **direct object pronouns** (who or what directly receives the action) and **indirect object pronouns** (to/for whom the action is done). Mastering their placement and the key rule — **le/les become se before lo/la/los/las** — unlocks fluent, natural-sounding Spanish.

## Direct Object Pronouns (DO)

The direct object answers *What?* or *Whom?* directly after the verb:

| Person | Pronoun | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| yo | **me** | Me llaman — They call me |
| tú | **te** | Te veo — I see you |
| él / usted | **lo** | Lo veo — I see him / it |
| ella / usted (f.) | **la** | La veo — I see her / it |
| nosotros | **nos** | Nos escuchan — They listen to us |
| vosotros | **os** | Os invito — I invite you all (Spain) |
| ellos / ustedes | **los** | Los llamo — I call them |
| ellas / ustedes (f.) | **las** | Las llamo — I call them |

- **¿El libro? Lo tienes tú.** (loh TYEH-nehs) — The book? You have it.
- **Compré la pizza. La comemos ahora.** — I bought the pizza. We eat it now.
- **Busco a María. La busco.** (lah BOOS-koh) — I look for María. I look for her.

## Indirect Object Pronouns (IO)

The indirect object answers *To/For whom?* — it identifies the recipient:

| Person | Pronoun | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| yo | **me** | Me das el libro — You give me the book |
| tú | **te** | Te escribo — I write to you |
| él / ella / usted | **le** | Le doy el regalo — I give him/her the gift |
| nosotros | **nos** | Nos explica la tarea — She explains the homework to us |
| vosotros | **os** | Os mando un mensaje — I send you all a message |
| ellos / ellas / ustedes | **les** | Les cuento una historia — I tell them a story |

- **Le pregunto al profesor** (leh preh-GOON-toh) — I ask the professor
- **¿Qué te parece?** (keh teh pah-REH-seh) — What do you think? (lit. How does it seem to you?)

## Pronoun Placement

Object pronouns go **before** the conjugated verb in most sentences:

- **Lo veo** (loh VEH-oh) — I see it / him
- **Te llamo mañana** — I'll call you tomorrow
- **No me gusta** (noh meh GOOS-tah) — I don't like it

With **infinitives, gerunds, or affirmative imperatives**, the pronoun attaches to the end:

- **Quiero verlo** (KYEH-roh VEHR-loh) — I want to see it *(or: Lo quiero ver)*
- **Estoy llamándote** (ehs-TOH-ee yah-MAHN-doh-teh) — I am calling you *(or: Te estoy llamando)*
- **¡Dámelo!** (DAH-meh-loh) — Give it to me! *(imperative — must attach)*

## When Two Pronouns Meet: IO Comes First

When a sentence has both an IO and a DO pronoun, the **IO always comes first**:

- **Me lo das** (meh loh dahs) — You give it to me *(IO=me, DO=lo)*
- **Te la mando** — I send it to you *(IO=te, DO=la)*
- **Nos los explica** — She explains them to us *(IO=nos, DO=los)*

## Le / Les → Se Before Lo / La / Los / Las

When **le** or **les** (IO) would immediately precede **lo, la, los, las** (DO), they **must** change to **se**. The sequence *le lo* is forbidden in Spanish:

| ✗ Incorrect | ✓ Correct | Meaning |
|-------------|-----------|---------|
| Le lo doy | **Se lo doy** (seh loh DOH-ee) | I give it to him/her |
| Les la mando | **Se la mando** | I send it to them |
| Le los explico | **Se los explico** | I explain them to him/her |

Because *se* is ambiguous (him/her/you/them), you can add a clarifier:

- **Se lo doy a ella** — I give it to *her* (specifically)
- **Se lo explico a ustedes** — I explain it to *you all*

## Leísmo (Spain)

In Spain, it is common to use **le** instead of **lo** for masculine animate direct objects:

- **Le vi** (Spain) = **Lo vi** (Latin America) — I saw him

This *leísmo* is non-standard but accepted by the RAE when referring to male persons. In Latin America, **lo/la/los/las** are consistently used as direct object pronouns.
