# Pronunciation & Accent Marks (Tildes)

Spanish pronunciation is remarkably consistent — once you learn the rules, you can read almost any word aloud correctly. This lesson covers the core sounds, written stress rules, and the role of the accent mark (tilde), plus the key regional differences between Latin America and Spain.

## The Five Pure Vowels

Spanish has exactly five vowel sounds, each crisp and unwavering — no "schwa" or gliding like English:

| Letter | Sound | Example |
|--------|-------|---------|
| **a** | "ah" (like "father") | **mapa** (MAH-pah) — map |
| **e** | "eh" (like "bed") | **mesa** (MEH-sah) — table |
| **i** | "ee" (like "feet") | **vivir** (bee-BEER) — to live |
| **o** | "oh" (like "go") | **libro** (LEE-broh) — book |
| **u** | "oo" (like "food") | **mucho** (MOO-choh) — a lot |

Always pronounce each vowel fully and separately: **ciudad** = syoo-DAHD (three distinct vowels).

## Tricky Consonants

### H — Always Silent

The letter **h** is never pronounced in Spanish:

- **hablar** (ah-BLAR) — to speak
- **hombre** (OHM-breh) — man
- **hotel** (oh-TEHL) — hotel

### J — Always "H"-Like

**j** produces a guttural sound like a strong English "h":

- **jamón** (hah-MOHN) — ham
- **jugar** (hoo-GAR) — to play
- **trabajo** (trah-BAH-hoh) — work

### C — Changes Before E and I

- Before **a, o, u**: hard "k" — **casa** (KAH-sah), **cosa** (KOH-sah)
- Before **e, i** in Latin America: "s" — **cena** (SEH-nah), **ciudad** (syoo-DAHD)

> 🌍 In Spain (distinción), **c** before **e/i** sounds like English "th": *cena* = "THEH-nah"

### G — Softens Before E and I

- Before **a, o, u**: hard "g" — **gato** (GAH-toh)
- Before **e, i**: soft "h" sound — **gente** (HEHN-teh) — people; **girar** (hee-RAR) — to turn

### Z — Like S in Latin America

In Latin America, **z** is always pronounced as "s":

- **zapato** (sah-PAH-toh) — shoe
- **plaza** (PLAH-sah) — square

> 🌍 In Spain, **z** sounds like "th": *zapato* = "thah-PAH-toh"

### Ñ — The "NY" Sound

The **ñ** is its own letter in Spanish, pronounced "ny" as in "canyon":

- **mañana** (mah-NYAH-nah) — tomorrow / morning
- **niño** (NEE-nyoh) — child
- **español** (ehs-pah-NYOHL) — Spanish

### RR — The Rolled R

The double-r **rr** (and a single **r** at the start of a word) is a trill:

- **perro** (PEH-rroh) — dog
- **carro** (KAH-rroh) — car
- **rosa** (RROH-sah) — rose (initial r is also trilled)

## Stress Rules

Spanish stress follows clear, predictable rules — most words need no accent mark.

### Rule 1 — Words ending in a vowel, N, or S

Stress falls on the **second-to-last syllable** (penultimate):

- **ha-blan** → **HA**-blan
- **ca-sa** → **CA**-sa
- **co-men** → **CO**-men
- **li-bros** → **LI**-bros

### Rule 2 — Words ending in any other consonant

Stress falls on the **last syllable**:

- **ha-blar** → ha-**BLAR**
- **es-pa-ñol** → es-pa-**ÑIOL**
- **ciu-dad** → ciu-**DAD**

### Rule 3 — The Tilde Overrides Everything

A written accent mark (tilde) always overrides Rules 1 and 2:

- **café** (kah-**FEH**) — last syllable stressed, overrides rule 1
- **fácil** (**FAH**-seel) — first syllable stressed, overrides rule 2
- **médico** (**MEH**-dee-koh) — three syllables, stress on first

Tildes also distinguish same-spelling words:

| With tilde | Meaning | Without tilde | Meaning |
|------------|---------|---------------|---------|
| **sí** | yes | **si** | if |
| **tú** | you | **tu** | your |
| **él** | he | **el** | the |
| **más** | more | **mas** | but (literary) |

## Regional Variation

### Seseo — Latin America (This Course)

In all of Latin America, both **c** (before e/i) and **z** sound like **s**. This is called *seseo* and is the default for this course.

### Distinción — Spain

In most of Spain, **c** (before e/i) and **z** sound like English "th" in "think," while **s** keeps its own distinct sound.

### Yeísmo

In most of the Spanish-speaking world, **ll** (double-l) and **y** are both pronounced like English "y":

- **llegar** (yeh-GAR) — to arrive
- **yo** (YOH) — I
- **calle** (KAH-yeh) — street

## The Diaeresis — Ü

The **ü** (u with two dots) appears only in **güe** and **güi** to indicate the u is pronounced:

- **pingüino** (peen-GWEE-noh) — penguin
- **güero** (GWEH-roh) — fair-haired (Mexican Spanish)

Without the diaeresis, **gue** / **gui** keep the u silent: **guerra** (GEH-rrah) — war.
