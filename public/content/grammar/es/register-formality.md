# Register & Formality (tú / usted / vosotros)

Choosing the right form of address in Spanish is one of the most socially sensitive decisions a learner faces. The wrong choice can sound rude, cold, or overly familiar. This lesson maps all second-person pronouns, their verb forms, and the cultural norms that govern when to use each.

## Overview of Second-Person Pronouns

| Pronoun | Number | Formality | Region |
|---|---|---|---|
| **tú** | singular | informal | most of Spanish-speaking world |
| **usted** | singular | formal | universal |
| **vos** | singular | informal | Argentina, Uruguay, Central America, parts of Colombia |
| **vosotros/as** | plural | informal | Spain only |
| **ustedes** | plural | formal + informal | universal (replaces vosotros in Latin America) |

## tú — Informal Singular

Use **tú** (too) with friends, family of similar age, children, and peers in casual settings:

- **¿Tú hablas inglés?** (too AH-blahs een-GLEHS) — Do you speak English?
- **¿Adónde vas?** — Where are you going?

The subject pronoun is usually dropped in practice since the verb ending makes the person clear.

## usted — Formal Singular

**Usted** (oos-TEHD) uses **third-person singular** verb forms. It is used with strangers, elders, authority figures, customers, and in professional contexts:

- **¿Usted habla inglés?** — Do you speak English? (formal)
- **¿Cómo está usted?** — How are you? (formal)
- **Por favor, tome asiento.** — Please take a seat. (usted implied)

## vos — Informal Singular (Voseo Regions)

**Vos** (bohs) is used in Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay, and parts of Central America and Colombia. Verb endings stress the last syllable with no diphthong:

| Verb | tú form | vos form |
|---|---|---|
| hablar | tú hablAS | vos hablÁS |
| tener | tú tienES | vos tenÉS |
| ser | tú erES | vos SOS |
| poder | tú puedES | vos podÉS |
| comer | tú comES | vos comÉS |
| vivir | tú vivES | vos vivÍS |

- **¿Vos qué pensás?** (bohs keh pen-SAHS) — What do you think?
- **¿De dónde sos vos?** — Where are you from?

## vosotros — Informal Plural (Spain Only)

In Spain, the informal plural is **vosotros/as** (boh-SOH-trohs). Its verb endings are distinctive:

- -ar verbs: **habláis** (ah-BLAIS) — you all speak
- -er verbs: **coméis** (koh-MAYS) — you all eat
- -ir verbs: **vivís** (bee-BEES) — you all live

- **¿Vosotros venís a la fiesta?** — Are you all coming to the party?

Latin American Spanish has no equivalent form — **ustedes** covers both formal and informal plural.

## ustedes — Plural (Universal)

**Ustedes** (oos-TEH-des) takes **third-person plural** verb forms and is understood everywhere:

- **¿Ustedes quieren más café?** — Would you all like more coffee?
- **¿De dónde son ustedes?** — Where are you all from?

## Regional Address Guide

| Country / Region | Informal singular | Formal singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mexico | tú | usted | ustedes (both) |
| Colombia | tú (+ usted even casually) | usted | ustedes (both) |
| Argentina / Uruguay | vos | usted | ustedes (both) |
| Spain | tú | usted | vosotros (inf.) / ustedes (form.) |

Colombia is notable: even close friends and family members often address each other as **usted**, which conveys warmth and closeness rather than distance in that context.

## Switching Register

A common social move is starting with **usted** with a stranger and switching to **tú** after rapport is established. The older or higher-status person typically initiates the switch:

- **"Podemos tutearnos."** (poh-DEH-mohs too-teh-AHR-nos) — We can use tú with each other.

Related verbs: **tutear** — to address with tú; **ustedear** — to address with usted; **vosear** — to address with vos.
