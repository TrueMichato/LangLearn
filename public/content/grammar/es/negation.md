# Negation & Double Negatives (no, nada, nunca, nadie)

In Spanish, negation works differently from English in one crucial way: **double negatives are not only allowed — they are required**. Once you internalize this rule, building negative sentences becomes fast and natural.

## Basic Negation with no

Place **no** directly before the conjugated verb:

- **No hablo francés.** (noh AH-bloh frahn-SEHS) — I do not speak French.
- **No tengo hambre.** — I am not hungry.
- **No vamos al mercado.** — We are not going to the market.

If object pronouns appear before the verb, **no** goes before all of them:

- **No lo sé.** — I do not know it.
- **No me gusta el café.** — I do not like coffee.

## Negative Words

| Word | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **nada** | NAH-dah | nothing / anything |
| **nunca** | NOON-kah | never / ever |
| **jamás** | hah-MAHS | never / ever (emphatic) |
| **nadie** | NAH-dyeh | nobody / anybody |
| **ninguno/a** | neen-GOO-noh | none / any |
| **tampoco** | tahm-POH-koh | neither / not either |
| **ni...ni** | nee...nee | neither...nor |

**ninguno** shortens to **ningún** before a masculine singular noun: **ningún problema** — no problem.

## The Double Negative Rule

When a negative word **follows** the verb, **no** must also appear before the verb. This looks like a double negative to English speakers, but it is standard and required:

- **No tengo nada.** — I do not have anything. (literally: "I do not have nothing")
- **No conozco a nadie.** — I do not know anyone.
- **No voy nunca al cine.** — I never go to the movies.
- **No quiero ni café ni té.** — I want neither coffee nor tea.

You can stack multiple negatives freely:

- **No le doy nada a nadie nunca.** — I never give anything to anyone.

## Negative Word Before the Verb

When the negative word **precedes** the verb, **no** is not needed — the negative word alone is sufficient:

| no + verb + negative word | negative word + verb |
|---|---|
| **No voy nunca al cine.** | **Nunca voy al cine.** |
| **No vino nadie.** | **Nadie vino.** |
| **No me gusta nada.** | **Nada me gusta.** |
| **No tengo ningún problema.** | **Ningún problema tengo.** |

Both sentences in each pair carry exactly the same meaning.

## tampoco — Neither / Not Either

**tampoco** (tahm-POH-koh) mirrors **también** (also). Use it when agreeing with a negative statement:

- **— No me gusta el brócoli.**  — I do not like broccoli.
- **— A mí tampoco.** — Me neither. / Neither do I.

As with other negative words, **no** is required when **tampoco** follows the verb:

- **A mí no me gusta tampoco.** — I do not like it either.

When **tampoco** precedes the verb, no **no** is needed:

- **Tampoco me gusta a mí.** — I do not like it either.
