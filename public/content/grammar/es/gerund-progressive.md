# Gerund & Progressive (estar + -ndo)

The **gerundio** (gerund) is the Spanish equivalent of the English "-ing" form — but it's used far more narrowly. While English "-ing" serves as noun, adjective, and verb, the Spanish gerund is primarily a verbal form describing an ongoing action. Understanding where it does and doesn't apply will help you avoid the most common translation trap for English speakers.

## Gerund Formation

### Regular Patterns

| Verb type | Rule | Example |
|-----------|------|---------|
| -ar verbs | drop -ar → add **-ando** | hablar → **hablando** |
| -er verbs | drop -er → add **-iendo** | comer → **comiendo** |
| -ir verbs | drop -ir → add **-iendo** | vivir → **viviendo** |

### Spelling and Stem Changes

When the stem ends in a vowel, **-iendo** becomes **-yendo** to avoid three vowels in a row:

- leer → le**y**endo (leh-YEN-doh)
- oír → o**y**endo (oh-YEN-doh)
- ir → **yendo** (YEN-doh)
- traer → tra**y**endo

Stem-changing **-ir** verbs apply the same vowel shift as in the preterite (e→i, o→u):

- dormir (o→u) → **durmiendo** (door-MYEN-doh)
- pedir (e→i) → **pidiendo** (pee-DYEN-doh)
- seguir (e→i) → **siguiendo**
- poder (o→u) → **pudiendo**
- venir (e→i) → **viniendo**

## The Progressive: estar + gerundio

Combine **estar** (in any tense) with an invariable gerund to express an **ongoing action**:

| Tense of estar | Example | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|---------|
| Present | **Estoy comiendo** | I am eating (right now) |
| Preterite | **Estuve estudiando** | I was studying (for a period) |
| Imperfect | **Estaba durmiendo** | I was sleeping (ongoing in past) |
| Future | **Estaré trabajando** | I will be working |
| Conditional | **Estaría esperando** | I would be waiting |

The gerund never changes — it stays in its **-ando/-iendo** form regardless of subject.

- **Estoy comiendo** (es-TOY koh-MYEN-doh) — I am eating
- **Estaban hablando** (es-TAH-ban ah-BLAN-doh) — They were talking
- **¿Estás durmiendo?** — Are you sleeping?

## Other Auxiliaries with the Gerund

### seguir / continuar + gerundio = keep doing

- **Sigue lloviendo** (SEE-geh yoh-VYEN-doh) — It keeps raining / It's still raining
- **Continúa estudiando** — She keeps on studying

### llevar + time expression + gerundio = have been doing for X time

- **Llevo dos años estudiando español** (YEH-voh) — I have been studying Spanish for two years
- **Lleva una hora esperando** — He has been waiting for an hour
- **¿Cuánto tiempo llevas viviendo aquí?** — How long have you been living here?

## ⚠️ Key Differences from English

The Spanish gerund is **not** a noun, adjective, or future indicator. Three critical restrictions:

### DON'T use gerund as a noun → use infinitive

| ❌ Spanish gerund as noun | ✅ Correct — use infinitive |
|--------------------------|----------------------------|
| *Fumando es malo* | **Fumar es malo** — Smoking is bad |
| *Me gusta corriendo* | **Me gusta correr** — I like running |
| *Estudiando es importante* | **Estudiar es importante** — Studying is important |

### DON'T use gerund after a preposition → use infinitive

| ❌ Gerund after preposition | ✅ Correct — use infinitive |
|---------------------------|----------------------------|
| *antes de comiendo* | **antes de comer** — before eating |
| *sin estudiando* | **sin estudiar** — without studying |
| *para trabajando* | **para trabajar** — in order to work |

### DON'T use gerund for future → use ir + a or present

| ❌ Gerund for future | ✅ Correct |
|---------------------|-----------|
| *Mañana estoy yendo a Madrid* | **Mañana voy a Madrid** — Tomorrow I'm going to Madrid |

### Simple present covers English continuous in many cases

English "I'm studying" often just becomes Spanish **Estudio** (not *Estoy estudiando*) when describing habitual or general present actions:

- **Estudio español todos los días** — I'm studying / I study Spanish every day
- Reserve **estoy estudiando** for right-now, in-this-moment actions
