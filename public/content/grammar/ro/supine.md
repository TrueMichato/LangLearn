# The Supine (de făcut, ușor de învățat)

Romanian has a useful verbal form called the **supine**. It often looks like **de + past participle**: **de făcut**, **de citit**, **de mâncat**. English may translate it as **to do**, **for reading**, **to eat**, or **to be done**.

## The Basic Form

The supine is usually built with **de** plus the same short form used as a past participle.

| Infinitive | Supine | English idea |
|------------|--------|--------------|
| **a face** | **de făcut** | to do / to be done |
| **a citi** | **de citit** | to read / for reading |
| **a mânca** | **de mâncat** | to eat / food to eat |
| **a vedea** | **de văzut** | to see / worth seeing |
| **a spăla** | **de spălat** | to wash / for washing |

- **Am ceva de făcut.** (ahm CHEH-vah deh fuh-KOOT) — I have something to do.
- **Nu avem nimic de mâncat.** — We have nothing to eat.
- **Mai este mult de lucru.** — There is still a lot of work to do.

## After a avea: Obligation or Task

A very common pattern is **a avea + de + supine**, meaning **to have to do something** or **to have something to do**.

| Romanian | English |
|----------|---------|
| **Am de învățat.** | I have studying to do. |
| **Ai de scris un mesaj.** | You have a message to write. |
| **Avem multe de pregătit.** | We have many things to prepare. |
| **Nu are nimic de pierdut.** | He/she has nothing to lose. |

- **Am de citit trei pagini.** — I have three pages to read.
- **Ea are multe de rezolvat azi.** — She has many things to solve today.
- **Copiii nu au nimic de făcut.** — The children have nothing to do.

## After Adjectives: Easy, Hard, Good

The supine appears after adjectives such as **ușor**, **greu**, **plăcut**, **important**, and **imposibil**.

- **Româna este frumoasă și plăcută de învățat.** — Romanian is beautiful and pleasant to learn.
- **Textul acesta este greu de citit.** — This text is hard to read.
- **Regula este ușor de ținut minte.** — The rule is easy to remember.
- **Greșeala este imposibil de ignorat.** — The mistake is impossible to ignore.

In this use, the thing described is the object of the action: **textul este greu de citit** means **the text is hard to read**.

## Noun-Like Uses

The supine can describe the purpose of an object, much like English **for washing** or **drinking**.

| Romanian | English |
|----------|---------|
| **mașină de spălat** | washing machine |
| **apă de băut** | drinking water |
| **carte de citit** | book to read / reading book |
| **haine de purtat la serviciu** | clothes to wear at work |

- **Caut o carte bună de citit în tren.** — I am looking for a good book to read on the train.
- **Avem apă de băut pentru toată lumea.** — We have drinking water for everyone.
- **Aceasta este lista de cumpărat.** — This is the shopping list.

## Supine vs. Infinitive and Subjunctive

Romanian often uses **să + verb** where English uses an infinitive, but the supine has its own common environments.

| Use | Natural Romanian |
|-----|------------------|
| I want to read. | **Vreau să citesc.** |
| I have something to read. | **Am ceva de citit.** |
| It is easy to read. | **Este ușor de citit.** |

Do not replace every English **to** with the supine. Learn the fixed patterns: **am de...**, **ușor de...**, **greu de...**, and nouns like **mașină de spălat**.
