# Impersonal & Weather Verbs (plouă, trebuie, se pare)

Impersonal verbs do not have a normal personal subject like **eu** or **noi**. Romanian uses them for weather, necessity, possibility, appearance, and many feelings or needs.

## Weather Verbs

Weather verbs usually appear in the third person singular.

| Romanian | English |
|----------|---------|
| **plouă** | it is raining |
| **ninge** | it is snowing |
| **tună** | it is thundering |
| **fulgeră** | there is lightning |
| **se înseninează** | it is clearing up |
| **se întunecă** | it is getting dark |

- **Plouă de dimineață.** (PLOH-uh deh dee-mee-NYAH-tsuh) — It has been raining since morning.
- **Mâine o să ningă.** — Tomorrow it is going to snow.
- **Tună și fulgeră.** — It is thundering and lightning.

## Weather with e / este

Romanian also uses **e** or **este** with weather nouns and adjectives.

| Romanian | English |
|----------|---------|
| **E frig.** | It is cold. |
| **E cald.** | It is hot. |
| **E soare.** | It is sunny. |
| **E ceață.** | It is foggy. |
| **E vânt.** | It is windy. |
| **Bate vântul.** | The wind is blowing. |

- **Azi e frig, dar e soare.** — Today it is cold, but sunny.
- **Dimineața era ceață.** — In the morning it was foggy.
- **La munte bate vântul tare.** — In the mountains the wind is blowing hard.

## trebuie, se poate, se pare

These impersonal expressions are extremely common.

| Romanian | English |
|----------|---------|
| **trebuie să...** | it is necessary to / must |
| **se poate** | it is possible / one may |
| **nu se poate** | it is not possible / not allowed |
| **se pare că...** | it seems that... |
| **merită să...** | it is worth it to... |

- **Trebuie să plec acum.** — I have to leave now.
- **Se poate plăti cu cardul?** — Can one pay by card? / Is card payment possible?
- **Se pare că trenul întârzie.** — It seems that the train is late.
- **Merită să înveți expresia aceasta.** — It is worth learning this expression.

## Dative Experiencers

Some Romanian expressions put the experiencer in the dative, not as a normal subject. English often uses **I** as the subject.

| Romanian | Literal idea | Natural English |
|----------|--------------|-----------------|
| **Îmi place cafeaua.** | To me is pleasing the coffee. | I like coffee. |
| **Îmi trebuie o hartă.** | To me is needed a map. | I need a map. |
| **Mi-e foame.** | To me is hunger. | I am hungry. |
| **Mi-e frig.** | To me is cold. | I am cold. |
| **Mi-e dor de tine.** | To me is longing for you. | I miss you. |

- **Îți place muzica românească?** — Do you like Romanian music?
- **Ne trebuie două bilete.** — We need two tickets.
- **Le e frică de furtună.** — They are afraid of the storm.

The thing liked or needed often controls the verb: **Îmi place cartea** (singular), but **Îmi plac cărțile** (plural).

## Body and Feeling Expressions

Romanian often uses body-part subjects with **a durea**.

- **Mă doare capul.** — My head hurts / I have a headache.
- **O doare mâna.** — Her hand hurts.
- **Îl dor ochii.** — His eyes hurt.

Notice the verb agrees with the body part: **capul doare** (singular), **ochii dor** (plural).
