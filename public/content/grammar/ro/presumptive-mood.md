# The Presumptive Mood (o fi, o fi făcând)

Romanian has a distinctive **presumptive mood** for guesses, probability, uncertainty, and wondering aloud. English often translates it with **probably**, **must have**, **I wonder**, or **maybe**.

## The Core Idea

The presumptive does not state a fact directly. It presents the action as something the speaker supposes.

- **O fi acasă.** (oh fee ah-KAH-suh) — He/she is probably at home.
- **O fi plecat.** — He/she has probably left.
- **Unde o fi cheia?** — I wonder where the key is.

The small word **o** is a reduced future auxiliary, common in speech for third person singular. For plural, use **or**:

- **Or fi obosiți.** — They are probably tired.
- **Or fi ajuns deja.** — They have probably arrived already.

## Perfect Presumptive: o fi + participle

For a guessed completed action, use **o fi** plus the past participle.

| Romanian | English |
|----------|---------|
| **o fi plecat** | he/she has probably left |
| **o fi uitat** | he/she has probably forgotten |
| **o fi înțeles** | he/she has probably understood |
| **or fi venit** | they have probably come |

- **Maria nu răspunde; o fi adormit.** — Maria is not answering; she has probably fallen asleep.
- **De ce întârzie? O fi pierdut autobuzul.** — Why is he late? He probably missed the bus.
- **Copiii sunt liniștiți; or fi terminat tema.** — The children are quiet; they have probably finished the homework.

## Present / Progressive Presumptive: o fi + gerund

For an action imagined as happening now, Romanian can use **o fi** plus a gerund in **-ând** or **-ind**.

| Romanian | English |
|----------|---------|
| **o fi dormind** | he/she is probably sleeping |
| **o fi citind** | he/she is probably reading |
| **o fi lucrând** | he/she is probably working |
| **or fi așteptând** | they are probably waiting |

- **Ce-o fi făcând acum?** — I wonder what he/she is doing now.
- **Nu răspunde la telefon; o fi conducând.** — He/she is not answering the phone; he/she is probably driving.
- **E liniște în clasă; elevii or fi scriind.** — It is quiet in class; the students are probably writing.

The gerund form often makes the guess feel more vivid or in progress.

## Person Forms

In everyday Romanian, the third-person forms **o fi** and **or fi** are the most common. Other persons exist, especially in questions or colloquial speech.

| Person | Colloquial form | Example |
|--------|-----------------|---------|
| eu | **oi fi** | **Oi fi greșit?** — Maybe I was wrong? |
| tu | **ăi fi** | **Ăi fi uitat?** — Maybe you forgot? |
| el / ea | **o fi** | **O fi știind.** — He/she probably knows. |
| noi | **om fi** | **Om fi ajuns prea devreme.** — Maybe we arrived too early. |
| voi | **oți fi** | **Oți fi obosiți.** — You all are probably tired. |
| ei / ele | **or fi** | **Or fi plecat.** — They probably left. |

More formal or literary Romanian can use full future-like forms such as **voi fi**, **vei fi**, **va fi**, **vom fi**, **veți fi**, **vor fi**.

## Rhetorical Wonder

The presumptive is very common in questions where the speaker wonders rather than asks for exact information.

- **Unde o fi Andrei?** — I wonder where Andrei is.
- **Cine o fi la ușă?** — I wonder who is at the door.
- **Ce-o fi însemnând asta?** — I wonder what this means.

These questions often sound softer than a direct question with **este** or **înseamnă**.
