# Indefinite Articles (un, o, niște)

Indefinite articles introduce something as **a**, **an**, or **some**. Romanian articles agree with the gender and number of the noun.

## Singular: un and o

Romanian has two singular indefinite articles:

| Gender | Article | Example |
|--------|---------|---------|
| **Masculine** | **un** | **un băiat** — a boy |
| **Feminine** | **o** | **o fată** — a girl |
| **Neuter** | **un** | **un scaun** — a chair |

- **Am un telefon.** (ahm oon teh-leh-FON) — I have a phone.
- **Văd o pisică.** (vuhd oh PEE-see-kuh) — I see a cat.
- **Cumpăr un caiet.** (koom-PUHR oon kah-YET) — I am buying a notebook.

Neuter nouns use **un** in the singular because they behave like masculine singular nouns.

## Plural: niște

The general plural indefinite article is **niște**, meaning **some**. It can be used with masculine, feminine, and neuter plural nouns.

| Noun | Plural with niște | English |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| **băieți** | **niște băieți** | some boys |
| **fete** | **niște fete** | some girls |
| **scaune** | **niște scaune** | some chairs |
| **cărți** | **niște cărți** | some books |

- **Pe masă sunt niște cărți.** (peh MAH-suh soont NEESH-teh kuhrtsʲ) — There are some books on the table.
- **Am cumpărat niște mere.** (ahm koom-puh-RAHT NEESH-teh MEH-reh) — I bought some apples.

**Niște** can also mean an indefinite amount with mass nouns:

- **Vreau niște apă.** (vrow NEESH-teh AH-puh) — I want some water.
- **Avem niște pâine.** (ah-VEM NEESH-teh PUH-ee-neh) — We have some bread.

## Indefinite Article vs Number One

**Un** and **o** also mean **one**. Context tells you whether the meaning is an article or a number.

- **Am un frate.** — I have a brother / I have one brother.
- **Am o soră.** — I have a sister / I have one sister.

When counting carefully, Romanian often stresses the number:

- **Am doar un frate.** (ahm doar oon FRAH-teh) — I have only one brother.
- **Vreau o singură cafea.** (vrow oh SEEN-goo-ruh kah-FEAH) — I want one single coffee.

## With Professions and Roles

After **a fi** (to be), Romanian often omits the article before a profession or role when the noun simply identifies someone.

- **Ea este studentă.** (yah YES-teh stoo-DEN-tuh) — She is a student.
- **El este medic.** (yel YES-teh MEH-deek) — He is a doctor.

Use the article when you add a description or make the noun more specific:

- **Ea este o studentă foarte bună.** — She is a very good student.
- **El este un medic cunoscut.** — He is a well-known doctor.

## Comparing Definite and Indefinite

| Indefinite | Definite | English |
|------------|----------|---------|
| **un băiat** | **băiatul** | a boy / the boy |
| **o fată** | **fata** | a girl / the girl |
| **un scaun** | **scaunul** | a chair / the chair |
| **niște case** | **casele** | some houses / the houses |

- **Văd un câine. Câinele este negru.** — I see a dog. The dog is black.
- **Citesc o carte. Cartea este scurtă.** — I am reading a book. The book is short.
