# Reported Speech & Indirect Questions

**Reported speech** tells what someone said without repeating the exact words. Romanian usually changes the person and time words, but it does **not** require the strict tense back-shift that English often uses.

## Statements with că

For statements, Romanian commonly uses **că** (that).

| Direct speech | Reported speech | English |
|---------------|-----------------|---------|
| **„Vin mâine.”** | **A spus că vine a doua zi.** | He/she said that he/she was coming the next day. |
| **„Sunt obosită.”** | **Maria a spus că este obosită.** | Maria said that she was tired. |
| **„Am terminat.”** | **El a spus că a terminat.** | He said that he had finished / finished. |

Notice that **vine** stays present in Romanian if the coming is still presented from the speaker's reported viewpoint. English often changes **come** to **was coming**, but Romanian is more flexible.

- **Ana spune: „Învăț româna.”** — Ana says: “I am learning Romanian.”
- **Ana spune că învață româna.** — Ana says that she is learning Romanian.
- **Ana a spus că învață româna.** — Ana said that she is/was learning Romanian.

## Person and Deictic Shifts

Words tied to the original speaker or moment often change.

| Direct word | Common reported form | Example |
|-------------|----------------------|---------|
| **eu** | **el / ea** or a name | **A spus că el pleacă.** |
| **mă** | **se** or another pronoun | **A spus că se întoarce.** |
| **aici** | **acolo** | **A spus că locuiește acolo.** |
| **mâine** | **a doua zi** | **A spus că vine a doua zi.** |
| **ieri** | **cu o zi înainte** | **A spus că venise cu o zi înainte.** |

- Direct: **„Mă întorc aici mâine.”** — “I am coming back here tomorrow.”
- Reported: **A spus că se întoarce acolo a doua zi.** — He/she said that he/she was coming back there the next day.

The exact shifts depend on the situation. If you are still in the same place, **aici** may remain natural.

## No Strict English-Style Back-Shift

English often changes tenses mechanically: **I am** becomes **he said he was**. Romanian does not have to do this.

- **Ea a spus că este bolnavă.** — She said that she is/was sick.
- **Ea a spus că era bolnavă.** — She said that she was sick. (more clearly past/backgrounded)
- **El a zis că vine mai târziu.** — He said that he is/was coming later.

Use the Romanian tense that best expresses the timing. Do not automatically change every present into an imperfect.

## Indirect Questions

For information questions, keep the question word: **unde**, **cine**, **ce**, **când**, **de ce**, **cum**.

| Direct question | Indirect question | English |
|-----------------|-------------------|---------|
| **„Unde ești?”** | **M-a întrebat unde sunt.** | He/she asked me where I am/was. |
| **„Ce faci?”** | **M-a întrebat ce fac.** | He/she asked me what I am/was doing. |
| **„Când pleci?”** | **M-a întrebat când plec.** | He/she asked me when I am/was leaving. |

For yes/no questions, use **dacă** (if/whether).

- **„Vii azi?”** → **M-a întrebat dacă vin azi.** — He/she asked me whether I am coming today.
- **„Ai timp?”** → **M-a întrebat dacă am timp.** — He/she asked me if I have time.

Indirect questions do not need a question mark when they are part of a statement.

## Reporting Commands

Commands and requests usually become **să** clauses.

| Direct command | Reported command | English |
|----------------|------------------|---------|
| **„Pleacă!”** | **Mi-a spus să plec.** | He/she told me to leave. |
| **„Nu pleca!”** | **Mi-a spus să nu plec.** | He/she told me not to leave. |
| **„Veniți aici!”** | **Ne-a spus să venim acolo.** | He/she told us to come there. |

Use the right person after **să**:

- **Ți-a spus să aștepți.** — He/she told you to wait.
- **Le-a spus să intre.** — He/she told them to enter.
