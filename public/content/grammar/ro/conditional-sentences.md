# Conditional Sentences (real & unreal dacă-clauses)

Romanian conditional sentences use **dacă** to mean **if**. The tense and mood show whether the condition is real, possible, unlikely, or contrary to fact.

## Real Conditions

For real or open conditions, Romanian often uses present tense in the **dacă** clause and present, future, or imperative in the main clause.

| Romanian | English |
|----------|---------|
| **Dacă plouă, stăm acasă.** | If it rains, we stay home. |
| **Dacă ai timp, vino la noi.** | If you have time, come to our place. |
| **Dacă termini, poți pleca.** | If you finish, you can leave. |
| **Dacă o să am timp, o să te sun.** | If I have time, I will call you. |

- **Dacă înveți zilnic, faci progrese.** (DAH-kuh uhn-VETSʲ ZEEL-nik) — If you study daily, you make progress.
- **Dacă ajunge trenul la timp, mergem direct la hotel.** — If the train arrives on time, we go straight to the hotel.

## Future Real Conditions

Romanian can use future forms in both clauses, but the present is also natural when the future meaning is clear.

- **Dacă va ninge mâine, vom pleca mai târziu.** — If it snows tomorrow, we will leave later.
- **Dacă o să fie cald, o să mâncăm afară.** — If it is warm, we will eat outside.
- **Dacă primesc mesajul, îți răspund imediat.** — If I receive the message, I will answer you immediately.

The **dacă** clause does not need a special subjunctive marker. Do not say **dacă să plouă** for **if it rains**.

## Unreal Present or Future Conditions

For imagined or unlikely situations, Romanian commonly uses the conditional in both clauses.

| Romanian | English |
|----------|---------|
| **Dacă aș avea bani, aș cumpăra o casă.** | If I had money, I would buy a house. |
| **Dacă ai veni mai devreme, am avea timp.** | If you came earlier, we would have time. |
| **Dacă ar fi soare, am merge la plajă.** | If it were sunny, we would go to the beach. |

- **Dacă aș ști răspunsul, ți l-aș spune.** — If I knew the answer, I would tell it to you.
- **Dacă am locui mai aproape, ne-am vedea des.** — If we lived closer, we would see each other often.

In colloquial Romanian, you may also hear imperfect forms: **Dacă aveam bani, cumpăram o casă.** Learn the conditional pattern first because it is clear and standard.

## Unreal Past Conditions

For conditions that did not happen in the past, Romanian uses the conditional perfect: **aș fi**, **ai fi**, **ar fi**, **am fi**, **ați fi**, **ar fi** plus participle.

| Romanian | English |
|----------|---------|
| **Dacă aș fi știut, ți-aș fi spus.** | If I had known, I would have told you. |
| **Dacă ar fi plecat mai devreme, ar fi ajuns la timp.** | If he/she had left earlier, he/she would have arrived on time. |
| **Dacă am fi avut bilete, am fi intrat.** | If we had had tickets, we would have entered. |

- **Dacă nu ar fi plouat, am fi mers pe jos.** — If it had not rained, we would have walked.
- **Dacă m-ai fi sunat, te-aș fi ajutat.** — If you had called me, I would have helped you.

## Punctuation and Word Order

When the **dacă** clause comes first, a comma is common after it. When it comes second, no comma is usually needed in a short sentence.

- **Dacă ești gata, plecăm.** — If you are ready, we are leaving.
- **Plecăm dacă ești gata.** — We are leaving if you are ready.

Romanian word order is flexible, but the conditional auxiliaries stay with the verb: **aș veni**, **aș fi venit**, **ți-aș spune**, **ți-aș fi spus**.
