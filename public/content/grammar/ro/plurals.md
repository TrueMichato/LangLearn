# Noun Plurals

Romanian plurals are rich and sometimes unpredictable. The safest habit is to learn each noun with its singular article and plural form: **un băiat, doi băieți**; **o casă, două case**; **un scaun, două scaune**.

## Plural and Gender

Plural forms interact with gender:

| Gender | Singular | Plural with Number | English |
|--------|----------|--------------------|---------|
| **Masculine** | **un băiat** | **doi băieți** | two boys |
| **Feminine** | **o fată** | **două fete** | two girls |
| **Neuter** | **un scaun** | **două scaune** | two chairs |

Neuter nouns use **două** in the plural, just like feminine nouns.

- **Am doi prieteni.** (ahm doy pree-EH-tenʲ) — I have two friends.
- **Am două prietene.** (ahm DOH-uh pree-EH-teh-neh) — I have two female friends.
- **Am două telefoane.** (ahm DOH-uh teh-leh-FOH-ah-neh) — I have two phones.

## Common Masculine Plurals

Many masculine nouns form the plural with **-i**, often with spelling or sound changes.

| Singular | Plural | English |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **băiat** | **băieți** | boy / boys |
| **student** | **studenți** | student / students |
| **român** | **români** | Romanian / Romanians |
| **frate** | **frați** | brother / brothers |
| **câine** | **câini** | dog / dogs |

- **Studenții sunt aici.** (stoo-DEN-tsee soont ah-CH) — The students are here.
- **Frații mei locuiesc la Cluj.** (FRAH-tsee may loh-koo-YESK lah kloozh) — My brothers live in Cluj.

## Common Feminine Plurals

Feminine nouns have several common plural endings.

| Singular | Plural | English |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **casă** | **case** | house / houses |
| **fată** | **fete** | girl / girls |
| **carte** | **cărți** | book / books |
| **floare** | **flori** | flower / flowers |
| **lecție** | **lecții** | lesson / lessons |

The ending **-ă → -e** is common, but many nouns ending in **-e** form plural **-i**: **carte → cărți**, **floare → flori**.

## Common Neuter Plurals

Neuter nouns are masculine-like in singular and feminine-like in plural. Their plural endings are often **-e** or **-uri**.

| Singular | Plural | English |
|----------|--------|---------|
| **scaun** | **scaune** | chair / chairs |
| **telefon** | **telefoane** | phone / phones |
| **oraș** | **orașe** | city / cities |
| **lucru** | **lucruri** | thing / things |
| **hotel** | **hoteluri** | hotel / hotels |

- **Orașele sunt frumoase.** (oh-RAH-sheh-leh soont froo-MOH-ah-seh) — The cities are beautiful.
- **Lucrurile sunt simple.** (LOO-kroo-ree-leh soont SEEM-pleh) — The things are simple.

## Sound Changes in Plurals

Some plural forms change the stem vowel or consonant. These are normal Romanian patterns.

| Singular | Plural | Note |
|----------|--------|------|
| **masă** | **mese** | a → e |
| **seară** | **seri** | ea → e |
| **țară** | **țări** | final consonant softens |
| **băiat** | **băieți** | stem changes and -i plural |

Do not try to invent every plural from rules alone. Use rules to recognize patterns, then memorize common nouns.

## Definite Plurals

When the plural noun is definite, add the plural definite article:

- **băieți → băieții** — boys → the boys
- **fete → fetele** — girls → the girls
- **scaune → scaunele** — chairs → the chairs
- **lucruri → lucrurile** — things → the things
