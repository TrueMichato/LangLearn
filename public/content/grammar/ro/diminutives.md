# Diminutives & Augmentatives (-uț, -ică, -aș)

Romanian uses diminutives not only for small size, but also for affection, familiarity, and sometimes humor. **casă** becomes **căsuță** (little house), and **copil** can become **copilaș** (dear little child).

## What Diminutives Do

A diminutive can mean **small**, **cute**, **dear**, or **less intense**. Context matters.

| Base Word | Diminutive | English idea |
|-----------|------------|--------------|
| **casă** | **căsuță** | little house, cozy house |
| **masă** | **măsuță** | small table |
| **fată** | **fetiță** | little girl |
| **băiat** | **băiețel** | little boy |
| **copil** | **copilaș** | little/dear child |
| **pisică** | **pisicuță** | little cat / kitty |

- **Avem o căsuță la țară.** (AH-vem oh kuh-SOO-tsuh lah TSAH-ruh) — We have a little house in the countryside.
- **Fetița citește o poveste.** — The little girl is reading a story.
- **Pisicuța doarme pe canapea.** — The kitty is sleeping on the sofa.

## Common Diminutive Endings

There is no single mechanical rule, but several endings are common.

| Ending | Examples | Notes |
|--------|----------|-------|
| **-uț / -uță** | **drăguț**, **căsuță**, **pisicuță** | very common, often affectionate |
| **-el / -ică** | **băiețel**, **scăunel**, **floricică** | common with people, objects, plants |
| **-aș / -așă** | **copilaș**, **iepuraș**, **fluturaș** | affectionate, often child-friendly |
| **-ior / -ioară** | **obrăjor**, **inimioară**, **aripioară** | many lexicalized forms |

Some diminutives change the stem: **casă → căsuță**, **masă → măsuță**, **câine → cățel**. Learn frequent words as vocabulary items.

## Gender and Agreement

A diminutive has its own gender and ending, so adjectives must agree with the diminutive form.

| Diminutive | Gender | Correct Agreement |
|------------|--------|-------------------|
| **căsuță** | feminine | **căsuță frumoasă** |
| **băiețel** | masculine | **băiețel curios** |
| **măsuță** | feminine | **măsuță rotundă** |
| **scăunel** | neuter/masculine singular | **scăunel mic** |

- **Băiețelul este foarte curios.** — The little boy is very curious.
- **Am cumpărat o măsuță rotundă.** — I bought a small round table.
- **Pe balcon sunt două scăunele mici.** — On the balcony there are two small chairs.

## Diminutives in Tone

Diminutives can make speech warmer, but too many can sound childish or ironic.

- **Vrei o cafeluță?** — Would you like a little coffee? (friendly, informal)
- **Mai așteaptă un minuțel.** — Wait just a tiny minute. (softening)
- **A venit cu o problemuță.** — He/she came with a little problem. (maybe small, maybe understated)

In formal writing, use ordinary nouns unless the diminutive is part of the normal word.

## Augmentatives

Augmentatives suggest largeness, roughness, or sometimes a negative attitude. They are informal and must be used carefully.

| Base Word | Augmentative | English idea |
|-----------|--------------|--------------|
| **casă** | **căsoi** | big, bulky house |
| **carte** | **cărțoi** | big book, sometimes heavy/boring book |
| **piatră** | **pietroi** | large stone / boulder |
| **băiat** | **băietan** | big lad, strapping boy |

- **Au construit un căsoi la marginea satului.** — They built a huge house at the edge of the village.
- **Cară un cărțoi în ghiozdan.** — He/she is carrying a huge book in the backpack.

Augmentatives can sound humorous or judgmental, so avoid them in polite formal contexts.
