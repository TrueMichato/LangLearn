# The Vocative Case (Direct Address)

Romanian uses the **vocative case** when you call, greet, warn, or address someone directly. English usually just uses the name or title; Romanian often changes the ending: **băiat** → **băiete!**, **domn** → **domnule!**, **fetiță** → **fetițo!**.

## When to Use the Vocative

Use the vocative for direct address, often with a comma in writing.

| Normal Form | Direct Address | English |
|-------------|----------------|---------|
| **băiat** | **Băiete!** | Boy! |
| **Ion** | **Ioane!** | Ion! / John! |
| **doamnă** | **Doamnă!** | Ma'am! |
| **fetiță** | **Fetițo!** | Little girl! |

- **Băiete, vino aici!** (buh-YEH-teh VEE-noh ah-CHʲ) — Boy, come here!
- **Ioane, unde ești?** (yoh-AH-neh OON-deh yeshʲ) — Ion, where are you?
- **Doamnă, vă rog să așteptați.** — Ma'am, please wait.

## Masculine Singular: -e and -ule

Many masculine nouns and names take **-e** in direct address. Some common nouns and titles take **-ule**, especially when the normal definite form ends in **-ul**.

| Base Form | Vocative | Note |
|-----------|----------|------|
| **băiat** | **băiete!** | boy! |
| **prieten** | **prietene!** | friend! |
| **Ion** | **Ioane!** | male name |
| **om** | **omule!** | man! / person! |
| **domn** | **domnule!** | sir! / Mr.! |

- **Prietene, ascultă-mă!** (pryeh-TEH-neh ahs-KOOL-tuh muh) — Friend, listen to me!
- **Omule, ai grijă!** (OH-moo-leh ay GREE-juh) — Man, be careful!
- **Domnule Popescu, aveți un telefon.** — Mr. Popescu, you have a phone call.

## Feminine Singular: Often Same Form or -o

Feminine vocatives are common with the same form as the noun, especially in polite address. Familiar or emotional address often uses **-o**.

| Base Form | Vocative | Typical Feeling |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| **doamnă** | **doamnă!** | polite, neutral |
| **domnișoară** | **domnișoară!** | polite, neutral |
| **mamă** | **mamă!** | family, neutral |
| **fetiță** | **fetițo!** | familiar |
| **Ana** | **Ano!** | familiar name form |

- **Mamă, unde sunt cheile?** (MAH-muh OON-deh soont KEY-leh) — Mom, where are the keys?
- **Fetițo, nu alerga!** (feh-TEE-tsoh noo ah-lehr-GAH) — Little girl, don't run!
- **Ano, hai cu noi!** — Ana, come with us!

## Plural Vocatives

Plural direct address often uses the normal plural form. Formal or respectful plural address can use **-lor**, especially with titles or groups.

| Group | Vocative | English |
|-------|----------|---------|
| **băieți** | **băieți!** | boys! |
| **copii** | **copii!** | children! |
| **doamne** | **doamnelor!** | ladies! |
| **domni** | **domnilor!** | gentlemen! |
| **oameni** | **oamenilor!** | people! |

- **Băieți, veniți aici!** (buh-YETSʲ veh-NEETSʲ ah-CHʲ) — Boys, come here!
- **Doamnelor și domnilor, bună seara!** — Ladies and gentlemen, good evening!
- **Oamenilor, ascultați!** — People, listen!

## Commas and Tone

In writing, put a comma before or after the person addressed. In speech, the vocative is often marked by intonation.

- **Maria, vino la masă!** — Maria, come to the table!
- **Vino, Maria, la masă!** — Come, Maria, to the table!
- **Domnule doctor, mă doare capul.** — Doctor, my head hurts.

Vocative forms can sound warm, urgent, or rude depending on tone. **Domnule!** is polite in a shop; **Omule!** can sound impatient if said sharply.
