# The Definite Article (Enclitic -ul, -a, -le)

Romanian has a signature feature: the definite article **the** is usually attached to the end of the noun. This is called an **enclitic article**.

## The Basic Idea

English puts **the** before the noun. Romanian attaches it after the noun:

| Indefinite | Definite | English |
|------------|----------|---------|
| **băiat** | **băiatul** | boy → the boy |
| **casă** | **casa** | house → the house |
| **scaun** | **scaunul** | chair → the chair |
| **floare** | **floarea** | flower → the flower |

- **Băiatul citește.** (buh-YAHT-ool chee-TESH-teh) — The boy is reading.
- **Casa este mare.** (KAH-sah YES-teh MAH-reh) — The house is big.
- **Floarea este frumoasă.** (FLOH-ah-ryah YES-teh froo-MOH-ah-suh) — The flower is beautiful.

## Masculine and Neuter Singular

Most masculine and neuter singular nouns add **-ul**. If the noun already ends in **-u**, the final form often looks like **-ul** after that stem.

| Base Noun | Definite | English |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| **băiat** | **băiatul** | the boy |
| **om** | **omul** | the person / man |
| **scaun** | **scaunul** | the chair |
| **drum** | **drumul** | the road |
| **teatru** | **teatrul** | the theater |

- **Omul are o carte.** (OH-mool AH-reh oh KAR-teh) — The man has a book.
- **Drumul este lung.** (DROO-mool YES-teh loong) — The road is long.

Some masculine nouns ending in **-e** take **-le**:

- **frate → fratele** — brother → the brother
- **câine → câinele** — dog → the dog

## Feminine Singular

Feminine nouns commonly change the ending:

| Base Noun | Definite | English |
|-----------|----------|---------|
| **casă** | **casa** | the house |
| **masă** | **masa** | the table |
| **carte** | **cartea** | the book |
| **floare** | **floarea** | the flower |
| **zi** | **ziua** | the day |

The pattern **-ă → -a** is very common. Nouns ending in **-e** often add **-a**, producing **-ea** in spelling: **carte → cartea**.

## Plural Definite Articles

Plural definite forms are just as important:

| Indefinite Plural | Definite Plural | English |
|-------------------|-----------------|---------|
| **băieți** | **băieții** | the boys |
| **studenți** | **studenții** | the students |
| **case** | **casele** | the houses |
| **scaune** | **scaunele** | the chairs |
| **lucruri** | **lucrurile** | the things |

Masculine plurals in **-i** often show an extra written **i** in the definite form: **băieți → băieții**. Feminine and neuter plurals often add **-le**: **casele**, **scaunele**.

- **Băieții joacă fotbal.** (buh-YEH-tsee zhwah-kuh fot-BAHL) — The boys play football.
- **Casele sunt vechi.** (KAH-seh-leh soont vekʲ) — The houses are old.
- **Scaunele sunt noi.** (SKOW-neh-leh soont noy) — The chairs are new.

## Using the Definite Article

Use the definite article when referring to a specific or known noun:

- **Am o carte. Cartea este interesantă.** — I have a book. The book is interesting.
- **Văd un câine. Câinele aleargă.** — I see a dog. The dog is running.

Romanian also uses the definite article in many general statements:

- **Româna este frumoasă.** (roh-MUH-nah YES-teh froo-MOH-ah-suh) — Romanian is beautiful.
- **Cafeaua este caldă.** (kah-FEH-ah-wah YES-teh KAHL-duh) — The coffee is hot.

## With Adjectives

Usually the noun receives the article, and the adjective follows:

- **băiatul bun** — the good boy
- **fata bună** — the good girl
- **scaunele noi** — the new chairs

If an adjective comes before the noun, the article may attach to the adjective in a more literary style: **bunul prieten** — the good friend. As a beginner, use the normal pattern: **prietenul bun**.
