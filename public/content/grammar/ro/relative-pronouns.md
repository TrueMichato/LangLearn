# Relative Pronouns (care, pe care, ce, cine)

Romanian relative pronouns connect two ideas: **omul care vorbește** means **the person who is speaking**. They let you describe a noun without starting a new sentence.

## The Basic Relative Pronoun care

The most common relative pronoun is **care**. It can mean **who**, **which**, or **that** when it is the subject of the relative clause.

| Romanian | English |
|----------|---------|
| **fata care citește** | the girl who is reading |
| **cartea care este pe masă** | the book that is on the table |
| **orașul care îmi place** | the city that I like |
| **oamenii care lucrează aici** | the people who work here |

- **Fata care citește este sora mea.** (FAH-tah KAH-reh chee-TESH-teh) — The girl who is reading is my sister.
- **Trenul care vine acum merge la Brașov.** — The train that is coming now goes to Brașov.
- **Am găsit cheia care lipsea.** — I found the key that was missing.

## Direct Objects: pe care + clitic

When the relative pronoun is a definite or personal **direct object**, Romanian usually uses **pe care** and also keeps a matching object clitic before the verb.

| Antecedent | Romanian | English |
|------------|----------|---------|
| masculine singular | **bărbatul pe care îl văd** | the man whom I see |
| feminine singular | **cartea pe care o citesc** | the book that I am reading |
| masculine plural | **copiii pe care îi ajut** | the children whom I help |
| feminine plural | **scrisorile pe care le trimit** | the letters that I send |

- **Profesorul pe care îl ascult este foarte clar.** — The teacher I am listening to is very clear.
- **Filmul pe care l-am văzut ieri a fost bun.** — The film that I saw yesterday was good.
- **Prietena pe care o aștept întârzie.** — The friend I am waiting for is late.

The clitic is not optional in careful standard Romanian: **pe care îl văd**, **pe care o citesc**, **pe care i-am invitat**, **pe care le-am cumpărat**.

## ce and ceea ce

**ce** can mean **what** or **that which**, especially when it does not refer to a specific noun. A more formal version is **ceea ce**.

- **Îmi place ce ai spus.** (uhmʲ PLAH-cheh che ay spoos) — I like what you said.
- **Nu înțeleg ce vrei.** — I do not understand what you want.
- **Ceea ce spui este important.** — What you are saying is important.

In everyday speech, **ce** is also sometimes used like **care** after a noun, but **care** is the safer standard form for learners: **cartea care îmi place**, not **cartea ce îmi place** in neutral speech.

## cine in Free Relative Clauses

**cine** means **who**. In relative-like sentences, it often means **whoever** or **the person who**.

- **Cine învață câștigă.** — Whoever studies wins.
- **Cine vine primul primește cafea.** — Whoever comes first gets coffee.
- **Spune-mi cine a sunat.** — Tell me who called.

After prepositions, **cine** is also common in questions and indirect questions:

- **Cu cine vorbești?** — Who are you speaking with?
- **Nu știu cu cine vorbește.** — I do not know who he/she is speaking with.

## Dative and Genitive Relative Forms

For **to whom** or **whose**, Romanian uses special forms of **care**. These are more advanced but very useful.

| Meaning | Form | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| to whom, masculine/neuter singular | **căruia** | **studentul căruia i-am scris** — the student to whom I wrote |
| to whom, feminine singular | **căreia** | **femeia căreia i-am trimis mesajul** — the woman to whom I sent the message |
| to whom, plural | **cărora** | **oamenii cărora le-am mulțumit** — the people whom I thanked |
| whose | **al cărui / a cărei / ai căror / ale căror** | **autorul a cărui carte o citesc** — the author whose book I am reading |

- **Am un prieten căruia îi place muzica.** — I have a friend who likes music.
- **Aceasta este profesoara a cărei lecție a fost excelentă.** — This is the teacher whose lesson was excellent.
