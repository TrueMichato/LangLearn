# Register and Formality (Registro e Formalidade)

Brazilian Portuguese has a wide range of formality levels — from academic writing to street slang. Understanding when to use formal vs. informal language is crucial for effective communication. This lesson explores the different registers and how to navigate them.

## Levels of Formality

### 1. Very Formal (Muito Formal)
Used in legal documents, academic papers, official correspondence:

- **Venho por meio desta solicitar informações a respeito do concurso.** (VEN-yoo por MAY-oo DES-tah) — I hereby request information regarding the exam.
- **Prezado(a) Senhor(a), segue em anexo o documento solicitado.** (preh-ZAH-doo seh-NYOR) — Dear Sir/Madam, please find attached the requested document.
- **Outrossim, informamos que o prazo foi prorrogado.** (OH-troh-seen) — Furthermore, we inform you that the deadline has been extended.

### 2. Formal (Formal)
Used with strangers, elderly people, professional settings:

- **O senhor poderia me informar onde fica a estação?** (oo seh-NYOR poh-deh-REE-ah) — Could you (sir) tell me where the station is?
- **A senhora gostaria de um café?** (ah seh-NYOH-rah gohs-tah-REE-ah) — Would you (ma'am) like a coffee?
- **Com licença, posso fazer uma pergunta?** (con lee-SEN-sah) — Excuse me, may I ask a question?

### 3. Standard / Neutral
Everyday polite language, used with acquaintances:

- **Você pode me ajudar?** (vo-SEH POH-jee mee ah-joo-DAR) — Can you help me?
- **Obrigado pela ajuda!** (oh-bree-GAH-doo PEH-lah ah-JOO-dah) — Thank you for the help!
- **Desculpe o incômodo.** (des-KOOW-pee oo een-KOH-moh-doo) — Sorry for the inconvenience.

### 4. Informal (Informal)
Used with friends and family:

- **Cê pode me ajudar?** (seh POH-jee) — Can you help me?
- **Valeu pela ajuda!** (vah-LEH-oo) — Thanks for the help!
- **Foi mal, cara.** (foy mow KAH-rah) — My bad, dude.

### 5. Very Informal / Slang (Gíria)
Used among close friends, youth:

- **Mano, tô precisando de uma força.** (MAH-noo toh preh-see-ZAN-doo) — Dude, I need some help.
- **Irmão, foi tenso demais!** (eer-MOWN foy TEN-soo) — Bro, it was way too intense!

## Forms of Address: Você vs. Tu vs. O Senhor

### Você (Standard — Most of Brazil)

The default "you" in most Brazilian contexts:

- **Você quer sair hoje?** (vo-SEH kehr sah-EER) — Do you want to go out today?
- **Onde você mora?** (ON-jee vo-SEH MOH-rah) — Where do you live?
- **Você já comeu?** (vo-SEH jah koh-MEH-oo) — Have you eaten yet?

### Tu (Regional — South, Northeast, some cities)

Used in Rio Grande do Sul, Santa Catarina, Pará, Maranhão, and parts of Rio. Often mixed with "você" verb forms:

- **Tu quer ir comigo?** (too kehr eer) — Do you want to go with me? *(common but grammatically informal)*
- **Tu viu o jogo ontem?** (too vee-oo) — Did you see the game yesterday?
- **Proper conjugation: Tu queres ir comigo?** — (grammatically correct but less common in casual speech)

### O Senhor / A Senhora (Formal)

Used with older people, authority figures, customers, or in professional settings:

- **O senhor deseja mais alguma coisa?** (oo seh-NYOR deh-ZEH-jah) — Would you (sir) like anything else?
- **A senhora pode assinar aqui, por favor?** (ah seh-NYOH-rah POH-jee) — Could you (ma'am) sign here, please?
- **Como o senhor se chama?** (KOH-moo oo seh-NYOR see SHAH-mah) — What is your name, sir?

### When to Use Each

| Context | Form of Address |
|---------|----------------|
| Friends, family | você / tu / cê |
| Strangers (same age) | você |
| Older people, elders | o senhor / a senhora |
| Boss, professor | o senhor / a senhora (or by title) |
| Doctor, judge | doutor(a) / meritíssimo(a) |
| Customer service | o senhor / a senhora |

## Written vs. Spoken Brazilian Portuguese

Brazilian spoken language differs significantly from the written form:

### Common Simplifications in Speech

| Written (Standard) | Spoken (Informal) | English |
|--------------------|--------------------|---------|
| Você está | Cê tá | You are |
| Estou | Tô | I am |
| Para | Pra | For/To |
| Para o | Pro | For the |
| Para a | Pra | For the |
| Não é? | Né? | Right? |
| Vamos | Vamo / Bora | Let's go |
| Está bem | Tá bom / Tá | OK |
| O que é que | O quê | What |

- **Written: Você está com fome? Vamos para o restaurante.** — Are you hungry? Let's go to the restaurant.
- **Spoken: Cê tá com fome? Vamo pro restaurante.** (seh tah con FOH-mee VAH-moo proo) — You hungry? Let's go to the restaurant.

- **Written: Eu estou indo para a casa dela.** — I'm going to her house.
- **Spoken: Tô indo pra casa dela.** (toh IN-doo prah) — Going to her house.

## Formal Writing Conventions

### Email/Letter Openings

| Formality | Opening |
|-----------|---------|
| Very formal | Prezado(a) Senhor(a) / Excelentíssimo(a) |
| Formal | Caro(a) [Name] |
| Semi-formal | Olá, [Name] |
| Informal | Oi, [Name] / E aí, [Name] |

### Email/Letter Closings

| Formality | Closing |
|-----------|---------|
| Very formal | Atenciosamente / Respeitosamente |
| Formal | Cordialmente / Att. (Atenciosamente) |
| Semi-formal | Abraços / Um abraço |
| Informal | Beijos / Bjos / Falou / Valeu |

### Example Formal Email

- **Prezado Dr. Silva,** (preh-ZAH-doo doh-TOR SEEW-vah)
- **Venho por meio desta solicitar uma reunião para discutir o projeto.** — I hereby request a meeting to discuss the project.
- **Agradeço antecipadamente pela atenção.** — Thank you in advance for your attention.
- **Atenciosamente,** — Sincerely,

## Brazilian Slang (Gírias)

Common slang you'll hear in everyday Brazilian conversation:

### People References
- **Cara** (KAH-rah) — dude, guy: **Cara, que incrível!** — Dude, how incredible!
- **Mano** (MAH-noo) — bro: **E aí, mano, tudo certo?** — What's up, bro, all good?
- **Véi / Velho** (vay / VEH-lyoo) — dude (lit. "old"): **Véi, não acredito!** — Dude, I can't believe it!
- **Galera** (gah-LEH-rah) — group of friends, crowd: **A galera toda vai.** — Everyone's going.

### Reactions and Fillers
- **Tipo** (CHEE-poo) — like (filler word): **Ele é, tipo, muito legal.** — He's, like, really cool.
- **Massa** (MAH-sah) — awesome: **Que massa!** — How awesome!
- **Sinistro** (see-NEES-troo) — crazy/awesome: **O show foi sinistro!** — The show was amazing!
- **Zoar** (zoh-AR) — to joke/mess around: **Para de me zoar!** — Stop messing with me!
- **Mó** (moh) — very (abbreviation of "maior"): **Mó legal!** — Super cool!
- **Suave** (SWAH-vee) — no problem, easy: **Pode deixar, suave.** — Don't worry, it's all good.

### Actions
- **Dar um rolê** (dar oon hoh-LEH) — to go out, hang out: **Vamos dar um rolê?** — Shall we go hang out?
- **Curtir** (koor-CHEER) — to enjoy, to like: **Estou curtindo muito.** — I'm really enjoying it.
- **Mandar bem** (man-DAR behn) — to do well: **Você mandou bem na prova!** — You did great on the test!
- **Pirar** (pee-RAR) — to freak out: **Eu pirei quando soube!** — I freaked out when I found out!
