# Register and Formality (Registro e Formalidade)

<!-- vocab-inventory: {"mode":"explicit"} -->

Brazilian Portuguese has a wide range of formality levels — from academic writing to street slang. Understanding when to use formal vs. informal language is crucial for effective communication. This lesson explores the different registers and how to navigate them.

## Levels of Formality

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"register-context-choice","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Levels of Formality","rule":"Register depends on setting, relationship, purpose, and expectations","example":"What should guide the choice between official-letter wording and slang among friends?","answer":"The relationship, setting, purpose, and listener's expectations.","explanation":"Formal and informal registers serve different social contexts; the lesson's levels are guidance rather than rigid categories for every interaction."} -->

### 1. Very Formal (Muito Formal)
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-solicitar","word":"solicitar","meaning":"to request (formal)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"1. Very Formal (Muito Formal)"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-outrossim","word":"outrossim","meaning":"furthermore; likewise (very formal)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"1. Very Formal (Muito Formal)"} -->

Used in legal documents, academic papers, official correspondence:

- **Venho por meio desta solicitar informações a respeito do concurso.** (VEN-yoo por MAY-oo DES-tah) — I hereby request information regarding the exam.
- **Prezado(a) Senhor(a), segue em anexo o documento solicitado.** (preh-ZAH-doo seh-NYOR) — Dear Sir/Madam, please find attached the requested document.
- **Outrossim, informamos que o prazo foi prorrogado.** (OH-troh-seen) — Furthermore, we inform you that the deadline has been extended.

### 2. Formal (Formal)
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-com-licenca","word":"com licença","meaning":"excuse me","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"2. Formal (Formal)"} -->

Used with strangers, elderly people, professional settings:

- **O senhor poderia me informar onde fica a estação?** (oo seh-NYOR poh-deh-REE-ah) — Could you (sir) tell me where the station is?
- **A senhora gostaria de um café?** (ah seh-NYOH-rah gohs-tah-REE-ah) — Would you (ma'am) like a coffee?
- **Com licença, posso fazer uma pergunta?** (con lee-SEN-sah) — Excuse me, may I ask a question?

### 3. Standard / Neutral
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-desculpe","word":"desculpe o incômodo","meaning":"sorry for the inconvenience","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"3. Standard / Neutral"} -->

Everyday polite language, used with acquaintances:

- **Você pode me ajudar?** (vo-SEH POH-jee mee ah-joo-DAR) — Can you help me?
- **Obrigado pela ajuda!** (oh-bree-GAH-doo PEH-lah ah-JOO-dah) — Thank you for the help!
- **Desculpe o incômodo.** (des-KOOW-pee oo een-KOH-moh-doo) — Sorry for the inconvenience.

### 4. Informal (Informal)
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-valeu","word":"valeu","meaning":"thanks (informal Brazilian use)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"4. Informal (Informal)"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-foi-mal","word":"foi mal","meaning":"my bad; sorry (informal)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"4. Informal (Informal)"} -->

Used with friends and family:

- **Cê pode me ajudar?** (seh POH-jee) — Can you help me?
- **Valeu pela ajuda!** (vah-LEH-oo) — Thanks for the help!
- **Foi mal, cara.** (foy mow KAH-rah) — My bad, dude.

### 5. Very Informal / Slang (Gíria)
Used among close friends, youth:

- **Mano, tô precisando de uma força.** (MAH-noo toh preh-see-ZAN-doo) — Dude, I need some help.
- **Irmão, foi tenso demais!** (eer-MOWN foy TEN-soo) — Bro, it was way too intense!

## Forms of Address: Você vs. Tu vs. O Senhor

### Você (Standard — Most of Brazil)

The default "you" in most Brazilian contexts:

- **Você quer sair hoje?** (vo-SEH kehr sah-EER) — Do you want to go out today?
- **Onde você mora?** (ON-jee vo-SEH MOH-rah) — Where do you live?
- **Você já comeu?** (vo-SEH jah koh-MEH-oo) — Have you eaten yet?

### Tu (Regional — South, Northeast, some cities)

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"regional-tu-standard","kind":"rule","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Tu (Regional — South, Northeast, some cities)","rule":"Standard written tu agreement differs from some regional informal usage","example":"Which is standard written tu agreement: tu queres or tu quer?","answer":"Tu queres; tu quer also occurs in regional informal Brazilian speech.","explanation":"Regional usage varies; standard written agreement is a register target, not a judgment about speakers."} -->

Used in Rio Grande do Sul, Santa Catarina, Pará, Maranhão, and parts of Rio. Often mixed with "você" verb forms:

- **Tu quer ir comigo?** (too kehr eer) — Do you want to go with me? *(common but grammatically informal)*
- **Tu viu o jogo ontem?** (too vee-oo) — Did you see the game yesterday?
- **Standard written agreement: Tu queres ir comigo?** — Informal regional agreement varies; this is a register distinction, not a judgment about speakers.

### O Senhor / A Senhora (Formal)

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"formal-address","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"O Senhor / A Senhora (Formal)","rule":"O senhor / A senhora for formal 'you'","example":"___ ___ gostaria de um café? (formal, to a woman)","answer":"A senhora","explanation":"A senhora is a formal feminine form of you and takes third-person singular agreement."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"rule-senhor-agreement","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"O Senhor / A Senhora (Formal)","rule":"O senhor and a senhora take third-person singular verbs","example":"Which verb person and number do the formal forms of address meaning sir and ma'am take?","answer":"Third-person singular, as with você (o senhor deseja, a senhora pode).","explanation":"Like você, o senhor and a senhora take third-person singular verbs: O senhor deseja mais alguma coisa?"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-senhor","word":"o senhor","meaning":"you (formal masculine address); sir","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"O Senhor / A Senhora (Formal)"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-senhora","word":"a senhora","meaning":"you (formal feminine address); ma'am","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"O Senhor / A Senhora (Formal)"} -->

Used with older people, authority figures, customers, or in professional settings:

- **O senhor deseja mais alguma coisa?** (oo seh-NYOR deh-ZEH-jah) — Would you (sir) like anything else?
- **A senhora pode assinar aqui, por favor?** (ah seh-NYOH-rah POH-jee) — Could you (ma'am) sign here, please?
- **Como o senhor se chama?** (KOH-moo oo seh-NYOR see SHAH-mah) — What is your name, sir?

Both take **third-person singular** verb forms, like *você*: **O senhor deseja**, **A senhora pode**.

### When to Use Each

| Context | Form of Address |
|---------|----------------|
| Friends, family | você / tu / cê |
| Strangers (same age) | você |
| Older people, elders | o senhor / a senhora |
| Boss, professor | o senhor / a senhora (or by title) |
| Doctor, judge | doutor(a) / meritíssimo(a) |
| Customer service | o senhor / a senhora |

| Written (Standard) | Spoken (Informal) | English |
|--------------------|--------------------|---------|
| Você está | Cê tá | You are |
| Estou | Tô | I am |
| Para | Pra | For/To |
| Para o | Pro | For the |
| Para a | Pra | For the |
| Não é? | Né? | Right? |

| O que é que...? | O que...? / O quê? (standing alone) | What |

- **Written: Você está com fome? Vamos para o restaurante.** — Are you hungry? Let's go to the restaurant.
- **Spoken: Cê tá com fome? Vamo pro restaurante.** (seh tah con FOH-mee VAH-moo proo) — You hungry? Let's go to the restaurant.

- **Written: Eu estou indo para a casa dela.** — I'm going to her house.
- **Spoken: Tô indo pra casa dela.** (toh IN-doo prah) — Going to her house.

### Example Formal Email

- **Prezado Dr. Silva,** (preh-ZAH-doo doh-TOR SEEW-vah)
- **Venho por meio desta solicitar uma reunião para discutir o projeto.** — I hereby request a meeting to discuss the project.
- **Agradeço antecipadamente pela atenção.** — Thank you in advance for your attention.
- **Atenciosamente,** — Sincerely,

## Brazilian Slang (Gírias)

Common slang you'll hear in everyday Brazilian conversation:

Slang varies by region, generation, relationship, and situation. Recognizing a phrase does not mean it suits every professional or social context.

### People References
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-cara","word":"cara","meaning":"dude; guy (Brazilian slang)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"People References"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-mano","word":"mano","meaning":"bro; dude (slang)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"People References"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-vei","word":"véi","meaning":"dude (slang variant of velho)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"People References"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-galera","word":"galera","meaning":"crowd; group of friends (informal)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"People References"} -->

- **Cara** (KAH-rah) — dude, guy: **Cara, que incrível!** — Dude, how incredible!
- **Mano** (MAH-noo) — bro: **E aí, mano, tudo certo?** — What's up, bro, all good?
- **Véi / Velho** (vay / VEH-lyoo) — dude (lit. "old"): **Véi, não acredito!** — Dude, I can't believe it!
- **Galera** (gah-LEH-rah) — group of friends, crowd: **A galera toda vai.** — Everyone's going.

### Reactions and Fillers
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-tipo","word":"tipo","meaning":"like (informal filler)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Reactions and Fillers"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-massa","word":"massa","meaning":"awesome; cool (regional Brazilian slang)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Reactions and Fillers"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-sinistro","word":"sinistro","meaning":"crazy; awesome or alarming (slang, context-dependent)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Reactions and Fillers"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-zoar","word":"zoar","meaning":"to joke; to mess around; to tease (informal)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Reactions and Fillers"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-mo","word":"mó","meaning":"very; really (Brazilian slang intensifier)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Reactions and Fillers"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-suave","word":"suave","meaning":"all good; no problem (informal use)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Reactions and Fillers"} -->

- **Tipo** (CHEE-poo) — like (filler word): **Ele é, tipo, muito legal.** — He's, like, really cool.
- **Massa** (MAH-sah) — awesome: **Que massa!** — How awesome!
- **Sinistro** (see-NEES-troo) — crazy/awesome: **O show foi sinistro!** — The show was amazing!
- **Zoar** (zoh-AR) — to joke/mess around: **Para de me zoar!** — Stop messing with me!
- **Mó** (moh) — very (abbreviation of "maior"): **Mó legal!** — Super cool!
- **Suave** (SWAH-vee) — no problem, easy: **Pode deixar, suave.** — Don't worry, it's all good.

### Actions
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-role","word":"dar um rolê","meaning":"to go out; to hang out (informal)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Actions"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-curtir","word":"curtir","meaning":"to enjoy; to like","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Actions"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-mandar-bem","word":"mandar bem","meaning":"to do well (informal)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Actions"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-pirar","word":"pirar","meaning":"to freak out (informal)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Actions"} -->

- **Dar um rolê** (dar oon hoh-LEH) — to go out, hang out: **Vamos dar um rolê?** — Shall we go hang out?
- **Curtir** (koor-CHEER) — to enjoy, to like: **Estou curtindo muito.** — I'm really enjoying it.
- **Mandar bem** (man-DAR behn) — to do well: **Você mandou bem na prova!** — You did great on the test!
- **Pirar** (pee-RAR) — to freak out: **Eu pirei quando soube!** — I freaked out when I found out!
