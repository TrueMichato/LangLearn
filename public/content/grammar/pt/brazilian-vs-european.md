# Brazilian vs. European Portuguese (Português Brasileiro vs. Europeu)

Brazilian Portuguese (PT-BR) and European Portuguese (PT-PT) are the same language, but with significant differences in pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar, and daily usage. Think of it like American English vs. British English — mutually intelligible, but noticeably different. This lesson highlights the key differences you'll encounter.

## Pronunciation Differences

### Vowels
European Portuguese "swallows" unstressed vowels, making words sound more compressed. Brazilian Portuguese pronounces all vowels more openly:

- **telefone**: BR *teh-leh-FOH-nee* vs. PT *tluh-FOHN*
- **português**: BR *por-too-GEHZ* vs. PT *pur-too-GEHSH*
- **menino**: BR *meh-NEE-noo* vs. PT *mnEE-noo*

### The Letter "t" and "d" Before "i" and "e"
In Brazilian Portuguese, "t" becomes "ch" and "d" becomes "j" before "i" and unstressed "e":

- **tia** (aunt): BR *CHEE-ah* vs. PT *TEE-ah*
- **dia** (day): BR *JEE-ah* vs. PT *DEE-ah*
- **noite** (night): BR *NOY-chee* vs. PT *NOYT*
- **cidade** (city): BR *see-DAH-jee* vs. PT *see-DAHD*

### The Letter "s" at End of Syllables
In Rio de Janeiro and parts of Brazil, final "s" is pronounced "sh" (like in European Portuguese). In São Paulo and most of Brazil, it's a plain "s":

- **festas**: Rio/PT *FESH-tahsh* vs. SP *FES-tahs*
- **mesmo**: Rio/PT *MEHZH-moo* vs. SP *MEHZ-moo*

### Example Sentences

- **BR: Vou pegar o ônibus para o trabalho.** (voh peh-GAR oo OH-nee-boos) — I'll take the bus to work.
- **PT: Vou apanhar o autocarro para o trabalho.** — I'll take the bus to work.

- **BR: Esqueci meu celular no banheiro.** (es-keh-SEE meu seh-loo-LAR) — I forgot my cell phone in the bathroom.
- **PT: Esqueci o meu telemóvel na casa de banho.** — I forgot my phone in the bathroom.

- **BR: Que legal! Vamos tomar um sorvete?** (kee leh-GOW) — How cool! Shall we have some ice cream?
- **PT: Que fixe! Vamos comer um gelado?** — How cool! Shall we have some ice cream?

## Grammar Differences

### "Você" vs. "Tu"

In Brazil, **você** is the standard "you" in most regions. In Portugal, **tu** is the standard informal "you":

- **BR: Você quer ir ao cinema?** (vo-SEH kehr eer) — Do you want to go to the movies?
- **PT: Tu queres ir ao cinema?** — Do you want to go to the movies?

Note: Some regions of Brazil (southern states, parts of the northeast) do use "tu," but often with the third person verb form: "Tu quer ir?" (grammatically incorrect but extremely common).

### "A gente" vs. "Nós"

Brazilians heavily prefer **a gente** (lit. "the people") as an informal replacement for **nós** (we). It takes third person singular conjugation:

- **BR: A gente vai ao parque amanhã.** (ah JEN-chee vai) — We're going to the park tomorrow.
- **PT: Nós vamos ao parque amanhã.** — We're going to the park tomorrow.
- **BR: A gente se diverte muito lá.** (ah JEN-chee see jee-VEHR-chee) — We have a lot of fun there.

### Gerund vs. "a + infinitive"

This is one of the most immediately recognizable differences:

- **BR: Estou falando com você.** (es-TOH fah-LAN-doo) — I'm talking to you. *(gerund)*
- **PT: Estou a falar contigo.** — I'm talking to you. *(a + infinitive)*

- **BR: Ele está comendo.** (EH-lee es-TAH koh-MEN-doo) — He's eating.
- **PT: Ele está a comer.** — He's eating.

- **BR: O que você está fazendo?** — What are you doing?
- **PT: O que é que estás a fazer?** — What are you doing?

### Object Pronoun Placement

Brazilian Portuguese tends to place object pronouns **before** the verb (proclisis), while European Portuguese places them **after** (enclisis):

- **BR: Me dá um copo de água.** (mee dah) — Give me a glass of water.
- **PT: Dá-me um copo de água.** — Give me a glass of water.

- **BR: Ele me ligou ontem.** (EH-lee mee lee-GOH) — He called me yesterday.
- **PT: Ele ligou-me ontem.** — He called me yesterday.

- **BR: Te amo.** (chee AH-moo) — I love you.
- **PT: Amo-te.** — I love you.

### "Ter" vs. "Haver" for Existence

Brazilians use **ter** (to have) where Europeans use **haver** (there is/are):

- **BR: Tem muita gente aqui.** (tehn MOO-ee-tah JEN-chee) — There are a lot of people here.
- **PT: Há muita gente aqui.** — There are a lot of people here.

- **BR: Não tem problema.** (nown tehn pro-BLEH-mah) — There's no problem.
- **PT: Não há problema.** — There's no problem.

### "É que" Construction

European Portuguese frequently uses **é que** for emphasis, especially in questions:

- **PT: O que é que tu queres?** — What do you want?
- **BR: O que você quer?** — What do you want?

- **PT: Onde é que vais?** — Where are you going?
- **BR: Onde você vai?** — Where are you going?

## Spelling Differences

The 2009 Spelling Agreement (Acordo Ortográfico) unified most spelling, but some differences remain in practice:

| Feature | Brazilian | European |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| Silent consonants | removed: fa**t**o | sometimes kept: fa**ct**o |
| Accent on -eia | id**ei**a (no accent) | id**ei**a (no accent, post-agreement) |
| Dieresis (trema) | removed in 2009 | never used |

## Quick Reference

| Feature | Brazilian | European |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| "You" | você | tu |
| "We" (informal) | a gente | nós |
| Progressive | estar + gerund (-ando) | estar a + infinitive |
| Pronouns | before verb (Me dá) | after verb (Dá-me) |
| "There is" | tem | há |
| Questions | O que você quer? | O que é que tu queres? |
