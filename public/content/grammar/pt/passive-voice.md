# The Passive Voice (Voz Passiva)

<!-- vocab-inventory: {"mode":"explicit"} -->

The passive voice in Portuguese shifts focus from who performs the action to what receives the action. Portuguese has two main passive constructions: the **analytical passive** (with ser) and the **pronominal passive** (with se). Passive participles agree with the subject in both Brazilian and European Portuguese.

## Analytical Passive: ser + Past Participle

(ser + compor, past)","answer":"foi composta","explanation":"The analytical passive uses conjugated ser plus a participle agreeing with the receiving subject."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"passive-focus","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Analytical Passive: ser + Past Participle","rule":"The action's receiver is the passive subject","example":"What becomes the grammatical subject when an active sentence is made passive?","answer":"The receiver of the action rather than its doer.","explanation":"The passive focuses on what receives the action; the agent may be expressed separately."} -->

The most straightforward passive construction uses **ser** (conjugated) + **past participle**. The participle must agree in gender and number with the subject:

- **O livro foi escrito por Machado de Assis.** (oo LEE-vroo foy es-KREE-too) — The book was written by Machado de Assis.
- **A carta foi enviada ontem.** (ah KAR-tah foy en-vee-AH-dah) — The letter was sent yesterday.
- **Os prédios foram construídos em 2010.** (ooz PREH-jee-ohs FOH-rahn cons-troo-EE-dohs) — The buildings were built in 2010.
- **As casas foram vendidas rapidamente.** (ahz KAH-zahs FOH-rahn ven-JEE-dahz) — The houses were sold quickly.
- **O problema será resolvido amanhã.** (oo pro-BLEH-mah seh-RAH heh-sow-VEE-doo) — The problem will be solved tomorrow.

### Agent with "por" / "pelo" / "pela"

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"passive-agent","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Agent with \"por\" / \"pelo\" / \"pela\"","rule":"Por introduces a passive agent and contracts with articles","example":"O gol foi marcado ___ atacante. (by the)","answer":"pelo","explanation":"The passive agent is introduced by por; por + o contracts to pelo."} -->

The agent (who does the action) is introduced by **por** (or its contractions **pelo/pela/pelos/pelas**):

- **A música foi composta por Tom Jobim.** (ah MOO-zee-kah foy com-POHS-tah) — The song was composed by Tom Jobim.
- **O gol foi marcado pelo atacante.** (oo gowl foy mar-KAH-doo PEH-loo) — The goal was scored by the striker.
- **As leis foram aprovadas pelo congresso.** (ahz lays FOH-rahn ah-pro-VAH-dahz) — The laws were approved by congress.

## Regular Past Participles

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"regular-participles","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Regular Past Participles","rule":"Regular participles replace -ar with -ado and -er/-ir with -ido","example":"How do regular -ar, -er, and -ir infinitives form their past participles?","answer":"Replace -ar with -ado, and -er or -ir with -ido.","explanation":"The regular pattern yields falado, comido, and partido; irregular participles must be learned."} -->

| Conjugation | Formation | Example |
|-------------|-----------|---------|
| -ar → -ado | falar → fal**ado** | spoken |
| -er → -ido | comer → com**ido** | eaten |
| -ir → -ido | partir → part**ido** | left |

- **A comida foi preparada com carinho.** (ah koh-MEE-dah foy preh-pah-RAH-dah) — The food was prepared with care.
- **O texto foi lido pela turma toda.** (oo TEKS-too foy LEE-doo) — The text was read by the whole class.
- **As portas foram fechadas às dez.** (ahz POR-tahz FOH-rahn feh-SHAH-dahz) — The doors were closed at ten.

Many common verbs have irregular past participles that you must memorize:

| Infinitive | Regular | Irregular | Meaning |
|-----------|---------|-----------|---------|
| abrir | — | **aberto** | opened |
| escrever | — | **escrito** | written |
| fazer | — | **feito** | done/made |
| dizer | — | **dito** | said |
| ver | — | **visto** | seen |
| pôr | — | **posto** | put |
| vir | — | **vindo** | come |
| cobrir | — | **coberto** | covered |
| descobrir | — | **descoberto** | discovered |

Some verbs have **both** regular and short participles. The traditional guide favors the **short** form with ser/estar (passive/state) and the **regular** with ter/haver (compound tenses), but usage depends on the verb and variety: **tenho pago** and **tinha aceito** are also used. The table shows common pairings, not absolute bans. **Aceite** is another short form of aceitar, especially in European Portuguese.

Not every participle in the first table can form a passive: intransitive **vir** has **vindo** in compound tenses, but no ordinary passive receiver.

| Infinitive | Regular (with ter) | Irregular (with ser/estar) |
|-----------|-------------------|---------------------------|
| pagar | pagado | **pago** |
| aceitar | aceitado | **aceito** |
| entregar | entregado | **entregue** |
| prender | prendido | **preso** |
| eleger | elegido | **eleito** |

- **O trabalho foi feito com cuidado.** (oo trah-BAH-lyoo foy FAY-too) — The work was done carefully.
- **A porta estava aberta.** (ah POR-tah es-TAH-vah ah-BEHR-tah) — The door was open.
- **O presidente foi eleito pelo povo.** (oo preh-zee-DEN-chee foy eh-LAY-too) — The president was elected by the people.

## Pronominal Passive with "se"

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"se-passive-plural","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Pronominal Passive with \"se\"","rule":"A standard se passive agrees with its plural subject","example":"___-se apartamentos. <!-- grammar-card: {"id":"se-impersonal-contrast","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Pronominal Passive with \"se\"","rule":"Impersonal se is not a passive with an agreeing subject","example":"Why does Precisa-se de funcionários stay singular in formal usage while Alugam-se apartamentos is plural?","answer":"Precisar de has an impersonal se construction without a passive subject; apartamentos is the plural subject of the passive alugar construction.","explanation":"Not every verb + se is passive; come-se bem is also impersonal."} -->

The passive with **se** is very common in Brazilian Portuguese, especially in signs and general statements:

- **Fala-se português aqui.** (FAH-lah see por-too-GEHZ) — Portuguese is spoken here.
- **Vende-se esta casa.** (VEN-dee see) — This house is for sale. / This house is being sold.
- **Alugam-se apartamentos.** (ah-LOO-gahn see) — Apartments for rent.
Not every construction with **se** is passive. These are **impersonal** constructions, without a passive subject:

- **Come-se bem neste restaurante.** (KOH-mee see behn) — One eats well at this restaurant.
- **Precisa-se de funcionários.** (preh-SEE-zah see) — Employees needed. Here **precisar de** requires **de**, so **funcionários** is not a passive subject.

Note: In formal grammar, the verb agrees with the subject (**Vendem-se carros** = Cars are sold), but in spoken Brazilian Portuguese, singular is often used regardless (**Vende-se carros**).

## Participle Agreement

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"passive-plural-agreement","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Participle Agreement","rule":"Passive participles agree in gender and number","example":"As pesquisas foram ___. (terminar)","answer":"terminadas","explanation":"A passive participle agrees with feminine plural pesquisas."} -->

The past participle in passive constructions **must agree** with the subject in gender and number:

- **O relatório foi terminado.** (masculine singular) — The report was finished.
- **A pesquisa foi terminada.** (feminine singular) — The research was finished.
- **Os relatórios foram terminados.** (masculine plural) — The reports were finished.
- **As pesquisas foram terminadas.** (feminine plural) — The researches were finished.
