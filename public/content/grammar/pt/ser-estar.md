# Ser vs Estar (Two Verbs for "To Be")

<!-- vocab-inventory: {"mode":"explicit"} -->

One of the biggest challenges for English speakers learning Portuguese is that there are **two verbs** for "to be": **ser** and **estar**. Each has specific uses, and mixing them up can change the meaning of your sentence entirely. A useful starting distinction is **identity/characterization** (ser) versus **state/condition** (estar), not a strict permanent-versus-temporary test: a profession can change, and an estar state can last a long time.

## Conjugation: Ser

**Ser** (sehr) is irregular:

| Pronoun | Conjugation | Pronunciation |
|---------|------------|---------------|
| Eu | **sou** | soh |
| Você / Ele / Ela | **é** | eh |
| Nós | **somos** | soh-mooz |
| A gente | **é** | eh |
| Vocês / Eles / Elas | **são** | sow |

## When to Use Ser

Use **ser** for identity, classification, origin, and characterization, as well as time and events:

### Identity and Definitions

- **Eu sou o João** (eh-oo soh oo zhoo-ow) — I am João
- **Isso é um livro** (ee-soo eh oon lee-vroo) — This is a book
- **Ela é minha irmã** (eh-lah eh mee-nyah eer-mah) — She is my sister

### Origin and Nationality

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"nationality-ser","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Origin and Nationality","rule":"Nationality uses ser; a current state uses estar","example":"Ela ___ brasileira. (She is Brazilian.)","answer":"é","explanation":"Nationality is a classification with ser, not a test of whether change is physically possible.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"Ser = permanent/inherent, Estar = temporary/changeable","example":"Ela ___ brasileira. Ela ___ cansada."}]} -->

- **Eu sou brasileiro** (eh-oo soh brah-zee-lay-roo) — I am Brazilian
- **Ela é de São Paulo** (eh-lah eh jee sow pow-loo) — She is from São Paulo
- **Nós somos americanos** (nohz soh-mooz ah-meh-ree-kah-nooz) — We are American

### Profession

- **Ele é professor** (eh-lee eh proh-feh-sohr) — He is a teacher
- **Eu sou médica** (eh-oo soh meh-jee-kah) — I am a doctor
- **Eles são engenheiros** (eh-leez sow ehn-zheh-nyay-rooz) — They are engineers

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"profession-ser","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Profession","rule":"Ser classifies someone by profession","example":"Ele ___ professor. (He is a teacher.)","answer":"é","explanation":"A profession uses ser even though a person's job can change."} -->

### Inherent Characteristics

- **O café é quente** (oo kah-feh eh kehn-chee) — Coffee is hot (by nature)
- **Ela é inteligente** (eh-lah eh een-teh-lee-zhen-chee) — She is intelligent
- **O Brasil é grande** (oo brah-zeew eh grahn-jee) — Brazil is big
- **A neve é fria** (ah neh-vee eh free-ah) — Snow is cold (inherently)

### Time, Dates, and Events

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"clock-time-ser","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Time, Dates, and Events","rule":"Time and dates always use 'ser'","example":"___ três horas da tarde.","answer":"São","explanation":"Clock time uses ser: plural são três horas, singular é uma hora."} -->

- **Hoje é segunda-feira** (oh-zhee eh seh-goon-dah fay-rah) — Today is Monday
- **São três horas** (sow trehz oh-rahz) — It's three o'clock
- **A festa é no sábado** (ah fehs-tah eh noo sah-bah-doo) — The party is on Saturday

## When to Use Estar

Use **estar** for temporary states, conditions, locations, and ongoing actions:

### Temporary States and Feelings

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"current-state-estar","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Temporary States and Feelings","rule":"A current feeling or condition uses estar","example":"Ela ___ cansada. (She is tired right now.)","answer":"está","explanation":"Estar describes her present condition, with third-person singular está."} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-cansado","word":"cansado","meaning":"tired","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Temporary States and Feelings"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-doente","word":"doente","meaning":"sick; ill","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Temporary States and Feelings"} -->

- **Eu estou cansado** (eh-oo esh-toh kahn-sah-doo) — I am tired (right now)
- **Ela está feliz** (eh-lah esh-tah feh-leez) — She is happy (right now)
- **Nós estamos com fome** (nohz esh-tah-mooz kohn foh-mee) — We are hungry
- **Você está doente?** (voh-say esh-tah doh-ehn-chee) — Are you sick?

### Location

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"physical-location-estar","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Location","rule":"Physical location of a person or object normally uses estar","example":"O livro ___ na mesa. (estar; present)","answer":"está","explanation":"Use estar for the book's location; event venues use ser, and geographical ser is also possible.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"Location always uses estar, even for permanent places","example":"O Rio de Janeiro ___ no Brasil. O Rio de Janeiro ___ uma cidade linda."}]} -->

- **Eu estou em casa** (eh-oo esh-toh ayn kah-zah) — I am at home
- **O livro está na mesa** (oo lee-vroo esh-tah nah meh-zah) — The book is on the table
- **Onde você está?** (ohn-jee voh-say esh-tah) — Where are you?
- **Eles estão no Brasil** (eh-leez esh-tow noo brah-zeew) — They are in Brazil

### Weather and Temperature (current)

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"weather-state","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Weather and Temperature (current)","rule":"Estar presents current weather conditions","example":"___ quente hoje. (It is hot today.)","answer":"Está","explanation":"The sentence describes today's condition rather than a general characterization."} -->

- **Está quente hoje** (esh-tah kehn-chee oh-zhee) — It's hot today
- **Está frio** (esh-tah free-oo) — It's cold (right now)
- **Está chovendo** (esh-tah shoh-vehn-doo) — It's raining

### Progressive Actions (estar + gerund)

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"progressive-estar","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Progressive Actions (estar + gerund)","rule":"Estar plus a gerund expresses ongoing action in the Brazilian construction","example":"Eu ___ estudando. - **Eu estou estudando** (eh-oo esh-toh esh-too-dahn-doo) — I am studying
- **Ela está trabalhando** (eh-lah esh-tah trah-bah-lyahn-doo) — She is working
- **Nós estamos aprendendo** (nohz esh-tah-mooz ah-prehn-dehn-doo) — We are learning

## The Famous Ser vs Estar Difference

Some adjectives change meaning depending on which verb you use:

| With Ser (inherent) | With Estar (temporary) |
|---------------------|----------------------|
| **Ele é bonito** — He is handsome (always) | **Ele está bonito** — He looks handsome (today) |
| **Ela é magra** — She is thin (naturally) | **Ela está magra** — She has gotten thin |
| **O café é quente** — Coffee is hot (by nature) | **O café está quente** — The coffee is hot (right now) |
| **Ele é chato** — He is boring (personality) | **Ele está chato** — He is being annoying (right now) |
| **Ela é nervosa** — She is a nervous person | **Ela está nervosa** — She is nervous (at this moment) |
| **A fruta é verde** — The fruit is green (color) | **A fruta está verde** — The fruit is unripe |

- **Ele é vivo** (eh-lee eh vee-voo) — He is lively/clever (personality)
- **Ele está vivo** (eh-lee esh-tah vee-voo) — He is alive (state)

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### ❌ Wrong: Eu sou em casa
### ✅ Right: Eu estou em casa — I am at home

Physical location of a person or object normally uses **estar**, even for a long-lasting location. Event venues normally use **ser**; geographical location can also use **ser**, as in **O Rio é no Brasil**.

### ❌ Wrong: Ela está brasileira
### ✅ Right: Ela é brasileira — She is Brazilian

Nationality is always **ser** (it's an inherent characteristic).

### ❌ Wrong: São três horas? Não, estão quatro.
### ✅ Right: São três horas? Não, são quatro. — Is it three o'clock? No, it's four.

Time is always **ser**.

## Quick Decision Guide

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"ser-estar-heuristic","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Quick Decision Guide","rule":"Identity versus state is a guide, not a permanence test","example":"Why is can it change an unreliable test for choosing ser or estar?","answer":"Ser can describe changeable classifications such as professions, and estar states can last a long time.","explanation":"Use the construction and intended meaning: identity/characterization versus state/condition."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"essence-state-mnemonic","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Quick Decision Guide","rule":"Essence versus state is a limited memory aid","example":"What does the cue ser = essence, estar = state help you remember, and what is its limit?","answer":"Ser often identifies or characterizes; estar often presents a state or condition. It is not a permanent-versus-temporary law.","explanation":"A profession may change but still uses ser; choose by meaning and construction."} -->

Memory cue: **ser = essence, estar = state**. Here “essence” means identification or characterization, not an unchangeable property; “state” means how something is presented in context, not necessarily a short duration.

Ask yourself:

1. **Is it a state or condition?** → Usually **estar** (feelings, health, physical location, current weather)
2. **Is it who/what something IS?** → Use **ser** (identity, profession, origin, inherent traits)
3. **Is it ongoing right now?** → Use **estar** + gerund (estar fazendo)
4. **Is it time or a date?** → Use **ser** (são duas horas, hoje é terça)
