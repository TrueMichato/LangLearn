# Possessive Pronouns & Adjectives

Possessives in Portuguese tell us who owns or is associated with something. Unlike English, Portuguese possessives agree in **gender and number** with the thing being possessed — not with the owner. This is a crucial difference that takes some getting used to!

## Possessive Forms

Here are all the possessive adjectives/pronouns:

| Owner | Masc. Sing. | Fem. Sing. | Masc. Plur. | Fem. Plur. |
|-------|------------|-----------|------------|-----------|
| Eu | **meu** | **minha** | **meus** | **minhas** |
| Você | **seu** | **sua** | **seus** | **suas** |
| Ele | **dele** | **dele** | **dele** | **dele** |
| Ela | **dela** | **dela** | **dela** | **dela** |
| Nós | **nosso** | **nossa** | **nossos** | **nossas** |
| Vocês | **de vocês** | **de vocês** | **de vocês** | **de vocês** |
| Eles | **deles** | **deles** | **deles** | **deles** |
| Elas | **delas** | **delas** | **delas** | **delas** |

> **Key insight:** **Meu/minha/seu/sua/nosso/nossa** change to match what's owned. **Dele/dela/deles/delas** never change — they match the owner.

## Meu / Minha — My

**Meu** (masculine) and **minha** (feminine) agree with the thing owned:

- **O meu carro** (oo meh-oo kah-hoo) — My car (carro = masculine)
- **A minha casa** (ah mee-nyah kah-zah) — My house (casa = feminine)
- **Os meus livros** (ooz meh-ooz lee-vrooz) — My books (livros = masculine plural)
- **As minhas chaves** (ahz mee-nyahz shah-veez) — My keys (chaves = feminine plural)

Notice: whether the speaker is male or female doesn't matter — the possessive matches the **noun**:

- A man says: **A minha mãe** — My mother (minha because mãe is feminine)
- A woman says: **O meu pai** — My father (meu because pai is masculine)

## Seu / Sua — Your (Ambiguous!)

**Seu/sua** technically means "your," but in Brazilian Portuguese it can be ambiguous because it could also mean "his" or "her":

- **O seu livro** (oo seh-oo lee-vroo) — Your book / His book / Her book
- **A sua casa** (ah soo-ah kah-zah) — Your house / His house / Her house

Because of this ambiguity, Brazilians have developed a workaround...

## Dele / Dela — His / Hers (The Brazilian Solution)

To avoid confusion, Brazilian Portuguese uses **dele** (of him) and **dela** (of her) after the noun:

- **O livro dele** (oo lee-vroo deh-lee) — His book
- **O livro dela** (oo lee-vroo deh-lah) — Her book
- **A casa dele** (ah kah-zah deh-lee) — His house
- **A casa dela** (ah kah-zah deh-lah) — Her house

This way, **seu/sua** is used mainly for "your":

- **O seu livro** — Your book (clear meaning)
- **O livro dele** — His book (clear meaning)
- **O livro dela** — Her book (clear meaning)

## Nosso / Nossa — Our

**Nosso** (masculine) and **nossa** (feminine) work just like meu/minha:

- **O nosso apartamento** (oo noh-soo ah-pahr-tah-mehn-too) — Our apartment
- **A nossa escola** (ah noh-sah esh-koh-lah) — Our school
- **Os nossos amigos** (ooz noh-sooz ah-mee-gooz) — Our friends
- **As nossas férias** (ahz noh-sahz feh-ree-ahz) — Our vacation

**Nossa!** is also a common exclamation:

- **Nossa, que lindo!** (noh-sah kee leen-doo) — Wow, how beautiful!
- **Nossa, que difícil!** (noh-sah kee jee-fee-see-oo) — Wow, how difficult!

## De Vocês / Deles / Delas — Your (pl.) / Their

For plural owners, use **de + pronoun** after the noun:

- **O carro de vocês** (oo kah-hoo jee voh-sehz) — Your (all) car
- **A casa deles** (ah kah-zah deh-leez) — Their house (masc.)
- **A casa delas** (ah kah-zah deh-lahz) — Their house (fem.)
- **Os filhos deles** (ooz fee-lyooz deh-leez) — Their children

## With or Without Articles?

In Brazilian Portuguese, possessives can appear with or without articles. Both are correct, but there are tendencies:

### With articles (more common in Brazil)

- **O meu nome é João** — My name is João
- **A minha família é grande** — My family is big

### Without articles (also correct)

- **Meu nome é João** — My name is João
- **Minha família é grande** — My family is big

> **Tip:** Using articles before possessives sounds more natural in Brazilian Portuguese. When in doubt, include them!

## Common Expressions with Possessives

- **Meu Deus!** (meh-oo deh-ooz) — My God!
- **Meu bem** (meh-oo bayn) — My dear / sweetheart
- **Meu amor** (meh-oo ah-mohr) — My love
- **Nossa Senhora!** (noh-sah seh-nyoh-rah) — Our Lady! (exclamation of surprise)
- **É minha culpa** (eh mee-nyah koo-pah) — It's my fault
- **Na minha opinião** (nah mee-nyah oh-pee-nee-ow) — In my opinion

## Possessives as Pronouns (Without a Noun)

When the noun is already understood, the possessive can stand alone:

- **Esse livro é meu** (eh-see lee-vroo eh meh-oo) — This book is mine
- **Essa casa é sua?** (eh-sah kah-zah eh soo-ah) — Is this house yours?
- **O meu carro é preto. E o seu?** (oo meh-oo kah-hoo eh preh-too. ee oo seh-oo) — My car is black. And yours?
- **Essa bolsa é minha, não é dela** (eh-sah bow-sah eh mee-nyah, now eh deh-lah) — This bag is mine, not hers

## Complete Examples in Context

**Talking about family:**

- **A minha mãe é brasileira** (ah mee-nyah mayn eh brah-zee-lay-rah) — My mother is Brazilian
- **O nosso filho estuda medicina** (oo noh-soo fee-lyoo esh-too-dah meh-jee-see-nah) — Our son studies medicine
- **Os pais dela moram no interior** (ooz pai-eez deh-lah moh-rawn noo een-teh-ree-ohr) — Her parents live in the countryside

**At work:**

- **O meu chefe é muito legal** (oo meh-oo sheh-fee eh mween-too leh-gow) — My boss is very cool
- **As minhas reuniões são pela manhã** (ahz mee-nyahz heh-oo-nee-oyns sow peh-lah mah-nyah) — My meetings are in the morning
- **O escritório dele é grande** (oo esh-kree-toh-ree-oo deh-lee eh grahn-jee) — His office is big
