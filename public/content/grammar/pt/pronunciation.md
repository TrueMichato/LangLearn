# Pronunciation & Accent Marks

Brazilian Portuguese has a rich sound system that differs significantly from European Portuguese. Understanding pronunciation early will make every other lesson easier. Don't worry about perfection — Brazilians are famously warm and encouraging toward learners!

## Vowels

Portuguese has both oral and nasal vowels. The oral vowels are similar to Spanish or Italian, while nasal vowels are a distinctive feature of the language.

### Oral Vowels

- **a** — open like "ah" in "father": **casa** (kah-zah) — house
- **e** — can be open like "eh" in "bet" or closed like "ay" in "day": **café** (kah-feh) — coffee
- **i** — like "ee" in "see": **dia** (jee-ah) — day
- **o** — can be open like "aw" in "law" or closed like "oh" in "go": **bolo** (boh-loo) — cake
- **u** — like "oo" in "moon": **tudo** (too-doo) — everything

In Brazilian Portuguese, unstressed **e** at the end of a word often sounds like "ee":

- **grande** (grahn-jee) — big
- **leite** (lay-chee) — milk

Similarly, unstressed **o** at the end of a word often sounds like "oo":

- **livro** (lee-vroo) — book
- **gato** (gah-too) — cat

### Nasal Vowels

Nasal vowels are pronounced by letting air flow through both the mouth and nose. They are marked by **~** (til) or by **m/n** after the vowel:

- **ã** — nasal "ah": **maçã** (mah-sah) — apple
- **ão** — nasal "ow": **não** (now) — no
- **ões** — nasal "oyns": **corações** (koh-rah-soyns) — hearts
- **em/en** — nasal "ay": **bem** (bayn) — well
- **im/in** — nasal "ee": **sim** (seen) — yes
- **om/on** — nasal "oh": **bom** (bohn) — good
- **um/un** — nasal "oo": **um** (oon) — one

## Accent Marks

Portuguese uses several accent marks, each affecting pronunciation or stress:

### Acute Accent (´)

Opens the vowel and indicates stress:

- **á** — open "ah": **água** (ah-gwah) — water
- **é** — open "eh": **café** (kah-feh) — coffee
- **ó** — open "aw": **avó** (ah-vaw) — grandmother

### Circumflex (^)

Closes the vowel and indicates stress:

- **ê** — closed "ay": **você** (voh-say) — you
- **ô** — closed "oh": **avô** (ah-voh) — grandfather

### Til (~)

Makes the vowel nasal:

- **ã** — nasal "ah": **irmã** (eer-mah) — sister
- **õ** — nasal "oh": **põe** (poyng) — puts

### Grave Accent (`)

Only used on **à** to indicate a contraction (a + a = à). Does NOT change pronunciation:

- **Vou à escola** (voh ah esh-koh-lah) — I go to the school

### Cedilla (ç)

Makes **c** sound like "s" before **a**, **o**, **u**:

- **coração** (koh-rah-sow) — heart
- **França** (frahn-sah) — France
- **açúcar** (ah-soo-kar) — sugar

## Consonants with Special Rules

### The Letter H

**H** is always silent at the beginning of words:

- **hoje** (oh-zhee) — today
- **hora** (oh-rah) — hour
- **homem** (oh-mayn) — man

### The Letters R and RR

R has two sounds in Brazilian Portuguese:

- **Initial R / RR** — sounds like English "h": **rio** (hee-oo) — river, **carro** (kah-hoo) — car
- **R between vowels** — soft tap like Spanish R: **caro** (kah-roo) — expensive
- **R at end of word** — varies by region, often like English "h" or dropped: **falar** (fah-lah) — to speak

### The Letter S

- **S at start or after consonant** — like English "s": **sol** (sow) — sun
- **S between vowels** — like English "z": **casa** (kah-zah) — house
- **SS** — always like "s": **pássaro** (pah-sah-roo) — bird

### The Letters D and T

Before **i** or final unstressed **e** (which sounds like "ee"), these change in most Brazilian dialects:

- **D** → sounds like "j" (as in "jeans"): **dia** (jee-ah) — day, **cidade** (see-dah-jee) — city
- **T** → sounds like "ch" (as in "cheese"): **tia** (chee-ah) — aunt, **gente** (zhen-chee) — people

### The Letter X

X is tricky — it has four possible sounds:

- Like "sh": **xícara** (shee-kah-rah) — cup
- Like "s": **próximo** (proh-see-moo) — next
- Like "z": **exame** (eh-zah-mee) — exam
- Like "ks": **táxi** (tahk-see) — taxi

### LH and NH

These are digraphs (two letters making one sound):

- **lh** — like "lli" in "million": **filho** (fee-lyoo) — son
- **nh** — like "ny" in "canyon": **vinho** (vee-nyoo) — wine

## Diphthongs

Diphthongs are two vowels pronounced together as one syllable. Portuguese has many:

### Common Diphthongs

| Diphthong | Sound | Example |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| **ai** | like "eye" | **pai** (pah-ee) — father |
| **ei** | like "ay" in "day" | **leite** (lay-chee) — milk |
| **oi** | like "oy" in "toy" | **noite** (noy-chee) — night |
| **au** | like "ow" in "cow" | **mau** (mow) — bad |
| **eu** | "ay-oo" | **meu** (may-oo) — my |
| **ou** | like "oh" | **outro** (oh-troo) — other |
| **ão** | nasal "ow" | **não** (now̃) — no |
| **ãe** | nasal "ay" | **mãe** (mah-eẽ̯) — mother |
| **õe** | nasal "oy" | **põe** (poỹ̯) — puts |

### Vowel Combinations (Hiatus)

These are NOT diphthongs — each vowel is a separate syllable:

- **ia** — "ee-ah": **dia** (jee-ah) — day
- **io** — "ee-oh": **rio** (hee-oo) — river
- **ua** — "oo-ah": **água** (ah-gwah) — water
- **ue** — "oo-eh": **frequente** (freh-kwen-chee) — frequent

### Digraphs (Two Letters, One Sound)

| Digraph | Sound | Example |
|---------|-------|---------|
| **ch** | "sh" as in "shoe" | **chave** (shah-vee) — key |
| **lh** | "ly" as in "million" | **trabalho** (trah-bah-lyoo) — work |
| **nh** | "ny" as in "canyon" | **amanhã** (ah-mah-nyah) — tomorrow |
| **rr** | "h" as in "hat" | **carro** (kah-hoo) — car |
| **ss** | "s" as in "miss" | **pássaro** (pah-sah-roo) — bird |
| **qu** | "k" before e/i | **que** (kee) — what |
| **gu** | "g" before e/i | **guerra** (geh-hah) — war |

## Stress Rules

Portuguese has predictable stress patterns:

1. **Words ending in -a, -e, -o, -am, -em** → stress the **second-to-last** syllable:
   - **casa** (KAH-zah) — house
   - **grande** (GRAHN-jee) — big

2. **Words ending in -i, -u, -r, -l, -z, -im, -um** → stress the **last** syllable:
   - **falar** (fah-LAH) — to speak
   - **azul** (ah-ZOO) — blue

3. **Any accent mark overrides these rules** — the accented syllable gets the stress:
   - **café** (kah-FEH) — coffee (accent on last syllable)
   - **música** (MOO-zee-kah) — music (accent on third-to-last)

## Common Pronunciation Mistakes

Here are mistakes English speakers often make:

- **Don't pronounce H** — "hotel" is (oh-TEW), not "ho-tel"
- **Don't roll every R** — Initial R and RR sound like English "h"
- **Watch out for nasal vowels** — "pão" (bread) is not "pow" but a nasal "pow" (let air through your nose)
- **Final E and O change** — "leite" ends with an "ee" sound, not "eh"
