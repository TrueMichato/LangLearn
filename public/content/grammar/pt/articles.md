# Articles (o/a/os/as, um/uma)

Articles are small words that come before nouns. Portuguese uses them much more frequently than English — you'll even use articles before people's names! Mastering articles early is essential because they reveal the gender and number of every noun.

## Definite Articles — "The"

Portuguese has **four** definite articles, depending on the gender and number of the noun:

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| **Masculine** | o | os |
| **Feminine** | a | as |

- **O livro** (oo lee-vroo) — The book
- **A casa** (ah kah-zah) — The house
- **Os livros** (ooz lee-vrooz) — The books
- **As casas** (ahz kah-zahz) — The houses

## Indefinite Articles — "A / An / Some"

Portuguese also has **four** indefinite articles:

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| **Masculine** | um | uns |
| **Feminine** | uma | umas |

- **Um livro** (oon lee-vroo) — A book
- **Uma casa** (oo-mah kah-zah) — A house
- **Uns livros** (oonz lee-vrooz) — Some books
- **Umas casas** (oo-mahz kah-zahz) — Some houses

## When to Use Definite Articles

Brazilian Portuguese uses definite articles in many situations where English does not:

### Before Names

In casual Brazilian Portuguese, you often use articles before people's names:

- **A Maria é minha amiga** (ah mah-ree-ah eh mee-nyah ah-mee-gah) — Maria is my friend
- **O João trabalha aqui** (oo zhoo-ow trah-bah-lyah ah-kee) — João works here

### Before Possessives

Articles often appear before possessive pronouns:

- **O meu carro é vermelho** (oo meh-oo kah-hoo eh vehr-meh-lyoo) — My car is red
- **A sua mãe é simpática** (ah soo-ah mayn eh seen-pah-chee-kah) — Your mother is nice

### With Countries and Continents

Most country names take an article:

- **O Brasil é grande** (oo brah-zee-oo eh grahn-jee) — Brazil is big
- **A França é bonita** (ah frahn-sah eh boo-nee-tah) — France is beautiful
- **Os Estados Unidos** (ooz esh-tah-dooz oo-nee-dooz) — The United States

Exceptions (no article): **Portugal**, **Cuba**, **Israel**, **Moçambique**

### With Languages

- **O português é bonito** (oo por-too-gesh eh boo-nee-too) — Portuguese is beautiful
- **Eu estudo o inglês** (eh-oo esh-too-doo oo een-glesh) — I study English

## When to Omit Articles

Despite being used more than in English, there are cases where articles are omitted:

### After "ser" with Professions (no article)

- **Ela é professora** (eh-lah eh proh-feh-soh-rah) — She is a teacher
- **Ele é médico** (eh-lee eh meh-jee-koo) — He is a doctor

### In General Statements with Indefinite Meaning

- **Eu gosto de café** (eh-oo gosh-too jee kah-feh) — I like coffee (in general)
- **Ela come carne** (eh-lah koh-mee kahr-nee) — She eats meat (in general)

### With Some Prepositions

After certain prepositions, articles are often dropped:

- **Eu moro em São Paulo** (eh-oo moh-roo ayn sow pow-loo) — I live in São Paulo
- **Ela é de Portugal** (eh-lah eh jee por-too-gaw) — She is from Portugal

## Articles with Prepositions — Contractions

Articles combine with common prepositions to form contractions. These are mandatory — you cannot say "de o" or "em a":

### de + article

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| **Masculine** | de + o = **do** | de + os = **dos** |
| **Feminine** | de + a = **da** | de + as = **das** |

- **O livro do professor** (oo lee-vroo doo proh-feh-sohr) — The teacher's book
- **A cor da casa** (ah kohr dah kah-zah) — The color of the house

### em + article

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| **Masculine** | em + o = **no** | em + os = **nos** |
| **Feminine** | em + a = **na** | em + as = **nas** |

- **O gato está no jardim** (oo gah-too esh-tah noo zhahr-jeen) — The cat is in the garden
- **As chaves estão na mesa** (ahz shah-veez esh-tow nah meh-zah) — The keys are on the table

### a + article

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| **Masculine** | a + o = **ao** | a + os = **aos** |
| **Feminine** | a + a = **à** | a + as = **às** |

- **Vou ao mercado** (voh ow mehr-kah-doo) — I go to the market
- **Ela vai à escola** (eh-lah vai ah esh-koh-lah) — She goes to the school

## Comparing with English

| English | Portuguese | Notes |
|---------|-----------|-------|
| The book | **O livro** | Must match gender |
| A house | **Uma casa** | Must match gender |
| Ø Coffee (general) | **Ø Café** or **O café** | Both possible |
| Ø Maria | **A Maria** | Articles with names in Brazil |
| She is a teacher | **Ela é Ø professora** | No article with professions after ser |
