# Subject Pronouns & Verb Agreement

Understanding subject pronouns is crucial because Portuguese verbs change form based on who is performing the action. Brazilian Portuguese has its own unique pronoun system that differs from European Portuguese — most notably the widespread use of **você** and **a gente**.

## The Brazilian Portuguese Pronoun System

| Person | Pronoun | Pronunciation | English |
|--------|---------|--------------|---------|
| 1st singular | **eu** | eh-oo | I |
| 2nd singular | **você** | voh-say | you |
| 3rd singular masc. | **ele** | eh-lee | he |
| 3rd singular fem. | **ela** | eh-lah | she |
| 1st plural (formal) | **nós** | nohz | we |
| 1st plural (informal) | **a gente** | ah zhen-chee | we |
| 2nd plural | **vocês** | voh-sehz | you (all) |
| 3rd plural masc. | **eles** | eh-leez | they (masc.) |
| 3rd plural fem. | **elas** | eh-lahz | they (fem.) |

## Você — The Brazilian "You"

In Brazilian Portuguese, **você** is the standard way to say "you" in most situations. It's neither too formal nor too informal:

- **Você fala português?** (voh-say fah-lah por-too-gesh) — Do you speak Portuguese?
- **Você é de onde?** (voh-say eh jee ohn-jee) — Where are you from?
- **Você quer café?** (voh-say kehr kah-feh) — Do you want coffee?

> **Important:** Although **você** means "you" (2nd person), it conjugates as **3rd person** (like ele/ela). This is because **você** historically comes from **Vossa Mercê** (Your Grace).

### Tu — Regional Variation

Some regions of Brazil (especially the south and northeast) use **tu** instead of **você**. However, in casual speech, many Brazilians use **tu** with **você** conjugation:

- **Tu fala português?** (too fah-lah por-too-gesh) — Do you speak Portuguese? (informal, common in some regions)

For learning purposes, stick with **você** — it's understood everywhere in Brazil.

## A Gente — The Informal "We"

**A gente** literally means "the people" but is used as an informal alternative to **nós** (we). It's extremely common in spoken Brazilian Portuguese:

- **A gente vai ao cinema** (ah zhen-chee vai ow see-neh-mah) — We're going to the movies
- **A gente fala português** (ah zhen-chee fah-lah por-too-gesh) — We speak Portuguese
- **A gente mora aqui** (ah zhen-chee moh-rah ah-kee) — We live here

> **Key rule:** **A gente** conjugates as 3rd person singular (like você/ele/ela), NOT as nós!

| | Nós | A gente |
|--|-----|---------|
| falar | falamos | fala |
| comer | comemos | come |
| ir | vamos | vai |
| ser | somos | é |
| estar | estamos | está |

- **Nós falamos português** = **A gente fala português** (same meaning, different formality)
- **Nós vamos à festa** = **A gente vai à festa** (same meaning)

## Verb Agreement Patterns

### Group 1: Eu (always unique)

The **eu** form always has its own ending (-o for present tense):

- **Eu falo** — I speak
- **Eu como** — I eat
- **Eu sou** — I am
- **Eu tenho** — I have

### Group 2: Você / Ele / Ela / A gente (all conjugate the same)

These four pronouns all share the same verb form:

- **Você fala / Ele fala / Ela fala / A gente fala** — You speak / He speaks / She speaks / We speak
- **Você come / Ele come / Ela come / A gente come** — You eat / He eats / She eats / We eat

### Group 3: Nós (unique)

**Nós** has its own ending (-amos, -emos, -imos):

- **Nós falamos** — We speak
- **Nós comemos** — We eat
- **Nós partimos** — We leave

### Group 4: Vocês / Eles / Elas (all conjugate the same)

These pronouns share the same plural verb form:

- **Vocês falam / Eles falam / Elas falam** — You all speak / They speak
- **Vocês comem / Eles comem / Elas comem** — You all eat / They eat

## Dropping Pronouns

Because verb conjugations are distinctive, you can often **drop the pronoun** and let the verb speak for itself:

- **Falo português** (fah-loo por-too-gesh) — I speak Portuguese (only eu → -o)
- **Falamos português** (fah-lah-mooz por-too-gesh) — We speak Portuguese (only nós → -amos)

However, since **você/ele/ela/a gente** all share the same form, you usually keep the pronoun for clarity:

- **Ela fala português** (not just "fala português," which could mean you/he/she/we)

## Formal Address

For formal situations (e.g., addressing elderly people, business settings), Brazilians use:

- **O senhor** (oo seh-nyohr) — sir / you (formal, to men)
- **A senhora** (ah seh-nyoh-rah) — ma'am / you (formal, to women)

These conjugate like **você** (3rd person):

- **O senhor quer café?** (oo seh-nyohr kehr kah-feh) — Would you like coffee, sir?
- **A senhora mora aqui?** (ah seh-nyoh-rah moh-rah ah-kee) — Do you live here, ma'am?

## Mixed Gender Groups

When referring to a mixed-gender group, use the masculine plural:

- **Eles** (eh-leez) — They (mixed group or all male)
- **Elas** (eh-lahz) — They (all female)

- **Maria e João? Eles são amigos** (eh-leez sow ah-mee-gooz) — Maria and João? They are friends
- **Maria e Ana? Elas são amigas** (eh-lahz sow ah-mee-gahz) — Maria and Ana? They are friends

## Putting It All Together

Here's a complete example showing all pronouns with the verb **morar** (to live):

- **Eu moro em Brasília** (eh-oo moh-roo ayn brah-zee-lee-ah) — I live in Brasília
- **Você mora em São Paulo** (voh-say moh-rah ayn sow pow-loo) — You live in São Paulo
- **Ele mora no Rio** (eh-lee moh-rah noo hee-oo) — He lives in Rio
- **Ela mora em Salvador** (eh-lah moh-rah ayn sah-vah-dohr) — She lives in Salvador
- **Nós moramos em Curitiba** (nohz moh-rah-mooz ayn koo-ree-chee-bah) — We live in Curitiba
- **A gente mora em Recife** (ah zhen-chee moh-rah ayn heh-see-fee) — We live in Recife
- **Vocês moram em Manaus** (voh-sehz moh-rown ayn mah-nowz) — You all live in Manaus
- **Eles moram em Porto Alegre** (eh-leez moh-rown ayn pohr-too ah-leh-gree) — They live in Porto Alegre
