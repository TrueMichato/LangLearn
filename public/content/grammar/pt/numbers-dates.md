# Numbers, Dates, and Time (Números, Datas e Horas)

Numbers, dates, and time expressions are essential for daily communication. Portuguese has some unique features in this area — including days of the week that are unlike any other Romance language. This lesson covers everything you need to handle numbers, dates, and time in Brazilian Portuguese.

## Cardinal Numbers (Números Cardinais)

### 0–20

| Number | Portuguese | Pronunciation |
|--------|-----------|---------------|
| 0 | zero | zeh-roo |
| 1 | um / uma | oong / oo-mah |
| 2 | dois / duas | doysh / doo-ahz |
| 3 | três | trehz |
| 4 | quatro | kwah-troo |
| 5 | cinco | seen-koo |
| 6 | seis | says |
| 7 | sete | seh-chee |
| 8 | oito | oy-too |
| 9 | nove | noh-vee |
| 10 | dez | dehz |
| 11 | onze | on-zee |
| 12 | doze | doh-zee |
| 13 | treze | treh-zee |
| 14 | quatorze | kwah-tor-zee |
| 15 | quinze | keen-zee |
| 16 | dezesseis | deh-zeh-says |
| 17 | dezessete | deh-zeh-seh-chee |
| 18 | dezoito | deh-zoy-too |
| 19 | dezenove | deh-zeh-noh-vee |
| 20 | vinte | veen-chee |

> **Note:** The numbers 1 and 2 have masculine and feminine forms: **um/uma** and **dois/duas**. They agree with the noun they modify.

- **Um gato e uma gata** (oong gah-too ee oo-mah gah-tah) — One male cat and one female cat
- **Dois livros e duas canetas** (doysh lee-vrooz ee doo-ahz kah-neh-tahz) — Two books and two pens

### 21–100

Numbers 21–29 use **vinte e + digit**. This pattern repeats for 30s, 40s, etc.

| Number | Portuguese |
|--------|-----------|
| 21 | vinte e um |
| 22 | vinte e dois |
| 30 | trinta |
| 31 | trinta e um |
| 40 | quarenta |
| 50 | cinquenta |
| 60 | sessenta |
| 70 | setenta |
| 80 | oitenta |
| 90 | noventa |
| 100 | cem / cento |

> **Cem vs. Cento:** Use **cem** for exactly 100, and **cento** when combined with other numbers (cento e um, cento e vinte).

- **Eu tenho trinta e cinco anos** (eh-oo ten-yoo treen-tah ee seen-koo ah-nooz) — I'm thirty-five years old
- **A conta é noventa e nove reais** (ah kon-tah eh noh-ven-tah ee noh-vee heh-ahyz) — The bill is ninety-nine reais

### 100–1000+

| Number | Portuguese |
|--------|-----------|
| 100 | cem |
| 101 | cento e um |
| 200 | duzentos/duzentas |
| 300 | trezentos/trezentas |
| 400 | quatrocentos |
| 500 | quinhentos |
| 600 | seiscentos |
| 700 | setecentos |
| 800 | oitocentos |
| 900 | novecentos |
| 1.000 | mil |
| 2.000 | dois mil |
| 1.000.000 | um milhão |

> **Note:** In Brazil, periods separate thousands and commas separate decimals: **1.500,50** (one thousand five hundred and fifty cents).

- **Duzentos e cinquenta gramas** (doo-zen-tooz ee seen-kwen-tah grah-mahz) — Two hundred and fifty grams
- **Mil novecentos e noventa e nove** (meel noh-veh-sen-tooz ee noh-ven-tah ee noh-vee) — One thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine (1999)

## Ordinal Numbers (Números Ordinais)

Ordinal numbers agree in gender with the noun they describe:

| Ordinal | Masculine | Feminine |
|---------|-----------|----------|
| 1st | primeiro | primeira |
| 2nd | segundo | segunda |
| 3rd | terceiro | terceira |
| 4th | quarto | quarta |
| 5th | quinto | quinta |
| 6th | sexto | sexta |
| 7th | sétimo | sétima |
| 8th | oitavo | oitava |
| 9th | nono | nona |
| 10th | décimo | décima |

- **É a primeira vez que eu venho aqui** (eh ah pree-may-rah vez kee eh-oo ven-yoo ah-kee) — It's the first time I've come here
- **Ele mora no terceiro andar** (eh-lee moh-rah noo ter-say-roo an-dar) — He lives on the third floor
- **Ela ficou em segundo lugar** (eh-lah fee-koh eng seh-goon-doo loo-gar) — She got second place

> **In practice:** Brazilians often use cardinal numbers instead of ordinals for large numbers: "andar quinze" (floor fifteen) instead of "décimo quinto andar."

## Days of the Week (Dias da Semana)

Portuguese is unique among Romance languages — its weekdays are numbered rather than named after gods or planets:

| Day | Portuguese | Abbreviation | Origin |
|-----|-----------|-------------|--------|
| Monday | segunda-feira | seg. | "second fair/market day" |
| Tuesday | terça-feira | ter. | "third fair" |
| Wednesday | quarta-feira | qua. | "fourth fair" |
| Thursday | quinta-feira | qui. | "fifth fair" |
| Friday | sexta-feira | sex. | "sixth fair" |
| Saturday | sábado | sáb. | from Sabbath |
| Sunday | domingo | dom. | from Lord's Day |

> **In conversation**, Brazilians often drop "-feira": "Vejo você na segunda" (See you on Monday).

- **Hoje é segunda-feira** (oh-zhee eh seh-goon-dah fay-rah) — Today is Monday
- **Eu trabalho de segunda a sexta** (eh-oo trah-bah-lyoo jee seh-goon-dah ah sess-tah) — I work Monday to Friday
- **No sábado, eu descanso** (noo sah-bah-doo eh-oo desh-kan-soo) — On Saturday, I rest
- **Aos domingos, nós almoçamos juntos** (owz doh-meen-gooz nohs ow-moh-sah-mooz zhoon-tooz) — On Sundays, we have lunch together

## Months (Meses)

| Month | Portuguese | Pronunciation |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| January | janeiro | zhah-nay-roo |
| February | fevereiro | feh-veh-ray-roo |
| March | março | mar-soo |
| April | abril | ah-breel |
| May | maio | mah-ee-oo |
| June | junho | zhoon-yoo |
| July | julho | zhool-yoo |
| August | agosto | ah-gosh-too |
| September | setembro | seh-tem-broo |
| October | outubro | ow-too-broo |
| November | novembro | noh-vem-broo |
| December | dezembro | deh-zem-broo |

> **Note:** Months are not capitalized in Portuguese (unless at the beginning of a sentence).

- **Meu aniversário é em março** (meh-oo ah-nee-ver-sah-ree-oo eh eng mar-soo) — My birthday is in March
- **O Natal é em dezembro** (oo nah-tow eh eng deh-zem-broo) — Christmas is in December

## Telling Dates

Dates in Portuguese use cardinal numbers (not ordinal), except for the first day of the month:

- **Primeiro de janeiro** (pree-may-roo jee zhah-nay-roo) — January 1st
- **Dois de fevereiro** (doysh jee feh-veh-ray-roo) — February 2nd
- **Quinze de março** (keen-zee jee mar-soo) — March 15th
- **Vinte e cinco de dezembro** (veen-chee ee seen-koo jee deh-zem-broo) — December 25th

To say the full date:

- **Hoje é dia quinze de julho de dois mil e vinte e quatro** (oh-zhee eh jee-ah keen-zee jee zhool-yoo jee doysh meel ee veen-chee ee kwah-troo) — Today is July 15th, 2024
- **Eu nasci no dia dez de outubro** (eh-oo nah-see noo jee-ah dehz jee ow-too-broo) — I was born on October 10th

## Telling Time (Horas)

### Asking the time:

- **Que horas são?** (kee oh-rahz sow) — What time is it?
- **Você tem horas?** (voh-seh teng oh-rahz) — Do you have the time?

### Full hours:

- **É uma hora** (eh oo-mah oh-rah) — It's one o'clock (singular!)
- **São duas horas** (sow doo-ahz oh-rahz) — It's two o'clock
- **São três horas** (sow trehz oh-rahz) — It's three o'clock
- **É meio-dia** (eh may-oo jee-ah) — It's noon
- **É meia-noite** (eh may-ah noy-chee) — It's midnight

> **Note:** "Uma hora" uses **é** (singular). All other hours use **são** (plural).

### Minutes:

- **São três e quinze** (sow trehz ee keen-zee) — It's 3:15
- **São quatro e meia** (sow kwah-troo ee may-ah) — It's 4:30 (lit. "four and half")
- **São cinco e quarenta e cinco** (sow seen-koo ee kwah-ren-tah ee seen-koo) — It's 5:45
- **São dez para as seis** (sow dehz pah-rah ahz says) — It's 5:50 (lit. "ten to six")

### Time of day:

- **Da manhã** (dah mahn-yang) — In the morning (AM)
- **Da tarde** (dah tar-jee) — In the afternoon (PM)
- **Da noite** (dah noy-chee) — In the evening/at night (PM)

- **São oito horas da manhã** (sow oy-too oh-rahz dah mahn-yang) — It's 8 AM
- **São três da tarde** (sow trehz dah tar-jee) — It's 3 PM
- **São nove da noite** (sow noh-vee dah noy-chee) — It's 9 PM
