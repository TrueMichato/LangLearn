# Relative Pronouns (Pronomes Relativos)

<!-- vocab-inventory: {"mode":"explicit"} -->

Relative pronouns connect clauses and refer back to a noun mentioned earlier (the antecedent). In Portuguese, relative pronouns are essential for building complex sentences. The most common is **que**, which covers both "that," "which," and "who" in English.

## Que — The Universal Relative Pronoun

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"relative-que","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Que — The Universal Relative Pronoun","rule":"Que can refer to people or things as subject or object","example":"O livro ___ eu comprei é ótimo.","answer":"que","explanation":"Que can refer to people or things and serve as subject or object in a relative clause.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"'Que' as universal relative pronoun","example":"O filme ___ eu assisti é bom."}]} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-que","word":"que","meaning":"that; which; who (relative pronoun)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Que — The Universal Relative Pronoun"} -->

**Que** is by far the most used relative pronoun in Portuguese. It works for people, things, subjects, and objects:

- **O livro que eu comprei é ótimo.** (oo LEE-vroo kee eu com-PREY) — The book that I bought is great.
- **A mulher que mora aqui é professora.** (ah moo-LYEHR kee MOH-rah) — The woman who lives here is a teacher.
- **O filme que nós assistimos foi incrível.** (oo FEEL-mee kee nois ah-sees-CHEE-mohs) — The movie that we watched was incredible.
- **As coisas que ele disse são verdade.** (ahz COY-zahs kee EH-lee JEE-see) — The things that he said are true.
- **A comida que ela faz é deliciosa.** (ah koh-MEE-dah kee EH-lah fahz) — The food that she makes is delicious.

## Quem — For People (After Prepositions)

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"relative-quem","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Quem — For People (After Prepositions)","rule":"'Quem' after prepositions for people","example":"A pessoa com ___ eu trabalho é legal.","answer":"quem","explanation":"Quem refers to people after prepositions and does not change for gender or number."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"relative-free-quem","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Quem — For People (After Prepositions)","rule":"Quem can introduce a free relative without an explicit antecedent","example":"What does Quem estuda, aprende mean, and does quem refer to an earlier noun here?","answer":"Whoever studies learns; quem does not need an earlier noun in this construction.","explanation":"Quem can introduce a free relative meaning whoever or the one who."} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-quem","word":"quem","meaning":"who; whom; whoever (depending on construction)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Quem — For People (After Prepositions)"} -->

**Quem** is used for people, especially after prepositions. It does not change for gender or number:

- **A pessoa com quem eu falei é o gerente.** (ah peh-SOH-ah con kehn eu fah-LAY) — The person with whom I spoke is the manager.
- **O amigo para quem eu mandei a mensagem respondeu.** (oo ah-MEE-goo PAH-rah kehn) — The friend to whom I sent the message replied.
- **A professora de quem eu gosto muito se aposentou.** (ah pro-feh-SOH-rah jee kehn) — The teacher whom I like a lot retired.
- **As pessoas com quem trabalho são legais.** (ahz peh-SOH-ahz con kehn) — The people with whom I work are nice.

**Quem** can also start a sentence meaning "whoever" or "the one who":

- **Quem estuda, aprende.** (kehn es-TOO-dah) — Whoever studies, learns.
- **Quem disse isso não sabe nada.** (kehn JEE-see EE-soo) — Whoever said that doesn't know anything.

## O qual / A qual / Os quais / As quais — Formal Usage

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"relative-qual-agreement","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"O qual / A qual / Os quais / As quais — Formal Usage","rule":"O qual and its variants agree with the antecedent","example":"As cidades nas ___ moramos eram pequenas.","answer":"quais","explanation":"O qual and its variants agree with the antecedent; em + as quais gives nas quais."} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-o-qual","word":"o qual","meaning":"which; who (formal masculine singular relative)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"O qual / A qual / Os quais / As quais — Formal Usage"} -->

These forms agree in gender and number with the antecedent. They are more formal and used especially after compound prepositions:

- **A empresa na qual eu trabalho é grande.** (ah en-PREH-zah nah kwaw) — The company in which I work is big.
- **Os livros sobre os quais falamos são raros.** (ooz LEE-vrohs SOH-bree ooz kwaish) — The books about which we spoke are rare.
- **A razão pela qual ele saiu não é clara.** (ah hah-ZOWN PEH-lah kwaw) — The reason for which he left is not clear.
- **As cidades nas quais moramos eram pequenas.** (ahz see-DAH-jeez nahz kwaish) — The cities in which we lived were small.

## Onde — Where

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"relative-onde","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Onde — Where","rule":"Onde refers to location in a place","example":"A cidade ___ eu nasci é linda. (where)","answer":"onde","explanation":"Onde refers to location in a place; it is not a general replacement for every que."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"relative-aonde-condition","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Onde — Where","rule":"Aonde requires the preposition a rather than movement alone","example":"Is any movement verb enough to require aonde rather than onde?","answer":"No. Aonde combines a with onde and is appropriate when the construction calls for a, as with ir a.","explanation":"Preposition choice matters: movement toward a place with para can instead use para onde."} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-onde","word":"onde","meaning":"where","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Onde — Where"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-aonde","word":"aonde","meaning":"to where; where to","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Onde — Where"} -->

**Onde** is used for places:

- **A cidade onde eu nasci é linda.** (ah see-DAH-jee ON-jee eu nah-SEE) — The city where I was born is beautiful.
- **O restaurante onde jantamos é novo.** (oo hes-tow-RAN-chee ON-jee) — The restaurant where we had dinner is new.
- **A casa onde ela mora é grande.** (ah KAH-zah ON-jee) — The house where she lives is big.
- **O país onde vivemos é tropical.** (oo pah-EES ON-jee) — The country where we live is tropical.

**Aonde** combines **a + onde** when the construction calls for **a**, as with **ir a**. Movement alone is not enough: **para onde** can express a destination with **para**.

- **A cidade aonde nós vamos é longe.** (ah see-DAH-jee ah-ON-jee) — The city where we're going is far.

## Cujo / Cuja / Cujos / Cujas — Whose

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"relative-cujo","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Cujo / Cuja / Cujos / Cujas — Whose","rule":"'Cujo' agrees with the possessed noun","example":"A mulher ___ filho é médico mora aqui.","answer":"cujo","explanation":"Cujo agrees with filho, not mulher, and no article is inserted after it."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"relative-cujo-no-article","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Cujo / Cuja / Cujos / Cujas — Whose","rule":"No article follows possessive relative cujo","example":"Is the sequence cuja a filha correct in this relative construction?","answer":"No. Use cuja filha, without an article after the relative.","explanation":"Cujo and its agreeing forms precede the possessed noun directly."} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-cujo","word":"cujo","meaning":"whose (masculine singular possessed noun)","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Cujo / Cuja / Cujos / Cujas — Whose"} -->

**Cujo** means "whose" and agrees with the **possessed noun** (not the possessor). It never takes an article after it:

- **O homem cujo carro é vermelho é meu vizinho.** (oo OH-mehn KOO-joo KAH-hoo) — The man whose car is red is my neighbor.
- **A mulher cuja filha estuda medicina é médica.** (ah moo-LYEHR KOO-jah FEE-lyah) — The woman whose daughter studies medicine is a doctor.
- **Os alunos cujos pais vieram são novos.** (ooz ah-LOO-nohs KOO-johs paysh) — The students whose parents came are new.
- **A empresa cujas regras são rígidas perdeu funcionários.** (ah en-PREH-zah KOO-jahz HEH-grahz) — The company whose rules are strict lost employees.

## Common Patterns: O que / Tudo o que

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"relative-o-que","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Common Patterns: O que / Tudo o que","rule":"Tudo o que means everything that","example":"Tudo ___ ele falou é verdade. (that which)","answer":"o que","explanation":"Tudo o que means everything that; o que can mean what or that which."} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-o-que","word":"o que","meaning":"what; that which","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Common Patterns: O que / Tudo o que"} -->

- **O que você quer?** (oo kee vo-SEH kehr) — What do you want?
- **Faço o que posso.** (FAH-soo oo kee POH-soo) — I do what I can.
- **Tudo o que ele falou é verdade.** (TOO-doo oo kee EH-lee fah-LOH) — Everything (that) he said is true.
- **Nada do que ela disse fez sentido.** (NAH-dah doo kee EH-lah JEE-see) — Nothing (of what) she said made sense.
- **Isso é tudo o que eu preciso.** (EE-soo eh TOO-doo oo kee) — This is everything I need.
