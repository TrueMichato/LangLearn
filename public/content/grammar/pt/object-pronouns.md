# Object Pronouns (me, te, o/a, nos)

Object pronouns replace nouns that receive the action of a verb. In English, we say "I see **her**" instead of "I see **Maria**" — Portuguese works similarly, but with different placement rules. Brazilian Portuguese is especially interesting because spoken usage often differs from formal grammar.

## Direct Object Pronouns

Direct object pronouns replace the noun that **directly receives** the action:

| Person | Pronoun | English |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 1st sing. | **me** | me |
| 2nd sing. | **te** | you |
| 3rd sing. masc. | **o** | him / it |
| 3rd sing. fem. | **a** | her / it |
| 1st plural | **nos** | us |
| 2nd plural | **vocês** | you (all) |
| 3rd plural masc. | **os** | them |
| 3rd plural fem. | **as** | them |

### Examples with Direct Objects

- **Ela me viu** (eh-lah mee vee-oo) — She saw me
- **Eu te amo** (eh-oo chee ah-moo) — I love you
- **Eu o conheço** (eh-oo oo koh-nyeh-soo) — I know him
- **Eu a conheço** (eh-oo ah koh-nyeh-soo) — I know her
- **Eles nos convidaram** (eh-leez nooz kohn-vee-dah-rawn) — They invited us
- **Eu os vi ontem** (eh-oo ooz vee ohn-tayn) — I saw them (masc.)

## Spoken Brazilian Portuguese: Replacing o/a/os/as

In everyday spoken Brazilian Portuguese, the 3rd person direct object pronouns **o, a, os, as** are rarely used. Instead, Brazilians use:

### 1. The pronoun **ele/ela** as object

- **Eu vi ele ontem** (eh-oo vee eh-lee ohn-tayn) — I saw him yesterday (spoken)
- **Eu conheço ela** (eh-oo koh-nyeh-soo eh-lah) — I know her (spoken)

### 2. The person's name

- **Eu vi a Maria** (eh-oo vee ah mah-ree-ah) — I saw Maria

### 3. Dropping the pronoun entirely (when context is clear)

- **Você viu o filme? Vi.** (voh-say vee-oo oo fee-ooh-mee? vee) — Did you see the movie? I saw (it).

> **Important:** Using **o/a/os/as** is grammatically correct and used in formal writing. In conversation, using **ele/ela** as an object is standard in Brazil.

## Indirect Object Pronouns

Indirect object pronouns indicate **to whom** or **for whom** the action is done:

| Person | Pronoun | English |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 1st sing. | **me** | to/for me |
| 2nd sing. | **te** | to/for you |
| 3rd sing. | **lhe** | to/for him/her/you |
| 1st plural | **nos** | to/for us |
| 2nd plural | **lhes** | to/for you all |
| 3rd plural | **lhes** | to/for them |

### Examples with Indirect Objects

- **Ele me deu um presente** (eh-lee mee deh-oo oon preh-zehn-chee) — He gave me a gift
- **Eu te disse a verdade** (eh-oo chee jee-see ah vehr-dah-jee) — I told you the truth
- **Ela nos contou uma história** (eh-lah nooz kohn-toh oo-mah ees-toh-ree-ah) — She told us a story
- **Eu lhe expliquei o problema** (eh-oo lyee esh-plee-kay oo proh-bleh-mah) — I explained the problem to him/her

### In Spoken Brazilian: para + pronoun

In casual Brazilian Portuguese, **lhe/lhes** is often replaced with **para + pronoun**:

- **Eu dei o livro para ele** (eh-oo day oo lee-vroo pah-rah eh-lee) — I gave the book to him
- **Ela falou para mim** (eh-lah fah-loh pah-rah meen) — She told me
- **Eu comprei um presente para você** (eh-oo kohn-pray oon preh-zehn-chee pah-rah voh-say) — I bought a gift for you

## Pronoun Placement in Brazilian Portuguese

This is where Brazilian Portuguese differs significantly from European Portuguese and formal grammar rules.

### Spoken Brazilian: Before the verb (proclisis)

In everyday Brazilian speech, pronouns almost always go **before** the verb:

- **Eu te amo** (eh-oo chee ah-moo) — I love you
- **Ela me ligou** (eh-lah mee lee-goh) — She called me
- **Ele nos ajudou** (eh-lee nooz ah-zhoo-doh) — He helped us
- **Você me entende?** (voh-say mee ehn-tehn-jee) — Do you understand me?

### Formal Writing: After the verb (enclisis)

In formal writing, pronouns go **after** the verb (attached with a hyphen) at the beginning of sentences:

- **Diga-me a verdade** (jee-gah mee ah vehr-dah-jee) — Tell me the truth
- **Chamo-me João** (shah-moo mee zhoo-ow) — My name is João (literally: I call myself João)

### Words that "attract" the pronoun before the verb

Even in formal writing, certain words force the pronoun to come before the verb:

- **Não** (negation): **Não me diga!** — Don't tell me!
- **Question words**: **Quem te disse?** — Who told you?
- **Que/quem/onde** (relative pronouns): **O homem que me ajudou** — The man who helped me
- **Advérbios**: **Sempre te amei** — I always loved you
- **Conjunctions**: **Quando me viu, sorriu** — When she saw me, she smiled

## Me and Te — The Most Common

**Me** and **te** are by far the most frequently used object pronouns because they cover both direct and indirect objects:

- **Ele me ajudou** (eh-lee mee ah-zhoo-doh) — He helped me (direct)
- **Ele me deu dinheiro** (eh-lee mee deh-oo jee-nyay-roo) — He gave me money (indirect)
- **Eu te liguei** (eh-oo chee lee-gay) — I called you (direct)
- **Eu te mandei uma mensagem** (eh-oo chee mahn-day oo-mah mehn-sah-zhayn) — I sent you a message (indirect)

More examples in everyday contexts:

- **Me desculpe** (mee desh-koo-pee) — Excuse me / I'm sorry
- **Me ajuda?** (mee ah-zhoo-dah) — Can you help me?
- **Te vejo amanhã** (chee veh-zhoo ah-mah-nyah) — I'll see you tomorrow
- **Te amo muito** (chee ah-moo mween-too) — I love you very much

## Reflexive Pronouns (Preview)

Reflexive pronouns are a special type of object pronoun where the subject and object are the same person. We'll cover these in depth in a later lesson, but here's a quick preview:

- **me** — myself: **Eu me chamo João** — My name is João (I call myself João)
- **se** — yourself/himself/herself: **Ela se chama Maria** — Her name is Maria
- **nos** — ourselves: **Nós nos divertimos** — We had fun
