# Gender & Number Agreement

Every noun in Portuguese has a grammatical gender — either masculine or feminine — and a number — singular or plural. Adjectives, articles, and other words must agree with the noun they describe. This is one of the most fundamental rules in Portuguese grammar.

## Masculine and Feminine Nouns

### The Basic Rule: -o and -a Endings

Most masculine nouns end in **-o** and most feminine nouns end in **-a**:

- **O menino** (oo meh-nee-noo) — The boy (masculine)
- **A menina** (ah meh-nee-nah) — The girl (feminine)
- **O gato** (oo gah-too) — The cat (male)
- **A gata** (ah gah-tah) — The cat (female)
- **O amigo** (oo ah-mee-goo) — The friend (male)
- **A amiga** (ah ah-mee-gah) — The friend (female)

### Common Endings by Gender

**Typically Masculine:**

- **-o**: o livro (book), o carro (car), o sapato (shoe)
- **-or**: o amor (love), o professor (male teacher), o valor (value)
- **-ema**: o problema (problem), o sistema (system), o tema (topic)
- **-ão**: o coração (heart), o avião (airplane), o pão (bread)

**Typically Feminine:**

- **-a**: a casa (house), a mesa (table), a porta (door)
- **-ade**: a cidade (city), a liberdade (freedom), a verdade (truth)
- **-ção**: a canção (song), a ação (action), a nação (nation)
- **-gem**: a viagem (trip), a mensagem (message), a garagem (garage)

### Tricky Exceptions

Some common nouns break the pattern:

**Masculine words ending in -a:**

- **O dia** (oo jee-ah) — The day
- **O mapa** (oo mah-pah) — The map
- **O problema** (oo proh-bleh-mah) — The problem
- **O cinema** (oo see-neh-mah) — The cinema
- **O programa** (oo proh-grah-mah) — The program

**Feminine words ending in -o or consonant:**

- **A mão** (ah mow) — The hand
- **A foto** (ah foh-too) — The photo (short for fotografia)
- **A moto** (ah moh-too) — The motorcycle (short for motocicleta)
- **A flor** (ah flohr) — The flower
- **A noite** (ah noy-chee) — The night

### Words with Different Meanings by Gender

Some words change meaning depending on their article:

- **O capital** (the capital/money) vs **A capital** (the capital city)
- **O rádio** (the radio device) vs **A rádio** (the radio station)
- **O grama** (the gram) vs **A grama** (the grass)

## Adjective Agreement

Adjectives must match the noun in both gender and number:

### Adjectives ending in -o/-a

- **O menino bonito** (oo meh-nee-noo boo-nee-too) — The handsome boy
- **A menina bonita** (ah meh-nee-nah boo-nee-tah) — The pretty girl
- **Os meninos bonitos** (ooz meh-nee-nooz boo-nee-tooz) — The handsome boys
- **As meninas bonitas** (ahz meh-nee-nahz boo-nee-tahz) — The pretty girls

### Adjectives ending in -e (no gender change)

- **O homem inteligente** (oo oh-mayn een-teh-lee-zhen-chee) — The intelligent man
- **A mulher inteligente** (ah moo-lyehr een-teh-lee-zhen-chee) — The intelligent woman
- **Um carro grande** (oon kah-hoo grahn-jee) — A big car
- **Uma casa grande** (oo-mah kah-zah grahn-jee) — A big house

### Adjectives ending in a consonant (usually no gender change)

- **O exame difícil** (oo eh-zah-mee jee-fee-see-oo) — The difficult exam
- **A prova difícil** (ah proh-vah jee-fee-see-oo) — The difficult test
- **O homem feliz** (oo oh-mayn feh-leez) — The happy man
- **A mulher feliz** (ah moo-lyehr feh-leez) — The happy woman

## Plural Formation

### Rule 1: Add -s to words ending in a vowel

This is the most common pattern:

- **gato → gatos** (cats)
- **casa → casas** (houses)
- **livro → livros** (books)
- **grande → grandes** (big, plural)

### Rule 2: Add -es to words ending in -r, -z, -s

- **flor → flores** (flowers)
- **vez → vezes** (times)
- **país → países** (countries)
- **professor → professores** (teachers)

### Rule 3: Words ending in -m change to -ns

- **homem → homens** (men)
- **viagem → viagens** (trips)
- **bom → bons** (good)
- **jardim → jardins** (gardens)

### Rule 4: Words ending in -ão

This is the trickiest pattern. There are three possible plural forms:

**-ão → -ões** (most common):

- **coração → corações** (hearts)
- **avião → aviões** (airplanes)
- **canção → canções** (songs)

**-ão → -ães** (less common):

- **pão → pães** (breads)
- **cão → cães** (dogs)
- **alemão → alemães** (Germans)

**-ão → -ãos** (least common):

- **mão → mãos** (hands)
- **irmão → irmãos** (brothers)
- **cidadão → cidadãos** (citizens)

### Rule 5: Words ending in -l

Replace **-l** with **-is**:

- **animal → animais** (animals)
- **jornal → jornais** (newspapers)
- **papel → papéis** (papers)
- **azul → azuis** (blue, plural)
- **fácil → fáceis** (easy, plural)

### Rule 6: Words ending in -il

- **Stressed -il → -is**: **civil → civis** (civil, plural)
- **Unstressed -il → -eis**: **fóssil → fósseis** (fossils)

## Full Agreement in Sentences

Everything must agree — articles, adjectives, and nouns:

- **O carro novo é vermelho** (oo kah-hoo noh-voo eh vehr-meh-lyoo) — The new car is red
- **A casa nova é vermelha** (ah kah-zah noh-vah eh vehr-meh-lyah) — The new house is red
- **Os carros novos são vermelhos** (ooz kah-hooz noh-vooz sow vehr-meh-lyooz) — The new cars are red
- **As casas novas são vermelhas** (ahz kah-zahz noh-vahz sow vehr-meh-lyahz) — The new houses are red

When a group has mixed gender, use the **masculine plural**:

- **Os meninos e as meninas são bonitos** (ooz meh-nee-nooz ee ahz meh-nee-nahz sow boo-nee-tooz) — The boys and girls are pretty
