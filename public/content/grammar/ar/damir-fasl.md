# The Separating Pronoun (Ḍamīr al-Faṣl)

**ضَمِيرُ الْفَصْلِ** (ḍamīru al-faṣli) is an independent pronoun placed between two definite elements. It helps separate subject and predicate, adds emphasis, and prevents the second element from being read as a simple adjective.

## Core idea

Compare **زَيْدٌ الْمُدَرِّسُ** (Zaydun al-mudarrisu) — Zayd, the teacher or Zayd is the teacher, with **زَيْدٌ هُوَ الْمُدَرِّسُ** (Zaydun huwa al-mudarrisu) — Zayd is indeed the teacher. The pronoun **هُوَ** (huwa) makes the predicate relationship clear and emphatic.

## Forms and examples

| Topic | Separating pronoun | Predicate | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | **هُوَ** (huwa) | definite noun | **زَيْدٌ هُوَ الْمُدَرِّسُ** (Zaydun huwa al-mudarrisu) — Zayd is the teacher |
| feminine singular | **هِيَ** (hiya) | definite noun | **لَيْلَى هِيَ الْمُدِيرَةُ** (Laylā hiya al-mudīratu) — Layla is the director |
| masculine plural | **هُمْ** or **هُمُ** (hum, humu) | definite plural | **هَؤُلَاءِ هُمُ الْفَائِزُونَ** (hāʾulāʾi humu al-fāʾizūna) — these are the winners |
| after **إِنَّ** | matching pronoun | emphatic predicate | **إِنَّ الْعِلْمَ هُوَ النُّورُ** (inna al-ʿilma huwa an-nūru) — knowledge is truly the light |

## Usage notes

Ḍamīr al-faṣl usually has no new case role of its own; it points back to the first element and highlights the second as the predicate. It is common between definite nouns and after particles such as **إِنَّ** (inna) — indeed: **إِنَّ الْقُرْآنَ هُوَ الْهُدَى** (inna al-Qurʾāna huwa al-hudā) — the Qur'an is guidance itself.

## Practice patterns

Insert a matching pronoun when the intended meaning is emphatic identity:

- **الْمُجْتَهِدُ هُوَ النَّاجِحُ** (al-mujtahidu huwa an-nājiḥu) — the hardworking one is the successful one.
- **الْمَدِينَةُ هِيَ الْقَاهِرَةُ** (al-madīnatu hiya al-Qāhiratu) — the city is Cairo.
- **أُولَٰئِكَ هُمُ الصَّادِقُونَ** (ulāʾika humu aṣ-ṣādiqūna) — those are the truthful ones.
