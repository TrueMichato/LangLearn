# Sun & Moon Letters in Depth

Arabic has one definite article, **الْـ** (al-) — the, but two pronunciations. **حُرُوفٌ شَمْسِيَّةٌ** (ḥurūfun shamsiyyatun) — sun letters assimilate the lām, while **حُرُوفٌ قَمَرِيَّةٌ** (ḥurūfun qamariyyatun) — moon letters keep it.

## Core idea

The spelling of the article stays **الـ**, but the sound changes. Before a sun letter, the **لَامٌ** (lāmun) — l of the article disappears into the next consonant, and that consonant receives **شَدَّةٌ** (shaddatun) — doubling: **الشَّمْسُ** (ash-shamsu) — the sun. Before a moon letter, the l is pronounced: **الْقَمَرُ** (al-qamaru) — the moon.

## Letter groups

| Type | Letters | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sun letters | **ت ث د ذ ر ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ل ن** | **الدَّرْسُ** (ad-darsu) — the lesson |
| Moon letters | **ا ب ج ح خ ع غ ف ق ك م هـ و ي** | **الْكِتَابُ** (al-kitābu) — the book |

## In connected speech

The article begins with **هَمْزَةُ وَصْلٍ** (hamzatu waṣlin) — connecting hamza. At the beginning you may hear a helping vowel, as in **اَلشَّمْسُ** (ash-shamsu) — the sun. After another word, the helping vowel drops: **فِي الشَّمْسِ** (fī sh-shamsi) — in the sun, **مِنَ الْبَيْتِ** (mina l-bayti) — from the house.

## Why spelling still matters

Even when the l is not pronounced, the written **ل** remains. This helps you recognize definiteness, dictionary forms, and compounds: **بَابُ الشَّرِكَةِ** (bābu sh-sharikati) — the company's door, **مُدِيرُ الْمَكْتَبِ** (mudīru l-maktabi) — the office manager.

> Pronunciation follows the letter; grammar still follows definiteness.
