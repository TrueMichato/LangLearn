# Defective Verbs (weak final: mashā, daʿā)

Defective verbs end in a weak root sound. They are common, and their final vowel or weak letter changes when endings are added.

## Core idea

The **الْفِعْلُ النَّاقِصُ** (al-fiʿl an-nāqiṣ) — defective verb has a weak final root sound, often **وَاوٌ** (wāwun) — wāw or **يَاءٌ** (yāʾun) — yāʾ. In many past-tense dictionary forms, the ending is written with **أَلِفٌ مَقْصُورَةٌ** (alif maqṣūrah) — final ā letter.

## Forms and examples

| Verb | Present | Imperative |
|---|---|---|
| **مَشَى** (mashā) — he walked | **يَمْشِي** (yamshī) — he walks | **اِمْشِ** (imshi) — walk! |
| **دَعَا** (daʿā) — he called / prayed | **يَدْعُو** (yadʿū) — he calls / prays | **اُدْعُ** (udʿu) — call! / pray! |
| **رَمَى** (ramā) — he threw | **يَرْمِي** (yarmī) — he throws | **اِرْمِ** (irmi) — throw! |

## Usage notes

When endings are added, the final weak sound may change: **مَشَى** (mashā) — he walked, but **مَشَيْتُ** (mashaytu) — I walked. In the jussive, the final long vowel often disappears: **لَمْ يَمْشِ** (lam yamshi) — he did not walk.

## Practice patterns

The mood endings are especially important:

- **يَمْشِي** (yamshī) — he walks.
- **لَنْ يَمْشِيَ** (lan yamshiya) — he will not walk.
- **لَمْ يَمْشِ** (lam yamshi) — he did not walk.

These changes are predictable once you know that the final root sound is weak.
