# Adverbials of Time & Place (ẓarf)

Arabic often expresses when or where something happens with a noun in an adverbial role. Traditional grammar calls this الظَّرْفُ (aẓ-ẓarf) — the ظرف of time or place.

## Core idea

A ظرف answers مَتَى؟ (matā) — when? or أَيْنَ؟ (ayna) — where? Many ẓarf words are accusative in meaning. If the ẓarf is followed by a noun, it often forms an iḍāfa, so the following noun is genitive.

## Forms and examples

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Time | سَافَرْنَا لَيْلًا (sāfarnā laylan) — We traveled at night |
| Time | يَبْدَأُ الدَّرْسُ صَبَاحًا (yabdaʾu ad-darsu ṣabāḥan) — The lesson starts in the morning |
| Place | وَقَفَ الْوَلَدُ أَمَامَ الْبَابِ (waqafa al-waladu amāma al-bābi) — The boy stood in front of the door |
| Place | الْكِتَابُ فَوْقَ الطَّاوِلَةِ (al-kitābu fawqa aṭ-ṭāwilati) — The book is on the table |

## Time adverbials

Common time adverbials include يَوْمًا (yawman) — one day, لَيْلًا (laylan) — at night, صَبَاحًا (ṣabāḥan) — in the morning, مَسَاءً (masāʾan) — in the evening, and بَعْدَ (baʿda) — after.

- دَرَسْتُ سَاعَةً (darastu sāʿatan) — I studied for an hour.
- نَلْتَقِي بَعْدَ الظُّهْرِ (naltaqī baʿda aẓ-ẓuhri) — We meet after noon.

## Place adverbials and iḍāfa

Place words such as أَمَامَ (amāma) — in front of, خَلْفَ (khalfa) — behind, فَوْقَ (fawqa) — above, تَحْتَ (taḥta) — under, and عِنْدَ (ʿinda) — at or with often govern a following genitive noun because they are in iḍāfa.

- جَلَسَتِ الْقِطَّةُ تَحْتَ الْكُرْسِيِّ (jalasati al-qiṭṭatu taḥta al-kursiyyi) — The cat sat under the chair.
- أَنْتَظِرُكَ عِنْدَ الْبَابِ (antaẓiruka ʿinda al-bābi) — I am waiting for you at the door.

## Meaning over word class

A ẓarf is a role in the sentence. The same root may appear in other roles elsewhere, so identify the question it answers: when or where?
