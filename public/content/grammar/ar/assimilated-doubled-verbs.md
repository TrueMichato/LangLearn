# Assimilated & Doubled Verbs (waṣala, radda)

Two weak-looking verb types are common enough to recognize early: verbs with a weak first root sound, and verbs whose second and third root sounds are the same.

## Core idea

The **الْفِعْلُ الْمِثَالُ** (al-fiʿl al-mithāl) — assimilated verb begins with a weak root sound, often **وَاوٌ** (wāwun) — wāw. The **الْفِعْلُ الْمُضَعَّفُ** (al-fiʿl al-muḍaʿʿaf) — doubled verb has a repeated final root sound, often written with **شَدَّةٌ** (shaddatun) — doubling mark.

## Forms and examples

| Type | Past | Present | Imperative |
|---|---|---|---|
| assimilated | **وَصَلَ** (waṣala) — he arrived | **يَصِلُ** (yaṣilu) — he arrives | **صِلْ** (ṣil) — arrive! |
| assimilated | **وَعَدَ** (waʿada) — he promised | **يَعِدُ** (yaʿidu) — he promises | **عِدْ** (ʿid) — promise! |
| doubled | **رَدَّ** (radda) — he returned / replied | **يَرُدُّ** (yaruddu) — he returns / replies | **رُدَّ** (rudda) — return! / reply! |
| doubled | **مَدَّ** (madda) — he stretched | **يَمُدُّ** (yamuddu) — he stretches | **مُدَّ** (mudda) — stretch! |

## Usage notes

In many assimilated verbs, the initial **وَاوٌ** (wāwun) — wāw disappears in the present and imperative: **وَصَلَ** (waṣala) — he arrived, **يَصِلُ** (yaṣilu) — he arrives, **صِلْ** (ṣil) — arrive! In doubled verbs, the doubled consonant may split when suffixes are added: **رَدَّ** (radda) — he replied, **رَدَدْتُ** (radadtu) — I replied.

## Practice patterns

Doubled verbs are compact in the third-person masculine past, but expanded forms are still regular enough to learn:

- **رَدَّ** (radda) — he replied; **رَدَدْنَا** (radadnā) — we replied.
- **شَدَّ** (shadda) — he tightened; **شَدَدْتُ** (shadadtu) — I tightened.
