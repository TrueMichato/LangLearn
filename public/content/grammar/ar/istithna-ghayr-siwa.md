# Exception with ghayr and siwā

Modern Standard Arabic can make exceptions with **غَيْرُ** (ghayru) and **سِوَى** (siwā) as well as with **إِلَّا** (illā). The key difference is that **غَيْرُ** and **سِوَى** act like nouns in an iḍāfa, so the following excluded noun is genitive.

## Core idea

Think of **غَيْرُ** (ghayru) as “other than” and **سِوَى** (siwā) as “except for.” The word after either one is always the second term of an iḍāfa: **غَيْرَ طَالِبٍ** (ghayra ṭālibin) — other than a student.

## Case patterns

| Pattern | What changes | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Complete affirmative exception | **غَيْرَ** is usually accusative | **حَضَرَ الطُّلَّابُ غَيْرَ طَالِبٍ** (ḥaḍara aṭ-ṭullābu ghayra ṭālibin) — The students attended except one student. |
| Negative with only the exception remaining | **غَيْرُ** can be the subject | **مَا حَضَرَ غَيْرُ طَالِبٍ** (mā ḥaḍara ghayru ṭālibin) — Only one student attended. |
| After a preposition | **غَيْرِ** follows the preposition | **مَرَرْتُ بِغَيْرِ مُعَلِّمٍ** (marartu bighayri muʿallimin) — I passed by someone other than a teacher. |
| With **سِوَى** | Its case is often estimated, but the next noun is genitive | **قَرَأْتُ الْكِتَابَ سِوَى صَفْحَةٍ** (qaraʾtu al-kitāba siwā ṣafḥatin) — I read the book except one page. |

## Usage notes

With **إِلَّا** (illā), the excluded noun itself carries the case required by the exception pattern. With **غَيْرُ** (ghayru), the case lands on **غَيْرُ** while the excluded noun stays genitive: **رَأَيْتُ الْمُدَرِّسِينَ غَيْرَ مُدَرِّسٍ** (raʾaytu al-mudarrisīna ghayra mudarrisin) — I saw the teachers except one teacher.

## Practice patterns

Use **غَيْرُ** (ghayru) when the exception should behave like a noun phrase: **لَا أَعْرِفُ غَيْرَ هَذَا الْجَوَابِ** (lā aʿrifu ghayra hādhā al-jawābi) — I know no answer except this answer. Use **سِوَى** (siwā) similarly, especially in formal writing: **لَا أَمْلِكُ سِوَى قَلَمٍ** (lā amliku siwā qalamin) — I own nothing except a pen.
