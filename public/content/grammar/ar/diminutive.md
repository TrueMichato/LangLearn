# The Diminutive (at-Taṣghīr)

Arabic can make a noun feel smaller, dearer, or sometimes less important by changing its internal pattern. This is formal and literary, but useful for recognizing words.

## Core idea

The **التَّصْغِيرُ** (at-taṣghīr) — diminutive reshapes a noun to suggest smallness, affection, or reduction. A common three-consonant pattern is **فُعَيْلٌ** (fuʿaylun) — little-form pattern.

## Forms and examples

| Base noun | Diminutive | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **كَلْبٌ** (kalbun) — dog | **كُلَيْبٌ** (kulaybun) — little dog | small / dear dog |
| **جَبَلٌ** (jabalun) — mountain | **جُبَيْلٌ** (jubaylun) — little mountain / hill | smaller mountain |
| **نَهْرٌ** (nahrun) — river | **نُهَيْرٌ** (nuhayrun) — little river | stream-like river |
| **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — book | **كُتَيِّبٌ** (kutayyibun) — booklet | small book |

## Usage notes

The diminutive is not the everyday way to say “small” in modern speech; **صَغِيرٌ** (ṣaghīrun) — small is more general. But **التَّصْغِيرُ** (at-taṣghīr) — diminutive appears in names, literature, and lexicalized words.

## Practice patterns

For longer words, the shape can adjust. The goal is not to build every diminutive on day one, but to recognize the signal:

- **رَجُلٌ** (rajulun) — man; **رُجَيْلٌ** (rujaylun) — little man.
- **قَلَمٌ** (qalamun) — pen; **قُلَيْمٌ** (qulaymun) — little pen.
