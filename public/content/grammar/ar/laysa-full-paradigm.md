# Laysa: Full Paradigm and Usage

لَيْسَ (laysa) is a frozen past-shaped verb used for present-time negation: “is not,” “are not,” or “am not.” It behaves like a sister of كَانَ (kāna): its subject stays nominative, and its predicate is accusative unless introduced by extra بِـ (bi-).

## Core idea

لَيْسَ does not have a normal present tense, imperative, or maṣdar. Instead, it conjugates for person, gender, and number in one paradigm and negates nominal sentences.

## Forms and examples

| Pronoun | Form | Example |
|---|---|---|
| أَنَا (anā) | لَسْتُ (lastu) | لَسْتُ مُتْعَبًا (lastu mutʿaban) — I am not tired |
| أَنْتَ (anta) | لَسْتَ (lasta) | لَسْتَ طَالِبًا جَدِيدًا (lasta ṭāliban jadīdan) — you are not a new student |
| هُوَ (huwa) | لَيْسَ (laysa) | لَيْسَ الْبَابُ مَفْتُوحًا (laysa al-bābu maftūḥan) — the door is not open |
| هِيَ (hiya) | لَيْسَتْ (laysat) | لَيْسَتِ السَّاعَةُ بَعِيدَةً (laysati as-sāʿatu baʿīdatan) — the hour is not far |
| نَحْنُ (naḥnu) | لَسْنَا (lasnā) | لَسْنَا غَافِلِينَ (lasnā ghāfilīna) — we are not heedless |
| هُمْ (hum) | لَيْسُوا (laysū) | لَيْسُوا حَاضِرِينَ (laysū ḥāḍirīna) — they are not present |

## Usage notes

The predicate of لَيْسَ is normally accusative: لَيْسَ الْجَوُّ بَارِدًا (laysa al-jawwu bāridan) — the weather is not cold. With an extra بِـ, the predicate becomes genitive in form: لَيْسَ الْجَوُّ بِبَارِدٍ (laysa al-jawwu bi-bāridin) — the weather is not cold.

## Practice patterns

Memorize the common endings as if لَيْسَ were a past-tense verb:

- لَسْتِ مُتَأَخِّرَةً (lasti mutaʾakhkhiratan) — you feminine singular are not late.
- لَسْتُمْ مُسَافِرِينَ (lastum musāfirīna) — you masculine plural are not traveling.
- لَسْنَ مُهْمِلَاتٍ (lasna muhmilātin) — they feminine plural are not negligent.
