# The Passive Participle (ism al-mafʿūl)

The passive participle lets Arabic describe what has been done to something: written, opened, understood, heard. It is one of the most common bridges between verbs and adjectives.

## Core idea

The **اِسْمُ الْمَفْعُولِ** (ism al-mafʿūl) — passive participle names the receiver of an action. For many Form I sound verbs, it uses the pattern **مَفْعُولٌ** (mafʿūlun) — thing-done-to pattern.

## Forms and examples

| Verb | Passive participle | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| **كَتَبَ** (kataba) — he wrote | **مَكْتُوبٌ** (maktūbun) — written | something written |
| **فَتَحَ** (fataḥa) — he opened | **مَفْتُوحٌ** (maftūḥun) — open / opened | something opened |
| **سَمِعَ** (samiʿa) — he heard | **مَسْمُوعٌ** (masmūʿun) — heard / audible | something heard |
| **فَهِمَ** (fahima) — he understood | **مَفْهُومٌ** (mafhūmun) — understood | something understood |

## Usage notes

The passive participle often works like an adjective: **الْبَابُ مَفْتُوحٌ** (al-bābu maftūḥun) — the door is open. It can also be a noun: **مَفْهُومٌ جَدِيدٌ** (mafhūmun jadīdun) — a new concept / thing understood.

## Practice patterns

Pair active and passive participles to hear the contrast:

- **كَاتِبٌ** (kātibun) — writer; **مَكْتُوبٌ** (maktūbun) — written.
- **فَاتِحٌ** (fātiḥun) — opener; **مَفْتُوحٌ** (maftūḥun) — opened.
- **سَامِعٌ** (sāmiʿun) — listener; **مَسْمُوعٌ** (masmūʿun) — heard.
