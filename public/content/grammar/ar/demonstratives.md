# Demonstratives (hādhā / hādhihi / dhālika)

Demonstratives are words like “this,” “that,” and “these.” Arabic demonstratives agree with gender and number, and they often begin useful beginner sentences.

## Core idea

The two most important near demonstratives are **هٰذَا** (hādhā) — this, masculine and **هٰذِهِ** (hādhihi) — this, feminine. Far demonstratives include **ذٰلِكَ** (dhālika) — that, masculine and **تِلْكَ** (tilka) — that, feminine.

## Forms and examples

| Demonstrative | Example |
|---|---|
| **هٰذَا** (hādhā) — this, masculine | **هٰذَا كِتَابٌ** (hādhā kitābun) — this is a book |
| **هٰذِهِ** (hādhihi) — this, feminine | **هٰذِهِ مَدْرَسَةٌ** (hādhihi madrasatun) — this is a school |
| **ذٰلِكَ** (dhālika) — that, masculine | **ذٰلِكَ بَيْتٌ** (dhālika baytun) — that is a house |
| **تِلْكَ** (tilka) — that, feminine | **تِلْكَ سَيَّارَةٌ** (tilka sayyāratun) — that is a car |
| **هٰؤُلَاءِ** (hāʾulāʾi) — these | **هٰؤُلَاءِ طُلَّابٌ** (hāʾulāʾi ṭullābun) — these are students |

## Usage notes

With a definite noun, demonstratives can form a phrase like “this book”: **هٰذَا الْكِتَابُ** (hādhā al-kitābu) — this book. With an indefinite noun, they form a sentence: **هٰذَا كِتَابٌ** (hādhā kitābun) — this is a book.

## Practice patterns

Notice the definiteness contrast:

- **هٰذِهِ طَالِبَةٌ** (hādhihi ṭālibatun) — this is a female student.
- **هٰذِهِ الطَّالِبَةُ** (hādhihi aṭ-ṭālibatu) — this female student.

Both are useful; the noun’s definiteness tells you phrase vs. sentence.
