# The Specification (tamyīz) of Numbers & Measures

Many Arabic quantities need a following word that says what is being counted or measured. That clarifying word is a kind of specification, and its form changes with the number range.

## Core idea

**التَّمْيِيزُ** (at-tamyīz) — specification removes ambiguity from a quantity, number, comparison, or measure. With numbers, it answers **مَاذَا؟** (mādhā?) — what? after a numeral: twenty what, three what, one hundred what.

## Number specifications

| Quantity | Arabic | Form of the specified noun |
|---|---|---|
| 3–10 | **ثَلَاثَةُ أَقْلَامٍ** (thalāthatu aqlāmin) — three pens | plural genitive |
| 11–99 | **أَحَدَ عَشَرَ قَلَمًا** (aḥada ʿashara qalaman) — eleven pens | singular accusative |
| tens | **عِشْرُونَ قَلَمًا** (ʿishrūna qalaman) — twenty pens | singular accusative |
| hundreds | **مِئَةُ قَلَمٍ** (miʾatu qalamin) — one hundred pens | singular genitive |

## Usage notes

The word after 11–99 is the classic accusative **تَمْيِيزٌ** (tamyīz) — specification: **خَمْسَةٌ وَعِشْرُونَ طَالِبًا** (khamsatun wa-ʿishrūna ṭāliban) — twenty-five students. With 3–10, the counted noun is plural genitive: **سَبْعَةُ بُيُوتٍ** (sabʿatu buyūtin) — seven houses. With 100 and 1000, it is usually singular genitive: **أَلْفُ دِينَارٍ** (alfu dīnārin) — a thousand dinars.

## Measures and amounts

Measure words also need specification. Classical and formal Arabic may use an accusative specification, while modern prose often uses an iḍāfa or **مِنْ** (min) — of phrase.

- **اِشْتَرَيْتُ كِيلُوغْرَامًا تَمْرًا** (ishtaraytu kīlūghrāman tamran) — I bought a kilogram of dates.
- **اِشْتَرَيْتُ كِيلُوغْرَامَ تَمْرٍ** (ishtaraytu kīlūghrāma tamrin) — I bought a kilogram of dates.
- **اِشْتَرَيْتُ كِيلُوغْرَامًا مِنَ التَّمْرِ** (ishtaraytu kīlūghrāman mina at-tamri) — I bought a kilogram of dates.
