# Specification (at-Tamyīz)

Arabic sometimes adds a clarifying noun after a number, measure, comparison, or vague statement. This noun answers “in what respect?” or “of what?”

## Core idea

The **التَّمْيِيزُ** (at-tamyīz) — specification is usually an indefinite noun that clarifies an amount, measure, comparison, or ambiguity. It is often accusative in formal MSA.

## Forms and examples

| Trigger | Arabic | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| number | **عِنْدِي عِشْرُونَ كِتَابًا** (ʿindī ʿishrūna kitāban) | I have twenty books |
| measure | **اِشْتَرَيْتُ لِتْرًا حَلِيبًا** (ishtaraytu litran ḥalīban) | I bought a liter of milk |
| comparison | **هُوَ أَكْثَرُ مِنِّي صَبْرًا** (huwa aktharu minnī ṣabran) | he is more patient than I am |
| vague quality | **طَابَ الْمَكَانُ هَوَاءً** (ṭāba al-makānu hawāʾan) | the place is pleasant in air |

## Usage notes

A common tamyīz is singular even when English uses a plural: **ثَلَاثُونَ طَالِبًا** (thalāthūna ṭāliban) — thirty students. The number is plural in meaning, but the specification is singular accusative.

## Practice patterns

Tamyīz can make comparisons precise:

- **أَنْتَ أَسْرَعُ مِنِّي قِرَاءَةً** (anta asraʿu minnī qirāʾatan) — you are faster than me in reading.
- **هِيَ أَكْثَرُ خِبْرَةً** (hiya aktharu khibratan) — she is more experienced.

Ask: “More in what respect?” The answer may be **التَّمْيِيزُ** (at-tamyīz) — specification.
