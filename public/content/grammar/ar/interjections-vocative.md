# Interjections & the Vocative in Depth (yā, ayyuhā, wā)

Calling to someone is a small structure with a lot of grammar behind it. MSA uses special particles and special case behavior for the noun being addressed.

## Core idea

**النِّدَاءُ** (an-nidāʾ) — vocative is direct address. The most common particle is **يَا** (yā) — O. The noun addressed is **الْمُنَادَى** (al-munādā) — the called noun. Interjections such as **وَا** (wā) — alas / O for lament can add emotion.

## Forms and examples

| Pattern | Arabic | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| proper name | **يَا زَيْدُ** (yā zaydu) | O Zayd |
| iḍāfa | **يَا طَالِبَ الْعِلْمِ** (yā ṭāliba al-ʿilmi) | O seeker of knowledge |
| definite common noun | **يَا أَيُّهَا الْقَارِئُ** (yā ayyuhā al-qāriʾu) | O reader |
| lament | **وَا أَسَفَاهُ** (wā asafāhu) | alas, what sorrow |

## Case of the called noun

A single definite person or intended indefinite noun is usually built on ḍamma in the place of the accusative: **يَا مُحَمَّدُ** (yā muḥammadu) — O Muḥammad, and **يَا رَجُلُ** (yā rajulu) — O man, when a specific man is meant. A possessed or iḍāfa vocative is accusative: **يَا صَاحِبَ الْبَيْتِ** (yā ṣāḥiba al-bayti) — O owner of the house.

## Ayyuhā, ayyatuhā, and emotional calls

For a definite noun with **الـ** (al-) — the article, MSA usually inserts **أَيُّهَا** (ayyuhā) — O you masculine or **أَيَّتُهَا** (ayyatuhā) — O you feminine. The following noun is usually nominative as a descriptive word: **يَا أَيَّتُهَا الطَّالِبَةُ** (yā ayyatuhā aṭ-ṭālibatu) — O female student.

The particle **وَا** (wā) — O / alas is common in lament or emotional exclamation: **وَا حَسْرَتَاهُ** (wā ḥasratāhu) — alas, my regret.
