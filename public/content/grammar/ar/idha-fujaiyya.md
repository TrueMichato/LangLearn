# Idhā al-Fujāʾiyya in Narrative

The word **إِذَا** (idhā) often means “when” or “if.” In narrative, **إِذَا الْفُجَائِيَّةُ** (idhā al-fujāʾiyyatu) creates a sudden scene: “and suddenly,” “and behold.”

## Core idea

Sudden **إِذَا** (idhā) usually comes after another clause, often with **فَـ** (fa-), and is followed by a nominal sentence. It does not introduce a normal condition and does not need a conditional answer.

## Forms and examples

| Type of **إِذَا** (idhā) | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal or conditional | **إِذَا** (idhā) plus verb clause | **إِذَا دَرَسْتَ نَجَحْتَ** (idhā darasta najaḥta) — when you study, you succeed |
| Sudden narrative | **فَإِذَا** (fa-idhā) plus nominal sentence | **خَرَجْتُ فَإِذَا الْمَطَرُ نَازِلٌ** (kharajtu fa-idhā al-maṭaru nāzilun) — I went out, and suddenly the rain was falling |
| Sudden discovery | action plus **فَإِذَا** (fa-idhā) | **فَتَحْتُ الْبَابَ فَإِذَا صَدِيقِي وَاقِفٌ** (fataḥtu al-bāba fa-idhā ṣadīqī wāqifun) — I opened the door, and there was my friend standing |

## Usage notes

Do not confuse sudden **إِذَا** (idhā) with conditional **إِذَا** (idhā). In **إِذَا دَرَسْتَ نَجَحْتَ** (idhā darasta najaḥta) — when you study, you succeed — the first clause sets a condition. In **نَظَرْتُ فَإِذَا السَّيَّارَةُ قَرِيبَةٌ** (naẓartu fa-idhā as-sayyāratu qarībatun) — I looked, and suddenly the car was near — it reveals an unexpected state.

## Practice patterns

Look for a completed action followed by a surprising nominal sentence:

- **دَخَلْتُ الْقَاعَةَ فَإِذَا الْمَقَاعِدُ مُمْتَلِئَةٌ** (dakhaltu al-qāʿata fa-idhā al-maqāʿidu mumtaliʾatun) — I entered the hall, and suddenly the seats were full.
- **رَفَعْتُ رَأْسِي فَإِذَا الْهِلالُ ظَاهِرٌ** (rafaʿtu raʾsī fa-idhā al-hilālu ẓāhirun) — I raised my head, and there was the crescent visible.
- **بَحَثْتُ عَنِ الْمِفْتَاحِ فَإِذَا هُوَ فِي جَيْبِي** (baḥathtu ʿani al-miftāḥi fa-idhā huwa fī jaybī) — I looked for the key, and suddenly it was in my pocket.
