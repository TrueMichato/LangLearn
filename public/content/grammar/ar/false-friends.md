# False Friends between Arabic and English

A **صَدِيقٌ زَائِفٌ** (ṣadīqun zāʾifun) — false friend is a word or expression that tempts you toward the wrong meaning. Arabic and English false friends may be borrowed words, look-alikes in translation, or words with a narrower range than you expect.

## Core idea

Do not translate a familiar-looking word automatically. Check context, register, and the usual collocations. **فِعْلِيًّا** (fiʿliyyan) — actually / in practice is not the same as **حَالِيًّا** (ḥāliyyan) — currently, and **أَخِيرًا** (ʾakhīran) — finally is not the same as recently.

## Common traps

| Arabic | Safer meaning | Trap to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **أَخِيرًا** (ʾakhīran) | finally | not usually actually |
| **حَالِيًّا** (ḥāliyyan) | currently | not happily |
| **فِعْلِيًّا** (fiʿliyyan) | actually / in practice | not currently |
| **كُلِّيَّةٌ** (kulliyyatun) | college / faculty | not quality |
| **جَامِعَةٌ** (jāmiʿatun) | university | not the same as **جَامِعٌ** (jāmiʿun) — mosque |
| **مَجَلَّةٌ** (majallatun) | magazine / journal | not merely English magazine in every register |

## Usage notes

Arabic often distinguishes meanings that English compresses. **جَامِعَةٌ** (jāmiʿatun) — university and **جَامِعٌ** (jāmiʿun) — mosque share a root sense of gathering, but the tāʾ marbūṭa changes the common noun. Likewise, **مُنَاسَبَةٌ** (munāsabatun) — occasion / event may fit context better than a literal suitable-ness.

## Checking collocations

Look for nearby words:

- **كُلِّيَّةُ الطِّبِّ** (kulliyyatu ṭ-ṭibbi) — faculty of medicine.
- **جَامِعَةُ الْقَاهِرَةِ** (jāmiʿatu l-qāhirati) — Cairo University.
- **جَامِعُ الْمَدِينَةِ** (jāmiʿu l-madīnati) — the city's mosque.
- **مُنَاسَبَةٌ سَعِيدَةٌ** (munāsabatun saʿīdatun) — a happy occasion.
