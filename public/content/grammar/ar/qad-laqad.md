# Qad and Laqad (certainty, possibility, recentness)

**قَدْ** (qad) is a small particle with a large effect. With a past verb it often marks certainty, completion, or “already.” With a present verb it often means “may,” “might,” or “sometimes.”

## Core idea

The tense after **قَدْ** matters. **قَدْ وَصَلَ الْقِطَارُ** (qad waṣala al-qiṭāru) means the train has arrived or indeed arrived. **قَدْ يَتَأَخَّرُ الْقِطَارُ** (qad yataʾakhkharu al-qiṭāru) means the train may be delayed.

## Forms and examples

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **قَدْ** + past | **قَدْ فَهِمْتُ الدَّرْسَ** (qad fahimtu ad-darsa) | I have understood the lesson |
| **لَقَدْ** + past | **لَقَدْ نَجَحَ الطَّالِبُ** (laqad najaḥa aṭ-ṭālibu) | the student certainly succeeded |
| **قَدْ** + present | **قَدْ يَنْفَعُ الدَّوَاءُ** (qad yanfaʿu ad-dawāʾu) | the medicine may help |
| Habitual possibility | **قَدْ يَغْفُلُ الْإِنْسَانُ** (qad yaghfulu al-insānu) | a person may sometimes become heedless |

## Usage notes

**لَقَدْ** (laqad) strengthens a past-tense statement and is common in formal MSA. With the imperfect, **قَدْ** does not normally mean “already”; it weakens or generalizes the statement. Compare **قَدْ كَتَبْتُ الرِّسَالَةَ** (qad katabtu ar-risālata) — I have written the letter, with **قَدْ أَكْتُبُ الرِّسَالَةَ غَدًا** (qad aktubu ar-risālata ghadan) — I may write the letter tomorrow.

## Practice patterns

Build contrast by changing only the verb tense:

- **قَدْ حَضَرَ الْمُدِيرُ** (qad ḥaḍara al-mudīru) — the manager has arrived.
- **قَدْ يَحْضُرُ الْمُدِيرُ** (qad yaḥḍuru al-mudīru) — the manager may attend.
- **لَقَدْ سَمِعْنَا الْخَبَرَ** (laqad samiʿnā al-khabara) — we certainly heard the news.
