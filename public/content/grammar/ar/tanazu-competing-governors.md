# Tanāzuʿ: Competing Verb Governors

Tanāzuʿ is an advanced classical-MSA analysis for a sentence where two nearby verbs both seem to need the same noun. It explains why one noun can complete two actions.

## Core idea

In **التَّنَازُعُ** (at-tanāzuʿu), two operators compete for one later noun. Usually the nearer verb governs the visible noun, while the other verb is understood with an implied pronoun or omitted complement.

## Forms and examples

| Shared role | Example | How to read it |
|---|---|---|
| Shared subject | **حَضَرَ وَتَكَلَّمَ الْمُدِيرُ** (ḥaḍara wa-takallama al-mudīru) | The manager came and spoke. |
| Shared object | **قَرَأْتُ وَفَهِمْتُ الدَّرْسَ** (qaraʾtu wa-fahimtu ad-darsa) | I read and understood the lesson. |
| Shared destination | **ذَهَبْتُ وَجَلَسْتُ فِي الْمَكْتَبِ** (dhahabtu wa-jalastu fī al-maktabi) | I went and sat in the office. |
| Repeated for clarity | **قَرَأْتُ الدَّرْسَ وَفَهِمْتُهُ** (qaraʾtu ad-darsa wa-fahimtuhu) | I read the lesson and understood it. |

## Usage notes

Learners do not need to force tanāzuʿ in everyday writing. MSA often prefers the clearer pattern with a pronoun: **قَرَأْتُ الدَّرْسَ وَفَهِمْتُهُ** (qaraʾtu ad-darsa wa-fahimtuhu) — I read the lesson and understood it.

Tanāzuʿ matters when reading formal prose, grammar examples, or Qurʾānic/classical style. It helps you avoid thinking that a verb is incomplete just because its complement appears only once.

## Practice patterns

Turn a compact tanāzuʿ-style sentence into a clearer pronoun pattern:

- **سَمِعْتُ وَفَهِمْتُ السُّؤَالَ** (samiʿtu wa-fahimtu as-suʾāla) — I heard and understood the question.
- **سَمِعْتُ السُّؤَالَ وَفَهِمْتُهُ** (samiʿtu as-suʾāla wa-fahimtuhu) — I heard the question and understood it.
- **زَارَ وَشَكَرَ الضَّيْفُ الْمُعَلِّمَ** (zāra wa-shakara aḍ-ḍayfu al-muʿallima) — the guest visited and thanked the teacher.
