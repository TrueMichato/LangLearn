# How Ism al-Tafḍīl Governs Complements

The elative pattern **أَفْعَلُ** (afʿalu), called **اِسْمُ التَّفْضِيلِ** (ismu at-tafḍīli), can mean “more” or “most.” Its complement changes depending on whether you compare two things, name the group, or specify the quality.

## Core idea

For a direct comparison, use **مِنْ** (min): **زَيْدٌ أَطْوَلُ مِنْ عَلِيٍّ** (zaydun aṭwalu min ʿaliyyin) — Zayd is taller than Ali. For “the best of a group,” use an iḍāfa: **زَيْدٌ أَفْضَلُ الطُّلَّابِ** (zaydun afḍalu aṭ-ṭullābi) — Zayd is the best of the students.

## Complement patterns

| Use | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Comparative | **أَفْعَلُ مِنْ** plus genitive | **هَذَا أَسْهَلُ مِنْ ذَلِكَ** (hādhā ashalu min dhālika) — This is easier than that. |
| Superlative in a group | **أَفْعَلُ** in iḍāfa | **هِيَ أَسْرَعُ الْعَدَّائِينَ** (hiya asraʿu al-ʿaddāʾīna) — She is the fastest of the runners. |
| Specifying the quality | Accusative tamyīz | **هُوَ أَكْثَرُ عِلْمًا مِنِّي** (huwa aktharu ʿilman minnī) — He has more knowledge than I do. |
| Definite adjective | Agreement with the noun | **الْمَدِينَةُ الْكُبْرَى جَمِيلَةٌ** (al-madīnatu al-kubrā jamīlatun) — The largest city is beautiful. |

## Tamyīz after the elative

When the comparison needs a quality noun, Arabic often uses an accusative specification: **أَنْتَ أَحْسَنُ خُلُقًا مِنِّي** (anta aḥsanu khuluqan minnī) — You are better in character than I am. The tamyīz explains the respect in which the comparison is made.

## Practice patterns

The same form can shift meaning by construction. **هُوَ أَكْرَمُ مِنْ أَخِيهِ** (huwa akramu min akhīhi) — He is more generous than his brother. **هُوَ أَكْرَمُ الْإِخْوَةِ** (huwa akramu al-ikhwati) — He is the most generous of the brothers.
