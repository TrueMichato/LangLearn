# Apposition & Substitution (al-badal)

Arabic often places one noun after another to clarify, rename, or substitute for it. This second noun follows the first in case and lets the sentence become more precise without starting over.

## Core idea

**الْبَدَلُ** (al-badal) — apposition or substitution is a follower that replaces or clarifies the noun before it, called **الْمُبْدَلُ مِنْهُ** (al-mubdal minhu) — the word substituted from. The badal follows the earlier noun in case: nominative, accusative, or genitive.

## Forms and examples

| Case | Arabic | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| nominative | **جَاءَ أَخُوكَ زَيْدٌ** (jāʾa akhūka zaydun) | your brother Zayd came |
| accusative | **رَأَيْتُ أَخَاكَ زَيْدًا** (raʾaytu akhāka zaydan) | I saw your brother Zayd |
| genitive | **مَرَرْتُ بِأَخِيكَ زَيْدٍ** (marartu bi-akhīka zaydin) | I passed by your brother Zayd |

## Whole-for-whole badal

The simplest type is **بَدَلُ كُلٍّ مِنْ كُلٍّ** (badal kullin min kullin) — whole-for-whole substitution. The second noun identifies the first: **الْخَلِيفَةُ عُمَرُ** (al-khalīfatu ʿumaru) — the caliph ʿUmar. In a sentence, its ending follows the role of the first noun.

## Part and inclusion

Arabic also uses **بَدَلُ بَعْضٍ مِنْ كُلٍّ** (badal baʿḍin min kullin) — part-for-whole substitution and **بَدَلُ اشْتِمَالٍ** (badal ishtimālin) — inclusion or associated quality. These often include a pronoun referring back to the first noun.

- **قَرَأْتُ الْكِتَابَ نِصْفَهُ** (qaraʾtu al-kitāba niṣfahu) — I read the book, half of it.
- **أَعْجَبَنِي الْكِتَابُ أُسْلُوبُهُ** (aʿjabanī al-kitābu uslūbuhu) — the book pleased me, its style.
- **زُرْتُ الْمَدِينَةَ مَسْجِدَهَا** (zurtu al-madīnata masjidahā) — I visited the city, its mosque.
