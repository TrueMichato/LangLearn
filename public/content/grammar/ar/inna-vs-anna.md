# Inna vs Anna in Usage

Arabic has two closely related particles, **إِنَّ** (inna) and **أَنَّ** (anna). Both put their noun in the accusative, but they appear in different sentence environments.

## Core idea

Use **إِنَّ** (inna) to open or emphasize an independent statement. Use **أَنَّ** (anna) when the whole clause means “that...” after a verb, noun, or preposition.

## Forms and examples

| Particle | Usual job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **إِنَّ** (inna) — indeed, truly | Begins an independent clause or direct quotation | **إِنَّ الطَّالِبَ مُجْتَهِدٌ** (inna aṭ-ṭāliba mujtahidun) — indeed, the student is diligent |
| **أَنَّ** (anna) — that | Makes its clause a noun-like complement | **عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ الطَّالِبَ مُجْتَهِدٌ** (ʿalimtu anna aṭ-ṭāliba mujtahidun) — I knew that the student is diligent |
| **لِأَنَّ** (li-anna) — because | Follows the preposition **لِـ** (li-) | **بَقِيتُ لِأَنَّ الْبَابَ مُغْلَقٌ** (baqītu li-anna al-bāba mughlaqun) — I stayed because the door is closed |

## Usage notes

After a verb of saying, direct quotation normally starts with **إِنَّ** (inna): **قَالَ الْمُعَلِّمُ: إِنَّ الدَّرْسَ سَهْلٌ** (qāla al-muʿallimu: inna ad-darsa sahlun) — the teacher said, “The lesson is easy.” After a verb of knowing, believing, or hearing, **أَنَّ** (anna) is the normal choice: **أَعْرِفُ أَنَّ الدَّرْسَ سَهْلٌ** (aʿrifu anna ad-darsa sahlun) — I know that the lesson is easy.

## Practice patterns

Try reading the first particle as emphasis and the second as a complement marker:

- **إِنَّ الصِّدْقَ فَضِيلَةٌ** (inna aṣ-ṣidqa faḍīlatun) — honesty is truly a virtue.
- **أُؤْمِنُ بِأَنَّ الصِّدْقَ فَضِيلَةٌ** (uʾminu bi-anna aṣ-ṣidqa faḍīlatun) — I believe that honesty is a virtue.
- **سَمِعْتُ أَنَّ الْقِصَّةَ صَحِيحَةٌ** (samiʿtu anna al-qiṣṣata ṣaḥīḥatun) — I heard that the story is true.
