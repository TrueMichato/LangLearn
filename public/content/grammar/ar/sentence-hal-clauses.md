# Sentence Ḥāl Clauses

A ḥāl can be more than one accusative word. In polished MSA, an entire verbal or nominal clause can describe the state in which someone does an action.

## Core idea

A sentence ḥāl is called **جُمْلَةٌ حَالِيَّةٌ** (jumlatun ḥāliyyatun). It gives the circumstance of a definite participant, and it normally contains a clear link back to that participant: a pronoun, a matching verb subject, or **وَاوُ الْحَالِ** (wāwu al-ḥāli), the circumstantial wāw.

## Forms and examples

| Pattern | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Verbal clause without wāw | **جَاءَ الطَّالِبُ يَضْحَكُ** (jāʾa aṭ-ṭālibu yaḍḥaku) | The student came laughing. |
| Nominal clause with wāw | **دَخَلَتِ الْبِنْتُ وَالْكِتَابُ فِي يَدِهَا** (dakhalati al-bintu wa-l-kitābu fī yadihā) | The girl entered with the book in her hand. |
| Verbal clause with wāw and pronoun | **رَأَيْتُ الْمُدَرِّسَ وَهُوَ يَكْتُبُ** (raʾaytu al-mudarrisa wa-huwa yaktubu) | I saw the teacher while he was writing. |
| Past-state clause with qad | **رَجَعَ الْمُسَافِرُ وَقَدْ تَعِبَ** (rajaʿa al-musāfiru wa-qad taʿiba) | The traveler returned having become tired. |

## Usage notes

A single-word ḥāl is usually accusative, as in **جَاءَ الطَّالِبُ ضَاحِكًا** (jāʾa aṭ-ṭālibu ḍāḥikan) — the student came laughing. A sentence ḥāl does not receive one visible accusative ending on the whole sentence; instead, the entire clause fills the ḥāl function.

When the ḥāl clause is nominal, **وَاوُ الْحَالِ** is very common: **خَرَجَ الْوَلَدُ وَالشَّمْسُ طَالِعَةٌ** (kharaja al-waladu wa-sh-shamsu ṭāliʿatun) — the boy went out while the sun was rising.

## Practice patterns

Try changing a single-word ḥāl into a sentence ḥāl:

- **عَادَ الْجُنْدِيُّ مُتْعَبًا** (ʿāda al-jundiyyu mutʿaban) — the soldier returned tired.
- **عَادَ الْجُنْدِيُّ وَقَدْ تَعِبَ** (ʿāda al-jundiyyu wa-qad taʿiba) — the soldier returned having become tired.
- **سَمِعْتُ الطِّفْلَ بَاكِيًا** (samiʿtu aṭ-ṭifla bākiyan) — I heard the child crying.
- **سَمِعْتُ الطِّفْلَ وَهُوَ يَبْكِي** (samiʿtu aṭ-ṭifla wa-huwa yabkī) — I heard the child while he was crying.
