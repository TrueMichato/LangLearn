# Defective Verbs in Depth (mashā, daʿā, nasiya types)

Defective verbs have a weak final root letter. They are common, so it is worth learning their recurring shapes instead of memorizing each verb as a surprise.

## Core idea

اَلْفِعْلُ النَّاقِصُ (al-fiʿl an-nāqiṣ) — defective verb ends in a weak root letter, usually وَاوٌ (wāwun) — wāw or يَاءٌ (yāʾun) — yāʾ. The final weak sound changes across past, present, jussive, and imperative forms.

| Type | Past | Present | With “I” in past | Jussive after لَمْ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| مَشَى type | مَشَى (mashā) — he walked | يَمْشِي (yamshī) — he walks | مَشَيْتُ (mashaytu) — I walked | لَمْ يَمْشِ (lam yamshi) — he did not walk |
| دَعَا type | دَعَا (daʿā) — he called | يَدْعُو (yadʿū) — he calls | دَعَوْتُ (daʿawtu) — I called | لَمْ يَدْعُ (lam yadʿu) — he did not call |
| نَسِيَ type | نَسِيَ (nasiya) — he forgot | يَنْسَى (yansā) — he forgets | نَسِيتُ (nasītu) — I forgot | لَمْ يَنْسَ (lam yansa) — he did not forget |

## Past tense before suffixes

The long final ā in مَشَى (mashā) — he walked and دَعَا (daʿā) — he called changes when a suffix is added: مَشَيْتُ (mashaytu) — I walked, دَعَوْتُ (daʿawtu) — I called. نَسِيَ (nasiya) — he forgot keeps a clearer y-sound in نَسِيتُ (nasītu) — I forgot.

## Jussive and imperative forms

After لَمْ (lam) — did not, the present becomes jussive and the final long vowel is shortened or removed. The imperative is also short:

- اِمْشِ (imshi) — walk!
- اُدْعُ (udʿu) — call!
- اِنْسَ (insa) — forget!

> Weak-final verbs look irregular only if you expect the final vowel to stay still. Once you track the weak root, the changes become predictable.
