# The Elative & Colours/Defects (afʿal pattern)

Arabic uses the أَفْعَلُ (afʿalu) — elative pattern for many comparative and superlative meanings. The same visible pattern also appears in many color and defect adjectives, but they behave differently.

## Core idea

اِسْمُ التَّفْضِيلِ (ism at-tafḍīl) — the elative can mean “more” or “most,” depending on context. It often has the pattern أَفْعَلُ (afʿalu) — more / most.

| Use | Arabic | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Comparative | أَكْبَرُ مِنْ (akbaru min) — bigger than | with مِنْ (min) — than |
| Superlative with definite noun | اَلْبَيْتُ الْأَكْبَرُ (al-baytu al-akbaru) — the bigger / biggest house | adjective agrees in definiteness |
| Superlative in iḍāfa | أَكْبَرُ بَيْتٍ (akbaru baytin) — the biggest house | first word stays in elative form |
| Quality | أَفْضَلُ (afḍalu) — better / best | from فَضْلٌ (faḍlun) — excellence |

## Comparative and superlative

هَذَا الْكِتَابُ أَطْوَلُ مِنْ ذَلِكَ الْكِتَابِ (hādhā al-kitābu aṭwalu min dhālika al-kitābi) — this book is longer than that book. With a following indefinite noun, أَطْوَلُ كِتَابٍ (aṭwalu kitābin) — the longest book.

## Colors and defects

Color and defect adjectives often look like أَفْعَلُ (afʿalu) too: أَحْمَرُ (aḥmaru) — red masculine, حَمْرَاءُ (ḥamrāʾu) — red feminine; أَعْرَجُ (aʿraju) — lame masculine, عَرْجَاءُ (ʿarjāʾu) — lame feminine. They are descriptive adjectives, not ordinary comparatives. To say “redder,” Arabic commonly uses أَشَدُّ حُمْرَةً (ashaddu ḥumratan) — more intense in redness.

- أَسْوَدُ (aswadu) — black masculine; سَوْدَاءُ (sawdāʾu) — black feminine.
- أَبْيَضُ (abyaḍu) — white masculine; بَيْضَاءُ (bayḍāʾu) — white feminine.
- أَعْمَى (aʿmā) — blind masculine; عَمْيَاءُ (ʿamyāʾu) — blind feminine.

> Do not translate every أَفْعَلُ (afʿalu) shape as “more.” First ask whether the word is an elative or a color/defect adjective.
