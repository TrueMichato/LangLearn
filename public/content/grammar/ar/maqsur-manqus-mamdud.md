# Maqṣūr, Manqūṣ & Mamdūd Nouns

Some Arabic nouns have special endings that affect visible case marks. Three important classes are **الْمَقْصُورُ** (al-maqṣūru), **الْمَنْقُوصُ** (al-manqūṣu), and **الْمَمْدُودُ** (al-mamdūdu).

## Core idea

A maqṣūr noun ends in a long **ā** written **ى** or **ا**, so its case endings are usually estimated rather than pronounced. A manqūṣ noun ends in **ـِي** after kasra, and its yāʾ behaves differently in nominative, genitive, and accusative. A mamdūd noun ends in hamza after alif, and its case endings are usually visible.

## Forms and examples

| Type | Nominative | Accusative | Genitive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maqṣūr | **جَاءَ الْفَتَى** (jāʾa al-fatā) — the young man came | **رَأَيْتُ الْفَتَى** (raʾaytu al-fatā) — I saw the young man | **مَرَرْتُ بِالْفَتَى** (marartu bi-l-fatā) — I passed by the young man |
| Manqūṣ, definite | **جَاءَ الْقَاضِي** (jāʾa al-qāḍī) — the judge came | **رَأَيْتُ الْقَاضِيَ** (raʾaytu al-qāḍiya) — I saw the judge | **مَرَرْتُ بِالْقَاضِي** (marartu bi-l-qāḍī) — I passed by the judge |
| Manqūṣ, indefinite | **هٰذَا قَاضٍ عَادِلٌ** (hādhā qāḍin ʿādilun) — this is a just judge | **رَأَيْتُ قَاضِيًا عَادِلًا** (raʾaytu qāḍiyan ʿādilan) — I saw a just judge | **مَرَرْتُ بِقَاضٍ عَادِلٍ** (marartu bi-qāḍin ʿādilin) — I passed by a just judge |
| Mamdūd | **بَدَتْ سَمَاءٌ صَافِيَةٌ** (badat samāʾun ṣāfiyatun) — a clear sky appeared | **رَأَيْتُ سَمَاءً صَافِيَةً** (raʾaytu samāʾan ṣāfiyatan) — I saw a clear sky | **نَظَرْتُ إِلَى سَمَاءٍ صَافِيَةٍ** (naẓartu ilā samāʾin ṣāfiyatin) — I looked at a clear sky |

## Usage notes

For maqṣūr nouns, the ending is too heavy to show normal short vowels, so the case is understood from syntax: **الْفَتَى** (al-fatā) looks the same as subject, object, or after a preposition. For indefinite manqūṣ nouns, the yāʾ drops in nominative and genitive: **قَاضٍ** (qāḍin). It returns in the accusative: **قَاضِيًا** (qāḍiyan).

## Practice patterns

Use context to recover case:

- After a verb as subject: **جَاءَ الْقَاضِي** (jāʾa al-qāḍī) — the judge came.
- As direct object: **سَأَلْتُ الْقَاضِيَ** (saʾaltu al-qāḍiya) — I asked the judge.
- After a preposition: **تَحَدَّثْتُ مَعَ الْقَاضِي** (taḥaddathtu maʿa al-qāḍī) — I spoke with the judge.
