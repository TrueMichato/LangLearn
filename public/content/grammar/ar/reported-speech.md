# Reported Speech & Quoting (qāla inna…)

Arabic can report speech directly, with a quoted sentence, or indirectly, with a clause that depends on a verb such as قَالَ (qāla) — he said or أَخْبَرَ (akhbara) — he informed. A common pattern after قَالَ (qāla) is إِنَّ (inna) — indeed / that.

## Core idea

When a speaker quotes a full nominal sentence after قَالَ (qāla), MSA often begins the quoted content with إِنَّ (inna). إِنَّ (inna) makes its noun accusative and leaves its predicate nominative.

- قَالَ الْمُعَلِّمُ: إِنَّ الدَّرْسَ سَهْلٌ (qāla al-muʿallimu: inna ad-darsa sahlun) — the teacher said, The lesson is easy.
- قَالَتْ فَاطِمَةُ: إِنَّ الْقِطَارَ مُتَأَخِّرٌ (qālat Fāṭimatu: inna al-qiṭāra mutaʾakhkhirun) — Fatima said, The train is late.
- قَالَ الطَّبِيبُ: إِنَّ الرَّاحَةَ مُهِمَّةٌ (qāla aṭ-ṭabību: inna ar-rāḥata muhimmatun) — the doctor said, Rest is important.

## Direct quotation

Direct speech can be introduced with قَالَ (qāla) or قَالَتْ (qālat), followed by the exact words. Commands, questions, and prohibitions may remain in their original form.

- قَالَ لِي أَبِي: لَا تَتَأَخَّرْ (qāla lī abī: lā tataʾakhkhar) — my father said to me, Do not be late.
- سَأَلَتْ نُورٌ: هَلْ تَذْهَبُ إِلَى السُّوقِ؟ (saʾalat Nūrun: hal tadhhabu ilā as-sūqi?) — Noor asked, Are you going to the market?
- قَالَتِ الْمُدِيرَةُ: اِجْلِسُوا هُنَا (qālati al-mudīratu: ijlisū hunā) — the manager said, Sit here.

## Indirect reporting

For indirect reporting after verbs such as أَخْبَرَ (akhbara) — he informed or عَلِمَ (ʿalima) — he knew, Arabic often uses أَنَّ (anna) — that. With قَالَ (qāla), both direct quotation and reported clauses are common, but إِنَّ (inna) is especially frequent when the quoted words begin the report.

- أَخْبَرَنَا الْمُدِيرُ أَنَّ اللِّقَاءَ غَدًا (akhbaranā al-mudīru anna al-liqāʾa ghadan) — the manager informed us that the meeting is tomorrow.
- عَلِمْتُ أَنَّ السَّفَرَ طَوِيلٌ (ʿalimtu anna as-safara ṭawīlun) — I learned that the journey is long.
- قَالَتْ لَنَا زَيْنَبُ إِنَّهَا مَشْغُولَةٌ (qālat lanā Zaynabu innahā mashghūlatun) — Zaynab told us that she is busy.

## Practice patterns

Pay attention to who spoke and to whom. قَالَ لِـ (qāla li-) means said to, while قَالَ إِنَّ (qāla inna) introduces what was said.

- قُلْتُ لَهُ: إِنَّنِي جَاهِزٌ (qultu lahu: innanī jāhizun) — I said to him, I am ready.
- قَالُوا إِنَّهُمْ سَيَعُودُونَ قَرِيبًا (qālū innahum sayaʿūdūna qarīban) — they said that they will return soon.
- قَالَتْ لَهَا أُمُّهَا: اُكْتُبِي الرِّسَالَةَ (qālat lahā ummuhā: uktubī ar-risālata) — her mother said to her, Write the letter.
