# Adjectives & Agreement

Arabic adjectives usually come after the noun they describe. They agree with the noun in gender, number, definiteness, and case. That sounds like four things, but each one is visible in small endings.

## Core idea

An indefinite noun takes an indefinite adjective: **بَيْتٌ كَبِيرٌ** (baytun kabīrun) — a big house. A definite noun takes a definite adjective: **الْبَيْتُ الْكَبِيرُ** (al-baytu al-kabīru) — the big house.

## Forms and examples

| Noun + adjective | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **وَلَدٌ صَغِيرٌ** (waladun ṣaghīrun) — a small boy | masculine singular |
| **بِنْتٌ صَغِيرَةٌ** (bintun ṣaghīratun) — a small girl | feminine singular |
| **الطَّالِبُ الذَّكِيُّ** (aṭ-ṭālibu adh-dhakiyyu) — the smart male student | definite masculine |
| **الطَّالِبَةُ الذَّكِيَّةُ** (aṭ-ṭālibatu adh-dhakiyyatu) — the smart female student | definite feminine |

## Usage notes

A helpful contrast: **الْبَيْتُ الْكَبِيرُ** (al-baytu al-kabīru) — the big house is a phrase, while **الْبَيْتُ كَبِيرٌ** (al-baytu kabīrun) — the house is big is a sentence. The adjective’s definiteness makes the difference.

## Practice patterns

Non-human plurals often take feminine singular agreement in MSA:

- **كُتُبٌ جَدِيدَةٌ** (kutubun jadīdatun) — new books.
- **الْمُدُنُ الْكَبِيرَةُ** (al-mudunu al-kabīratu) — the big cities.

This is normal and very common.
