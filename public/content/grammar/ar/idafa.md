# The Iḍāfa Construct (Possession)

The iḍāfa is Arabic’s elegant possession structure. Instead of saying “of” as a separate word, Arabic places two nouns together: the possessed thing first, the owner or specifying noun second.

## Core idea

In an iḍāfa, the first noun cannot take **الْـ** (al-) — the and cannot take tanwīn. The second noun is genitive and often decides whether the whole phrase is definite or indefinite.

## Forms and examples

| Iḍāfa | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **كِتَابُ طَالِبٍ** (kitābu ṭālibin) — a student’s book / a book of a student | indefinite owner |
| **كِتَابُ الطَّالِبِ** (kitābu aṭ-ṭālibi) — the student’s book | definite owner |
| **بَابُ الْبَيْتِ** (bābu al-bayti) — the door of the house | second noun genitive |
| **مُدَرِّسُ اللُّغَةِ** (mudarrisu al-lughati) — the language teacher | specifying relationship |

## Usage notes

Think of the first noun as leaning on the second. Because it is leaning, it loses tanwīn: **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — a book becomes **كِتَابُ** (kitābu) — book of... inside the construct.

## Practice patterns

Adjectives come after the whole iḍāfa and usually describe the noun they agree with:

- **كِتَابُ الطَّالِبِ الْجَدِيدُ** (kitābu aṭ-ṭālibi al-jadīdu) — the student’s new book.
- **كِتَابُ الطَّالِبِ الْجَدِيدِ** (kitābu aṭ-ṭālibi al-jadīdi) — the new student’s book.

The case ending helps show which noun the adjective describes.
