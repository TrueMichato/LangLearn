# Conditional Sentences: the Answer Clause (jawāb ash-sharṭ) & fa-

A conditional sentence has a condition and an answer. In Arabic grammar, the answer is جَوَابُ الشَّرْطِ (jawābu ash-sharṭi): what happens if the condition is met.

## Simple condition and answer

With particles such as إِنْ (in) — if and مَنْ (man) — whoever, the condition and answer can both be jussive verbs.

- إِنْ تَدْرُسْ تَنْجَحْ (in tadrus tanjaḥ) — if you study, you succeed.
- مَنْ يَصْبِرْ يَفُزْ (man yaṣbir yafuz) — whoever is patient wins.
- إِنْ تَسْأَلْ أُجِبْكَ (in tasʾal ujibka) — if you ask, I will answer you.

## When fa- links the answer

Sometimes the answer clause cannot be a plain jussive verb. Then Arabic often uses فَـ (fa-) to tie the answer back to the condition. This is called فَاءُ رَبْطِ الجَوَابِ (fāʾu rabṭi al-jawābi) — the fa of linking the answer.

Use fa- especially when the answer is a nominal sentence, an imperative, a prohibition, a question, or a clause with particles such as سَـ (sa-), سَوْفَ (sawfa), قَدْ (qad), لَنْ (lan), or مَا (mā).

| Answer type | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| nominal | إِنْ تَدْرُسْ فَأَنْتَ نَاجِحٌ (in tadrus fa-anta nājiḥun) | if you study, then you are successful |
| command | إِنْ تَحْتَجْ فَاسْأَلْنِي (in taḥtaj fa-sʾalnī) | if you need help, ask me |
| future | مَنْ يَصْبِرْ فَسَوْفَ يَفُوزُ (man yaṣbir fa-sawfa yafūzu) | whoever is patient will win |

## Fa- is not the same as ordinary then

The fa- in a conditional answer is grammatical glue. It may also feel like then, but its main job is to connect a non-jussive answer to the condition. Compare إِنْ تَزُرْنِي أُكْرِمْكَ (in tazurnī ukrimka) — if you visit me, I will honor you, with إِنْ تَزُرْنِي فَسَأُكْرِمُكَ (in tazurnī fa-sa-ukrimuka) — if you visit me, then I will honor you. The future marker سَـ makes fa- natural.
