# Adverbs & the Ḥāl (circumstantial)

Arabic can describe how an action happens with adverbs and with the ḥāl, a circumstantial accusative that describes someone’s state during the action.

## Core idea

Many adverbial expressions are accusative: **سَرِيعًا** (sarīʿan) — quickly, **بَطِيئًا** (baṭīʾan) — slowly, **غَدًا** (ghadan) — tomorrow. The ḥāl is often an indefinite accusative adjective or participle describing the subject or object during the action.

## Forms and examples

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| adverb | **سَارَ بَطِيئًا** (sāra baṭīʾan) — he walked slowly |
| adverb | **قَرَأَتْ سَرِيعًا** (qaraʾat sarīʿan) — she read quickly |
| time adverb | **سَأَدْرُسُ غَدًا** (sa-adrusu ghadan) — I will study tomorrow |
| ḥāl | **دَخَلَ الطَّالِبُ مُبْتَسِمًا** (dakhala aṭ-ṭālibu mubtasiman) — the student entered smiling |
| ḥāl | **دَخَلَتِ الطَّالِبَةُ مُبْتَسِمَةً** (dakhalati aṭ-ṭālibatu mubtasimatan) — the female student entered smiling |

## Usage notes

A ḥāl answers “in what state?” The main action is entering; the ḥāl says the student was smiling during that action. It is usually indefinite and accusative.

## Practice patterns

More practice:

- **رَجَعَ الْوَلَدُ تَعِبًا** (rajaʿa al-waladu taʿiban) — the boy returned tired.
- **سَمِعْتُ الْخَبَرَ فَرِحًا** (samiʿtu al-khabara fariḥan) — I heard the news happy.
- **تَكَلَّمَتْ بِهُدُوءٍ** (takallamat bihudūʾin) — she spoke calmly / with calm.
