# Broken Plural Patterns

Many Arabic plurals are not made by adding a suffix. Instead, the inside of the word changes. These are called “broken” plurals because the singular pattern is reshaped.

## Core idea

The **جَمْعُ التَّكْسِيرِ** (jamʿ at-taksīr) — broken plural changes the internal vowel-and-consonant pattern of a noun. There are common patterns, but many plurals are best learned with the singular.

## Forms and examples

| Singular | Broken plural | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — book | **كُتُبٌ** (kutubun) — books | internal vowels change |
| **بَيْتٌ** (baytun) — house | **بُيُوتٌ** (buyūtun) — houses | long ū appears |
| **رَجُلٌ** (rajulun) — man | **رِجَالٌ** (rijālun) — men | new pattern |
| **قَلَمٌ** (qalamun) — pen | **أَقْلَامٌ** (aqlāmun) — pens | initial أَـ (a-) |
| **مَدِينَةٌ** (madīnatun) — city | **مُدُنٌ** (mudunun) — cities | shorter plural |

## Usage notes

Non-human broken plurals often take feminine singular agreement. Arabic says **الْكُتُبُ جَدِيدَةٌ** (al-kutubu jadīdatun) — the books are new, using **جَدِيدَةٌ** (jadīdatun) — feminine singular “new.”

## Practice patterns

A few common broken plural shapes appear again and again:

- **أَفْعَالٌ** (afʿālun) — a common plural shape, as in **أَقْلَامٌ** (aqlāmun) — pens.
- **فُعُولٌ** (fuʿūlun) — a common plural shape, as in **بُيُوتٌ** (buyūtun) — houses.
- **فِعَالٌ** (fiʿālun) — a common plural shape, as in **رِجَالٌ** (rijālun) — men.
