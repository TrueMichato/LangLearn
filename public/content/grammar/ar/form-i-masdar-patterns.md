# Form I Maṣdar Patterns

<!-- vocab-inventory: {"mode":"explicit"} -->

The Form I verbal noun, or **الْمَصْدَرُ** (al-maṣdaru), names the action of a basic verb. Unlike derived forms, Form I has several common patterns, so learners should recognize patterns and check dictionaries.

## Core idea

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"masdar-object","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Core idea","rule":"An action noun can be an accusative object and a construct head.","example":"“I love reading books”: أُحِبُّ ___ الْكُتُبِ. (قِرَاءَةٌ)","answer":"قِرَاءَةَ","explanation":"The maṣdar is accusative as the object and loses tanwīn before its genitive complement."} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-qiraah","word":"قِرَاءَةَ","reading":"qirāʾata","meaning":"reading; accusative construct form here","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Core idea"} -->

A maṣdar behaves like a noun: it can be the subject, object, or part of an iḍāfa. In **أُحِبُّ قِرَاءَةَ الْكُتُبِ** (uḥibbu qirāʾata al-kutubi) — “I love reading books,” **قِرَاءَةَ** (qirāʾata) is the maṣdar of **قَرَأَ** (qaraʾa).

## Usage notes

<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-khuruj","word":"خُرُوجٌ","reading":"khurūjun","meaning":"exit; going out","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Usage notes"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-fath","word":"فَتْحٌ","reading":"fatḥun","meaning":"opening; the act of opening","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Usage notes"} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"not-productive-formula","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Usage notes","rule":"Form I maṣdar patterns are tendencies, not a fully productive prediction rule.","example":"Can you derive every motion verb's Form I maṣdar by applying فُعُولٌ?","answer":"No. It is a useful tendency, but the actual maṣdar must be learned or checked for each verb.","explanation":"Compare دُخُولٌ, كِتَابَةٌ and فَهْمٌ; their different shapes are lexical, not freely interchangeable."} -->

Form I maṣdars are partly lexical. Many verbs of motion use **فُعُولٌ** (fuʿūlun), such as **دُخُولٌ** (dukhūlun) and **خُرُوجٌ** (khurūjun). Many activity verbs use **فِعَالَةٌ** (fiʿālatun), such as **كِتَابَةٌ** (kitābatun) and **دِرَاسَةٌ** (dirāsatun). Short maṣdars like **فَهْمٌ** (fahmun) and **فَتْحٌ** (fatḥun) are also common.

## Practice patterns

Read the maṣdar as “the act of doing” the verb:

- **أَحْتَاجُ إِلَى فَهْمِ الْقَاعِدَةِ** (aḥtāju ilā fahmi al-qāʿidati) — I need to understand the rule.
- **قِرَاءَةُ الصُّحُفِ مُفِيدَةٌ** (qirāʾatu aṣ-ṣuḥufi mufīdatun) — reading newspapers is useful.
- **يَسْهُلُ حِفْظُ الْأَمْثِلَةِ** (yashulu ḥifẓu al-amthilati) — memorizing examples becomes easy.
