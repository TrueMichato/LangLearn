# Attached (Possessive & Object) Pronouns

Arabic pronouns can attach to nouns, prepositions, and verbs. The same small endings often mean “my,” “your,” “his,” or “him,” depending on what they attach to.

## Core idea

Attached pronouns are suffixes. On nouns, they usually show possession: **كِتَابِي** (kitābī) — my book. On verbs, they can be objects: **رَأَيْتُهُ** (raʾaytuhu) — I saw him / it. On prepositions, they complete the phrase: **لَهَا** (lahā) — to her / for her.

## Forms and examples

| Base | With suffix | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — book | **كِتَابِي** (kitābī) — my book | **ـِي** (-ī) — my |
| **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — book | **كِتَابُكَ** (kitābuka) — your book, masculine | **ـكَ** (-ka) — your |
| **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — book | **كِتَابُهُ** (kitābuhu) — his book | **ـهُ** (-hu) — his / him |
| **مَعَ** (maʿa) — with | **مَعَهُمْ** (maʿahum) — with them | attached to a preposition |

## Usage notes

When a noun takes an attached possessive suffix, it becomes definite in meaning. **كِتَابِي** (kitābī) — my book is already specific, so it does not take **الْـ** (al-) — the.

## Practice patterns

Object examples:

- **رَأَيْتُهُ** (raʾaytuhu) — I saw him / it.
- **سَأَلْتُهَا** (saʾaltuhā) — I asked her.
- **نَعْرِفُكُمْ** (naʿrifukum) — we know you all.

Treat the suffix as a little pronoun glued to the end.
