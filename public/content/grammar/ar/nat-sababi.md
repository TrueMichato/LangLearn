# Naʿt Sababī: Adjectives Linked to a Related Noun

Most adjectives directly describe the noun before them. Naʿt sababī is different: the adjective describes a related noun that comes after it, often with a possessive pronoun.

## Core idea

**النَّعْتُ السَّبَبِيُّ** (an-naʿtu as-sababiyyu) agrees with the first noun in case and definiteness, but its gender follows the related noun after it. The adjective normally stays singular.

## Forms and examples

| First noun | Related noun | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Masculine, nominative | Masculine | **جَاءَ الرَّجُلُ الْكَرِيمُ أَبُوهُ** (jāʾa ar-rajulu al-karīmu abūhu) | The man whose father is generous came. |
| Feminine, accusative | Masculine | **رَأَيْتُ الْمَرْأَةَ الْكَرِيمَ أَبُوهَا** (raʾaytu al-marʾata al-karīma abūhā) | I saw the woman whose father is generous. |
| Masculine, accusative | Feminine | **رَأَيْتُ الرَّجُلَ الْجَمِيلَةَ بِنْتُهُ** (raʾaytu ar-rajula al-jamīlata bintuhu) | I saw the man whose daughter is beautiful. |
| Indefinite, genitive | Masculine | **مَرَرْتُ بِطَالِبٍ حَسَنٍ خُلُقُهُ** (marartu bi-ṭālibin ḥasanin khuluquhu) | I passed by a student whose character is good. |

## Usage notes

Compare the direct adjective **رَأَيْتُ الرَّجُلَ الْكَرِيمَ** (raʾaytu ar-rajula al-karīma) — I saw the generous man — with the sababī adjective **رَأَيْتُ الرَّجُلَ الْكَرِيمَ أَبُوهُ** (raʾaytu ar-rajula al-karīma abūhu) — I saw the man whose father is generous.

The related noun after the adjective is often nominative because it behaves like the doer or bearer of the adjective.

## Practice patterns

Build the pattern in three steps:

- Choose the owner: **الرَّجُلُ** (ar-rajulu) — the man.
- Choose the related noun: **بِنْتُهُ** (bintuhu) — his daughter.
- Match the adjective to both sides: **الرَّجُلُ الْجَمِيلَةُ بِنْتُهُ** (ar-rajulu al-jamīlatu bintuhu) — the man whose daughter is beautiful.
