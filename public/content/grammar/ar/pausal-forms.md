# Pausal Forms and Case Endings

When Arabic is read aloud, the end of a phrase often changes at a pause. This is called waqf, and it explains why fully written case endings are not always pronounced.

## Core idea

**الْوَقْفُ** (al-waqfu) means stopping. In pause, short final vowels and most nunation are dropped in pronunciation. When continuing, the normal case ending returns.

## Forms and examples

| Connected reading | Pausal reading | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| **هٰذَا كِتَابٌ جَدِيدٌ** (hādhā kitābun jadīdun) | **هٰذَا كِتَابْ جَدِيدْ** (hādhā kitāb jadīd) | The final ḍammah and nunation are not pronounced. |
| **قَرَأْتُ كِتَابًا** (qaraʾtu kitāban) | **قَرَأْتُ كِتَابَا** (qaraʾtu kitābā) | Accusative tanwīn is paused as long ā. |
| **هٰذِهِ مَدْرَسَةٌ** (hādhihi madrasatun) | **هٰذِهِ مَدْرَسَهْ** (hādhihi madrasah) | Tāʾ marbūṭa is pronounced h in pause. |
| **فِي الْبَيْتِ** (fī al-bayti) | **فِي الْبَيْتْ** (fī al-bayt) | Final kasrah drops at the stop. |

## Usage notes

Waqf is about pronunciation, not meaning. A text may still print **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun), but a reader may stop with **كِتَابْ** (kitāb). This is why learners hear fewer case endings in recitation, speeches, and careful sentence-by-sentence reading.

Do not delete case endings when analyzing grammar. The underlying case is still nominative, accusative, or genitive even if the pausal sound is silent.

## Practice patterns

Read each phrase twice, once connected and once paused:

- **جَاءَ طَالِبٌ** (jāʾa ṭālibun) → **جَاءَ طَالِبْ** (jāʾa ṭālib) — a student came.
- **رَأَيْتُ طَالِبًا** (raʾaytu ṭāliban) → **رَأَيْتُ طَالِبَا** (raʾaytu ṭālibā) — I saw a student.
- **مَرَرْتُ بِطَالِبٍ** (marartu bi-ṭālibin) → **مَرَرْتُ بِطَالِبْ** (marartu bi-ṭālib) — I passed by a student.
