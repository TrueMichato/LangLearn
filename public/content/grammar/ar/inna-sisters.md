# inna & Her Sisters

Inna and her sisters add emphasis, contrast, hope, or wish to nominal sentences. They change the first noun after them into the accusative, while the predicate stays nominative.

## Core idea

The basic sentence **الْبَيْتُ كَبِيرٌ** (al-baytu kabīrun) — the house is big becomes **إِنَّ الْبَيْتَ كَبِيرٌ** (inna al-bayta kabīrun) — indeed, the house is big.

## Forms and examples

| Sister | Example |
|---|---|
| **إِنَّ** (inna) — indeed / surely | **إِنَّ الطَّالِبَ ذَكِيٌّ** (inna aṭ-ṭāliba dhakiyyun) — indeed, the student is smart |
| **أَنَّ** (anna) — that | **أَعْلَمُ أَنَّ الدَّرْسَ مُهِمٌّ** (aʿlamu anna ad-darsa muhimmun) — I know that the lesson is important |
| **لٰكِنَّ** (lākinna) — but / however | **لٰكِنَّ الْوَقْتَ قَصِيرٌ** (lākinna al-waqta qaṣīrun) — but the time is short |
| **لَعَلَّ** (laʿalla) — perhaps / hopefully | **لَعَلَّ الْجَوَّ جَمِيلٌ** (laʿalla al-jawwa jamīlun) — hopefully the weather is beautiful |
| **لَيْتَ** (layta) — would that / if only | **لَيْتَ السَّفَرَ سَهْلٌ** (layta as-safara sahlun) — if only travel were easy |

## Usage notes

In fully formal grammar, these particles are “inna and her sisters.” Their key pattern is: particle + accusative subject + nominative predicate.

## Practice patterns

Compare with kāna:

- **كَانَ الْبَيْتُ كَبِيرًا** (kāna al-baytu kabīran) — the house was big: predicate accusative.
- **إِنَّ الْبَيْتَ كَبِيرٌ** (inna al-bayta kabīrun) — indeed, the house is big: subject accusative.
