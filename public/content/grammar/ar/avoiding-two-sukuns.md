# Avoiding Two Sukūns

Arabic connected speech avoids pronouncing two vowelless consonants in a row. A small helping vowel often appears, especially when a sukūn-final word meets hamzat al-waṣl.

## Core idea

The issue is called **اِلْتِقَاءُ السَّاكِنَيْنِ** (iltiqāʾu as-sākinayni), the meeting of two sukūns. In many common cases, Arabic adds a kasrah to the first word: **قُلِ الْحَقَّ** (quli al-ḥaqqa), not a heavy pause between **قُلْ** and **الْحَقَّ**.

## Forms and examples

| Before connection | Connected form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **قُلْ + الْحَقَّ** (qul + al-ḥaqqa) | **قُلِ الْحَقَّ** (quli al-ḥaqqa) | Say the truth. |
| **مِنْ + الْبَيْتِ** (min + al-bayti) | **مِنَ الْبَيْتِ** (mina al-bayti) | From the house. |
| **عَنْ + الْمَدِينَةِ** (ʿan + al-madīnati) | **عَنِ الْمَدِينَةِ** (ʿani al-madīnati) | About the city. |
| **لَمْ يَكُنْ + الرَّجُلُ** (lam yakun + ar-rajulu) | **لَمْ يَكُنِ الرَّجُلُ** (lam yakuni ar-rajulu) | The man was not. |

## Usage notes

The helping vowel is usually not part of the dictionary form. The command is still **قُلْ** (qul) when alone, but before **الْـ** in connected reading it becomes **قُلِ الْـ** (quli al-). Some particles have fixed common connected forms, such as **مِنَ الْبَيْتِ** (mina al-bayti).

This topic connects pronunciation and grammar: you need to know where words begin, but you pronounce them smoothly as one phrase.

## Practice patterns

Read each pair first separately, then connected:

- **مَنْ + الطَّالِبُ** (man + aṭ-ṭālibu) → **مَنِ الطَّالِبُ؟** (mani aṭ-ṭālibu?) — Who is the student?
- **خُذْ + الْقَلَمَ** (khudh + al-qalama) → **خُذِ الْقَلَمَ** (khudhi al-qalama) — Take the pen.
- **لَمْ يَكُنْ + الْأَمْرُ** (lam yakun + al-amru) → **لَمْ يَكُنِ الْأَمْرُ** (lam yakuni al-amru) — The matter was not.
