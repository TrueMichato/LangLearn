# Kam: Question or Exclamation

The word **كَمْ** (kam) can ask “how many?” or exclaim “how many!” The spelling is the same, but the meaning, punctuation, and case of the following specification often reveal the difference.

## Core idea

Interrogative **كَمْ** (kam) asks for a number: **كَمْ كِتَابًا قَرَأْتَ؟** (kam kitāban qaraʾta?) — How many books did you read? Declarative or exclamatory **كَمْ** (kam) means “many”: **كَمْ كِتَابٍ قَرَأْتُ!** (kam kitābin qaraʾtu!) — How many books I have read!

## Two types of kam

| Type | Meaning | Typical tamyīz | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **كَمْ الِاسْتِفْهَامِيَّةُ** (kam al-istifhāmiyyatu) | A real question | Singular accusative | **كَمْ دَرْسًا حَفِظْتَ؟** (kam darsan ḥafiẓta?) — How many lessons did you memorize? |
| **كَمْ الْخَبَرِيَّةُ** (kam al-khabariyyatu) | Many, so many | Usually genitive | **كَمْ دَرْسٍ حَفِظْتُ!** (kam darsin ḥafiẓtu!) — So many lessons I memorized! |
| **كَمْ مِنْ** (kam min) | Emphatic many | Genitive after **مِنْ** | **كَمْ مِنْ طَالِبٍ نَجَحَ!** (kam min ṭālibin najaḥa!) — So many students succeeded! |

## Reading context

A question mark usually points to interrogative **كَمْ** (kam), while an exclamation or rhetorical tone often points to **كَمْ الْخَبَرِيَّةُ** (kam al-khabariyyatu). In unvowelled texts, context matters: **كَمْ مَرَّةً سَافَرْتَ؟** (kam marratan sāfarta?) — How many times did you travel?

## Practice patterns

Declarative **كَمْ** (kam) praises abundance or intensity: **كَمْ عَالِمٍ خَدَمَ اللُّغَةَ!** (kam ʿālimin khadama al-lughata!) — Many a scholar served the language! It can also be softened with **مِنْ**: **كَمْ مِنْ صَدِيقٍ سَاعَدَنِي!** (kam min ṣadīqin sāʿadanī!) — So many friends helped me!
