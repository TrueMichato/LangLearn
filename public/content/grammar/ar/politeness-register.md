# Politeness, Register & Forms of Address

Arabic politeness is built from titles, indirect requests, and register choice. **سِجِلٌّ** (sijillun) — register means the level of formality you choose. A sentence can be grammatically correct but socially too blunt.

## Core idea

Formal MSA often softens address with **حَضْرَتُكَ** (ḥaḍratuka) — your presence, masculine polite you, **حَضْرَتُكِ** (ḥaḍratuki) — your presence, feminine polite you, **سَيِّدٌ** (sayyidun) — Mr. / sir, **سَيِّدَةٌ** (sayyidatun) — Ms. / madam, and titles such as **أُسْتَاذٌ** (ʾustādhun) — professor / teacher or **دُكْتُورٌ** (duktūrun) — doctor.

## Request formulas

| Function | Phrase | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Please, masculine | **مِنْ فَضْلِكَ** (min faḍlika) — please | neutral polite request |
| Please, feminine | **مِنْ فَضْلِكِ** (min faḍliki) — please | addressed to one woman |
| If you permit | **لَوْ سَمَحْتَ** (law samaḥta) — if you please | polite attention-getter to a man |
| Thank you very much | **شُكْرًا جَزِيلًا** (shukran jazīlan) — many thanks | formal and warm |
| Excuse me | **عُذْرًا** (ʿudhran) — excuse me | apology or interruption |

## Usage notes

**حَضْرَتُكَ** (ḥaḍratuka) — your presence can replace or accompany second-person address in formal service contexts: **هَلْ تُرِيدُ حَضْرَتُكَ قَهْوَةً؟** (hal turīdu ḥaḍratuka qahwatan?) — would you like coffee, sir? Titles come before names: **السَّيِّدُ أَحْمَدُ** (as-sayyidu ʾaḥmadu) — Mr. Ahmad, **الدُّكْتُورَةُ لَيْلَى** (ad-duktūratu laylā) — Dr. Layla.

## Register choices

| More formal | Less formal | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **أُرِيدُ** (ʾurīdu) — I want | **بَدِّي** (baddī) — I want, Levantine | MSA vs dialect |
| **هَلْ يُمْكِنُكَ؟** (hal yumkinuka?) — can you? | **مُمْكِن؟** (mumkin?) — possible? | indirectness softens |
| **تَفَضَّلْ** (tafaḍḍal) — please go ahead, masculine | **خُذْ** (khudh) — take | invitation vs direct command |

> In formal writing, choose MSA titles and complete phrases. In friendly speech, dialect forms may be warmer.
