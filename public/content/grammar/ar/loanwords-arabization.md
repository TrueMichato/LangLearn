# Loanwords & Arabization (Taʿrīb)

**تَعْرِيبٌ** (taʿrībun) — Arabization is the process of adapting a foreign word to Arabic sounds, spelling, and sometimes patterns. A borrowed word is often called **كَلِمَةٌ دَخِيلَةٌ** (kalimatun dakhīlatun) — loanword.

## Core idea

MSA accepts many international words, but Arabic reshapes them. Sounds may be substituted, vowels may be inserted, and the word may receive Arabic endings: **بَنْكٌ** (bankun) — bank, **فِلْمٌ** (filmun) — film, **تِلِفِزْيُونٌ** (tilifizyūnun) — television, **إِنْتَرْنِتٌ** (ʾinternetun) — internet.

## Adaptation patterns

| Feature | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Missing p sound | often written/pronounced with **ب** (bāʾun) — b | **بَاكِسْتَانُ** (bākistānu) — Pakistan |
| Foreign v sound | often becomes **ف** (fāʾun) — f | **فِيدْيُو** (fīdyū) — video |
| Clusters | Arabic may insert helping vowels | **تِلِفِزْيُونٌ** (tilifizyūnun) — television |
| Arabic derivation | borrowed base can take nisba | **كَنَدِيٌّ** (kanadiyyun) — Canadian |
| Native pluralization | some loans receive Arabic plurals | **فِلْمٌ** (filmun) — film → **أَفْلَامٌ** (ʾaflāmun) — films |

## Usage notes

Loanwords vary by country and register. A news article may prefer **هَاتِفٌ** (hātifun) — telephone, while everyday speech may say **تِلِفُونٌ** (tilifūnun) — telephone. Technical MSA may coin Arabic roots too: **حَاسُوبٌ** (ḥāsūbun) — computer, beside **كُمْبْيُوتَرٌ** (kumbyūtarun) — computer.

## Arabized families

Borrowed words can become productive:

- **دِيمُقْرَاطِيَّةٌ** (dīmuqrāṭiyyatun) — democracy.
- **دِيمُقْرَاطِيٌّ** (dīmuqrāṭiyyun) — democratic.
- **تِلِفِزْيُونٌ** (tilifizyūnun) — television.
- **تِلِفِزْيُونِيٌّ** (tilifizyūniyyun) — televisual / television-related.
