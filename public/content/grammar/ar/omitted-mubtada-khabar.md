# Omitted Mubtadaʾ or Khabar

Arabic often leaves out a nominal-sentence part when context makes it obvious. The hidden part may be the mubtadaʾ, the topic, or the khabar, the predicate.

## Core idea

A full nominal sentence has a topic and a predicate, as in **الْمُعَلِّمُ فِي الصَّفِّ** (al-muʿallimu fī aṣ-ṣaffi) — the teacher is in the classroom. In answers, signs, and concise style, one side can be understood rather than spoken.

## Forms and examples

| Context | Short answer | Full understanding | Hidden part |
|---|---|---|---|
| **أَيْنَ الْمُعَلِّمُ؟** (ayna al-muʿallimu?) — where is the teacher? | **فِي الصَّفِّ** (fī aṣ-ṣaffi) — in the classroom | **الْمُعَلِّمُ فِي الصَّفِّ** (al-muʿallimu fī aṣ-ṣaffi) — the teacher is in the classroom | mubtadaʾ |
| **مَنْ حَاضِرٌ؟** (man ḥāḍirun?) — who is present? | **زَيْدٌ** (zaydun) — Zayd | **زَيْدٌ حَاضِرٌ** (zaydun ḥāḍirun) — Zayd is present | khabar |
| **كَيْفَ الْجَوُّ؟** (kayfa al-jawwu?) — how is the weather? | **جَمِيلٌ** (jamīlun) — beautiful | **الْجَوُّ جَمِيلٌ** (al-jawwu jamīlun) — the weather is beautiful | mubtadaʾ |

## Usage notes

Omission works only when the listener can restore the missing piece. A bare phrase like **فِي الْمَكْتَبِ** (fī al-maktabi) — in the office — is clear after a question about location, but it may be confusing without context. In careful prose, learners can write the full sentence first and then notice when native texts shorten it.

## Practice patterns

Read each short answer as a complete nominal sentence in context:

- **مَنْ عِنْدَ الْبَابِ؟ خَالِدٌ** (man ʿinda al-bābi? khālidun) — who is at the door? Khalid.
- **أَيْنَ الْكِتَابُ؟ عَلَى الطَّاوِلَةِ** (ayna al-kitābu? ʿalā aṭ-ṭāwilati) — where is the book? On the table.
- **كَيْفَ الطَّعَامُ؟ لَذِيذٌ** (kayfa aṭ-ṭaʿāmu? ladhīdhun) — how is the food? Delicious.
