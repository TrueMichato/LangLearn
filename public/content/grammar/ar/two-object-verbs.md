# Verbs with Two Objects

Some Arabic verbs naturally take **two direct objects**: a person who receives or learns something, and the thing received or learned. Both objects are in the accusative, even when English uses “to” or “for.”

## Core idea

In **أَعْطَى الْمُعَلِّمُ الطَّالِبَ قَلَمًا** (aʿṭā al-muʿallimu aṭ-ṭāliba qalaman) — “the teacher gave the student a pen,” **الطَّالِبَ** (aṭ-ṭāliba) and **قَلَمًا** (qalaman) are both objects. The first object is usually the recipient; the second is the item, information, or quality transferred.

## Forms and examples

| Verb type | Verb | First object | Second object | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| giving | **أَعْطَى** (aʿṭā) | recipient | thing given | **أَعْطَى الْأَبُ اِبْنَهُ كِتَابًا** (aʿṭā al-abu ibnahu kitāban) — the father gave his son a book |
| granting | **مَنَحَ** (manaḥa) | recipient | grant/quality | **مَنَحَتِ الْجَامِعَةُ الطَّالِبَ مِنْحَةً** (manaḥati al-jāmiʿatu aṭ-ṭāliba minḥatan) — the university granted the student a scholarship |
| teaching | **عَلَّمَ** (ʿallama) | learner | lesson | **عَلَّمَتِ الْمُدَرِّسَةُ الطِّفْلَ الْحُرُوفَ** (ʿallamati al-mudarrisatu aṭ-ṭifla al-ḥurūfa) — the teacher taught the child the letters |
| clothing | **كَسَا** (kasā) | person | clothing | **كَسَا الرَّجُلُ الْفَقِيرَ ثَوْبًا** (kasā ar-rajulu al-faqīra thawban) — the man clothed the poor person with a garment |

## Usage notes

A preposition can be used in looser modern style, especially with **إِلَى** (ilā) or **لِـ** (li-), but the classical pattern treats both complements as direct objects. With pronouns, the first object often attaches to the verb: **أَعْطَيْتُهُ كِتَابًا** (aʿṭaytuhu kitāban) — “I gave him a book.”

## Practice patterns

Turn the English “to someone” into a first object when the Arabic verb allows it:

- **سَأَلَتِ الْمُعَلِّمَةُ الطَّالِبَ سُؤَالًا** (saʾalati al-muʿallimatu aṭ-ṭāliba suʾālan) — the teacher asked the student a question.
- **أَلْبَسَتِ الْأُمُّ الطِّفْلَ قَمِيصًا** (albasati al-ummu aṭ-ṭifla qamīṣan) — the mother dressed the child in a shirt.
- **عَلَّمَ الْأَبُ بِنْتَهُ الْقِرَاءَةَ** (ʿallama al-abu bintahu al-qirāʾata) — the father taught his daughter reading.
