# Tāʾ Marbūṭa vs the Open Tāʾ

**تَاءٌ مَرْبُوطَةٌ** (tāʾ marbūṭatun) — tied tāʾ is the rounded final **ة / ـة** often used on feminine nouns and adjectives. **تَاءٌ مَفْتُوحَةٌ** (tāʾ maftūḥatun) — open tāʾ is the normal letter **ت / ـت** and is always pronounced as t.

## Core idea

Tāʾ marbūṭa behaves like a quiet feminine ending. In careful connected speech it is **-at**, as in **مَدْرَسَةٌ كَبِيرَةٌ** (madrasatun kabīratun) — a large school. In pause it is often pronounced **-ah** or **-a**: **مَدْرَسَةْ** (madrasah) — school. Open tāʾ stays t in all positions: **بَيْتٌ** (baytun) — house, **كَتَبَتْ** (katabat) — she wrote.

## Forms and examples

| Ending | What it usually signals | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **ـةٌ** | feminine noun/adjective | **طَالِبَةٌ** (ṭālibatun) — female student |
| **ـةُ** in iḍāfa | pronounced -atu / -ati before what follows | **مَدْرَسَةُ الْمَدِينَةِ** (madrasatu l-madīnati) — the city school |
| **ـتْ** | verb ending or fixed t | **ذَهَبَتْ** (dhahabat) — she went |
| **ـاتٌ** | sound feminine plural | **طَالِبَاتٌ** (ṭālibātun) — female students |
| **ـتٌ** | root letter t | **وَقْتٌ** (waqtun) — time |

## Usage notes

A possessive suffix reveals the t clearly. **مَدْرَسَةٌ** (madrasatun) — school becomes **مَدْرَسَتُهَا** (madrasatuhā) — her school. That does not mean the base word is written with open tāʾ; the suffix simply connects to the feminine ending.

## Common contrasts

- **بِنْتٌ** (bintun) — girl has open tāʾ because t is part of the word.
- **أُخْتٌ** (ʾukhtun) — sister has open tāʾ for the same reason.
- **غُرْفَةٌ** (ghurfatun) — room has tied tāʾ as a feminine ending.
- **سَيَّارَةٌ** (sayyāratun) — car has tied tāʾ as a feminine ending.

> If the t belongs to the root or to a verb ending, expect open **ت**. If it is a feminine noun/adjective ending, expect **ة**.
