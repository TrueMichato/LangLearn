# Answer Particles: naʿam, balā, kallā

Arabic has several short answer particles. They are small, but they matter because a negative question can flip the expected English yes or no.

## Core idea

**نَعَمْ** (naʿam) confirms a positive statement or question. **بَلَى** (balā) rejects a negative question and answers yes, actually. **كَلَّا** (kallā) strongly rejects or warns, and **أَجَلْ** (ajal) is a formal yes, indeed.

## Forms and examples

| Particle | Typical use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **نَعَمْ** (naʿam) | Yes to a positive question | **هَلْ حَضَرَ عَلِيٌّ؟ نَعَمْ، حَضَرَ** (hal ḥaḍara ʿaliyyun? naʿam, ḥaḍara) | Did Ali attend? Yes, he attended. |
| **بَلَى** (balā) | Yes to a negative question | **أَلَيْسَ الطَّرِيقُ طَوِيلًا؟ بَلَى، هُوَ طَوِيلٌ** (a-laysa aṭ-ṭarīqu ṭawīlan? balā, huwa ṭawīlun) | Is the road not long? Yes, it is long. |
| **كَلَّا** (kallā) | No, certainly not | **أَتَتْرُكُ الْعَمَلَ؟ كَلَّا** (a-tatruku al-ʿamala? kallā) | Will you leave the work? Certainly not. |
| **أَجَلْ** (ajal) | Formal yes | **أَهٰذَا صَحِيحٌ؟ أَجَلْ** (a-hādhā ṣaḥīḥun? ajal) | Is this correct? Indeed. |

## Usage notes

After a negative question, English speakers are tempted to use yes. In careful Arabic, choose **بَلَى** (balā) when you want to overturn the negative: **أَلَسْتَ طَالِبًا؟ بَلَى** (a-lasta ṭāliban? balā) — Aren't you a student? Yes, I am.

Using **نَعَمْ** (naʿam) after a negative question can sound like you are confirming the negative in traditional usage, so **بَلَى** is safer when you mean yes, actually.

## Practice patterns

Practice pairing the answer with the question type:

- **هَلْ فَهِمْتَ الدَّرْسَ؟ نَعَمْ** (hal fahimta ad-darsa? naʿam) — Did you understand the lesson? Yes.
- **أَلَمْ تَفْهَمِ الدَّرْسَ؟ بَلَى** (a-lam tafhami ad-darsa? balā) — Did you not understand the lesson? Yes, I did.
- **أَتَكْذِبُ؟ كَلَّا** (a-takdhibu? kallā) — Are you lying? Certainly not.
