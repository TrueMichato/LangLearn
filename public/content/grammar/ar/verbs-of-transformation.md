# Verbs of Transformation (ṣayrūra)

Arabic uses several kāna-like verbs to show that a subject **became**, **remained**, or **entered a state**. In grammar, this idea is often called **صَيْرُورَةٌ** (ṣayrūratun), “becoming” or transformation.

## Core idea

These verbs raise their subject and put their predicate in the accusative, just like **كَانَ** (kāna). In **صَارَ الْمَاءُ ثَلْجًا** (ṣāra al-māʾu thaljan) — “the water became ice,” **الْمَاءُ** (al-māʾu) is nominative, while **ثَلْجًا** (thaljan) is the accusative predicate.

## Forms and examples

| Verb | Core nuance | Subject | Accusative predicate | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **صَارَ** (ṣāra) | became | nominative | accusative | **صَارَ الْوَلَدُ طَبِيبًا** (ṣāra al-waladu ṭabīban) — the boy became a doctor |
| **أَصْبَحَ** (aṣbaḥa) | became by morning / came to be | nominative | accusative | **أَصْبَحَ الطَّرِيقُ آمِنًا** (aṣbaḥa aṭ-ṭarīqu āminan) — the road became safe |
| **أَمْسَى** (amsā) | became by evening | nominative | accusative | **أَمْسَى الْجَوُّ بَارِدًا** (amsā al-jawwu bāridan) — the weather became cold in the evening |
| **عَادَ** (ʿāda) | became again / returned to being | nominative | accusative | **عَادَ الْبَيْتُ هَادِئًا** (ʿāda al-baytu hādiʾan) — the house became quiet again |

## Usage notes

These verbs often translate as “became,” but each adds a shade of meaning. **أَصْبَحَ** (aṣbaḥa) can simply mean “became” in modern writing, not only “became in the morning.” **ظَلَّ** (ẓalla) and **بَاتَ** (bāta) may emphasize continuing in a state: **ظَلَّ الْبَابُ مَفْتُوحًا** (ẓalla al-bābu maftūḥan) — “the door remained open.”

## Practice patterns

Keep the subject nominative and mark the new state accusative:

- **صَارَتِ الْغُرْفَةُ نَظِيفَةً** (ṣārati al-ghurfatu naẓīfatan) — the room became clean.
- **بَاتَ الْحَارِسُ يَقِظًا** (bāta al-ḥārisu yaqẓan) — the guard remained awake at night.
- **أَضْحَى الْخَبَرُ وَاضِحًا** (aḍḥā al-khabaru wāḍiḥan) — the news became clear.
