# The Imperative (al-Amr)

The imperative gives commands or invitations. In kind learning, think of it as a useful form for instructions, recipes, signs, and friendly requests—not just orders.

## Core idea

For many Form I verbs, the imperative is built from the jussive “you” form. The beginning vowel helps pronounce the initial consonant cluster: **اُكْتُبْ** (uktub) — write!, **اِفْتَحْ** (iftaḥ) — open!, **اِجْلِسْ** (ijlis) — sit!

## Forms and examples

| Addressing | Imperative |
|---|---|
| one masculine | **اُكْتُبْ** (uktub) — write! |
| one feminine | **اُكْتُبِي** (uktubī) — write! |
| plural | **اُكْتُبُوا** (uktubū) — write! |
| one masculine | **اِقْرَأْ** (iqraʾ) — read! |
| one masculine | **قُلْ** (qul) — say! |

## Usage notes

Negative commands use **لَا** (lā) — do not with the jussive present: **لَا تَكْتُبْ** (lā taktub) — do not write!, **لَا تَتَأَخَّرْ** (lā tataʾakhkhar) — do not be late!

## Practice patterns

Polite commands often add softening words:

- **مِنْ فَضْلِكَ، اِفْتَحِ الْبَابَ** (min faḍlika, iftaḥi al-bāba) — please open the door, to a male.
- **مِنْ فَضْلِكِ، اِقْرَئِي الدَّرْسَ** (min faḍliki, iqraʾī ad-darsa) — please read the lesson, to a female.
