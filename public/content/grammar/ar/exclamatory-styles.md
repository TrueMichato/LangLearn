# Styles of Exclamation, Praise & Blame (niʿma, biʾsa)

Arabic has several compact ways to express wonder, praise, and blame. Some are very productive; others are fixed rhetorical patterns that appear often in formal writing.

## Core idea

For wonder, two famous patterns are مَا أَفْعَلَهُ (mā afʿalahu) and أَفْعِلْ بِهِ (afʿil bihi). For praise and blame, Arabic uses نِعْمَ (niʿma) — excellent is and بِئْسَ (biʾsa) — bad is.

## Exclamation patterns

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| مَا أَفْعَلَهُ | مَا أَجْمَلَ السَّمَاءَ (mā ajmala as-samāʾa) — How beautiful the sky is |
| مَا أَفْعَلَهُ | مَا أَسْرَعَ الْجَوَابَ (mā asraʿa al-jawāba) — How quick the answer is |
| أَفْعِلْ بِهِ | أَكْرِمْ بِالضَّيْفِ (akrim biḍ-ḍayfi) — How noble the guest is |
| أَفْعِلْ بِهِ | أَحْسِنْ بِالصَّبْرِ (aḥsin biṣ-ṣabri) — How fine patience is |

## The pattern مَا أَفْعَلَهُ

In this pattern, مَا is an exclamatory word, the following form looks like a Form IV past verb, and the thing admired is accusative.

- مَا أَطْيَبَ الطَّعَامَ (mā aṭyaba aṭ-ṭaʿāma) — How delicious the food is.
- مَا أَصْعَبَ السُّؤَالَ (mā aṣʿaba as-suʾāla) — How difficult the question is.

## Praise and blame

نِعْمَ (niʿma) praises, and بِئْسَ (biʾsa) blames. They behave like fixed past-tense verbs with a definite subject, often followed by the specific thing being praised or blamed.

- نِعْمَ الْخُلُقُ الصِّدْقُ (niʿma al-khuluqu aṣ-ṣidqu) — Truthfulness is an excellent trait.
- بِئْسَ الْخُلُقُ الْكَذِبُ (biʾsa al-khuluqu al-kadhibu) — Lying is a bad trait.
- نِعْمَ الرَّجُلُ زَيْدٌ (niʿma ar-rajulu Zaydun) — Zayd is an excellent man.

## Tone and register

These patterns sound formal, elevated, or literary. In everyday speech, speakers may use simpler adjectives, but in MSA the compact forms are useful for reading essays, speeches, and older texts.
