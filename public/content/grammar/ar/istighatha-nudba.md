# Istighātha and Nudba: Calling for Help or Lamenting

اَلِاسْتِغَاثَةُ (al-istighāthatu) is a formal call for help, and اَلنُّدْبَةُ (an-nudbatu) is a lamenting call, often over loss, pain, or grief.

## Core idea

Both styles grow out of the vocative, but they have special emotional force. Istighātha asks someone to help another person, while nudba cries out in sorrow or pity.

## Forms and examples

| Style | Common pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Calling a helper | يَا لَـ... لِـ... (yā la-... li-...) | يَا لَلرِّجَالِ لِلْغَرِيقِ (yā la-r-rijāli li-l-gharīqi) — Men, help the drowning person |
| Calling a group | يَا لَـ... لِـ... (yā la-... li-...) | يَا لَلْأَطِبَّاءِ لِلْمَرْضَى (yā la-l-aṭibbāʾi li-l-marḍā) — Doctors, help the sick |
| Lamenting a person | وَا + مُنَادًى (wā + munādan) | وَا أَبَتَاهُ (wā abatah) — Alas, my father |
| Lamenting pain | وَا + اسم (wā + ism) | وَا قَلْبَاهُ (wā qalbāh) — Alas, my heart |

## Usage notes

These forms are elevated and literary. In modern writing, you may also see direct phrases such as اَلنَّجْدَةَ اَلنَّجْدَةَ (an-najdata an-najdata) — help, help. Still, recognizing يَا لَـ and وَا helps with formal prose and older texts.

## Practice patterns

Read the emotional force before translating word by word:

- يَا لَلْقَوْمِ لِلضَّعِيفِ (yā la-l-qawmi li-ḍ-ḍaʿīfi) — People, help the weak person.
- وَا أُمَّاهُ (wā ummāh) — Alas, my mother.
- وَا حُزْنَاهُ (wā ḥuznāh) — Alas, my sorrow.
