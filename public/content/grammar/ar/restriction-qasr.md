# Restriction & Exclusivity (al-qaṣr: innamā, mā…illā)

Al-qaṣr limits a statement: only this, not others. English often uses only, nothing but, or no one except. Arabic has several compact ways to create that effect.

## إِنَّمَا

إِنَّمَا (innamā) usually restricts what follows it. It often means only or nothing but, with a calm explanatory tone.

- إِنَّمَا الأَعْمَالُ بِالنِّيَّاتِ (innamā al-aʿmālu bi-n-niyyāti) — actions are only by intentions.
- إِنَّمَا أَنَا طَالِبٌ (innamā anā ṭālibun) — I am only a student.
- إِنَّمَا النَّجَاحُ بِالصَّبْرِ (innamā an-najāḥu bi-ṣ-ṣabri) — success is only through patience.

## mā…illā

The frame مَا … إِلَّا (mā … illā) makes a negative sentence and then excludes one item from the negation. It is often stronger or more pointed than إِنَّمَا.

| Sentence | Meaning | Restricted part |
|---|---|---|
| مَا نَجَحَ إِلَّا خَالِدٌ (mā najaḥa illā khālidun) | no one succeeded except Khalid | the subject |
| مَا قَرَأْتُ إِلَّا كِتَابًا وَاحِدًا (mā qaraʾtu illā kitāban wāḥidan) | I read only one book | the object |
| مَا سَافَرْنَا إِلَّا صَبَاحًا (mā sāfarnā illā ṣabāḥan) | we traveled only in the morning | the time |

## Focus depends on position

Restriction is not just a particle; the sentence structure tells you what is being narrowed. Compare إِنَّمَا خَالِدٌ نَاجِحٌ (innamā khālidun nājiḥun) — only Khalid is successful, with إِنَّمَا خَالِدٌ نَاجِحٌ لَا شَاعِرٌ (innamā khālidun nājiḥun lā shāʿirun) — Khalid is only successful, not a poet. Context helps decide the exact scope.
