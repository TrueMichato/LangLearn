# kāna & Her Sisters (past & becoming)

Kāna and her sisters enter nominal sentences and add time, change, or state. They are called “sisters” because they behave similarly in grammar.

## Core idea

The basic sentence **الْبَيْتُ كَبِيرٌ** (al-baytu kabīrun) — the house is big becomes **كَانَ الْبَيْتُ كَبِيرًا** (kāna al-baytu kabīran) — the house was big. The subject stays nominative, while the predicate becomes accusative.

## Forms and examples

| Sister | Example |
|---|---|
| **كَانَ** (kāna) — was | **كَانَ الْجَوُّ بَارِدًا** (kāna al-jawwu bāridan) — the weather was cold |
| **أَصْبَحَ** (aṣbaḥa) — became in the morning / became | **أَصْبَحَ الطَّالِبُ نَشِيطًا** (aṣbaḥa aṭ-ṭālibu nashīṭan) — the student became active |
| **صَارَ** (ṣāra) — became | **صَارَ الْمَاءُ بَارِدًا** (ṣāra al-māʾu bāridan) — the water became cold |
| **لَيْسَ** (laysa) — is not | **لَيْسَ الدَّرْسُ صَعْبًا** (laysa ad-darsu ṣaʿban) — the lesson is not difficult |

## Usage notes

The predicate after these verbs is often called the “khabar of kāna,” and it is in the accusative: **كَبِيرًا** (kabīran) — big, accusative rather than **كَبِيرٌ** (kabīrun) — big, nominative.

## Practice patterns

Kind pattern:

1. Start with a nominal sentence: **الطَّالِبُ مُجْتَهِدٌ** (aṭ-ṭālibu mujtahidun) — the student is hardworking.
2. Add **كَانَ** (kāna) — was.
3. Change the predicate: **كَانَ الطَّالِبُ مُجْتَهِدًا** (kāna aṭ-ṭālibu mujtahidan) — the student was hardworking.
