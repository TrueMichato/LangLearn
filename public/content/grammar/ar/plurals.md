# Plurals: Sound & Broken

Arabic has sound plurals, which add predictable endings, and broken plurals, which change the inside pattern of the word. Broken plurals are not “bad”; they are a beautiful pattern system you learn gradually.

## Core idea

Sound masculine plurals often end in **ـُونَ** (-ūna) — nominative plural or **ـِينَ** (-īna) — accusative / genitive plural. Sound feminine plurals use **ـَاتٌ** (-ātun) — nominative plural. Broken plurals change the stem, as **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — book becomes **كُتُبٌ** (kutubun) — books.

## Forms and examples

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| **مُعَلِّمٌ** (muʿallimun) — male teacher | **مُعَلِّمُونَ** (muʿallimūna) — male teachers, nominative |
| **مُعَلِّمَةٌ** (muʿallimatun) — female teacher | **مُعَلِّمَاتٌ** (muʿallimātun) — female teachers |
| **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — book | **كُتُبٌ** (kutubun) — books |
| **رَجُلٌ** (rajulun) — man | **رِجَالٌ** (rijālun) — men |

## Usage notes

Broken plurals often need to be memorized with the noun. That is normal. Learn **بَيْتٌ** (baytun) — house with **بُيُوتٌ** (buyūtun) — houses, and **قَلَمٌ** (qalamun) — pen with **أَقْلَامٌ** (aqlāmun) — pens.

## Practice patterns

Agreement note: non-human broken plurals often take feminine singular adjectives:

- **كُتُبٌ مُفِيدَةٌ** (kutubun mufīdatun) — useful books.
- **بُيُوتٌ كَبِيرَةٌ** (buyūtun kabīratun) — big houses.

Human plurals usually use plural agreement.
