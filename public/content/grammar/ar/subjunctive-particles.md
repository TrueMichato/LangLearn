# Subjunctive Particles: an, lan, kay, idhan, ḥattā

The subjunctive mood, **النَّصْبُ** (an-naṣbu), appears after particles that express intention, negated future, purpose, result, or limit. These particles are often called **أَدَوَاتُ النَّصْبِ** (adawātu an-naṣbi) — subjunctive particles.

## Core idea

A regular imperfect verb takes fatḥa in the subjunctive: **يَدْرُسُ** (yadrusu) — he studies becomes **أَنْ يَدْرُسَ** (an yadrusa) — to study. The five verbs drop final nūn: **يَكْتُبُونَ** (yaktubūna) — they write becomes **لَنْ يَكْتُبُوا** (lan yaktubū) — they will not write.

## Forms and examples

| Particle | Meaning | Typical use | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **أَنْ** (an) | to, that | verbal noun meaning | **أُرِيدُ أَنْ أَدْرُسَ النَّحْوَ** (urīdu an adrusa an-naḥwa) — I want to study grammar |
| **لَنْ** (lan) | will not | future negation | **لَنْ نَتَأَخَّرَ** (lan nataʾakhkhara) — we will not be late |
| **كَيْ** (kay) / **لِكَيْ** (likay) | so that | purpose | **جِئْتُ لِكَيْ أَتَعَلَّمَ** (jiʾtu likay ataʿallama) — I came so that I may learn |
| **إِذَنْ** (idhan) | then, in that case | response-result | **إِذَنْ أُسَاعِدَكَ** (idhan usāʿidaka) — then I will help you |
| **حَتَّى** (ḥattā) | until, so that | limit or purpose | **اِدْرُسْ حَتَّى تَنْجَحَ** (idrus ḥattā tanjaḥa) — study so that you succeed |

## Usage notes

**أَنْ** can turn a whole verb phrase into a noun-like idea: **يَسُرُّنِي أَنْ تَحْضُرَ** (yasurrunī an taḥḍura) — it pleases me that you attend. **لَنْ** negates future expectation strongly. **حَتَّى** triggers the subjunctive when it points forward to a goal or result, as in **سِرْ حَتَّى تَصِلَ** (sir ḥattā taṣila) — walk until you arrive.

## Practice patterns

Notice how the final vowel changes:

- **أُسَافِرُ** (usāfiru) — I travel; **لَنْ أُسَافِرَ** (lan usāfira) — I will not travel.
- **تَفْهَمُونَ الدَّرْسَ** (tafhamūna ad-darsa) — you all understand the lesson; **لِكَيْ تَفْهَمُوا الدَّرْسَ** (likay tafhamū ad-darsa) — so that you all understand the lesson.
- **نَبْقَى هُنَا** (nabqā hunā) — we remain here; **حَتَّى نَبْقَى آمِنِينَ** (ḥattā nabqā āminīna) — so that we remain safe.
