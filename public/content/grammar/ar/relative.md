# Relative Clauses (alladhī / allatī)

Relative clauses let you say “the person who...” or “the book that...” Arabic uses relative pronouns with definite nouns and often no pronoun with indefinite nouns.

## Core idea

The main singular forms are **الَّذِي** (alladhī) — who / which, masculine and **الَّتِي** (allatī) — who / which, feminine. Plural human forms include **الَّذِينَ** (alladhīna) — who, masculine / mixed plural and **اللَّاتِي** (allātī) — who, feminine plural.

## Forms and examples

| Relative phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **الرَّجُلُ الَّذِي قَرَأَ الْكِتَابَ** (ar-rajulu alladhī qaraʾa al-kitāba) — the man who read the book | masculine singular |
| **الْمَرْأَةُ الَّتِي كَتَبَتِ الرِّسَالَةَ** (al-marʾatu allatī katabati ar-risālata) — the woman who wrote the letter | feminine singular |
| **الطُّلَّابُ الَّذِينَ دَرَسُوا** (aṭ-ṭullābu alladhīna darasū) — the students who studied | human plural |
| **السَّيَّارَةُ الَّتِي رَأَيْتُهَا** (as-sayyāratu allatī raʾaytuhā) — the car that I saw | resumptive pronoun **ـهَا** (-hā) — it/her |

## Usage notes

With indefinite nouns, Arabic often uses no relative pronoun: **رَأَيْتُ رَجُلًا يَقْرَأُ** (raʾaytu rajulan yaqraʾu) — I saw a man reading / who was reading.

## Practice patterns

Arabic often keeps a pronoun inside the relative clause when English would not:

- **الْكِتَابُ الَّذِي قَرَأْتُهُ** (al-kitābu alladhī qaraʾtuhu) — the book that I read.
- The suffix **ـهُ** (-hu) — it refers back to **الْكِتَابُ** (al-kitābu) — the book.
