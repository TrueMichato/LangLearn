# Tamyīz al-Nisba (Relational Specification)

تَمْيِيزُ النِّسْبَةِ (tamyīzu an-nisbati) is the accusative word that clarifies an entire relationship, degree, or judgment. It answers “in what respect?” rather than “how many?”

## Core idea

In English you may say “richer in experience” or “better in character.” In MSA, the clarifying noun is often singular, indefinite, and accusative: أَكْثَرُ خِبْرَةً (aktharu khibratan) — more in experience.

## Forms and examples

| Trigger | Tamyīz | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Comparative | عِلْمًا (ʿilman) | أَنَا أَكْثَرُ مِنْكَ عِلْمًا (anā aktharu minka ʿilman) — I am more knowledgeable than you |
| Increase/decrease | اجْتِهَادًا (ijtihādan) | زَادَ الطَّالِبُ اجْتِهَادًا (zāda aṭ-ṭālibu ijtihādan) — the student increased in diligence |
| Quality judgment | هَوَاءً (hawāʾan) | طَابَتِ الْمَدِينَةُ هَوَاءً (ṭābati al-madīnatu hawāʾan) — the city is pleasant in its air |
| Exclamation | خُلُقًا (khuluqan) | مَا أَجْمَلَهُ خُلُقًا (mā ajmalahu khuluqan) — how beautiful his character is |

## Usage notes

تَمْيِيزُ النِّسْبَةِ is usually not translated as a separate noun. It sharpens the whole statement: حَسُنَتِ الْمَقَالَةُ أُسْلُوبًا (ḥasunati al-maqālatu uslūban) — the article is good in style.

## Practice patterns

Try reading the accusative noun as “in respect to”:

- كَبُرَ الْوَلَدُ سِنًّا (kabura al-waladu sinnan) — the boy grew older in age.
- ضَاقَتِ الْغُرْفَةُ مَكَانًا (ḍāqati al-ghurfatu makānan) — the room became narrow in space.
- هُوَ أَقْوَى مِنِّي حُجَّةً (huwa aqwā minnī ḥujjatan) — he is stronger than me in argument.
