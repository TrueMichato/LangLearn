# The Past Tense (al-Māḍī)

The Arabic past tense, al-māḍī, is built mostly with suffixes. It often feels compact: the verb carries “I,” “you,” “he,” or “she” inside its ending.

## Core idea

The base past form **كَتَبَ** (kataba) — he wrote is the dictionary-style third masculine singular. Other subjects attach endings to that base.

## Forms and examples

| Subject | Past form |
|---|---|
| **أَنَا** (anā) — I | **كَتَبْتُ** (katabtu) — I wrote |
| **أَنْتَ** (anta) — you, masculine | **كَتَبْتَ** (katabta) — you wrote |
| **أَنْتِ** (anti) — you, feminine | **كَتَبْتِ** (katabti) — you wrote |
| **هُوَ** (huwa) — he | **كَتَبَ** (kataba) — he wrote |
| **هِيَ** (hiya) — she | **كَتَبَتْ** (katabat) — she wrote |
| **نَحْنُ** (naḥnu) — we | **كَتَبْنَا** (katabnā) — we wrote |
| **هُمْ** (hum) — they | **كَتَبُوا** (katabū) — they wrote |

## Usage notes

The past tense normally describes completed events. **ذَهَبْتُ إِلَى الْمَدْرَسَةِ** (dhahabtu ilā al-madrasati) — I went to the school. The suffix **ـتُ** (-tu) — I did is your friend.

## Practice patterns

More examples:

- **قَرَأَتْ فَاطِمَةُ الْكِتَابَ** (qaraʾat fāṭimatu al-kitāba) — Fatima read the book.
- **شَرِبْنَا مَاءً** (sharibnā māʾan) — we drank water.
- **فَهِمْتَ الدَّرْسَ** (fahimta ad-darsa) — you understood the lesson, masculine.
