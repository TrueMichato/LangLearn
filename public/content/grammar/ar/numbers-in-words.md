# Writing Numbers in Words

Arabic number agreement feels unusual at first because the number and the counted noun do not always match. Learn the main zones instead of memorizing every number separately.

## Core idea

The **التَّفْقِيطُ** (at-tafqīṭ) — writing numbers in words follows grammatical agreement rules. The counted noun is **الْمَعْدُودُ** (al-maʿdūd) — counted item, and its gender affects the number word.

## Forms and examples

| Range | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | number often follows and agrees | **كِتَابٌ وَاحِدٌ** (kitābun wāḥidun) — one book |
| 3–10 | polarity: opposite gender | **ثَلَاثَةُ كُتُبٍ** (thalāthatu kutubin) — three books |
| 3–10 | polarity with feminine noun | **ثَلَاثُ مَجَلَّاتٍ** (thalāthu majallātin) — three magazines |
| 11–99 | counted noun singular accusative | **أَحَدَ عَشَرَ كِتَابًا** (aḥada ʿashara kitāban) — eleven books |
| 21–99 | unit follows gender pattern | **خَمْسَةٌ وَعِشْرُونَ كِتَابًا** (khamsatun wa-ʿishrūna kitāban) — twenty-five books |

## Usage notes

For 3–10, masculine counted nouns usually take a number with **تَاءٌ مَرْبُوطَةٌ** (tāʾ marbūṭatun) — tied tāʾ, while feminine counted nouns usually take a number without that ending: **خَمْسَةُ أَقْلَامٍ** (khamsatu aqlāmin) — five pens, but **خَمْسُ صَفَحَاتٍ** (khamsu ṣafaḥātin) — five pages.

## Practice patterns

For 11–99, the counted noun is usually singular accusative, even when English uses a plural:

- **إِحْدَى عَشْرَةَ طَالِبَةً** (iḥdā ʿashrata ṭālibatan) — eleven female students.
- **ثَلَاثُونَ مَدِينَةً** (thalāthūna madīnatan) — thirty cities.
- **سَبْعَةٌ وَأَرْبَعُونَ دَرْسًا** (sabʿatun wa-arbaʿūna darsan) — forty-seven lessons.
