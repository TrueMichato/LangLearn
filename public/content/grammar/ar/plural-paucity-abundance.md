# Plural of Paucity vs Plural of Abundance

Some Arabic nouns have more than one broken plural. Traditional grammar may classify one pattern as a plural of paucity, often for a smaller number, and another as a plural of abundance.

## Core idea

The distinction is useful for reading older and formal texts, but modern usage often treats the common plural as neutral. Learn the pair when a noun has one, and let context decide how strongly “few” or “many” is meant.

## Forms and examples

| Singular | Plural of paucity | Plural of abundance | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **كَلْبٌ** (kalbun) — dog | **أَكْلُبٌ** (aklubun) — a few dogs | **كِلَابٌ** (kilābun) — dogs, many dogs | **فِي الْحَيِّ أَكْلُبٌ** (fī al-ḥayyi aklubun) — there are a few dogs in the neighborhood |
| **شَهْرٌ** (shahrun) — month | **أَشْهُرٌ** (ashhurun) — a few months | **شُهُورٌ** (shuhūrun) — months, many months | **مَرَّتْ شُهُورٌ طَوِيلَةٌ** (marrat shuhūrun ṭawīlatun) — long months passed |
| **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — book | **أَكْتُبٌ** (aktubun) — a few books | **كُتُبٌ** (kutubun) — books | **اشْتَرَيْتُ ثَلَاثَةَ أَكْتُبٍ** (ishtaraytu thalāthata aktubin) — I bought three books |

## Usage notes

Common paucity patterns include **أَفْعُلٌ** (afʿulun), **أَفْعَالٌ** (afʿālun), **أَفْعِلَةٌ** (afʿilatun), and **فِعْلَةٌ** (fiʿlatun). Abundance patterns are more numerous, such as **فُعُولٌ** (fuʿūlun), **فِعَالٌ** (fiʿālun), and **فَوَاعِلُ** (fawāʿilu). Not every noun has a living contrast, so check real usage.

## Practice patterns

Use the contrast as a reading clue, not as a rigid translation rule:

- **عِنْدِي أَشْهُرٌ قَلِيلَةٌ** (ʿindī ashhurun qalīlatun) — I have a few months.
- **مَرَّتْ شُهُورٌ كَثِيرَةٌ** (marrat shuhūrun kathīratun) — many months passed.
- **فِي الصُّنْدُوقِ أَقْلُبٌ صَغِيرَةٌ** (fī aṣ-ṣundūqi aqlubun ṣaghīratun) — there are small hearts in the box.
