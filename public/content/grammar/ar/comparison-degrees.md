# Degrees of Comparison in Depth (afʿal min, al-afʿal, elative of colours)

Arabic comparison centers on the elative pattern أَفْعَلُ (afʿalu). The same form can mean more X or most X, depending on its construction.

## Core idea

For a normal comparison, Arabic uses أَفْعَلُ مِنْ (afʿalu min) — more X than. The compared item after مِنْ (min) is genitive. For a superlative, Arabic often uses the definite form الْأَفْعَلُ (al-afʿalu) or an iḍāfa such as أَكْبَرُ مَدِينَةٍ (akbaru madīnatin) — the largest city.

## Forms and examples

| Degree | Example |
|---|---|
| Comparative | هٰذَا الْبَيْتُ أَكْبَرُ مِنْ ذٰلِكَ (hādhā al-baytu akbaru min dhālika) — This house is bigger than that one |
| Definite superlative | هٰذَا هُوَ الطَّرِيقُ الْأَقْصَرُ (hādhā huwa aṭ-ṭarīqu al-aqṣaru) — This is the shortest road |
| Iḍāfa superlative | الْقَاهِرَةُ مِنْ أَكْبَرِ الْمُدُنِ (al-Qāhiratu min akbari al-muduni) — Cairo is among the largest cities |
| Feminine elative | هٰذِهِ هِيَ الْمُشْكِلَةُ الْكُبْرَى (hādhihi hiya al-mushkilatu al-kubrā) — This is the greatest problem |

## Superlative patterns

The elative may be definite: الْأَكْبَرُ (al-akbaru) — the greater or the greatest. It may also form an iḍāfa, where the following noun is genitive: أَفْضَلُ طَالِبٍ (afḍalu ṭālibin) — the best student or a very excellent student, depending on context.

- هُوَ أَسْرَعُ عَدَّاءٍ فِي الْفَرِيقِ (huwa asraʿu ʿaddāʾin fī al-farīqi) — He is the fastest runner on the team.
- هِيَ أَصْغَرُ أُخْتٍ فِي الْأُسْرَةِ (hiya aṣgharu ukhtin fī al-usrati) — She is the youngest sister in the family.

## Colours and defects

Basic colour adjectives already use the أَفْعَلُ pattern: أَحْمَرُ (aḥmaru) — red, أَزْرَقُ (azraqu) — blue, أَعْوَرُ (aʿwaru) — one-eyed. Because of that, formal Arabic often avoids making a direct comparative from them. Use a helper word such as أَشَدُّ (ashaddu) — more intense or أَكْثَرُ (aktharu) — more, followed by an accusative specification.

- هٰذِهِ الْوَرْدَةُ أَشَدُّ حُمْرَةً مِنْ تِلْكَ (hādhihi al-wardatu ashaddu ḥumratan min tilka) — This rose is redder than that one.
- هٰذَا الْقَمِيصُ أَكْثَرُ زُرْقَةً مِنْ ذٰلِكَ (hādhā al-qamīṣu aktharu zurqatan min dhālika) — This shirt is bluer than that one.

## Reading tips

Elatives do not normally take tanwīn in simple indefinite use because many are diptotes. Case still matters: أَكْبَرُ (akbaru) may be nominative, while أَكْبَرَ (akbara) can be accusative in another structure.
