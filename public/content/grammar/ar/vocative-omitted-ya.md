# Vocative with Omitted Yā

The normal vocative particle is **يَا** (yā) — O, hey. In elevated style, prayers, poetry, and clear direct address, **يَا** (yā) may be omitted while the noun still behaves like a vocative.

## Core idea

Omitting **يَا** (yā) does not turn the addressed noun into an ordinary subject. The vocative meaning and its case behavior remain understood from context and intonation.

## Forms and examples

| Full vocative | With omitted **يَا** (yā) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **يَا زَيْدُ، أَقْبِلْ** (yā zaydu, aqbil) — Zayd, come here | **زَيْدُ، أَقْبِلْ** (zaydu, aqbil) — Zayd, come here | proper name built on ḍamma |
| **يَا رَبِّ، ارْحَمْنِي** (yā rabbi, irḥamnī) — my Lord, have mercy on me | **رَبِّ، ارْحَمْنِي** (rabbi, irḥamnī) — my Lord, have mercy on me | common in supplication |
| **يَا طَالِبَ الْعِلْمِ، اجْتَهِدْ** (yā ṭāliba al-ʿilmi, ijtahid) — seeker of knowledge, strive | **طَالِبَ الْعِلْمِ، اجْتَهِدْ** (ṭāliba al-ʿilmi, ijtahid) — seeker of knowledge, strive | annexed vocative remains accusative |

## Usage notes

The omission is clearest with names, divine address, and fixed rhetorical style. In ordinary prose, keeping **يَا** (yā) is usually safer. If the addressed word is annexed, as in **طَالِبَ الْعِلْمِ** (ṭāliba al-ʿilmi) — seeker of knowledge — it remains accusative whether **يَا** (yā) is spoken or merely understood.

## Practice patterns

Add the hidden **يَا** (yā) mentally, then translate:

- **أُخْتِي، اسْمَعِي** (ukhtī, ismaʿī) — my sister, listen.
- **صَدِيقِي، انْتَظِرْ** (ṣadīqī, intaẓir) — my friend, wait.
- **مُعَلِّمَ الْخَيْرِ، بَيِّنْ لَنَا** (muʿallima al-khayri, bayyin lanā) — teacher of goodness, explain to us.
