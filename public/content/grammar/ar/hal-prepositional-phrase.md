# Ḥāl as a Prepositional Phrase

A ḥāl describes the state or manner of a known person or thing. It is often a single accusative word, but it can also be a whole prepositional phrase.

## Core idea

When a prepositional phrase answers “in what state?” or “in what manner?”, it may function as ḥāl. The phrase itself does not show an accusative ending, but grammatically it occupies the place of an accusative ḥāl.

## Forms and examples

| Sentence | Prepositional ḥāl | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **خَرَجَ الطَّالِبُ بِسُرْعَةٍ** (kharaja aṭ-ṭālibu bi-surʿatin) — the student left quickly | **بِسُرْعَةٍ** (bi-surʿatin) — with speed | manner |
| **رَجَعَ الْمُسَافِرُ عَلَى تَعَبٍ** (rajaʿa al-musāfiru ʿalā taʿabin) — the traveler returned in fatigue | **عَلَى تَعَبٍ** (ʿalā taʿabin) — in fatigue | state |
| **وَقَفَ الْحَارِسُ بِسِلَاحِهِ** (waqafa al-ḥārisu bi-silāḥihi) — the guard stood with his weapon | **بِسِلَاحِهِ** (bi-silāḥihi) — with his weapon | accompanying state |

## Usage notes

A prepositional ḥāl usually describes a definite owner, called ṣāḥib al-ḥāl. In **وَقَفَ الْحَارِسُ بِسِلَاحِهِ** (waqafa al-ḥārisu bi-silāḥihi) — the guard stood with his weapon — the owner is **الْحَارِسُ** (al-ḥārisu) — the guard. The phrase gives his state while standing, not a separate location.

## Practice patterns

Compare the prepositional phrase with a simple accusative ḥāl:

- **جَاءَ الْوَلَدُ فِي فَرَحٍ** (jāʾa al-waladu fī faraḥin) — the boy came in joy.
- **جَاءَ الْوَلَدُ فَرِحًا** (jāʾa al-waladu fariḥan) — the boy came happy.
- **دَخَلَتِ الْمُعَلِّمَةُ بِهُدُوءٍ** (dakhalati al-muʿallimatu bi-hudūʾin) — the teacher entered calmly.
