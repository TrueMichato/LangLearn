# Present Moods: Subjunctive & Jussive (naṣb & jazm)

The Arabic present tense changes its ending after certain particles. These changes are small, but they carry important meaning in commands, negation, purpose, and “to” clauses.

## Core idea

The **الْمُضَارِعُ** (al-muḍāriʿ) — present / imperfect verb normally ends in **ـُ** (-u) for simple indicative mood. After certain particles, it enters **النَّصْبُ** (an-naṣb) — subjunctive mood with **ـَ** (-a), or **الْجَزْمُ** (al-jazm) — jussive mood with **ـْ** (-ø / sukūn).

## Forms and examples

| Particle | Mood | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **أَنْ** (an) — to / that | subjunctive | **أُرِيدُ أَنْ أَكْتُبَ** (urīdu an aktuba) — I want to write |
| **لَنْ** (lan) — will not | subjunctive | **لَنْ أَذْهَبَ** (lan adh-haba) — I will not go |
| **لَمْ** (lam) — did not | jussive | **لَمْ أَكْتُبْ** (lam aktub) — I did not write |
| **لَا** (lā) — do not | jussive in prohibition | **لَا تَكْتُبْ** (lā taktub) — do not write |

## Usage notes

For many sound verbs, the contrast is visible in the final vowel: **يَكْتُبُ** (yaktubu) — he writes, **أَنْ يَكْتُبَ** (an yaktuba) — that he write, **لَمْ يَكْتُبْ** (lam yaktub) — he did not write.

## Practice patterns

Purpose clauses often use **لِكَيْ** (likay) — so that, followed by the subjunctive:

- **أَدْرُسُ لِكَيْ أَنْجَحَ** (adrusu likay anjaḥa) — I study so that I succeed.
- **نَقْرَأُ لِكَيْ نَفْهَمَ** (naqraʾu likay naf-hama) — we read so that we understand.

The jussive also appears in many conditional patterns, so **الْجَزْمُ** (al-jazm) — jussive mood becomes more useful as your reading grows.
