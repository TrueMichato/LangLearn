# Describing with Clauses (ṣifa vs. relative)

Arabic can describe a noun with a full clause. The main choice is whether the noun is indefinite, which often takes a descriptive clause directly, or definite, which usually needs a relative pronoun.

## Core idea

صِفَةٌ (ṣifatun) — adjective / descriptor can be a single word or a clause. جُمْلَةٌ صِفَةٌ (jumlatun ṣifatun) — descriptive clause follows an indefinite noun. اِسْمٌ مَوْصُولٌ (ismun mawṣūlun) — relative pronoun follows a definite noun.

| Noun type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Indefinite | noun + clause | رَأَيْتُ طَالِبًا يَقْرَأُ كِتَابًا (raʾaytu ṭāliban yaqraʾu kitāban) — I saw a student who was reading a book |
| Definite | noun + relative pronoun + clause | رَأَيْتُ الطَّالِبَ الَّذِي يَقْرَأُ كِتَابًا (raʾaytu aṭ-ṭāliba alladhī yaqraʾu kitāban) — I saw the student who was reading a book |
| Feminine definite | noun + الَّتِي (allatī) — who / which feminine | قَرَأْتُ الْقِصَّةَ الَّتِي كَتَبَتْهَا (qaraʾtu al-qiṣṣata allatī katabathā) — I read the story that she wrote |
| Human plural | noun + الَّذِينَ (alladhīna) — who plural | سَلَّمْتُ عَلَى الطُّلَّابِ الَّذِينَ حَضَرُوا (sallamtu ʿalā aṭ-ṭullābi alladhīna ḥaḍarū) — I greeted the students who attended |

## Indefinite nouns: direct descriptive clauses

After an indefinite noun, Arabic can attach a clause directly: سَمِعْتُ صَوْتًا يَأْتِي مِنَ الْحَدِيقَةِ (samiʿtu ṣawtan yaʾtī mina al-ḥadīqati) — I heard a sound coming from the garden. There is no الَّذِي (alladhī) because صَوْتًا (ṣawtan) — a sound is indefinite.

## Definite nouns: relative pronouns and return pronouns

With a definite noun, use a relative pronoun: هَذَا هُوَ الْكِتَابُ الَّذِي قَرَأْتُهُ (hādhā huwa al-kitābu alladhī qaraʾtuhu) — this is the book that I read. The attached pronoun هُ (hu) — it returns to الْكِتَابُ (al-kitābu) — the book.

- الَّذِي (alladhī) — who / which masculine singular.
- الَّتِي (allatī) — who / which feminine singular.
- الَّذِينَ (alladhīna) — who masculine human plural.

> If the relative clause has a missing object, Arabic usually keeps a pronoun inside the clause to point back to the noun.
