# The Dual (al-Muthannā)

Arabic has a special form for exactly two. This is the dual, and it is one of the places where MSA is beautifully precise.

## Core idea

The nominative dual often ends in **ـَانِ** (-āni), while the accusative and genitive dual end in **ـَيْنِ** (-ayni). Feminine nouns with tāʾ marbūṭa use **ـتَانِ** (-atāni) or **ـتَيْنِ** (-atayni).

## Forms and examples

| Singular | Dual |
|---|---|
| **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — book | **كِتَابَانِ** (kitābāni) — two books, nominative |
| **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — book | **كِتَابَيْنِ** (kitābayni) — two books, accusative/genitive |
| **طَالِبَةٌ** (ṭālibatun) — female student | **طَالِبَتَانِ** (ṭālibatāni) — two female students, nominative |
| **طَالِبَةٌ** (ṭālibatun) — female student | **طَالِبَتَيْنِ** (ṭālibatayni) — two female students, accusative/genitive |

## Usage notes

The dual can stand alone without the number “two.” **كِتَابَانِ** (kitābāni) already means two books. You may add **اِثْنَانِ** (ithnāni) — two for emphasis, but you usually do not need it.

## Practice patterns

Examples in sentences:

- **جَاءَ طَالِبَانِ** (jāʾa ṭālibāni) — two male students came.
- **رَأَيْتُ طَالِبَيْنِ** (raʾaytu ṭālibayni) — I saw two male students.
- **جَلَسْتُ مَعَ طَالِبَتَيْنِ** (jalastu maʿa ṭālibatayni) — I sat with two female students.
