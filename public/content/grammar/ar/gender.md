# Grammatical Gender & Tāʾ Marbūṭa

Arabic nouns are grammatically masculine or feminine. This is a grammar category, not a judgment about the object. The common feminine marker is tāʾ marbūṭa, written **ـة** (tāʾ marbūṭa) — tied tāʾ.

## Core idea

Many feminine nouns end in **ـةٌ** (-atun) — feminine ending, as in **مَدْرَسَةٌ** (madrasatun) — school. Masculine nouns often have no special ending, as in **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — book. Adjectives and demonstratives agree with grammatical gender.

## Forms and examples

| Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|
| **مُدَرِّسٌ** (mudarrisun) — male teacher | **مُدَرِّسَةٌ** (mudarrisatun) — female teacher |
| **طَالِبٌ** (ṭālibun) — male student | **طَالِبَةٌ** (ṭālibatun) — female student |
| **كَبِيرٌ** (kabīrun) — big, masculine | **كَبِيرَةٌ** (kabīratun) — big, feminine |
| **جَدِيدٌ** (jadīdun) — new, masculine | **جَدِيدَةٌ** (jadīdatun) — new, feminine |

## Usage notes

Some feminine words do not end in **ـةٌ** (-atun) — feminine ending, such as **أُمٌّ** (ummun) — mother and **شَمْسٌ** (shamsun) — sun. Learn gender with the word, then let agreement reinforce it kindly over time.

## Practice patterns

Agreement examples:

- **هٰذَا طَالِبٌ جَدِيدٌ** (hādhā ṭālibun jadīdun) — this is a new male student.
- **هٰذِهِ طَالِبَةٌ جَدِيدَةٌ** (hādhihi ṭālibatun jadīdatun) — this is a new female student.
- **السَّيَّارَةُ كَبِيرَةٌ** (as-sayyāratu kabīratun) — the car is big.
