# Comparative & Superlative (afʿal)

Arabic often uses the pattern afʿal for comparatives and superlatives. One compact form can mean “bigger” or “biggest,” depending on the structure around it.

## Core idea

The comparative pattern is **أَفْعَلُ** (afʿalu) — more / most pattern. Use **مِنْ** (min) — than for comparison: **أَكْبَرُ مِنْ** (akbaru min) — bigger than.

## Forms and examples

| Adjective idea | Comparative |
|---|---|
| **كَبِيرٌ** (kabīrun) — big | **أَكْبَرُ** (akbaru) — bigger / biggest |
| **صَغِيرٌ** (ṣaghīrun) — small | **أَصْغَرُ** (aṣgharu) — smaller / smallest |
| **جَمِيلٌ** (jamīlun) — beautiful | **أَجْمَلُ** (ajmalu) — more beautiful / most beautiful |
| **جَيِّدٌ** (jayyidun) — good | **أَفْضَلُ** (afḍalu) — better / best |

## Usage notes

Comparatives usually do not change for gender in the simple “more than” structure: **فَاطِمَةُ أَذْكَى مِنْ خَالِدٍ** (fāṭimatu adhkā min khālidin) — Fatima is smarter than Khalid.

## Practice patterns

Superlative patterns:

- **أَفْضَلُ طَالِبٍ** (afḍalu ṭālibin) — the best student / a best student in a group.
- **الطَّالِبُ الْأَفْضَلُ** (aṭ-ṭālibu al-afḍalu) — the better / best student.
- **أَكْبَرُ مَدِينَةٍ** (akbaru madīnatin) — the largest city.
