# Participle Government: ism al-fāʿil and ism al-mafʿūl

Arabic participles are nouns, but they can sometimes **work like verbs**. This is called government: an active participle may take an object, and a passive participle may raise a noun like a passive verb.

## Core idea

The active participle **اِسْمُ الْفَاعِلِ** (ismu al-fāʿili) can point to a present or habitual action. In **هَذَا كَاتِبٌ رِسَالَةً** (hādhā kātibun risālatan) — “this is someone writing a letter,” **رِسَالَةً** (risālatan) is an accusative object governed by **كَاتِبٌ** (kātibun).

## Forms and examples

| Participle | Source verb | Working pattern | Governed noun | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **كَاتِبٌ** (kātibun) | **كَتَبَ** (kataba) | active participle + object | accusative object | **هَذَا كَاتِبٌ رِسَالَةً** (hādhā kātibun risālatan) — this is someone writing a letter |
| **قَارِئٌ** (qāriʾun) | **قَرَأَ** (qaraʾa) | active participle + object | accusative object | **رَأَيْتُ الطَّالِبَ الْقَارِئَ كِتَابًا** (raʾaytu aṭ-ṭāliba al-qāriʾa kitāban) — I saw the student reading a book |
| **مَفْهُومٌ** (mafhūmun) | **فَهِمَ** (fahima) | passive participle + raised noun | nominative deputy subject | **أَمَفْهُومٌ الدَّرْسُ؟** (a-mafhūmun ad-darsu) — is the lesson understood? |
| **مَفْتُوحٌ** (maftūḥun) | **فَتَحَ** (fataḥa) | passive participle as predicate | agrees with subject | **الْبَابُ مَفْتُوحٌ** (al-bābu maftūḥun) — the door is open |

## Usage notes

A participle most clearly governs when it has a present, actual, or habitual meaning and is supported by context, such as a demonstrative, negation, question, or description. If the participle is only an adjective, it usually just agrees with the noun it describes: **الطَّالِبُ الْمُجْتَهِدُ** (aṭ-ṭālibu al-mujtahidu) — “the hardworking student.”

## Practice patterns

Ask whether the participle is merely describing, or actively governing:

- **هَلْ أَنْتَ فَاهِمٌ السُّؤَالَ؟** (hal anta fāhimun as-suʾāla) — do you understand the question?
- **الْمُعَلِّمُ شَارِحٌ الدَّرْسَ** (al-muʿallimu shāriḥun ad-darsa) — the teacher is explaining the lesson.
- **أَمَكْتُوبٌ الِاسْمُ؟** (a-maktūbun al-ismu) — is the name written?
