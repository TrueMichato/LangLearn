# Absolute Negation (lā an-nāfiya lil-jins)

Arabic can negate an entire class with a compact structure: no member of this kind exists in the situation. This is stronger and more absolute than ordinary sentence negation.

## Core idea

**لَا النَّافِيَةُ لِلْجِنْسِ** (lā an-nāfiya lil-jins) — lā of absolute class negation means no X at all. It is followed by an indefinite noun called **اِسْمُ لَا** (ism lā) — the noun of lā, and then by a predicate called **خَبَرُ لَا** (khabar lā) — the predicate of lā, which is often a phrase.

## Forms and examples

| Pattern | Arabic | Translation |
|---|---|---|
| no person in a place | **لَا طَالِبَ فِي الصَّفِّ** (lā ṭāliba fī aṣ-ṣaffi) | there is no student in the classroom |
| no doubt | **لَا شَكَّ فِي الْخَبَرِ** (lā shakka fī al-khabari) | there is no doubt about the report |
| no escape | **لَا مَفَرَّ مِنَ الْمَسْؤُولِيَّةِ** (lā mafarra mina al-masʾūliyyati) | there is no escape from responsibility |

## Usage notes

When **اِسْمُ لَا** (ism lā) — the noun of lā is a single indefinite noun, it is usually built on fatḥa in the place of the accusative and has no tanwīn: **لَا رَجُلَ** (lā rajula) — no man, not **لَا رَجُلًا** (lā rajulan). The predicate may be stated or understood from context.

## Not the same as prohibition

Do not confuse absolute negation with **لَا النَّاهِيَةُ** (lā an-nāhiya) — prohibitive lā. Prohibition is followed by a jussive verb: **لَا تُهْمِلْ عَمَلَكَ** (lā tuhmil ʿamalaka) — do not neglect your work. Absolute class negation is followed by a noun: **لَا إِهْمَالَ فِي الْعَمَلِ** (lā ihmāla fī al-ʿamali) — there is no negligence at work.

- **لَا خَوْفَ فِي الْقَاعَةِ** (lā khawfa fī al-qāʿati) — there is no fear in the hall.
- **لَا تَخَفْ** (lā takhaf) — do not be afraid.
