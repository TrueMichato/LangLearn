# Expressing "to have" (ʿinda, laday, li-)

MSA usually does not use a normal present-tense verb for “have.” Instead, it says that something is “with,” “at,” or “to” someone.

## Core idea

The expressions **عِنْدَ** (ʿinda) — at / with, **لَدَى** (ladā) — with / in the possession of, and **لِـ** (li-) — to / belonging to can all express possession. The possessor is often shown with an attached pronoun.

## Forms and examples

| Arabic | Translation | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| **عِنْدِي كِتَابٌ** (ʿindī kitābun) — I have a book | literally “with me is a book” | common and neutral |
| **لَدَيَّ سُؤَالٌ** (ladayya suʾālun) — I have a question | formal / clear possession | common in MSA |
| **لَهَا بَيْتٌ** (lahā baytun) — she has a house | literally “to her is a house” | ownership / belonging |
| **عِنْدَنَا وَقْتٌ** (ʿindanā waqtun) — we have time | practical availability | everyday MSA |

## Usage notes

The possessed thing is often indefinite and nominative: **عِنْدَهُ سَيَّارَةٌ** (ʿindahu sayyāratun) — he has a car. When the thing is definite, the sentence points to a known item: **عِنْدِي الْمِفْتَاحُ** (ʿindī al-miftāḥu) — I have the key.

## Practice patterns

Choose the expression by tone:

- **عِنْدِي فِكْرَةٌ** (ʿindī fikratun) — I have an idea.
- **لَدَيْهِمْ مُشْكِلَةٌ** (ladayhim mushkilatun) — they have a problem.
- **لِلْمَدِينَةِ تَارِيخٌ طَوِيلٌ** (li-l-madīnati tārīkhun ṭawīlun) — the city has a long history.
