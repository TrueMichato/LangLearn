# Diptotes (al-mamnūʿ min aṣ-ṣarf)

A diptote is a noun or adjective that does not fully decline like an ordinary triptote. In Arabic grammar it is **المَمْنُوعُ مِنَ الصَّرْفِ** (al-mamnūʿu mina ṣ-ṣarfi) — prevented from full declension.

> The quick diagnostic: no tanwīn when indefinite, and fatḥa instead of kasra in the genitive unless a condition restores kasra.

## Core pattern

Compare a regular noun with a diptote:

| Function | Regular **مَكْتَبٌ** (maktabun) — office | Diptote **أَحْمَدُ** (aḥmadu) — Aḥmad |
|---|---|---|
| nominative | **مَكْتَبٌ** (maktabun) | **أَحْمَدُ** (aḥmadu) |
| accusative | **مَكْتَبًا** (maktaban) | **أَحْمَدَ** (aḥmada) |
| genitive | **مَكْتَبٍ** (maktabin) | **أَحْمَدَ** (aḥmada) |

## Genitive with fatḥa

After a preposition, many diptotes use fatḥa, not kasra.

- **سَلَّمْتُ عَلَى أَحْمَدَ** (sallamtu ʿalā aḥmada) — I greeted Aḥmad.
- **سَافَرْتُ إِلَى دِمَشْقَ** (sāfartu ilā dimashqa) — I traveled to Damascus.
- **مَرَرْتُ بِمَسَاجِدَ كَثِيرَةٍ** (marartu bi-masājida kathīratin) — I passed by many mosques.

## Common diptote families

You do not need to memorize every cause at once. Recognize the families that appear often.

| Family | Example | Note |
|---|---|---|
| many proper names | **أَحْمَدُ** (aḥmadu), **يُوسُفُ** (yūsufu) | names with certain patterns or endings |
| many place names | **دِمَشْقُ** (dimashqu), **بَغْدَادُ** (baghdādu) | common in reading |
| broken plural patterns | **مَسَاجِدُ** (masājidu) — mosques | patterns like **مَفَاعِلُ** (mafāʿilu) |
| colors and defects | **أَحْمَرُ** (aḥmaru) — red | **أَفْعَلُ** (afʿalu) pattern adjectives |
| feminine-ended forms | **صَحْرَاءُ** (ṣaḥrāʾu) — desert | final **ـَاءُ** (-āʾu) often matters |

## When kasra returns

A diptote usually regains ordinary genitive kasra when it is definite with **اَلْـ** (al-) — the, or when it is the first term of an iḍāfa.

- **فِي المَسَاجِدِ القَدِيمَةِ** (fī al-masājidi al-qadīmati) — in the old mosques.
- **فِي مَسَاجِدِ المَدِينَةِ** (fī masājidi al-madīnati) — in the mosques of the city.
- **مِنَ الصَّحْرَاءِ الكَبِيرَةِ** (mina ṣ-ṣaḥrāʾi al-kabīrati) — from the large desert.
