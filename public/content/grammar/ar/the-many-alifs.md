# The Many Alifs

Arabic uses several alif-like signs. **أَلِفٌ** (ʾalifun) — alif can carry hamza, lengthen a vowel, disappear in connection, or appear as a small historical sign. The shapes **آ** (maddatun) — madda, **ى** (ʾalifun maqṣūratun) — alif maqṣūra, **ٱ** (hamzatu waṣlin) — waṣla alif, and **ٰ** (ʾalifun khanjariyyatun) — dagger alif each have a job.

## Core idea

Do not treat every alif shape as the same letter in reading. **آ** usually combines hamza plus long ā, **ى** sounds like final ā, **ٱ** begins only when no vowel comes before it, and dagger alif marks a long ā written above the line.

## Forms and examples

| Shape | Name | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **آ** | **أَلِفُ الْمَدِّ** (ʾalifu l-maddi) — madda alif | **آدَمُ** (ʾādamu) — Adam |
| **ى** | **أَلِفٌ مَقْصُورَةٌ** (ʾalifun maqṣūratun) — shortened alif | **عَلَى** (ʿalā) — on; **مُوسَى** (mūsā) — Moses |
| **ٱ** | **هَمْزَةُ الْوَصْلِ** (hamzatu l-waṣli) — connecting hamza | **ٱسْمٌ** (ismun) — name; **ٱبْنٌ** (ibnun) — son |
| **ٰ** | **أَلِفٌ خَنْجَرِيَّةٌ** (ʾalifun khanjariyyatun) — dagger alif | **هٰذَا** (hādhā) — this; **ذٰلِكَ** (dhālika) — that |

## Usage notes

**هَمْزَةُ الْوَصْلِ** (hamzatu l-waṣli) — connecting hamza is pronounced only when beginning from silence: **ٱسْمُهُ عَلِيٌّ** (ismuhu ʿaliyyun) — his name is Ali. After a previous vowel, it connects: **وَٱسْمُهُ عَلِيٌّ** (wa-smuhu ʿaliyyun) — and his name is Ali.

## Practice patterns

- **قُرْآنٌ** (qurʾānun) — Qurʾān contains **آ** because hamza and long ā meet.
- **إِلَى** (ʾilā) — to ends with **ى**, pronounced ā.
- **هٰؤُلَاءِ** (hāʾulāʾi) — these contains dagger alif over hāʾ.
- **ٱلْكِتَابُ** (al-kitābu) — the book has a connecting hamza in fully marked Qurʾānic-style spelling.
