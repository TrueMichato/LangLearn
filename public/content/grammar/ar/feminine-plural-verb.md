# The Feminine Plural Verb (yafʿalna / faʿalna)

Arabic has special verb endings for groups of women or feminine plural subjects. These endings are common in books, news, Qurʾānic quotations, and formal speech, so recognizing them makes written MSA much clearer.

## Core idea

The marker **نُونُ النِّسْوَةِ** (nūn an-niswa) — feminine plural nūn is the final **ـْنَ** (-na) that marks feminine plural agreement. In the third person, the past pattern is **فَعَلْنَ** (faʿalna) — they feminine did, and the present pattern is **يَفْعَلْنَ** (yafʿalna) — they feminine do.

## Forms and examples

| Pronoun | Past | Present |
|---|---|---|
| **هُنَّ** (hunna) — they feminine | **كَتَبْنَ** (katabna) — they wrote | **يَكْتُبْنَ** (yaktubna) — they write |
| **أَنْتُنَّ** (antunna) — you feminine plural | **كَتَبْتُنَّ** (katabtunna) — you wrote | **تَكْتُبْنَ** (taktubna) — you write |
| **الْمُعَلِّمَاتُ** (al-muʿallimātu) — the female teachers | **شَرَحْنَ** (sharaḥna) — they explained | **يَشْرَحْنَ** (yashraḥna) — they explain |

## Usage notes

Use the feminine plural verb when the subject is **هُنَّ** (hunna) — they feminine or when a human feminine plural subject is placed before the verb: **الطَّالِبَاتُ يَدْرُسْنَ النَّحْوَ** (aṭ-ṭālibātu yadrusna an-naḥwa) — the female students study grammar.

When the verb comes first before an explicit noun subject, MSA often uses a singular verb with gender agreement only: **دَرَسَتِ الطَّالِبَاتُ النَّحْوَ** (darasati aṭ-ṭālibātu an-naḥwa) — the female students studied grammar.

## Mood and negation

The feminine plural present is not one of **الْأَفْعَالُ الْخَمْسَةُ** (al-afʿāl al-khamsa) — the five verbs. Its nūn does not disappear after **لَنْ** (lan) — will not or **لَمْ** (lam) — did not.

- **لَنْ يَخْرُجْنَ مِنَ الْقَاعَةِ** (lan yakhrujna mina al-qāʿati) — they feminine will not leave the hall.
- **لَمْ يَسْمَعْنَ السُّؤَالَ** (lam yasmaʿna as-suʾāla) — they feminine did not hear the question.
- **لَا تَتَعَجَّلْنَ** (lā tataʿajjalna) — do not rush, you feminine plural.
