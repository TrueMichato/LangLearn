# Hamzated Verbs and Hamza Changes

Hamzated verbs have hamza as one of the root letters. Their meanings are normal, but spelling and some common commands need special attention.

## Core idea

A **فِعْلٌ مَهْمُوزٌ** (fiʿlun mahmūzun) contains hamza as the first, second, or third root consonant. The hamza seat may change with nearby vowels, while the root meaning stays the same.

## Forms and examples

| Type | Past | Imperfect | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial hamza | **أَخَذَ** (akhadha) | **يَأْخُذُ** (yaʾkhudhu) | **أَخَذَ الطَّالِبُ الْقَلَمَ** (akhadha aṭ-ṭālibu al-qalama) — the student took the pen. |
| Middle hamza | **سَأَلَ** (saʾala) | **يَسْأَلُ** (yasʾalu) | **سَأَلَتِ الْمُعَلِّمَةُ سُؤَالًا** (saʾalati al-muʿallimatu suʾālan) — the teacher asked a question. |
| Final hamza | **قَرَأَ** (qaraʾa) | **يَقْرَأُ** (yaqraʾu) | **قَرَأَ الْوَلَدُ قِصَّةً** (qaraʾa al-waladu qiṣṣatan) — the boy read a story. |
| Common short command | **أَكَلَ** (akala) | **يَأْكُلُ** (yaʾkulu) | **كُلْ خُبْزًا** (kul khubzan) — eat bread. |

## Usage notes

Some very common initial-hamza verbs have short imperatives: **خُذْ** (khudh) — take, **كُلْ** (kul) — eat, and **مُرْ** (mur) — command. These are not new roots; they are high-frequency shortened commands.

For spelling, watch the vowel environment around hamza: **سَأَلَ** (saʾala), **سُؤَالٌ** (suʾālun), and **مَسْؤُولٌ** (masʾūlun) all keep the same root idea of asking or responsibility.

## Practice patterns

Conjugate slowly and keep the hamza audible:

- **أَخَذَ، يَأْخُذُ، خُذْ** (akhadha, yaʾkhudhu, khudh) — took, takes, take.
- **سَأَلَ، يَسْأَلُ، اِسْأَلْ** (saʾala, yasʾalu, isʾal) — asked, asks, ask.
- **قَرَأَ، يَقْرَأُ، اِقْرَأْ** (qaraʾa, yaqraʾu, iqraʾ) — read, reads, read.
