# Verbs of Beginning & Approach (kāda, sharaʿa, jaʿala…)

Arabic has a family of verbs that describe an action as almost happening, beginning to happen, or being hoped for. Traditional grammar groups many of them as أَفْعَالُ المُقَارَبَةِ وَالشُّرُوعِ وَالرَّجَاءِ (afʿālu al-muqārabati wa-sh-shurūʿi wa-r-rajāʾi).

## Verbs of approach

كَادَ (kāda) means almost. It behaves much like kāna: it has a noun and then a predicate, usually a present-tense verb.

- كَادَ الوَلَدُ يَسْقُطُ (kāda al-waladu yasquṭu) — the boy almost fell.
- كَادَتِ الشَّمْسُ تَغِيبُ (kādati ash-shamsu taghību) — the sun almost set.
- أَوْشَكَ القِطَارُ أَنْ يَصِلَ (awshaka al-qiṭāru an yaṣila) — the train was about to arrive.

## Verbs of beginning

Verbs such as شَرَعَ (sharaʿa), أَخَذَ (akhadha), بَدَأَ (badaʾa), جَعَلَ (jaʿala), طَفِقَ (ṭafiqa), and أَنْشَأَ (anshaʾa) can mean began to or set about doing. They are followed by a present verb that names the activity.

| Verb | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| شَرَعَ (sharaʿa) | شَرَعَ الطَّالِبُ يَكْتُبُ (sharaʿa aṭ-ṭālibu yaktubu) | the student began writing |
| أَخَذَ (akhadha) | أَخَذَ الطِّفْلُ يَضْحَكُ (akhadha aṭ-ṭiflu yaḍḥaku) | the child started laughing |
| جَعَلَ (jaʿala) | جَعَلَ المَطَرُ يَنْزِلُ (jaʿala al-maṭaru yanzilu) | the rain began to fall |

## Hope and expectation

Some related verbs express hope or expectation, especially عَسَى (ʿasā) — perhaps, may it be. In MSA, it is often followed by أَنْ (an) plus a subjunctive verb.

- عَسَى الطَّرِيقُ أَنْ يَكُونَ سَهْلًا (ʿasā aṭ-ṭarīqu an yakūna sahlan) — hopefully the road will be easy.
- عَسَى اللَّهُ أَنْ يَرْحَمَنَا (ʿasā allāhu an yarḥamanā) — may God have mercy on us.

Do not confuse جَعَلَ as a beginning verb with ordinary جَعَلَ meaning made or put. Context decides: جَعَلَ الطِّفْلُ يَبْكِي (jaʿala aṭ-ṭiflu yabkī) — the child began crying, but جَعَلَ الكِتَابَ عَلَى المَكْتَبِ (jaʿala al-kitāba ʿalā al-maktabi) — he put the book on the desk.
