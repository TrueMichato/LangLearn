# Definiteness, Indefiniteness & Genericness

Arabic definiteness is not just a matter of adding the article. A noun may be definite by article, pronoun, proper name, demonstrative, or iḍāfa. It may also be indefinite but still refer to someone specific in the story.

## Core idea

A definite noun points to an identifiable referent: الْكِتَابُ (al-kitābu) — the book, هٰذَا الْكِتَابُ (hādhā al-kitābu) — this book, كِتَابُ الطَّالِبِ (kitābu aṭ-ṭālibi) — the student's book. An indefinite noun often has tanwīn: كِتَابٌ (kitābun) — a book.

Generic meaning is different. A definite singular can refer to a whole kind: الطِّفْلُ يَتَعَلَّمُ بِالسَّمْعِ (aṭ-ṭiflu yataʿallamu bi-s-samʿi) — The child learns by hearing, meaning children in general.

## Forms and examples

| Meaning | Example |
|---|---|
| Indefinite, first mention | قَرَأْتُ كِتَابًا مُفِيدًا (qaraʾtu kitāban mufīdan) — I read a useful book |
| Definite with article | قَرَأْتُ الْكِتَابَ الْمُفِيدَ (qaraʾtu al-kitāba al-mufīda) — I read the useful book |
| Definite by iḍāfa | كِتَابُ الطَّالِبِ جَدِيدٌ (kitābu aṭ-ṭālibi jadīdun) — The student's book is new |
| Generic definite | الْعِلْمُ نُورٌ (al-ʿilmu nūrun) — Knowledge is light |

## Specific and non-specific indefinites

Indefinite does not always mean unknown to everyone. It can mean a certain but not yet identified person or thing.

- جَاءَ رَجُلٌ فَسَأَلَنِي عَنِ الطَّرِيقِ (jāʾa rajulun fa-saʾalanī ʿani aṭ-ṭarīqi) — A man came and asked me about the road. The man is specific in the story, even though first introduced indefinitely.
- أَبْحَثُ عَنْ مُعَلِّمٍ لِلْعَرَبِيَّةِ (abḥathu ʿan muʿallimin li-l-ʿarabiyyati) — I am looking for an Arabic teacher. This may be non-specific: any suitable teacher.

## Generic meaning

Arabic frequently uses a definite singular or a definite abstract noun for generic truths.

- الْإِنْسَانُ يَحْتَاجُ إِلَى اللُّغَةِ (al-insānu yaḥtāju ilā al-lughati) — Human beings need language.
- الصِّدْقُ فَضِيلَةٌ (aṣ-ṣidqu faḍīlatun) — Truthfulness is a virtue.

The English word the may disappear in translation because the Arabic article marks the whole category.

## Agreement reminders

Adjectives normally follow the noun in definiteness. Compare كِتَابٌ مُفِيدٌ (kitābun mufīdun) — a useful book with الْكِتَابُ الْمُفِيدُ (al-kitābu al-mufīdu) — the useful book.
