# The Present Tense (al-Muḍāriʿ)

The Arabic present tense, al-muḍāriʿ, uses prefixes and sometimes suffixes. Once you see the pattern, every new verb feels less mysterious.

## Core idea

For the root **ك-ت-ب** (k-t-b) — writing, the present verb stem is built around **يَكْتُبُ** (yaktubu) — he writes / is writing. Prefixes show person, and endings show gender/number in some forms.

## Forms and examples

| Subject | Present form |
|---|---|
| **أَنَا** (anā) — I | **أَكْتُبُ** (aktubu) — I write |
| **أَنْتَ** (anta) — you, masculine | **تَكْتُبُ** (taktubu) — you write |
| **أَنْتِ** (anti) — you, feminine | **تَكْتُبِينَ** (taktubīna) — you write |
| **هُوَ** (huwa) — he | **يَكْتُبُ** (yaktubu) — he writes |
| **هِيَ** (hiya) — she | **تَكْتُبُ** (taktubu) — she writes |
| **نَحْنُ** (naḥnu) — we | **نَكْتُبُ** (naktubu) — we write |
| **هُمْ** (hum) — they | **يَكْتُبُونَ** (yaktubūna) — they write |

## Usage notes

MSA present can mean simple present or present progressive depending on context: **أَدْرُسُ الْعَرَبِيَّةَ** (adrusu al-ʿarabiyyata) — I study Arabic / I am studying Arabic.

## Practice patterns

Common present verbs:

- **أَفْهَمُ** (afhamu) — I understand.
- **نَذْهَبُ** (nadhhabu) — we go.
- **تَشْرَبِينَ** (tashrabīna) — you drink, feminine singular.
- **يَقْرَأُونَ** (yaqraʾūna) — they read.
