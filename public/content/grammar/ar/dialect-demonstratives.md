# Demonstratives Across Dialects

MSA has formal demonstratives like هٰذَا (hādhā) and هٰذِهِ (hādhihi). Dialects shorten them, move them around the noun phrase, and often use a ha- sound to mark "this."

## Core idea

The MSA system keeps clear gender, number, and case behavior. Spoken dialects usually keep gender and plural contrasts, but the forms are shorter: Egyptian دَه (da), دِي (dī), دُول (dōl); Levantine هَاد (hād), هَاي (hāy), هَدُول (hadōl); Maghrebi هَاد (hād) before nouns.

## Forms and examples

| This, feminine | هٰذِهِ (hādhihi) — this | دِي (dī) — this | هَاي (hāy) — this | هَذِي (hādhī) — this | هَاي (hāy) — this | هَادِي (hādī) — this |
| These | هٰؤُلَاءِ (hāʾulāʾi) — these | دُول (dōl) — these | هَدُول (hadōl) — these | هَذُول (hadhōl) — these | هَذُول (hadhōl) — these | هَادُو (hādū) — these |

## Usage notes

Egyptian often places the demonstrative after a definite noun: الْبَيْت دَه (il-bēt da), "this house." Levantine, Gulf, Iraqi, and Maghrebi commonly use a prefixed هَا/هَـ (ha-) before the definite noun, as in هَالْكْتَاب (hal-kitāb) or هَاد الْكْتَاب (hād l-ktāb).

## Practice patterns

Try swapping the noun while keeping the dialect pattern:

- Egyptian: الْبِنْت دِي (il-bint dī) — this girl.
- Levantine: هَالْبَيْت (hal-bēt) — this house.
- Maghrebi: هَاد النَّهَار (hād n-nhār) — this day, today.
