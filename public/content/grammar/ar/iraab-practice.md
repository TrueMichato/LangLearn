# Reading Case Endings in Practice (a guided iʿrāb walkthrough)

Iʿrāb means explaining how each word functions in the sentence and why its ending appears. You do not need to name every technical detail at first; start by finding particles, verbs, subjects, objects, and prepositions.

## Walkthrough 1: a verbal sentence

قَرَأَ الطَّالِبُ الكِتَابَ فِي المَكْتَبَةِ (qaraʾa aṭ-ṭālibu al-kitāba fī al-maktabati) — the student read the book in the library.

| Word | Function | Ending clue |
|---|---|---|
| قَرَأَ (qaraʾa) | past verb | built on fatḥa |
| الطَّالِبُ (aṭ-ṭālibu) | subject | nominative ḍamma |
| الكِتَابَ (al-kitāba) | direct object | accusative fatḥa |
| فِي (fī) | preposition | causes genitive after it |
| المَكْتَبَةِ (al-maktabati) | object of preposition | genitive kasra |

## Walkthrough 2: a nominal sentence with a prepositional predicate

المُعَلِّمُ فِي الفَصْلِ (al-muʿallimu fī al-faṣli) — the teacher is in the classroom.

- المُعَلِّمُ (al-muʿallimu) is the topic, so it is nominative.
- فِي الفَصْلِ (fī al-faṣli) is a prepositional phrase acting as the predicate.
- الفَصْلِ (al-faṣli) is genitive because it follows فِي (fī).

Now add إِنَّ (inna): إِنَّ المُعَلِّمَ فِي الفَصْلِ (inna al-muʿallima fī al-faṣli) — indeed, the teacher is in the classroom. The noun after إِنَّ becomes accusative.

## A quick checklist

When you read a sentence, ask in this order:

1. Is there a particle that changes case, such as إِنَّ (inna), كَانَ (kāna), or a preposition?
2. Is the sentence verb-first or noun-first?
3. Who is the subject, and what is the direct object?
4. Are there iḍāfa chains where the second noun is genitive?
5. Is an ending written, pronounced, or only understood from context?

Example with iḍāfa: قَرَأْتُ كِتَابَ الطَّالِبِ (qaraʾtu kitāba aṭ-ṭālibi) — I read the student’s book. كِتَابَ (kitāba) is accusative because it is the object; الطَّالِبِ (aṭ-ṭālibi) is genitive because it is the second noun in the iḍāfa.
