# Going in the Dialects: rūḥ and mshī

MSA commonly uses ذَهَبَ (dhahaba), "to go." Many dialects use the root ر-و-ح (r-w-ḥ) for going, while Maghrebi very often uses مْشَى (mshā) and نَمْشِي (nmshī), which can mean "go" as well as "walk" depending on context.

## Core idea

Do not expect MSA أَذْهَبُ (adhhabu) to be the normal everyday form in conversation. Egyptian, Levantine, Gulf, and Iraqi commonly say forms like أَرُوح (arūḥ) or بَرُوح (barūḥ). Maghrebi commonly says نَمْشِي (nmshī).

## Usage notes

The ر-و-ح root can feel surprising because MSA رَاحَ (rāḥa) has meanings connected with going away or departing. In everyday dialects, however, forms like أَرُوح (arūḥ) and بَرُوح (barūḥ) are normal ways to say "I go."

## Practice patterns

Keep the same dialect verb and change the destination:

- Egyptian: أَرُوح الْبَيْت (arūḥ il-bēt) — I go home.
- Levantine: بَرُوح عَالْمَدْرَسِة (barūḥ ʿal-madrase) — I go to school.
- Maghrebi: نَمْشِي لِلدَّار (nmshī l-ddār) — I go home.
