# The Active Participle (ism al-fāʿil)

Arabic often turns a verb into a “doer” word. This is useful because the same form can mean “writer,” “writing,” or “one who writes,” depending on the sentence.

## Core idea

The **اِسْمُ الْفَاعِلِ** (ism al-fāʿil) — active participle names the person or thing doing an action. For many Form I sound verbs, it uses the pattern **فَاعِلٌ** (fāʿilun) — active-doer pattern.

## Forms and examples

| Verb | Active participle | Meaning clue |
|---|---|---|
| **كَتَبَ** (kataba) — he wrote | **كَاتِبٌ** (kātibun) — writer / writing | the one who writes |
| **دَرَسَ** (darasa) — he studied | **دَارِسٌ** (dārisun) — student / studying | the one who studies |
| **جَلَسَ** (jalasa) — he sat | **جَالِسٌ** (jālisun) — sitting | the one who sits |
| **قَرَأَ** (qaraʾa) — he read | **قَارِئٌ** (qāriʾun) — reader | the one who reads |

## Usage notes

The active participle behaves like a noun or adjective. It can show gender and number: **كَاتِبٌ** (kātibun) — male writer, **كَاتِبَةٌ** (kātibatun) — female writer, and **كَاتِبُونَ** (kātibūna) — male writers.

## Practice patterns

Compare the active participle with a simple adjective-like predicate:

- **الطَّالِبُ جَالِسٌ** (aṭ-ṭālibu jālisun) — the student is sitting.
- **الطَّالِبَةُ قَارِئَةٌ** (aṭ-ṭālibatu qāriʾatun) — the female student is reading / a reader.

In MSA, context decides whether English sounds better with “is doing” or “is a doer.”
