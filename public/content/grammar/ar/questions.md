# Question Words (mā, man, ayna, matā, kayfa, kam, limādhā)

Question words let you invite information kindly. Arabic also has a yes/no question particle, so you can ask many questions without changing word order very much.

## Core idea

Common question words include **مَا** (mā) — what, **مَنْ** (man) — who, **أَيْنَ** (ayna) — where, **مَتَى** (matā) — when, **كَيْفَ** (kayfa) — how, **كَمْ** (kam) — how many / how much, and **لِمَاذَا** (limādhā) — why. The particle **أَ** (a-) — question marker makes yes/no questions.

## Forms and examples

| Question | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **مَا هٰذَا؟** (mā hādhā?) — what is this? | asking about a thing |
| **مَنْ هُوَ؟** (man huwa?) — who is he? | asking about a person |
| **أَيْنَ الْبَيْتُ؟** (ayna al-baytu?) — where is the house? | place |
| **مَتَى الدَّرْسُ؟** (matā ad-darsu?) — when is the lesson? | time |
| **كَيْفَ حَالُكَ؟** (kayfa ḥāluka?) — how are you, to a male? | manner / condition |
| **لِمَاذَا تَدْرُسُ؟** (limādhā tadrusu?) — why do you study? | reason |

## Usage notes

For yes/no questions, place **أَ** (a-) — question marker before the sentence: **أَهٰذَا بَيْتٌ؟** (a-hādhā baytun?) — is this a house? In speech, intonation helps too, but the particle is clear in writing.

## Practice patterns

With **كَمْ** (kam) — how many, the counted noun is often singular accusative in formal Arabic:

- **كَمْ كِتَابًا عِنْدَكَ؟** (kam kitāban ʿindaka?) — how many books do you have, to a male?
- **كَمْ سَاعَةً دَرَسْتَ؟** (kam sāʿatan darasta?) — how many hours did you study, masculine?
