# The Full Relative Pronoun Paradigm

Relative pronouns connect a definite noun to a describing clause. Basic MSA uses **الَّذِي** (alladhī) and **الَّتِي** (allatī), but formal Arabic has a fuller paradigm for gender, number, and case.

## Core idea

Use a matching relative pronoun after a definite antecedent: **الرَّجُلُ الَّذِي وَصَلَ مُعَلِّمٌ** (ar-rajulu alladhī waṣala muʿallimun) — The man who arrived is a teacher. The relative clause usually contains a return pronoun when needed: **الْكِتَابُ الَّذِي قَرَأْتُهُ مُفِيدٌ** (al-kitābu alladhī qaraʾtuhu mufīdun) — The book that I read is useful.

## Paradigm table

| Antecedent | Relative pronoun | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Masculine singular | **الَّذِي** (alladhī) | **الطَّالِبُ الَّذِي نَجَحَ فَرِحٌ** (aṭ-ṭālibu alladhī najaḥa fariḥun) — The student who succeeded is happy. |
| Feminine singular | **الَّتِي** (allatī) | **الطَّالِبَةُ الَّتِي نَجَحَتْ فَرِحَةٌ** (aṭ-ṭālibatu allatī najaḥat fariḥatun) — The student who succeeded is happy. |
| Masculine dual nominative | **اللَّذَانِ** (alladhāni) | **الرَّجُلَانِ اللَّذَانِ وَصَلَا مُهَنْدِسَانِ** (ar-rajulāni alladhāni waṣalā muhandisāni) — The two men who arrived are engineers. |
| Masculine dual accusative or genitive | **اللَّذَيْنِ** (alladhayni) | **رَأَيْتُ الرَّجُلَيْنِ اللَّذَيْنِ وَصَلَا** (raʾaytu ar-rajulayni alladhayni waṣalā) — I saw the two men who arrived. |
| Feminine dual nominative | **اللَّتَانِ** (allatāni) | **الْمَرْأَتَانِ اللَّتَانِ حَضَرَتَا طَبِيبَتَانِ** (al-marʾatāni allatāni ḥaḍaratā ṭabībatāni) — The two women who attended are doctors. |
| Masculine human plural | **الَّذِينَ** (alladhīna) | **الْمُعَلِّمُونَ الَّذِينَ شَرَحُوا صَابِرُونَ** (al-muʿallimūna alladhīna sharaḥū ṣābirūna) — The teachers who explained are patient. |
| Feminine plural | **اللَّاتِي** (allātī) | **الطَّالِبَاتُ اللَّاتِي دَرَسْنَ مُجْتَهِدَاتٌ** (aṭ-ṭālibātu allātī darasna mujtahidātun) — The students who studied are diligent. |

## Man and mā

Use **مَنْ** (man) for “whoever” or “the one who,” especially when the antecedent is indefinite or general: **أُكْرِمُ مَنْ يُسَاعِدُ النَّاسَ** (ukrimu man yusāʿidu an-nāsa) — I honor whoever helps people. Use **مَا** (mā) for “what” or “that which”: **أَفْهَمُ مَا تَقُولُ** (afhamu mā taqūlu) — I understand what you say.

## Practice patterns

A resumptive pronoun is common when the antecedent is the object inside the relative clause: **هَذِهِ الرِّسَالَةُ الَّتِي كَتَبْتُهَا** (hādhihi ar-risālatu allatī katabtuhā) — This is the letter that I wrote. Without that return pronoun, the connection can feel incomplete in formal MSA.
