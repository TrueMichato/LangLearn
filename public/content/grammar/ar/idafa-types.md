# Real and Verbal Iḍāfa Types

The iḍāfa is more than simple possession. Traditional descriptions often distinguish a real or semantic iḍāfa, **إِضَافَةٌ مَحْضَةٌ** (iḍāfatun maḥḍatun), from a verbal or non-pure iḍāfa, **إِضَافَةٌ لَفْظِيَّةٌ** (iḍāfatun lafẓiyyatun), where an adjective-like word is linked to its complement.

## Core idea

In every iḍāfa, the first word, **الْمُضَافُ** (al-muḍāfu), drops tanwīn and the article, while the second word, **الْمُضَافُ إِلَيْهِ** (al-muḍāfu ilayhi), is genitive. The difference is what the relationship means.

## Forms and examples

| Type | Relationship | Definiteness effect | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| real iḍāfa | ownership, part, source, kind | often defines the first noun | **كِتَابُ الطَّالِبِ جَدِيدٌ** (kitābu aṭ-ṭālibi jadīdun) — the student's book is new |
| real iḍāfa | part-whole | second noun limits the first | **بَابُ الْبَيْتِ مَفْتُوحٌ** (bābu al-bayti maftūḥun) — the door of the house is open |
| verbal iḍāfa | adjective or participle plus complement | may remain descriptive rather than fully definite | **هَذَا رَجُلٌ حَسَنُ الْخُلُقِ** (hādhā rajulun ḥasanu al-khuluqi) — this is a man good in character |
| verbal iḍāfa | quality in respect to something | often paraphrases with a phrase | **هِيَ سَرِيعَةُ الْفَهْمِ** (hiya sarīʿatu al-fahmi) — she is quick of understanding |

## Usage notes

A real iḍāfa can make the first noun definite if the second noun is definite: **قَلَمُ الْمُعَلِّمِ** (qalamu al-muʿallimi) — the teacher's pen. A verbal iḍāfa often behaves like an adjective phrase: **طَالِبٌ قَوِيُّ الْحُجَّةِ** (ṭālibun qawiyyu al-ḥujjati) — a student strong in argument. In both types, never put tanwīn on the muḍāf.

## Practice patterns

Classify the iḍāfa by meaning:

- **سَقْفُ الْمَسْجِدِ عَالٍ** (saqfu al-masjidi ʿālin) — the roof of the mosque is high.
- **هَذَا طِفْلٌ جَمِيلُ الصَّوْتِ** (hādhā ṭiflun jamīlu aṣ-ṣawti) — this is a child beautiful in voice.
- **مَدِينَةُ دِمَشْقَ قَدِيمَةٌ** (madīnatu Dimashqa qadīmatun) — the city of Damascus is old.
