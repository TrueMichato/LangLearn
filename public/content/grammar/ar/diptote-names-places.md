# Diptote Names and Place Names

Many proper names and place names are **مَمْنُوعٌ مِنَ الصَّرْفِ** (mamnūʿun mina aṣ-ṣarfi), “diptote.” In MSA, that means they do not take tanwīn and they show fatḥa instead of kasra in the genitive when they are bare proper names.

## Core idea

Compare a regular genitive with a diptote genitive. **سَافَرْتُ إِلَى الْمَدِينَةِ** (sāfartu ilā al-madīnati) — I traveled to the city, but **سَافَرْتُ إِلَى دِمَشْقَ** (sāfartu ilā dimashqa) — I traveled to Damascus. The preposition still governs genitive, but **دِمَشْقَ** (dimashqa) shows fatḥa because it is a diptote place name.

## Common diptote names

| Type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Names on **أَفْعَلُ** (afʿalu) | Often masculine personal names | **تَحَدَّثْتُ مَعَ أَحْمَدَ** (taḥaddathtu maʿa aḥmada) — I spoke with Ahmad. |
| Foreign proper names | Many non-Arabic names | **قَرَأْتُ عَنْ إِبْرَاهِيمَ** (qaraʾtu ʿan ibrāhīma) — I read about Abraham. |
| Feminine proper names | Female names and some places | **مَرَرْتُ بِفَاطِمَةَ** (marartu bifāṭimata) — I passed by Fatimah. |
| Place names | Many city and country names | **أَقَمْتُ فِي بَغْدَادَ** (aqamtu fī baghdāda) — I stayed in Baghdad. |

## What stays the same

Diptotes still have sentence roles. **جَاءَ أَحْمَدُ** (jāʾa aḥmadu) — Ahmad came, is nominative. **رَأَيْتُ أَحْمَدَ** (raʾaytu aḥmada) — I saw Ahmad, is accusative. **مَرَرْتُ بِأَحْمَدَ** (marartu bi-aḥmada) — I passed by Ahmad, is genitive in role but marked with fatḥa.

## Practice patterns

Do not confuse spelling with role. **زُرْتُ مَكَّةَ** (zurtu makkata) — I visited Mecca, has **مَكَّةَ** (makkata) as a direct object. **صَلَّيْتُ فِي مَكَّةَ** (ṣallaytu fī makkata) — I prayed in Mecca, has **مَكَّةَ** (makkata) after a preposition. The ending looks the same, but the grammar is different.
