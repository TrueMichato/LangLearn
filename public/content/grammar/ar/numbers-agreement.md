# Number–Noun Agreement 3–10 (the reverse agreement / polarity rule)

Arabic numbers from three to ten use a famous pattern: the number shows the opposite grammatical gender from the counted noun, while the counted noun appears as a plural genitive.

> Think of 3–10 as a small iḍāfa-like package: number first, plural noun second, and gender polarity on the number.

## Core idea

The counted noun is the **مَعْدُودٌ** (maʿdūdun) — counted item. For **ثَلَاثَةٌ إِلَى عَشَرَةٍ** (thalāthatun ilā ʿasharatin) — three to ten, the number normally disagrees in gender with the singular form of that noun.

| Singular noun | Gender | Count phrase | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **كِتَابٌ** (kitābun) — book | masculine | **ثَلَاثَةُ كُتُبٍ** (thalāthatu kutubin) — three books | feminine-looking number |
| **سَيَّارَةٌ** (sayyāratun) — car | feminine | **ثَلَاثُ سَيَّارَاتٍ** (thalāthu sayyārātin) — three cars | masculine-looking number |
| **قَلَمٌ** (qalamun) — pen | masculine | **سَبْعَةُ أَقْلَامٍ** (sabʿatu aqlāmin) — seven pens | number has tāʾ marbūṭa |
| **مَدِينَةٌ** (madīnatun) — city | feminine | **سَبْعُ مُدُنٍ** (sabʿu mudunin) — seven cities | number lacks tāʾ marbūṭa |

## How to choose the form

1. Find the singular noun: **طَالِبٌ** (ṭālibun) — male student, **طَالِبَةٌ** (ṭālibatun) — female student.
2. Decide its grammatical gender.
3. Use the opposite number form.
4. Put the counted noun in plural genitive: **طُلَّابٍ** (ṭullābin) — students, **طَالِبَاتٍ** (ṭālibātin) — female students.

Examples:

- **خَمْسَةُ طُلَّابٍ** (khamsatu ṭullābin) — five students.
- **خَمْسُ طَالِبَاتٍ** (khamsu ṭālibātin) — five female students.
- **عَشَرَةُ بُيُوتٍ** (ʿasharatu buyūtin) — ten houses.
- **عَشْرُ غُرَفٍ** (ʿashru ghurafin) — ten rooms.

## Case on the number

The number itself changes case according to its role in the sentence. The counted noun remains plural genitive.

| Role | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject | **وَصَلَ خَمْسَةُ طُلَّابٍ** (waṣala khamsatu ṭullābin) — five students arrived |
| Object | **رَأَيْتُ خَمْسَةَ طُلَّابٍ** (raʾaytu khamsata ṭullābin) — I saw five students |
| After preposition | **مَعَ خَمْسَةِ طُلَّابٍ** (maʿa khamsati ṭullābin) — with five students |

## Common learner traps

Do not choose gender from the English translation; choose it from the Arabic singular noun. **بَيْتٌ** (baytun) — house is masculine even if “house” has no gender in English, so “four houses” is **أَرْبَعَةُ بُيُوتٍ** (arbaʿatu buyūtin).
