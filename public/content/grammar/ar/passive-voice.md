# The Passive Voice (al-Mabnī li-l-Majhūl)

The passive voice lets Arabic focus on what happened, not on who did it. This is common in news, formal writing, and careful explanations.

## Core idea

The **الْمَبْنِيُّ لِلْمَجْهُولِ** (al-mabnī li-l-majhūl) — passive voice changes the verb’s vowels and removes the actor. The former object becomes **نَائِبُ الْفَاعِلِ** (nāʾib al-fāʿil) — substitute subject, so it is usually nominative.

## Forms and examples

| Active | Passive | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **كَتَبَ الطَّالِبُ الدَّرْسَ** (kataba aṭ-ṭālibu ad-darsa) | **كُتِبَ الدَّرْسُ** (kutiba ad-darsu) | the lesson was written |
| **فَتَحَ الْمُوَظَّفُ الْبَابَ** (fataḥa al-muwaẓẓafu al-bāba) | **فُتِحَ الْبَابُ** (futiḥa al-bābu) | the door was opened |
| **يَشْرَحُ الْمُعَلِّمُ الْقَاعِدَةَ** (yashraḥu al-muʿallimu al-qāʿidata) | **تُشْرَحُ الْقَاعِدَةُ** (tushraḥu al-qāʿidatu) | the rule is explained |

## Usage notes

For many Form I past verbs, the passive has **u-i** vowels: **فَعَلَ** (faʿala) — he did becomes **فُعِلَ** (fuʿila) — it was done. In the present, the passive often begins with **يُـ** (yu-) or **تُـ** (tu-) and has **a** before the last root sound: **يَفْتَحُ** (yaftaḥu) — he opens becomes **يُفْتَحُ** (yuftaḥu) — it is opened.

## Practice patterns

Passive forms are especially useful when the actor is unknown, obvious, or not important:

> **أُعْلِنَ الْخَبَرُ صَبَاحًا** (uʿlina al-khabaru ṣabāḥan) — the news was announced in the morning.

Weak verbs and derived forms have their own details, so use reliable references and listen for the changed vowels.
