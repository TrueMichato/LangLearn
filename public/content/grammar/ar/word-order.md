# Verbal vs Nominal Sentence Word Order

Arabic can organize a sentence around an action or around a topic. The choice changes emphasis and sometimes agreement.

> A verbal sentence says “the event happened”; a nominal sentence says “as for this topic, here is information about it.”

## Two sentence types

A **جُمْلَةٌ فِعْلِيَّةٌ** (jumlatun fiʿliyyatun) — verbal sentence begins with a verb. A **جُمْلَةٌ اسْمِيَّةٌ** (jumlatun ismiyyatun) — nominal sentence begins with a noun or pronoun.

| Type | Arabic | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| verbal | **كَتَبَ الطَّالِبُ الرِّسَالَةَ** (kataba aṭ-ṭālibu ar-risālata) — the student wrote the letter | the action happened |
| nominal | **الطَّالِبُ كَتَبَ الرِّسَالَةَ** (aṭ-ṭālibu kataba ar-risālata) — the student wrote the letter | the student is the topic |
| nominal without present “to be” | **البَيْتُ كَبِيرٌ** (al-baytu kabīrun) — the house is big | topic plus predicate |

## Verbal sentence agreement

In a verb-first sentence, the verb usually agrees with the subject in gender but remains singular even when the subject is plural.

- **جَاءَ الطُّلَّابُ** (jāʾa aṭ-ṭullābu) — the students came.
- **جَاءَتِ الطَّالِبَاتُ** (jāʾati aṭ-ṭālibātu) — the female students came.
- **قَرَأَ الأَوْلَادُ القِصَّةَ** (qaraʾa al-awlādu al-qiṣṣata) — the boys read the story.

## Subject-first sentences

When the subject comes first, the following verb often agrees more fully with it because the first noun is now a topic, and the verbal clause comments on it.

| Subject first | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **الطُّلَّابُ جَاؤُوا** (aṭ-ṭullābu jāʾū) | the students came |
| **الطَّالِبَاتُ جِئْنَ** (aṭ-ṭālibātu jiʾna) | the female students came |
| **الأَوْلَادُ قَرَؤُوا القِصَّةَ** (al-awlādu qaraʾū al-qiṣṣata) | the boys read the story |

## Why word order matters

Use verbal order for narration: **دَخَلَ المُدَرِّسُ وَبَدَأَ الدَّرْسَ** (dakhala al-mudarrisu wa-badaʾa ad-darsa) — the teacher entered and began the lesson. Use nominal order to set up contrast or focus: **المُدَرِّسُ بَدَأَ الدَّرْسَ، لَا الطَّالِبُ** (al-mudarrisu badaʾa ad-darsa, lā aṭ-ṭālibu) — the teacher began the lesson, not the student.
