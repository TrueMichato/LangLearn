# Gulf Arabic Essentials (khaleeji)

Gulf Arabic, often called Khaleeji, covers related varieties around the Arabian Gulf. It shares many roots with MSA while using local pronunciation, vocabulary, and sentence rhythm.

## Core idea

Common Gulf learner-notice points include **شْلُونَكْ؟** (shlōnak?) — how are you, to a male, **وَيْنْ؟** (wēn?) — where?, and **أَبِي** (abī) — I want. In many Gulf varieties, **ق** (qāf) — q may be pronounced g in some words, though usage varies.

## Forms and examples

| Gulf pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| greeting | **شْلُونَكْ؟** (shlōnak?) — how are you, to a male? |
| want | **أَبِي أَرُوحْ** (abī arūḥ) — I want to go |
| where | **وَيْنْ الْبَيْتْ؟** (wēn il-bēt?) — where is the house? |
| now | **الْحِينْ** (il-ḥīn) — now |
| negation | **مَا أَعْرِفْ** (mā aʿrif) — I do not know |

## Usage notes

> 📌 Dialect note: “Gulf Arabic” is not one identical speech form. Kuwaiti, Bahraini, Qatari, Emirati, Omani, Saudi, and other local varieties have differences. Learn from the community or media you care about.

MSA **أُرِيدُ** (urīdu) — I want corresponds to Gulf **أَبِي** (abī) — I want in many everyday contexts.

## Practice patterns

Kind practice phrases:

- **زَيْنْ** (zēn) — good / fine.
- **مَا عَلَيْكْ أَمْرْ** (mā ʿalayk amr) — please / if it is not a burden, to a male.
- **يَعْطِيكَ الْعَافِيَةْ** (yaʿṭīka al-ʿāfiya) — may He give you wellness; thank-you style phrase, to a male.
