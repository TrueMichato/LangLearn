# The Accusative of Purpose (al-mafʿūl li-ajlih)

Arabic can state the reason for an action with an accusative verbal noun. This structure often translates as out of, for the sake of, or in order to.

## Core idea

**الْمَفْعُولُ لِأَجْلِهِ** (al-mafʿūl li-ajlih) — accusative of purpose is usually a **مَصْدَرٌ** (maṣdar) — verbal noun in the accusative that explains why the action happened. It answers **لِمَاذَا؟** (limādhā?) — why?

## Forms and examples

| Arabic | Translation |
|---|---|
| **جِئْتُ طَلَبًا لِلْعِلْمِ** (jiʾtu ṭalaban lil-ʿilmi) | I came seeking knowledge |
| **وَقَفْتُ احْتِرَامًا لِلْمُعَلِّمِ** (waqaftu iḥtirāman lil-muʿallimi) | I stood out of respect for the teacher |
| **سَافَرَتْ رَغْبَةً فِي الرَّاحَةِ** (sāfarat raghbatan fī ar-rāḥati) | she traveled out of a desire for rest |

## Usage notes

The purpose accusative is commonly indefinite and accusative: **طَلَبًا** (ṭalaban) — seeking, **احْتِرَامًا** (iḥtirāman) — respect, **خَوْفًا** (khawfan) — fear. It usually shares the same doer and time as the main verb: the person who came is also the one seeking knowledge.

A prepositional phrase with **لِـ** (li-) — for can often express the same idea: **جِئْتُ لِطَلَبِ الْعِلْمِ** (jiʾtu li-ṭalabi al-ʿilmi) — I came to seek knowledge.

## Compared with other accusatives

The purpose accusative differs from **الْمَفْعُولُ بِهِ** (al-mafʿūl bihi) — direct object, which receives the action, and from **الْمَفْعُولُ الْمُطْلَقُ** (al-mafʿūl al-muṭlaq) — cognate accusative, which emphasizes or describes the action itself.

- **قَرَأْتُ الْكِتَابَ** (qaraʾtu al-kitāba) — I read the book: direct object.
- **قَرَأْتُ قِرَاءَةً سَرِيعَةً** (qaraʾtu qirāʾatan sarīʿatan) — I read a quick reading: cognate accusative.
- **قَرَأْتُ رَغْبَةً فِي الْفَهْمِ** (qaraʾtu raghbatan fī al-fahmi) — I read out of a desire to understand: purpose accusative.
