# 
Particle の (Noun Modifier)

Particle

の is like a label maker. It turns a noun into a label that modifies another noun.

## The Basics

Particle の has various roles in Japanese grammar, such as to modify nouns and to turn words into nouns. On this page, we'll focus on particle の as noun modifier.

### Conceptualizing Particle の

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"label-mnemonic","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Conceptualizing Particle の","rule":"の makes a modifying label from the preceding noun","example":"In the label-maker image, what does 犬の contribute to 犬のおもちゃ?","answer":"It labels the toy as related to dogs, here a toy for dogs.","explanation":"The first noun supplies the label and the following noun is the head. の does not itself specify ownership in every relationship."} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-omocha","word":"おもちゃ","reading":"おもちゃ","meaning":"toy","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Conceptualizing Particle の"} -->

We like to think of particle の as a label maker. It changes the word that comes before it into a label, which can be stuck onto something else to give extra information about it.

For example, if you want to say "dog toy" in Japanese, you take the word 犬 (dog), stick の onto the end to make a label, and then attach this to the word おもちゃ (toy). And voilà! Now you have 犬のおもちゃ, or "dog toy." の creates a label that says "dog" so that everyone knows what kind of "toy" it is. It's for dogs! Not for human babies, or your pet iguana.

You'll see though that the way these labels get translated into English might depend on the relationship between the label and the noun. For example, ジェニーの手 is "Jenny's hands," レストランの予約 is "restaurant reservations," and 動物のドキュメンタリー is "a documentary about animals." On this page, we'll discover more about this useful label-making particle の.

### Patterns of Use

Before we jump into the many uses of の, let's take a look at what kinds of words and grammatical patterns it pops up in.

#### Noun + の (+ Noun)

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"omitted-head-noun","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Noun + の (+ Noun)","rule":"The modified noun can be omitted when recoverable","example":"Why can 私の mean mine without a following 車?","answer":"Context supplies the omitted thing, such as the car being discussed.","explanation":"The possessive label remains while the understood head noun is left out. This differs from turning a verb phrase into a noun with nominalizing の."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"stacked-label-head","kind":"rule","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Noun + の (+ Noun)","rule":"In a stack of noun labels the head comes last","example":"What is the main thing named by 犬のおもちゃの店, and what describes its kind?","answer":"It names a store; dog toys describe the kind of store.","explanation":"犬 modifies おもちゃ, and 犬のおもちゃ then modifies 店. Do not reverse this into a toy belonging to a store merely by following English word order."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"rule-no-noun-label","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Noun + の (+ Noun)","rule":"In Noun A + の + Noun B, A labels or modifies B, and B is the main thing","example":"In the pattern Noun A + の + Noun B, which noun is the main thing being talked about, and what does the other do?","answer":"Noun B (the last noun) is the main thing; Noun A labels or describes it.","explanation":"犬のおもちゃ is a toy (for dogs). As we've seen so far, the particle の can attach to a noun, which is then stuck to another noun to give more information about it.

  - 私の車

  - my car 

(a car that I own, a car that I designed, etc)

If it's clear from the context what you are talking about, you can leave that out and just say:

  - 私の

  - mine

This function is stackable, meaning that we can put labels on labels on labels. For example, let's say I want to go to the dog toy store to get a new toy for my lil' doggo friend 🐶 "Dog toy store" consists of three words, and we can turn these first two into stacked labels. You already know how to say "dog toy," right? That's 犬のおもちゃ. We can take this whole chunk and turn it into a label with の, as in 犬のおもちゃの. Then, we just stick that onto our main noun, which is 店 (store).

> 
> 
> 犬 + の + おもちゃ + の + 店
> 
> dog  🏷    toy      🏷   store
> 
> 

See how 店 comes at the end? In Japanese, the main word will always come at the end. On the other hand, "dog toy" is just the label, aka an extra detail to explain *what kind of store* we are talking about, so it comes before the store.

#### Another Particle + の + Noun

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"directional-label-contrast","kind":"rule","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Another Particle + の + Noun","rule":"A retained directional particle makes the noun label's relationship explicit","example":"What does へ add in アメリカへの飛行機 compared with アメリカの飛行機?","answer":"It specifies a plane going to the U.S., rather than merely an American-associated plane.","explanation":"からの instead marks origin, and との can attach a quotation to a message noun. の lets the whole particle phrase modify the head noun."} -->

The noun modifier の can be used with other particles. For example, particle へ marks a destination that something travels towards, so アメリカへ means "to the U.S." By adding の,  it becomes a label to stick to another noun. So, to say "airplanes to the U.S.," you can stick this onto  飛行機(ひこうき) (airplane) as a label:

  - アメリカへの飛行機

  - airplanes to the U.S.

If this example didn't have へ, as in アメリカの飛行機, it would mean "American airplanes" — airplanes made in the U.S., or airplanes that belong to the U.S. However, with the へ added, it clarifies that it's an airplane with the *destination* of the U.S. Now let's experiment with switching へ to some other particle.

  - アメリカからの飛行機

  - airplanes from the U.S.

Now being used with から, which means "from," this means that it's an airplane *coming from* the U.S.

It's also important to know that when の is used with another particle, it lets you modify a noun with a *non*-noun element. For example, you can describe 人生 (life) using the verb 寝る (sleep) and particle だけ (only).

  - 寝るだけの人生

  - a life of only sleeping

With particle と, you can use a quote to label a noun, such as メッセージ (message).

  - 「早く帰ってきてね！」とのメッセージ

  - a message saying "Come back soon!"

#### Non-noun Word + の

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"nominalizer-versus-modifier","kind":"rule","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Non-noun Word + の","rule":"Nominalizing の turns a predicate into a noun phrase","example":"Is の in 食べるのが好きだ merely connecting two ordinary nouns?","answer":"No. It nominalizes the verb 食べる, making eating available as a noun phrase.","explanation":"This lesson distinguishes nominalization from noun-to-noun labeling and refers its fuller treatment to a separate lesson."} -->

The particle の can also turn a non-noun word or phrase into a noun. For example, if の attaches to the verb 食べる (eat), you can use it as a noun in a sentence:

  - 食べるのが好きだ。

  - I like eating.

We call this use "nominalization" since it turns non-nouns into nouns. We won't focus on this use on this page, but you can read all about it on its dedicated page.

### Isn't の the Japanese Possessive?

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"relationship-context","kind":"rule","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Isn't の the Japanese Possessive?","rule":"Context determines the relationship expressed by a noun label","example":"Must バナナの本 mean a book owned by a banana?","answer":"No. It can mean a book about bananas, shaped like a banana, or associated with a person named Banana.","explanation":"Ownership is only one possible relation. The head noun and context resolve the intended interpretation."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"rule-no-relation-from-context","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Isn't の the Japanese Possessive?","rule":"の links two nouns without specifying the exact relationship","example":"When の links two nouns, how can you tell what relationship it expresses?","answer":"Infer it from the nouns and their context.","explanation":"The particle itself doesn't specify ownership. バナナの本 is likely a book about bananas, not one owned by them."} -->

You might think of particle の like Japanese equivalent of the possessive **'s** ending in English, as in **Tom's** car of **Granny's** kittens. However, the label maker is really a better way to think of particle の, since the relationship between the label and the noun can result in various different interpretations. For example, let's say your friend has a book recommendation:

  - バナナの本、面白いよ。

  - **Books about bananas** are interesting 
 **Banana-shaped books** are interesting.
 **Banana's book** is interesting.

By バナナの本, all we know is that the book has something to do with バナナ (banana). It might be a book *about* the fruit, banana 🍌 Or, if you were talking about all kinds of fruit-shaped books, it might mean a book *shaped like* a banana. It's also possible バナナの本 could be a book *written by* Banana. Of course, bananas (or any fruit really) can't write a book (that'd be *bananas* if they could!🤪). So it would have to be someone named Banana, and there just so happens to be a Japanese novelist named Banana Yoshimoto. It could even refer to a book that belongs to a person named Banana as well. …Now you see how バナナの本 could mean many different things? This is why we want you to think of the label meaning, and not of the possessive!

### Uses of の

As you just read with バナナの, particle の can be translated in many different ways depending on the words or the context. Some linguists break down the use of の into hundreds of categories, but really, it could be endless! Instead of giving you an endless list to scroll through, we've pulled up some of the most important uses here.

#### Ownership

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"possession-application","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Ownership","rule":"の marks possession between two nouns","hint":"My book; link the possessor and the thing owned.","example":"(\"This is my book.\") これは私(わたし)___本(ほん)です。","answer":"の","explanation":"In this context 私 labels the book's possessor. The same particle has many nonpossessive uses elsewhere.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"の marks possession between two nouns","example":"これは私(わたし)___本(ほん)です。"}]} -->

の can express ownership or possession when it comes after a person or a group of people. This is the use that is similar to …'s in English. You put your name on your things, right? Labelling is important for showing ownership!

  - ジェニーのバッグ

  - Jenny's bag

With a personal pronoun, の can be used to make equivalents of "my," "your," and "their." For example, using the first-person pronoun, 私 ("I"), you can say:

  - 私の母

  - my mother

#### Affiliation

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"affiliation-application","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Affiliation","rule":"An organization plus の can label a person's affiliation","hint":"A professor at Tokyo University.","example":"(\"a professor at Tokyo University\") 東京大学___教授","answer":"の","explanation":"The institution precedes the person or role in the Japanese label. This does not mean the university literally owns the professor.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"An organization plus の can label a person's affiliation","example":"東京大学___教授"}]} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-kyouju","word":"教授","reading":"きょうじゅ","meaning":"professor","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Affiliation"} -->

の is also often used after the name of an organization to show affiliation. In other words, it shows what organization a person belongs to. For example, when you make a business phone call and explain who you are — Kyoko, who works for a company called Tofugu — you can say:

  - [トーフグのキョーコ]です。

  - This is [Kyoko from Tofugu].

This can be followed by a role, job title, or position as well.

  - 東京大学の教授

  - a professor(s) at Tokyo University

It's interesting to note that in English, we tend to place the person's name *before* the organization, but in Japanese, the reverse is true. You are *Tofugu's* Kyoko or *Tokyo University's* professor.

#### Topic

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"explicit-about-application","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Topic","rule":"についての explicitly labels the subject matter of a noun","hint":"Research about whales; supply the topic phrase and its noun linker.","example":"(Spell out the topic explicitly: \"research about whales.\") クジラ___研究","answer":"についての","explanation":"This specifies whales as the research topic. クジラの研究 alone can leave more than one relationship open in a suitable context.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"についての explicitly labels the subject matter of a noun","example":"クジラ___研究"}]} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-chishiki","word":"知識","reading":"ちしき","meaning":"knowledge","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Topic"} -->

Particle の can also be used to label the topic of something. Typically, this creates an "about" relationship between the label noun and the main noun:

  - 日本語の知識

  - knowledge about the Japanese language

  - クジラの研究

  - research about whales

However, it's possible that there could be some ambiguity here. Perhaps the whales are *doing* the research, in which case クジラの研究 could mean "the whale's research." Unlikely, but who knows! To be crystal clear, you can always add 〜について (regarding) before の, to form クジラについての研究 to mean "research regarding whales."

#### Location and Time

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"time-label-application","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Location and Time","rule":"A time noun can label another noun with の without a verb","hint":"The five-a.m. alarm.","example":"(\"the 5 a.m. alarm\") 朝五時___アラーム","answer":"の","explanation":"の labels the alarm's time. に would instead normally mark the time of a predicate, not directly link these nouns.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"A time noun can label another noun with の without a verb","example":"朝五時___アラーム"}]} -->

While you might initially think of particle に when you think of location or time, particle の can be used as well. The difference is that with particle の, we don't need to use a verb, we just use the time or location noun as a label for our main noun:

  - ニューヨークの自由の女神

  - the New York Statue of Liberty

  - 朝五時のアラーム

  - the 5 a.m alarm.

#### Degree: Amount, Quantity, Price, Size, and More

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"price-label-application","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Degree: Amount, Quantity, Price, Size, and More","rule":"A measured amount or price can modify a noun through の","hint":"A four-thousand-yen mango.","example":"(\"a four-thousand-yen mango\") 四千円___マンゴー","answer":"の","explanation":"The price phrase labels the mango. Other examples label an amount or quantity using a numeral and the appropriate counter.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"A measured amount or price can modify a noun through の","example":"四千円___マンゴー"}]} -->

の can be used with a number and a counter to express the degree of something: the amount, quantity, price, size, and so on.

  - 五十人のアメリカ人

  - fifty American people

  - 四千円のマンゴー

  - a four-thousand-yen mango

#### Order and Rank

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"order-label-application","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Order and Rank","rule":"Order and rank expressions can modify nouns with の","hint":"The first test.","example":"(\"the first test\") 最初___テスト","answer":"の","explanation":"最初 labels which test. 三人目の孫 and 世界一の温泉 similarly express order or rank.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"Order and rank expressions can modify nouns with の","example":"最初___テスト"}]} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-saisho","word":"最初","reading":"さいしょ","meaning":"first; beginning","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Order and Rank"} -->

の gets used to express the order or the rank of something.

  - 最初のテスト

  - first test

  - 三人目の孫

  - third grandchild

  - 世界一の温泉

  - world's number one hot spring

#### Apposition

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"apposition-application","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Apposition","rule":"の can equate two nouns, meaning 'who is'","hint":"Tanaka, who is my friend; connect the description to the name.","example":"(\"This is my friend, Tanaka.\") こちらは友達(ともだち)___田中(たなか)さんです。","answer":"の","explanation":"友達 and 田中さん identify the same person. This is apposition, not a friend belonging to Tanaka.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"の can equate two nouns, meaning 'who is'","example":"こちらは友達(ともだち)___田中(たなか)さんです。"}]} -->

の is commonly used for apposition, which is the relationship between multiple words that refer to the same thing. In English, we use a comma (,) for apposition to say something like "banana, the fruit."

  - フルーツのバナナ

  - banana, the fruit

  - 校長のスズキ先生

  - Suzuki Sensei, the school principal

#### Materials and Ingredients

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"material-label-application","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Materials and Ingredients","rule":"の can connect a material label directly to a noun","hint":"A plastic cup.","example":"(\"a plastic cup\") プラスティック___コップ","answer":"の","explanation":"The first noun names the material; the second names the object. This is a noun phrase, unlike a full made-of predicate with で.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"の can connect a material label directly to a noun","example":"プラスティック___コップ"}]} -->

の can label the materials or ingredients of something. It's commonly used to explain what ingredients are used in a dish, for example.

  - アサリの味噌汁

  - miso soup with clam

  - プラスティックのコップ

  - plastic cup

## Beyond The Basics

#### Attributes (の-Adjectives)

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"attribute-no-application","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Attributes (の-Adjectives)","rule":"Some attribute nouns modify another noun with の rather than な","hint":"A normal car, using 普通.","example":"(\"a normal car\") 普通___車","answer":"の","explanation":"普通 and 久しぶり are used as noun-like attribute labels here. Learn their linking pattern rather than replacing every adjective's linker with の.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"Some attribute nouns modify another noun with の rather than な","example":"普通___車"}]} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-futsuu","word":"普通","reading":"ふつう","meaning":"normal; ordinary; usual","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Attributes (の-Adjectives)"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-hisashiburi","word":"久しぶり","reading":"ひさしぶり","meaning":"after a long time; first time in a long while","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Attributes (の-Adjectives)"} -->

In Japanese, we have adjective-like nouns that are used for abstract concepts. They are nouns like 普通 (normality) or 久しぶり (a concept of not doing something for a long time). To describe something "normal" or something that you haven't seen or experienced for a long time, you can use these nouns to modify another noun like adjectives do.

  - 普通の車

  - normal car

  - 久しぶりの再会

  - reunion for the first time in a long time

Some dictionaries and textbooks call these words "の-adjectives" because they express attributes, just like a typical い-adjective or な-adjective. However, their use of の to modify nouns makes them also quite similar to nouns. If you're interested in this weird hybrid word type, we recommend checking out our article about な-adjectives and の-adjectives for more information.

#### の Can Replace が in Relative Clauses

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"relative-clause-subject-no","kind":"rule","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"の Can Replace が in Relative Clauses","rule":"の can mark a subject in the illustrated noun-modifying relative clause","example":"What does の do in カナエの作ったネックレス compared with カナエが作ったネックレス?","answer":"It marks Kanae as the maker inside the relative clause, with the same basic meaning as が here.","explanation":"The clause modifies ネックレス. This is not permission to replace subject が with の in independent sentences or every embedded clause."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"rule-no-replaces-ga-relative","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"の Can Replace が in Relative Clauses","rule":"Inside a noun-modifying clause, の can replace the subject marker が","example":"Where can の stand in for the subject marker が, and where can it not?","answer":"Inside a relative (noun-modifying) clause, e.g. the subject of \"the necklace that Kanae made\"; not in an independent main sentence.","explanation":"カナエが作ったネックレス = カナエの作ったネックレス. Answers naming relative/noun-modifying clauses count."} -->

A noun can be modified in various ways. Using adjectives is a common way of doing so, and this page has introduced how a noun can be modified with another noun using の. Another way to modify a noun is to use a clause. A clause that modifies a noun is called a "relative clause," and this is when you might run into の in place of the subject-marking particle が.

  - [カナエ**が**作った]ネックレス

  - the necklace [that Kanae made]

  - [カナエ**の**作った]ネックレス

  - the necklace [that Kanae made]
