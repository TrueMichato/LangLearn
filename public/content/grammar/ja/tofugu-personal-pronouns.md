# Personal Pronouns

<!-- vocab-inventory: {"mode":"explicit"} -->

> **Source:** Content from [Tofugu](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-grammar/personal-pronouns/). For personal study.

Japanese personal pronouns work like regular nouns — they don't change form based on grammatical role (I/me/my). Instead, **particles** assign the role.

## Pronouns Don't Change Form

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"possessive-role","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Pronouns Don't Change Form","rule":"の marks a pronoun's possessive relation without changing the pronoun","example":"My nickname: 私___ニックネーム。","answer":"の","explanation":"私 remains the same lexical form; は, を, or の assigns the intended role."} -->

Unlike English, 私 serves as "I," "me," and "my":

- **私は**ジェニファーです。 — **I** am Jennifer. (は = topic)
- **私を**ジェニーと呼んでください。 — Please call **me** Jenny. (を = object)
- **私の**ニックネームです。 — It's **my** nickname. (の = possessive)

## Pronouns Are Often Omitted

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"omit-recoverable-pronouns","kind":"rule","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Pronouns Are Often Omitted","rule":"Recoverable pronouns are often omitted, especially direct second-person reference","example":"Why need どこの出身ですか not contain a word meaning you?","answer":"The addressee is understood from context, and omitting an explicit second-person pronoun is natural and often more polite.","explanation":"Names or roles can identify a person when reference must be explicit."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"rule-omit-pronouns","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"Pronouns Are Often Omitted","rule":"Pronouns are omitted when the referent is clear, especially second-person 'you'","example":"Which personal pronoun is especially often omitted in Japanese, since leaving it out is natural and often more polite?","answer":"'You' (second person).","explanation":"Asking どこの出身ですか needs no word for 'you'; the listener is clear from context. Japanese sentences function without explicit pronouns when context is clear:

- ジェニファーです。 — (I am) Jennifer.
- どこの出身ですか？ — Where (are you) from?

Second-person pronouns in particular are often avoided to maintain politeness.

| Pronoun | Reading | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| 私 | わたし / わたくし | Neutral-formal; わたくし is more elegant |
| 僕 | ぼく | Boyish, polite, somewhat masculine |
| 俺 | おれ | Strongly masculine, can sound vulgar |
| うち | うち | Informal/regional; associated with young female speakers in some varieties |

These gender and persona associations are tendencies, not restrictions on who may use a form. Relationship, setting, and regional usage matter.

All second-person pronouns carry some directness and can seem rude. It's common to use names or social roles instead.

| Pronoun | Reading | Nuance |
|---|---|---|
| あなた | あなた | Standard textbook choice, relatively neutral |
| 君 | きみ | Can sound condescending; literary/poetic |
| お前 | おまえ | Rough, masculine; also shows closeness |

## Plural Forms

Add a suffix to make pronouns plural:

- 私 + 達 = 私達 (we)
- Common suffixes: 〜達 (たち), 〜等 (ら), 〜方 (がた), 〜供 (ども)

## 自分 as a Pronoun

<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-jibun","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"自分 as a Pronoun","word":"自分","reading":"じぶん","meaning":"oneself; I or you in particular discourse or regional uses"} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"jibun-perspective","kind":"rule","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"自分 as a Pronoun","rule":"自分 can refer to different people according to construction and variety","example":"In Kansai 自分、何してんの？, who does 自分 refer to?","answer":"The listener (you); in 自分もそう思います it can instead refer to the speaker (I).","explanation":"Its ordinary reflexive meaning and the surrounding discourse still matter; the second-person use is not a nationwide neutral default.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"自分 can refer to different people according to construction and variety","example":"In Kansai 自分、何してんの？, must 自分 refer to the speaker?"}]} -->

自分 (じぶん) can be used as both first and second person:

- **自分**も、そう思います。 — **I** think so too. (gender-neutral first person)
- **自分**、何してんの？ — What are **you** doing? (Kansai dialect, second person)

## Addressing Children

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"child-perspective","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"Addressing Children","rule":"Adults can adopt a child's perspective for self-reference or address","example":"Why can a mother refer to herself as ママ when talking to her young child?","answer":"She uses her role from the child's viewpoint, so ママ means the speaker rather than a third person.","explanation":"Likewise 僕、迷子？ can address a boy with his expected self-reference; it is context-bound child-directed speech, not a general pronoun swap.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"Adults can adopt a child's perspective for self-reference or address","example":"Why can a mother say ママもお腹すいたよ when referring to herself?"}]} -->

With children, adults often use role-based pronouns from the child's perspective:

- **ママ**もお腹すいたよ。 — **I'm** hungry too. (Mom referring to herself as "Mama")
- **僕**、迷子？ — Are **you** lost? (Using the boy's own first-person pronoun to mean "you")
