# Numbers and Counters

<!-- vocab-inventory: {"mode":"explicit"} -->

> **Source:** Content from [Tofugu](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese-grammar/numbers-and-counters/). For personal study.

Japanese has two counting systems — **wago** (native Japanese) and **kango** (Sino-Japanese). Numbers are followed by **counters** (助数詞/じょすうし) that classify what is being counted.

## Counting Numbers

| Number | Kanji | Wago | Kango |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 一 | ひとつ | いち |
| 2 | 二 | ふたつ | に |
| 3 | 三 | みっつ | さん |
| 4 | 四 | よっつ | し/よん |
| 5 | 五 | いつつ | ご |
| 6 | 六 | むっつ | ろく |
| 7 | 七 | ななつ | なな/しち |
| 8 | 八 | やっつ | はち |
| 9 | 九 | ここのつ | きゅう |
| 10 | 十 | とお | じゅう |

For numbers above 10, use kango by combining: 11 = じゅういち, 99 = きゅうじゅうきゅう.

New numbers: 100 = 百 (ひゃく), 1000 = 千 (せん), 10000 = 万 (まん).

## What Are Counters?

<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-pen","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"What Are Counters?","word":"ペン","reading":"ペン","meaning":"pen"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-hon","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"What Are Counters?","word":"本","reading":"ほん","meaning":"book"} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"long-object-counter","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"What Are Counters?","rule":"本 counts long cylindrical objects, not books as bound volumes","example":"Two pens: ペンを二___買う。","answer":"本","explanation":"二本 is にほん. The same kanji 本 is also the lexical noun book, but books are counted with 冊."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"volume-counter","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"What Are Counters?","rule":"冊 counts books and other bound volumes","example":"Two books: 本を二___買う。","answer":"冊","explanation":"二冊 is にさつ. A counter classifies the counted item."} -->

Counters are suffixes that classify what you're counting. You can't just say "two pens" directly — you need the appropriate counter:

- ペンを **二本（にほん）** 買う。 — I'll buy **two pens**. (本 = long objects)
- 本を **二冊（にさつ）** 買う。 — I'll buy **two books**. (冊 = bound volumes)

### 〜つ / 〜個: Universal Counters

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"three-general","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"〜つ / 〜個: Universal Counters","rule":"つ is the general counter for objects","hint":"3; write the native general count with kanji","example":"りんごを___ください。","answer":"三つ","explanation":"三つ is みっつ, suitable for three apples. The native general count is useful for many objects, not unrestricted for every noun."} -->

The native general counting series **一つ through 十** works for 1–10 (ten is とお, without つ). **〜個 (こ)** combines with Sino-Japanese numerals for countable objects. Both are useful general choices for many objects, but neither freely replaces counters for people, animals, or every specialized category:

- ペンを **二つ（ふたつ）** 買う。 — I'll buy two pens.
- ミカンを **五つ（いつつ）** 食べた。 — I ate five oranges.
- レンガが **六個（ろっこ）** ある。 — There are six bricks.

- 〜匹 (ひき) — small to medium animals: ネコが **二匹（にひき）** いる。
- 〜頭 (とう) — large animals: ゾウが **三頭（さんとう）** いる。

## Sentence Patterns with Counters

**Pattern 1:** Counter before the verb (most common)
- ネコが **二匹（にひき）** いる。 — There are two cats.

**Pattern 2:** Counter + の + Noun (treats items as a set)
- **二匹（にひき）の** ネコがいる。 — (The) two cats are there.

The first pattern gives the count adverbially; the second packages the quantity with the noun as a set. Either can refer to newly introduced or already known cats depending on context; の does not automatically make the group definite.
