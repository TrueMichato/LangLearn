# 中 (なか) — Inside / Among

# 
中（なか）

Noun

中（なか）is a word for "inside." It can also mark items for comparison, as in "among" these things, or indicate the circumstances "in which" another action or event takes place.

## The Basics

At its most basic,  中(なか) is a word that refers to the inside of certain boundaries in space. So, "inside the box" is  箱(はこ)の 中(なか), and "inside the house" is  家(いえ)の 中(なか).

  - 箱の**中**

  - **inside** the box

  - 家の**中**

  - **inside** the house

In the same way,  中(なか) can also refer to a middle position, as it is essentially the inside position in regard to both sides, edges, or ends of something. Hence, "middle finger" in Japanese is called  中指(なかゆび) as it's the middle of five fingers.

  - **中**指

  - **middle** finger

Similarly, as a "central" or main location for business activity, the downtown area of a city/town is frequently referred to as 街中/町中（まちなか） in Japanese, because that is essentially the core — even though it may not actually be at the geographic center — of a city/town. 1

  - 街**中**／町**中**

  - the **central area** of a city/town

Getting the gist of  中(なか)? Let's move on to the Patterns of Use!

Note: We also have separate grammar pages for 〜中 (ちゅう) and 〜中(じゅう). If you aren't familiar with these topics, check their dedicated pages as well!

### Patterns of Use

#### 〜の中

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"inside-link","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"〜の中","rule":"の connects the reference noun to its inside","example":"Inside the car: 車___中。","answer":"の","explanation":"車の中 names the car's interior. The reference space can also be figurative, as in 記憶の中."} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"inside-omission","kind":"rule","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"〜の中","rule":"の中 can be omitted when the interior is already understood","example":"Can 車で寝た convey sleeping inside the car without explicitly saying の中?","answer":"Yes, when context makes the interior clear; の中 can be omitted without losing that intended location.","explanation":"Omission depends on context, not on all uses of 中 being redundant."} -->

As seen in the first set of examples, the particle の is used to mark the object whose "inside" you're talking about. For example, if something is inside the car, you can say:

  - 車**の**中

  - inside the car

The location marked by の can be a figurative place, too. For instance, to say that something is *in* your memory, you can use it like this:

  - 記憶**の**中

  - in my memory

That being said, 〜の 中(なか) is often omitted as the context and other elements, such as に or で, can imply that you are talking about the inside of something.

#### (〜の)中 + に/で

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"activity-location","kind":"rule","format":"cloze","priority":"essential","sourceSection":"(〜の)中 + に/で","rule":"で marks where an activity occurs","example":"I slept in the car: 昨日は車の中___寝た。","answer":"で","explanation":"Sleeping is the activity taking place in the car. Different particles can follow  中(なか), but the most common ones are に and で. Between the two, に simply pinpoints a location while で specifies where an event or an activity takes place.

So, if you simply want to indicate the current location of your keys, you would use に: 2

  - カギを車の**中に**忘れた。

  - I left my keys **in** the car.

When talking about a location where an action or an event *takes place* rather than where something *is* or is going, you would use で, like:

  - 昨日は車の**中で**寝た。

  - I slept **in** the car yesterday.

Here, で expresses the location (your car) as a backdrop to your action (sleeping).

If you aren't familiar with the difference between に and で, check out our article に vs で: Which Particle To Choose and Why.

Oh, and again, in both of the examples above 〜の 中(なか) can be omitted without changing the meaning of the sentence because the context and chosen particles imply that you're talking about the inside of the car.

### 中 For "Within Boundaries"

 中(なか) can be used to talk about things demarcated within certain boundaries. Okay, what does that mean, exactly? It's easier to understand with examples, so let's go through a few!

#### 〜の中から For "From Among The Choices…"

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"two-choice-alternative","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"〜の中から For \"From Among The Choices…\"","rule":"Two-choice comparisons commonly use うち or direct から instead of a larger-group framing","example":"What simple expression does this lesson recommend for choosing from just two options?","answer":"この二つから; for comparing the two, この二つのうちで or この二つなら are natural options.","explanation":"The three-or-more framing is a useful teaching preference here, not a universal prohibition on every two-member use of 中."} -->

Used with the particle から, 中(なか) sets a range of choices, typically presented here as three or more. For two choices, direct から or うち is often more natural; this is not an absolute ban on 中 with every two-member set.3
For instance, consider a scenario where you take your child to the toy store. They pick up three toys they really want, but they're only allowed to have one. You can use 〜の 中(なか)から and say:

  - 三つ**の中から**、一つ選んでね。

  - You can pick one of these. 
 (*Literally*: Pick one from among these three.)

If you want to be a little more specific, you can use the particle と to list the items instead of just saying "these three." So if the choices are a toy airplane, a bouncy ball, and a doll, you might say:

  - 飛行機**と**スーパーボール**と**人形**の中から**、好きなの一つ選んでね。

  - You can pick the airplane, the bouncy ball, or the doll. 
 (*Literally*: Pick the one you like from among the airplane, the bouncy ball, and the doll.)

In this case, 〜の 中(なか) can be omitted, because the boundary is already designated by your specifying the items.

#### 〜の中で For "(Out) of…"

<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"rule-naka-comparison-range","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"〜の中で For \"(Out) of…\"","rule":"〜の中で sets the group within which a superlative comparison is made","example":"Which particle follows 'group + の中' to set the range for a comparison such as 'the tallest of the three'?","answer":"で","explanation":"Answering で (de) counts: 三人の中で一番背が高い. うちで can set the same range."} -->

Used with the particle で, 中(なか) can set a range of items for comparison. The examples here use three or more; with just two, うちで or なら is often the more natural choice. 4

This pattern is frequently used to indicate who or what takes the highest or lowest position among the subjects up for comparison. 5

  - タカシは三人の**中で**一番背が高い。

  - Takashi is the tallest **of** the three.

You can also use  中(なか) to distinguish "the one" from the rest of the compared subjects. Like this:

  - 三人の**中で**面白いのはケンタだけだ。

  - **Of** the three, the only amusing one is Kenta.

Keep in mind that in some cases,  三人(さんにん)の 中(なか)で might also mean "*among* the three people" or "*among* the three of us." This usage is often literary and particularly alludes to something having been resolved or kept a secret "*among* the three people" because it still highlights the boundaries and the "within," like:

  - それはもう三人の**中で**解決したことでしょ？

  - That's already been resolved **among** the three of us/them, hasn't it?

Hmm, now I really want to know what went down between the three of them…

#### (〜の)中には For "Some of…"

Used with particle に and particle は,  中(なか) can define a range of subjects for comparison, as in "out of these, some of them…"

For  example, if you're a professor complaining that some of the students in the online class don't ever turn on their cameras, you may use this expression and say:

  - 生徒の**中には**、カメラを付けない者もいる。

  - Some **of** the students don't turn on their cameras.

If the context is clear, you can omit the 〜の part of 〜の 中(なか)には. For example, if it's obvious you're talking about students, you can simply say:

  - **中には**、カメラを付けない者もいる。

  - Some **of** (the students) don't turn on their cameras.

I guess it's pretty common nowadays as online classes become more mainstream!

## Beyond The Basics

### 中 For "Under Certain Circumstances"

<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-arashi","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"中 For \"Under Certain Circumstances\"","word":"嵐","reading":"あらし","meaning":"storm"} -->
<!-- vocab-card: {"id":"vocab-samayou","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"中 For \"Under Certain Circumstances\"","word":"さまよう","reading":"さまよう","meaning":"to wander; to roam"} -->
<!-- grammar-card: {"id":"circumstance-path","kind":"concept","format":"qa","priority":"optional","sourceSection":"中 For \"Under Certain Circumstances\"","rule":"を can mark circumstances conceived as a path through which one moves","example":"Why can を follow a circumstance phrase such as 嵐の中 when the verb is a motion verb like 歩く?","answer":"The circumstances are treated like a space or path moved through, not as a direct object being changed.","explanation":"を can be omitted in these expressions. お忙しい中 also frames an action against difficult circumstances.","legacyAliases":[{"rule":"を can mark circumstances conceived as a path through which one moves","example":"Why can を follow 嵐の中 in 嵐の中を歩いた?"}]} -->

 中(なか) can also be used to describe the circumstances under which an action takes place; i.e. "under certain circumstances."

When referring to physical actions, the circumstances  中(なか) describes are often related to bad weather, and the action itself is typically a motion verb, such as 歩く (walk), 走る (run), さまよう (wander/roam), or 来る (come).

  - 嵐の**中**(を)[歩いた・走った・さまよった・来た]。

  - I [walked・ran・wandered・came] **in** the middle of a storm.

You may have noticed that the particle を often comes after  中(なか), though it can be omitted. This is because the circumstance can be likened to a path you can go through, and "the path" is marked by the particle を in Japanese.

Sometimes, bad weather is figuratively described, as in:

  - お足元の悪い**中**(を)、お越し頂き有難うございます。

  - Thank you for coming in spite of the bad weather.

 (*Literally*: Thank you for coming in spite of the wet and muddy ground around your feet.)

This is a common way to start a speech at an event that just so happened to be held on a day with lousy weather.

Other than bad weather, you can also use  中(なか) to refer to negative circumstances, like:

  - バッシングの**中**(を)、歯を食いしばって頑張った。

  - Despite the bashing, I gritted my teeth and worked through it.

  - お忙しい**中**(を)、お出で頂き有難うございます。

  - Thank you for taking time out of your busy schedule to be here.

 街中(まちなか) and  町中(まちなか) can also be read まちじゅう. In these cases, the meaning changes to "[all over・throughout] the city/town." ↩

    

    - 
      
In a situation similar to asking yourself where you left your keys while trying to remember, you can use either に *or* で to mark the location, as in 車の**中[に・で]**忘れたんだ！(I left my keys **in** the car!) With に, the focus is on where the keys *are*, whereas with で it's on *where you perform the action* (though it's probably an involuntary action). In this case, having left your keys somewhere. ↩

    

    - 
      
When the are only two things to choose from, you would directly combine 二つ and から, as in この二つから (out of the two choices), or use どちらか or どっちか (either one) with から, as in どちらかから or どっちかから (from either one of the choices). ↩

    

    - 
      
With only two things to compare, 〜のうちで is often more natural, as in この二つのうちで、どっちがいい? (Which one do you prefer between the two?), or you can use the conditional form なら or だったら, as in この二つ[なら・だったら]、どっちがいい? (If it's between these two, which one do you prefer?). These are useful preferences, not a blanket prohibition on 中 with every two-member group. ↩

    

    - 
      
In these comparison examples, 中 (〜の中で) can be replaced with うち, another word for "inside": タカシは三人の**うちで**一番背が高い。(Takashi is the tallest **of** the three.) or 三人の**うちで**面白いのはケンタだけだ。(**Of** the three, the only amusing one is Kenta.). Do not assume the two words are interchangeable in every other construction. ↩
